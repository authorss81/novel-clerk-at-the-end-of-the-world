# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline — The Omission

Batch number: 0001
Chapter range: 1–10
Story time: C.Y. 487, the second week of the rain season, about fourteen days of story time. The transfer review sits off-page inside that span, is confirmed rather than contested, and Owen is not on the notice for it.

## Batch Goal

Take an apparent clerical omission and turn it into a public, docketed question: whether a neighborhood can file its own return. By the end of the batch, Lower Lock is on the docket, Owen's name is on the docket beside it, the crossing's legal status is lapsing, and the Council has closed the local-return route.

## Starting Condition

Owen Rusk is a senior copy clerk at the Registry's River Desk with eleven weeks of clean quarterly run behind him and no authority to change a status. Lower Lock is his home ward, which he has never treated as a professional fact. The Ninth Weir is load-restricted. Civic Zero is a calendar item thirteen years out. Owen's private fear is that the Office is careless; his private competence is that it is not.

## Starting Relationships

Owen trusts Ada's judgment about bridges and not her judgment about herself. Owen respects Pike and has never had to. Owen and Nell have not spoken in two years, and the last thing they said to each other was in a hearing room. Pell is new enough that Owen still believes seniority is a kind of argument.

## Batch Shape

- **Beginning (Ch 1–3):** ordinary work, the ward, the supervisor. The genre promise arrives in the second chapter: a living man who is officially a person who was never there.
- **Escalation (Ch 4):** the tool is offered by the person Owen is most wrong about, and the offer changes what he is trying to do.
- **Midpoint reversal (Ch 5):** the record is not false. The case reclassifies from error to scope, and the only remaining lever is a district return the Registry has spent ninety years calling nonbinding.
- **Escalation and cost (Ch 6–8):** Owen authors the trap, a vote ratifies it, and the crossing is closed to loads by a finding that is probably correct.
- **Batch climax (Ch 9):** Owen's best idea is refused by the junior clerk who is right, and the ward's steward saves the case with the cheapest lawful act in the volume — a petition.
- **Aftermath (Ch 10):** consequences land, a circular closes the route, a name will not come and then comes back anyway, and a brass tab in a drawer he is now trusted with names a ward that officially ceased to exist.

## Batch Midpoint (Chapter 5)

The thirteen names are not denied by omission. The transfer was *accepted* eleven months ago on a signed, witnessed, sealed **receipt** attributed to Tal Sarr, a Lower Lock resident who drowned in the spring flood and whose authority covered his own household only. The receipt is validly made; there is no version of this file in which the entry itself is wrong. Owen's error-correction path is dead, and he closes it himself: the one defect he can actually prove — the annex schedule's two-day backdating — he withdraws from his own filing, because the moment the Office can correct a date it closes the file as a clerical matter and keeps the scope. He then files the charge of scope in his own name and proposes the one thing that can work: Lower Lock performs a duty and claims it as its own.

## Batch Climax (Chapter 9)

Owen's short-form attestation — accurate, fast, and unwitnessed — is refused by Junia Pell in a corridor, and the window is lost. In the same session, Marguerite Bray files her own petition for a hearing in her own words, and the clerk on duty cannot refuse a request for a hearing without writing a reason. It is entered, not granted. The Office must answer the petition in writing within seven days — a clock that runs to the answer, not to any hearing, and lands in Batch 0002.

## Required Character Changes

- **Owen:** learns that his precision can author a trap, and that being right was the thing that made him dangerous. He ends the batch less competent-looking and more useful, and he does not yet have a method for the difference.
- **Owen and Nell:** reestablish contact as adversaries who recognize each other's work. She refuses to be his witness, his advocate, or his relief. He accepts the terms and keeps them.
- **Owen and Ada:** she refuses to be the subject of his rescue and refuses his money. He is present as a copyist who stayed, not as a protector.
- **Owen and Pell:** she corrects him in public and in private and will not become his instrument. He resents her and begins, unwillingly, to think with her question.
- **Owen and Pike:** Pike stops managing him and starts using him, which is worse.

## Required Power Changes

No stage change. Owen remains a **Copy Clerk (Stage 0)** and must not be written as signing anything, holding anything, or perceiving a closed record. He gains: a private ledger kept outside the Office book, one continuance inquiry filed in his own name, the working terms of a district return, and one signed statement of his own part entered in the docket against his own drafting. He pays: his posting, his standing in his own ward, the fast route to a fix, and the belief that accuracy is a defence. His **first qualified filing as an assisting clerk belongs to Batch 0002 (Ch 11–20)** and must not be credited here.

## Required World Reveals

- Unfiling is real, local, and already happening to named people.
- "No local record" is a positive status, not a gap, and it can be entered without a single resident's knowledge.
- A duty can be validly received *for* a community without that community ever being asked.
- A district return is a legal object with a named scope, current acts, independent witnesses, a seven-day review, an appeal route, and separately consenting adult branches. The Registry dislikes it, not because it is invalid but because it is slow. Lower Lock's instrument is a **district return** throughout, matching its final file number 487-LL; "local return" is the general category word for any community-filed return and is never a second instrument in this volume.
- The claim a ward can make about a structure is not "the structure is safe." It is "we perform this duty, here is the act, here is the review date."
- Form Zero, the Common Return, is the only instrument that could supersede a stability order. It has no valid signature path. It is due in thirteen years.
- An unattributed countersignature review standard is already in force at Docketford by Chapter 3 and cannot be traced to any Docketford office. The Provisional Council's circular in Chapter 10 names itself as the issuer, states the countersignature requirement, and sets the deadline that will close the route. **Who issued the Chapter 3 standard, and why, is reserved to Batch 0003.**

## Open Questions After the Batch

- Why was a dead man's household receipt accepted as authority over a whole ward, and who checked?
- Eleven months is a long time for a transfer entry to sit. What happened in those eleven months, and who was waiting?
- Why does a countersignature standard exist at all, who issued the Docketford copy in Chapter 3, and who is it protecting?
- Six of the thirteen erased entries are children or people unable to answer. Lower Lock has no protective-return structure and cannot build one alone.
- Four households voted against the transfer and still live inside it. What is owed to people who are right and outvoted?
- What is the brass tab in the drawer Owen is now trusted with, and why does the index say *never opened*?

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0001 — The Fourth River Desk

POV: Owen Rusk, third person limited.

Location/time: The Registry's River Desk, Docketford; C.Y. 487, second week of the rain season; before the lamps are lit through the last of the afternoon light.

Immediate goal: Get the Fourth Desk's weekly continuance batch copied and countersigned with not one correction, the way it has run clean for eleven weeks.

Resistance: The batch arrives short, and not by accident of staffing. Lower Lock's return is not missing and not late. It has been replaced by a *transfer* entry that assigns the ward's maintenance acts — the crossing, the Fetter Lane pump, the granary — to a district return administered from a Mill Ward annex. The transfer carries two Registry witnesses. It carries none from Lower Lock. The receipt itself is sound: signed, witnessed, sealed, and received on the date it was received. But the **annex schedule** that travels with it carries an effective date two days earlier than the earliest date any part of that schedule could lawfully take effect.

Information revealed: The five conditions of a supported fact enter through work, not exposition — an apprenticeship filing, a widow's name change, a ferry-inspection copy. Owen handles all three competently and without thinking, which is the point. A roll is evidence, not truth. A roll can also be an instrument, and this one has been used as one.

Action or decision: Owen refuses the routine countersignature on the Fourth Desk batch and marks the entry "referred for local confirmation," which is a real procedural act with a real cost. Then he starts his own ledger in a pocket notebook — his own book, not the Office's — and puts the transfer's number in it with the date it was received and the date it takes effect.

Emotional change: The private, orderly pleasure of a clean desk curdles into the specific dread he has felt exactly twice before, both times in flood years: something is being moved before anyone is asked to look at it. He makes a list. The sharpened pencil stays in the case; he uses a dull one.

Power or resource change: No magic, no authority, no hold. He acquires one thing that is not power and is the only thing he builds in this chapter: a private record that the Office does not control.

Continuity fact to preserve: Lower Lock is a real, inhabited ward of roughly two hundred and forty residents on the Office's last accepted roll. The Ninth Weir is load-restricted since the spring. The transfer names a Mill Ward return. Pike is Owen's direct supervisor. The Fourth River Desk produces the weekly continuance. The transfer's **receipt is validly made and cannot be corrected**; the only defect anywhere in it is the annex schedule's two-day backdating, which Owen proves here and deliberately withdraws from his own inquiry in Chapter 5.

Ending type: Discovery.

Next-chapter pull: Something in this building needed the ward to look already moved two days before anyone was told. Owen goes home to Lower Lock that evening to find out how much the ward already knows.

### Chapter 0002 — What the Ward Knows

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: Lower Lock; dusk into the small hours; the rain begins during the chapter.

Immediate goal: Learn whether Lower Lock has been told about the transfer, get his sister to stay out of the ward meeting, and take the problem back to the Office alone.

Resistance: The ward has been told, softly. A letter asks residents to "consolidate claims at the Mill Ward annex for better protection," and it does not mention a crossing. Some families are relieved, because consolidation carries a slate roof and a pump grant. Ada is not relieved, and Ada is also not the person who gets to decide: the meeting is her crew's wage meeting and she is going. Owen's attempt to run the room fails in the first five minutes. He is told, in front of his own neighbors, to sit down.

Information revealed: The Ninth Weir's real condition — a cracked abutment, load restriction, a repair estimate nobody will fund. The ward's actual economy: the crossing markers, the granary, the pump, one seed stall. And unfiling as a lived condition: **Cobb Rennie**, fifty-eight, has been off the Registry roll for two years while living at the same address, known by everyone, unable to draw a pension because no record places him there.

Action or decision: Owen does the one thing he is genuinely good at. He sits in the ward hall with **Marguerite Bray's** paper roll — sixty-one, keeping Lower Lock's roll by hand because the Office's is useless to her — and copies every name, act, and date the ward can prove, with two witnesses per entry where the ward can supply them. It takes the whole night. It is the first time his skill is aimed at a case he cannot win by being right.

Emotional change: Ada tells him, flatly, that he does not get to be the reason she is safe, and Owen understands it as a fact about filing rather than about courage. He understands it. He does not change what he does.

Power or resource change: He acquires the ward's own record, hand-kept and unanswerable by any Registry page, and his first real grasp of the difference between what a ward can *prove in the Office's format* and what it has actually *performed*.

Continuity fact to preserve: Marguerite Bray keeps Lower Lock's paper roll. Cobb Rennie has been off the Registry roll for two years. The ward's duties are the crossing, the Fetter Lane pump, the granary, and the seed stall. Ada's crew is paid from crossing and granary work. Ada refused Owen's money and his plan to handle it.

Ending type: Discovery, with cost.

Next-chapter pull: At dawn Owen finds thirteen names in the transfer's annex schedule marked "no local record" — not "missing." A positive statement is a statement someone vouched for. Owen takes the schedule to Pike and asks who stood behind it.

### Chapter 0003 — Two Witnesses

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: The copying hall, the Registry's interview alcove, and the stair; the following day.

Immediate goal: Identify the two Registry witnesses on the "no local record" schedule by ordinary request, and keep the ward's hand-copied roll out of Pike's hands.

Resistance: Pike is calm, correct, and gives Owen exactly what the rules require. The witnesses are genuine Registry officers acting under a current review standard. The two Registry witnesses on the "no local record" schedule are the same two officers who witnessed the transfer, and neither of them has any particular memory of Lower Lock. Pike's real resistance is not denial; it is arithmetic and a conscience he is using as a tool: eleven wards have failing returns this quarter, and a stability order has to be actionable by Friday. He says the sentence that carries the rest of the volume — "Every district I stabilize is a district that does not drown somewhere else" — and Owen files it away as an insult. Then Pike offers Owen a lateral transfer at better pay, on the grounds that he is wasted on a routine desk. Owen refuses it, which is the first time he chooses to be poorer.

Information revealed: Office witnesses can be Registry officers, and their separability of interest is thin when one review standard produced all of them. There is a countersignature review standard already in force at Docketford, and the copy in front of Owen carries no Docketford number and answers to no office any clerk in the hall can name. A person can be "no local record" and still be owed.

Action or decision: Owen files a **continuance inquiry** in his own name. It is not authority; it is a record, and it is public, and it says out loud that a Registry clerk doubts the Office. He does not file it against Pike. He files it against the schedule. He also learns, from Pell, that the inspection log he spent a day mourning was always available to any clerk who asked for it by its proper name.

Emotional change: Owen's pride curdles. He has spent eleven years believing his precision was a form of honesty, and Pell's question shows him that his precision had exactly one reader in mind, and the ward was not in the room.

Power or resource change: A continuance inquiry starts a visible docket and names its filer. Owen now holds two things he chose: a private ledger and a public doubt. He still cannot sign.

Continuity fact to preserve: A continuance inquiry is filed in the filer's name, is public, and suspends nothing. A countersignature review standard is in force at Docketford from this chapter; it is **unattributed here** and is named to the Provisional Council only by the Chapter 10 circular. The two Registry witnesses on the schedule are the same two officers who witnessed the transfer. Pike has said that a stability order has to be actionable by Friday; no such order has been signed yet. "No local record" is a positive status. Pell is twenty-two, junior, and is the first person in the Office to ask who consented.

Ending type: Decision and discovery.

Next-chapter pull: The inquiry's notices go out, including one to the Mill Ward annex, four streets from Arbour Kitchen. Three Lower Lock families are already there, and they did not go there for Owen.

### Chapter 0004 — Arbour Kitchen

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: Arbour Kitchen, Mill Ward; late afternoon into evening; steady rain.

Immediate goal: Get Nell Arbour to stop representing Lower Lock without its consent, and to bring her whatever evidence she has.

Resistance: She has been representing them for eleven days, informally, because the people came to her and because she no longer has an office in which to hide. She refuses his premise, his method, and his request. Her refusal is professionally exact: she asks whether the Office's schedule mentions the pump, the granary, the seed stall, or only the crossing. It mentions only the crossing. Owen has spent a week arguing about a ward when the filing is about a structure, and Nell is entitled to say so in front of a full room. She also refuses to be his witness — not because she does not believe him, but because a claimant advocate who becomes one ward's person stops being a person other claimants can use, and because she can tell he wants somebody to lean on.

Information revealed: Nell has seen consolidation three times before, in the Upland fringe and along the Claim Market, and every time it was lawful. The Mill Ward return that received the Ninth Weir's maintenance has no crew, no funds, and no way to reach the weir. And Nell's own history, offered without self-pity: she was pushed out of the Registry over a child whose family a flood had dispersed, and she learned exactly what a precise letter does to a precise person.

Action or decision: Owen stops arguing and asks a different question than the one he came with — not how to correct the schedule, but what the ward would need in order to file at all. Nell answers for two hours, and the answer is the sentence that reframes the whole volume: a ward does not get its name back by proving it existed. A ward gets its name back by performing a duty nobody else will perform, and having that performance witnessed by people it does not control.

Emotional change: Owen's first real humiliation arrives as respect, which is worse. He ends the evening not winning anything, and slightly more useful than he arrived.

Power or resource change: He leaves with the working terms of a district return: named scope, current act, independent witnesses, seven-day review, appeal route, separately consenting adult branches. This is the tool the whole volume runs on, and he was handed it by the person he has been arguing with.

Continuity fact to preserve: Arbour Kitchen is in Mill Ward. Nell is a former Registry examiner, gone after a dispersed-child case, and has her own cases and her own refusals. She will not be a witness for Lower Lock in this volume. Consolidation is a real and previously lawful tool. The receiving district return has no crew, no funds, and no access to the weir.

Ending type: Earned hook.

Next-chapter pull: Owen goes back to Lower Lock with the terms of a district return and finds the ward cannot supply independent witnesses for its own claims, because the people who would witness are the people who would be witnessed.

### Chapter 0005 — Nobody Else Will Perform It — Batch Midpoint

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: The Lower Lock ward hall and the weir path; one night; heavy rain.

Immediate goal: Get the ward to accept that it can file a district return, and get its first witnessed acts onto paper.

Resistance, and the reversal: The ward can supply acts but not witnesses, and the answer is worse. Owen arrives intending to prove the thirteen "no local record" entries false. Marguerite instead produces the ward's ledger of delivery: the transfer was never delivered as a proposal. It was entered as an *acceptance*. Eleven months ago the Office received something signed, witnessed, and sealed by **Tal Sarr**, a Lower Lock resident, on behalf of the ward. Sarr's authority covered his own household. He drowned in the spring flood. Marguerite never countersigned and was never asked, because the entry was a receipt of a claim, not a ward act requiring the ward's agreement. So the receipt stands, and the only thing in eleven months of paperwork that can be corrected is the two days Owen proved in Chapter 1. Ada's reading is brutal and correct: "Somebody didn't lie. That's worse."

Information revealed: A duty can be validly received for a community that never agreed to it. A person's own return and a duty imposed on his behalf are different instruments, and the Office has been using the first to do the work of the second. Owen's "who consented" question finally has an answer, and the answer is a dead man who consented lawfully to something narrower than what was done with it.

Action or decision: Owen abandons the error route in writing, and the first thing he gives up is the part he was right about. The annex schedule's two-day backdating is a real defect, provable in a morning, and correcting it would let the Office repair the date, close his inquiry as a clerical matter, and leave the scope untouched. He withdraws that proof from his own filing on the record and says why out loud, which is the first time he chooses a worse argument on purpose. He then adds a supplementary line withdrawing the charge of error and stating the charge of **scope** — an entry validly made and then used beyond the signer's authority — which makes the case far harder to win and impossible to file quietly. Then he tells the ward the only thing that can work: file a district return, perform the duty, and claim the crossing's status as the ward's own act instead of the Office's assignment. The ward understands the price immediately. Performing a duty means being responsible for it, and a ward that files can lose.

Emotional change: Owen's central belief cracks in the worst possible direction. He came to save a record and finds he must instead propose responsibility to the people the record was discarding. He is frightened by how much better that is. Marguerite does not thank him for the thing he proved. She asks him what it was worth, and he has to answer out loud: two days, and three requests refused inside them.

Power or resource change: The case reclassifies from error to scope, which lowers the Office's willingness to fix it. Owen has his first working use of the term "district return" as something to be *made* rather than quoted. The backdating defect stays uncorrected on the record by his own choice, and the three refusals logged inside that window stay logged. He still signs nothing.

Continuity fact to preserve: The transfer of the Ninth Weir's maintenance was recorded eleven months ago on a signed and sealed entry attributed to Tal Sarr, now dead, whose authority covered his own household only. The **receipt is validly made and cannot be corrected**; the only correctable defect is the annex schedule's two-day backdating, which Owen withdraws from his own inquiry rather than use. Marguerite Bray never countersigned and was never asked. Owen's continuance inquiry alleges scope, not error. A ward can perform and file a return without anyone's permission, and filing exposes the ward to loss. Ivy Sarr, twelve, is Tal's granddaughter and lives with an aunt; he had no authority over her.

Ending type: Reversal.

Next-chapter pull: A ward preparing to file needs a reason to stop. Pike supplies one by scheduling a ward ratification meeting — and the notice is assigned to Owen to draft.

### Chapter 0006 — Notice

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: The River Desk, the Glass Stair antechamber, the ward hall; a day and a half. Political pressure.

Immediate goal: Draft a notice of ward ratification that is accurate, limited, and survivable.

Resistance: Nobody hands Owen a fatal document. A committee instructs him, and the instructions are reasonable: state the scope, the benefit, and the deadline, and do not put crossing language in a ward notice, because ward notices do not carry crossing language. Every word he writes is true. The consequence is that a majority of the ward's households sign a witnessed, sealed consent to transfer the crossing's *maintenance duty* to a district that cannot maintain it, believing they are signing for a roof and a pump. Ada finds his draft on the hall table before the meeting, reads it twice, and asks him one question: which person does this letter treat as disposable? He cannot answer. He tries to amend it, and is refused, because the notice is already posted and posting is the act.

Information revealed: Institutions do not have to lie in order to do this. A ward will trade a duty for a grant, and the trade is often rational for the people making it. Owen's precision is not a defence of anything. Nell attends as an advocate, is invited to observe rather than speak, and says the sentence that will define him: "You write like a man who's already decided who the sacrifice is."

Action or decision: Owen does not hide. He stands up in the meeting as a Registry clerk, states plainly that the notice omits the crossing consequence, and is removed from the room by the hall warden. Ada finishes the meeting without him. The transfer is ratified nine to four, witnessed and sealed. The four who voted no are the ones the rest of the volume will turn out to need.

Emotional change: Owen's flaw is named by other people before he can name it. He does not collapse. He goes quiet and starts a list, which is his tell, and Ada sees the list and does not comment on it.

Power or resource change: The transfer becomes fully supported, which strengthens the state's position and weakens the ward's. Owen gains a public humiliation and a fact he cannot unlearn. No magic change; his standing in his own ward falls to nearly nothing, and the ward now knows the Office lies in the shape of accuracy.

Continuity fact to preserve: The notice was accurate and omitted the crossing consequence. The transfer was ratified at a ward meeting by nine votes to four, witnessed and sealed. Ada voted against with three others. Owen was removed from the meeting by the hall warden. The notice text will be entered in evidence later in the volume.

Ending type: Cost.

Next-chapter pull: The transfer now stands, and the crossing's status will lapse with the ward's own return. If Lower Lock files first, the crossing's status follows the ward's act instead. They have seven days — and the ward has just voted that it would rather have the roof.

### Chapter 0007 — Performing It

POV: Owen Rusk, third person limited. Ada Rusk and Marguerite Bray are present and drive the scene, but they are not POV carriers.

Location/time: The ward hall, the Fetter Lane pump, the granary, the weir marker; one day and one night. Training and controlled experimentation.

Immediate goal: Build a district return for Lower Lock that a Registry could actually accept, using only acts the ward can perform tonight.

Resistance: Every condition is inconvenient. Independent witnesses: the ward's own people are interested parties, so each branch needs a witness not carrying that branch, and the return needs **two** living observers with separable interests before it is a filing at all. Pell arrives, unasked, having followed the docket, and signs as the first outside witness — which is exactly right and career-damaging for her, and neither she nor Owen says so. The second is **Wend Voss**, 51, the counter clerk at the Mill Ward annex, the man whose counter took the transfer in. His interest is separable from Lower Lock's only because his district inherited the crossing's cost, and he separates it in writing, in the margin, where a later reviewer can find it. He signs what he personally saw performed and nothing else. Separately consenting adult branches: three households refuse to sign anything sharing a page with another adult's name, and they are right, so the return is drafted branch by branch, taking four times as long and meaning one withdrawn household narrows the return instead of destroying it. Scope: Owen and Ada argue for hours about the word "crossing," and Ada wins. The return does not claim the Ninth Weir is open or safe. It claims the ward *performs maintenance of a named public crossing* — a claim about a duty, not a structure.

Information revealed: The distinction between a claim to a status ("the crossing is open") and a claim to a duty ("the crossing is maintained; here is the act, here is the review date") is the actual hinge of the volume. A return can be narrowed without collapsing. Two witnesses clear the threshold and are not equally clean: Pell is a Registry officer, which is a question any hostile reviewer can ask about a ward's duty instrument, and the ward's own defect — an outsider shaping what it owes — is repeated in a new form. Neither Pell nor Owen pretends otherwise. And Owen perceives something he cannot yet use: the transfer file in Marguerite's hands produces no marginalia at all. A thin file answers him. A closed file is silent, and the silence is information he cannot read.

Action or decision: Owen writes the scope in the ward's words rather than the Registry's, and makes the ward's language the operative text. He also decides, against his own instinct and without being asked, to record the four dissenting households' objection *inside* the return rather than in a separate minute, so the return carries its own dissent. It costs him the appearance of unity. He keeps it.

Emotional change: Owen's first relief that is not his to manage: the ward is doing the work and telling him what it is. Ada calls him "copyist" and then, after the pump night, "copyist who stayed," which is not affection and is worth more.

Power or resource change: A real district return draft exists with named scope, separate adult branches, a seven-day review, an appeal route, two outside witnesses, and a recorded dissent. Owen can perceive marginalia on thin files and gets nothing from held ones. No stage change. Nobody has accepted anything.

Continuity fact to preserve: Lower Lock's draft return claims a maintenance duty for the Ninth Weir crossing, not its safety or availability. **Pell and Wend Voss have signed as the two outside witnesses**, satisfying the two-observer threshold; both witnessed acts only and neither touched the drafting, and Pell's independence is contestable on the record because she is a Registry officer. Three households declined to share a page with another adult's name. The engineer's report on the weir is held by the Registry and will not be released without a countersignature.

Ending type: Discovery.

Next-chapter pull: Pike does not attack the return. He does something honest — he accepts the engineer's report into the file, and on the same afternoon issues a load-restriction suspension on the Ninth Weir pending structural assessment. It is defensible. It is probably right. It removes the strongest thing the ward can claim.

### Chapter 0008 — Load Restriction

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: The Ninth Weir at dusk, the rising river, and the Registry's notice of suspension; one night and one morning. Action and survival.

Immediate goal: Get the ward's return ready before the crossing's status lapses, and keep every living person out of the water.

Resistance: Pike's suspension rests on a real engineering finding. The abutment crack has moved eleven inches since the spring inspection, and the Registry's own engineer would not sign a claim that the crossing is open. This is not villainy; it is a competent official declining to certify something false. Then the material cost arrives: a record has to be checked against a structure at the same moment, which means a night inspection in rising water, and Owen's marginalia only functions when he is comparing a genuine record with the physical act in front of him. Ada's crew sets the marker and a young apprentice goes into the spillway. Owen is not a swimmer and is not a rescuer. What he does is keep the witness count honest — calling out who is standing where, and refusing to let anyone claim to have seen what they did not see. Voss is on the bank with a lantern, counting the same people Owen is counting, for an entirely different reason. The crew gets the apprentice out. The apprentice is **Dory Anstruther**, nineteen, who is one of the thirteen the Office says has no local record.

Information revealed: A suspension preserves a status and an appeal window. It does not make a structure safe and it does not remove the people who still need the crossing. A suspended crossing's legal status can be preserved as a *hold* rather than an opening, and that narrow claim is the one the ward can actually make. And a young man pulled out of the water is alive, and simultaneously does not exist.

Action or decision: Owen faces his worst temptation in the volume. He drafts an engineer's continuity statement that would convert "not certified open" into "supported as maintained" — a small forgery dressed as a summary. He finishes it. He destroys it himself, in front of Pell, and says out loud what he was doing and why, which is the first time he admits he wanted to be the person who carried it. Then the ward does the honest version: it narrows the return to a preservation claim on the crossing's last supported status, with a repair commitment and a review date, and accepts that the crossing stays closed to loads. Ada's crew is the crew that will have to make the claim true, and she says so before anyone asks.

Emotional change: Owen is frightened of himself in a specific and useful way. He is not proud of his restraint. He is ashamed, and he knows that shame is not a system.

Power or resource change: The return is narrowed to preserve a legal status and an appeal window only. The crossing is not safe, funded, repaired, or usable. Owen loses the ability to pretend a summary is a fact, and gains the ward's willingness to file something weaker than what he wanted. Visible cost: Dory's injury, the crew's unpaid hours, and a great deal of the ward's remaining trust.

Continuity fact to preserve: The Ninth Weir is under a load-restriction suspension, technically defensible and in force. The crossing is closed to loads. Lower Lock's return is now a preservation claim on the crossing's last supported status with a repair duty and a review date. Dory Anstruther, nineteen, is injured in the spillway and is one of the thirteen "no local record" entries; his treatment record and the Office's schedule are now two different descriptions of the same man. Owen destroyed a continuity statement he had written; Pell witnessed the destruction.

Ending type: Cost.

Next-chapter pull: A preservation claim has to be entered, and only a qualified signer can enter it. Owen cannot sign. The engineer will not. The four dissenting households are the only people with standing who have not already signed the transfer. The route left is a hearing, and a hearing requires a petition.

### Chapter 0009 — Petition — Batch Climax

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: The Glass Stair public appeal hall and the River Desk; one long day. Political pressure, defeat, and a hard-won procedural foothold.

Immediate goal: Enter a petition for a hearing on the docket before the crossing's status lapses, using the ward's own witnesses.

Resistance: Owen's shortcut, and it is exactly his shortcut. A short-form attestation in his own hand, summarizing the ward's returns, ready for a countersignature. It is fast, it is accurate in every particular, and it is unsupported by a single living witness who is not also a beneficiary. Pell refuses to copy it. She does not refuse loudly and does not explain in front of anyone; in the corridor she asks him whether he is filing the ward's claim or his own version of it, and he says the ward's claim, and she says that is exactly the difference, and goes back to her tray. He loses the window. This is the batch's real defeat: he was right, and being right was the thing that made him dangerous.

The pivot comes from Marguerite, who has been in the corridor the whole time and does not want to be in any room. She files her own petition, in her own words, in her own name, as the ward's steward, and asks only for a hearing. The clerk on duty cannot refuse a request for a hearing without writing a reason, and nobody at the Office wants to be the one to write it. It is entered. Not granted: entered. That is all, and it is real.

Information revealed: A petition for a hearing is a request, and refusing one is a written act with consequences — the cheapest lawful act available, and the one nobody thought of because nobody had been in the position to need it. Owen's name is now on the record in a case he cannot win by being correct. And the Office must answer the petition in writing within seven days — a clock that runs to the **answer**, not to any hearing. That is why the volume's real hearing can sit forty chapters downstream without the clock expiring, and it is not a reprieve: what the answer eventually gives is a date instead of a hearing.

Action or decision: Owen accepts the lost window and does the harder thing: he asks Marguerite, not Ada, to be the filer, and accepts the position of petitioner rather than advocate. He has Pell enter the earlier record in the docket as part of the same file, so the Office's transfer and the ward's petition sit in one drawer. And he signs a statement of his own part in the notice — his drafting, the omission, his name — and files it. It is the first time his name goes on a public document adverse to the Office and to his own work, and it will be the exhibit that puts a clerk's hand inside the transfer's foundation. The transfer review he was never on the notice for has already happened; it confirmed the transfer, and Pell's entry of the earlier record is the only thing he gets for missing it.

Emotional change: Owen is corrected in public by a junior clerk, loses the thing he is best at, and finds that the loss leaves him lighter than it should. He is not humble. He is disoriented, and he goes back to his desk and starts a list, which is his tell.

Power or resource change: A petition is on the docket, filed by Marguerite Bray with Owen Rusk as co-petitioner, answer due in seven days. The transfer and the return sit in one file. Owen has no new authority, one less reputation, and one durable thing he cannot take back: his own signed statement.

Continuity fact to preserve: The petition was entered in the Glass Stair docket on Marguerite Bray's own name with Owen Rusk as co-petitioner; answer due in seven days. Owen's signed statement of his part in the notice is in the file. The short-form attestation was never filed. The transfer review has occurred off-page and confirmed the transfer; Owen was not on the notice and did not attend. Pell remains under no obligation to Owen.

Ending type: Reversal and cost, with a foothold won the lawful way.

Next-chapter pull: Seven days, a pump that has already failed, a ward that has just learned what it has agreed to, and an office that must write an answer. And a circular arrives from Concord Hall: the Provisional Council now requires central countersignature of every local return, and no community return will be accepted after the present review.

### Chapter 0010 — Unfiled Correspondence — Batch Aftermath

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: The River Desk and Lower Lock; two days. Recovery, consequences, and a hook.

Immediate goal: Keep working, keep his name off the file as much as is now possible, and work out what he can actually carry.

Resistance: Consequences land in order. Pike suspends him from the Fourth River Desk and reassigns him to the tray of unfiled correspondence, a punishment that is also the only posting in the building where nobody watches what he reads; Owen recognizes the ambiguity instantly and hates it, which is the right response to Pike. The Fetter Lane pump fails in the night and Ada's crew runs it by hand for eleven hours; Ada will not take Owen's money, and the ward's granary reserve is thinner than anyone admitted in public. The four dissenting households are in an impossible position, having voted against the transfer and still living inside it. Nell comes to the desk, not to comfort him, with a correction and a condition: she will help with the return's appeal route, and she will not be its witness, and she will not be his. And Pell, unasked, hands him the six names among the thirteen that are children or people unable to answer, because he asked once and never followed up — those names need protective returns, two unrelated witnesses, a current care act, an appeal route, and Lower Lock has none of those in place. That problem is live, unsolved, and cannot be solved this week.

Information revealed: Owen's first glimpse of Form Zero, through a clerk's aside at the seal stair: the only instrument that could supersede a stability order is the Common Return, it has no valid signature path, and it is not due for thirteen years. The counterweight to that aside is that no such order has been signed yet — Pike has said all quarter that he may have to sign one, and the clerk treats it as routine. The Council's countersignature standard has hardened into a deadline that will close the local-return route entirely. And a suspended clerk with a poisoned name is still a useful person, which is its own kind of humiliation.

Action or decision: Owen accepts the reassignment without appealing it, because appealing it would put Marguerite's petition back in front of the Deputy Registrar. He writes down what he can carry: the ward's acts, the four dissents, the six names needing protective returns, and — as an experiment rather than a plan — one household he is willing to be responsible for. He tells Nell one true thing about his part in the notice and nothing else, and she gives him the sentence that becomes the volume's engine: you are going to have to ask people to be responsible in public, and you are not going to be able to do it for them. Then, in the drawer he is now trusted with, he finds a brass tab that is not Docketford's: **ALDER ROW — 461**, a ward officially closed four streets from where he sleeps. He is not permitted to open the drawer. He does not open it. He writes the number in his own ledger and puts the pencil down.

Emotional change: Owen is a little less alone and much less in control. The relief is real and so is the shame and he lets both stand without arranging them. He makes no resolution.

Power or resource change: Owen is a suspended copy clerk with a private ledger, a public statement against himself, and no authority at all. He has formally begun to learn the thing the volume must teach: an unresolved case intrudes. At the unfiled tray a name will not come — a man he has copied for six years — and he finds it again in the ledger a minute later with his own hand beside it. He notices himself noticing, writes the number down, and cannot tell whether writing it down helped. **This is a near miss, not a cost.** Nothing is permanently lost in this batch, because he holds no duty that could take a name; the permanent loss is the price of the Stage 1 named duty and arrives in Batch 0003 and again after Chapter 46. He has not become a Sealed Witness and must not be written as one.

Continuity fact to preserve: Owen is reassigned to the unfiled correspondence tray and has not appealed. The Provisional Council's circular names itself as the issuer of the countersignature standard and states the "no community return after the present review" language; both take effect from this chapter. The unattributed Docketford standard of Chapter 3 is now attributed. Who at the Council issued it, and why, is not established until Batch 0003. The brass tab reading **ALDER ROW — 461** sits in the drawer Owen now works; he has recorded its number and has not opened the drawer. Six of the thirteen entries are children or people unable to answer, and Lower Lock has no protective-return structure. Owen remains a Stage 0 Copy Clerk and signs no continuation, certification, or hold.

Ending type: Earned hook.
