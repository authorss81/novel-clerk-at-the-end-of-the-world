# Chapter 85 — What the Ward Will Not Accept

She caught him at the top of the lane on the Thursday morning of the twentieth day of the third month at about eleven, on his way up to the building, and she did not slow down.

"**Hall. This evening. After the roster. About thirty will come and I have not asked them to and I have not asked any of them to stay.**"

"I will be there."

"You will be at the back." She said it without stopping either. "**You are going to sit at the back and you are not going to say one word, and I am telling you now so that you cannot stand in a hall in four years and say that nobody told you.**"

"Ada—"

"**You are not speaking for this ward and you are not being asked to and there is no version of tonight in which you are.** Go and do your tray."

He went and did his tray and it was on at a quarter past six and there was nothing on it from anywhere, and he was at the back of the ward hall at ten past seven on the Thursday evening of the twentieth day of the third month, which is about an hour after the roster had been read and about an hour after Marguerite Bray had ruled in the day-book the nine people who had been in the channel on the Wednesday night, and there were about thirty in the hall with the stove on and the long table pushed to one side.

---

Ada Rusk had a sheet of the ward's own paper in her own capitals and had had it in her hand since before anybody came down, and she did not read it out from the sheet, because a hall is not a document and a document is not a hall.

"**I am going to read out eight things this ward will not accept in exchange for being reconciled, and then I am going to read out three things it will, and I am going to read the three out last and not because they are better.**"

The hall was quiet in the way a hall is quiet when about thirty people have decided to be quiet.

"**One. This ward will not accept a return made by another office, whoever wrote it.**"

Somebody at the back said, "**That is yours.**"

"That is mine," said Ada Rusk, "and it is the first one and it is the one I have to say the rest of the reason for, and I am going to say the reason at the end and not now, because if I say it now I will not get to the other seven."

"**Two. This ward will not accept the pitch at the back of the granary as premises of record, and will not accept eleven doors as a settlement, and will not accept a ward as a place that a count can be done to.**"

"**Three. This ward will not accept the pump grant restored in exchange for a name beside the six, and the name is not going to be asked of us and we are not going to offer one, and if the office wants to put a person in that place it can come and put them there and then we will know their name, and not before.**"

Bram Tillet, who is forty-one, and who is one of the four names the word is not written beside, and who does not usually speak in a hall unless the roster is being read, said:

"**Say what is behind the third one.**"

"I have said it."

"**You have said the shape of it. Say what is behind it, because there is a person in this room who has been going to the district office since the month of rain about the pump, and she is not going to be able to come after Thursday, and I would like this hall to hear one sentence about her.**"

Ada Rusk did not use the wall voice and she did not use the other one either.

"**Behind the third one is that the grant is suspended and not extinguished, and suspended and not extinguished is the shape of about nine things in this ward, and if the office ever puts one name in one of those places to make a form complete, then the form is a reason and the name is in it, and every one of the five hours a week that the gap costs comes out of somebody's day and it is going to come out of the day of the person they put the name beside.**"

"That is behind it," said Bram Tillet, "and I have asked for it in a hall, and I have not asked for it eleven times, and I am not going to ask for it a twelfth."

Nobody said anything at that one. The hall knew what the six were. About five people in that hall were on the list of the six and about twenty-five were not, and there is a thing that happens in a room when a sentence goes round a room that has nothing to do with the sentence.

"**Four. This ward will not accept the crossing opened in exchange for a reconciliation, in either order. We are aware that the repair is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and unfunded and that the notice at the weir path has been in the weather since the flood. We are also aware that a person who opens a crossing and gets nothing is a person who has been used, and we do not intend to be used and we do not intend to be the reason anybody in a building looks good.**"

"**Five.**" And she did not change her voice, and everybody in that hall knew she had got to it. "**That the figure which goes in the reason box goes on the public board with this district's name against it, whatever it is, and before we see it. That is the second of our four terms. It is not performed. It is not withdrawn. It is in this list because it is one of the things this ward will not do without, and it is the one we are least likely ever to get, and I wrote it in the first month of the year and Bray ruled it and it is the best of the four.**"

"**Six. This ward will not accept a reconciliation conducted on a return this ward did not make, by a person who was not at a door in this ward, on figures carried forward from a season in which nobody in this ward was asked anything.**"

"**Seven. This ward will not accept the word settlement against this district on any paper that moves a figure, and this paper of ours will not be reworded. It says four words and the four words are *Duty, and no status*, and it has said them since the month of rain and it is going to say them when I am not here.**"

Cobb Rennie said, quietly, "**Read the fifth again.**"

"**You have heard it twice this week, Cobb.**"

"**I have heard it eleven times in two years and I would like it read the eleventh time out loud, and it is not for me, it is for the rest of us in this hall who are on the list of the six and who are not going to say so.**"

And Ada Rusk read the fifth one again, in the same voice, and did not look at anybody while she did it, and that is the whole of what she did about it.

"**Eight. This ward will not accept a day.**" She stopped for a second. "**The recitals bar was lifted on the twenty-fourth of the first month and the settlement may be reconciled on any day the Warden names, and she has named none, and Owen asked her for one in the first month of this year and she refused him and she was right twice. A date can be missed. The season of 488 was missed, by nine places, and two of them are on a board behind a tannery with no row against them for not being done. We performed through the whole of that season with the crossing shut and the pump primed at four and the channel done on a Wednesday, and we are not going to make a promise to be somewhere to a man in a building who has not told anybody what he will do with the date.**"

She turned the sheet over and put it face down on the table.

"**And that is eight. And the eight are longer than the three, and I have been told by a person in this hall in the last two years that the list of what a place will not accept being longer than the list of what it will is a sign of a difficult place, and it is not, it is a sign of a place that knows what it is, and I have nine years of knowing what it is and I am not going to soften it for a hall.**"

---

"**The three.**"

"**One. A person at a door in this ward, who can be asked a question, and who writes the answer down as it was given and not as it ought to have been given.**"

"**Two. Being counted as a draw at eleven doors, with no other office in it. That is what we are. That is the correct figure and it is the only figure and it is eleven and it is a figure of doors and it is not a figure of anything else and it has been on the count since the season before last and it is not going to move.**"

"**Three. Being left alone on the rest of it.**"

She stood in the middle of the floor and about thirty people looked at her and the stove ticked twice.

"**I am not going to say the thing you are all waiting for me to say. I said it in a hall in the first month and I said it at the gate on the seventh of the month and I have said it twice and I am not going to say it a third time, and the reason I am not going to say it a third time is that you are all quite capable of saying it yourselves and I am not going to be the person in this hall who keeps saying it until it stops meaning anything at all.**"

Then she said the thing about the statement, and it took her about nine seconds and it was the reason the eight were on the sheet.

"**There is a document in the district's record about this ward. It was entered on the fourth day of this month, in the district's own hand, with a man's name at the foot of it, and it is four paragraphs long and it is the truest thing any office has ever written about us, and Ivy read it off the sheet in this hall and asked whether it opened the crossing and was told no and said right.**"

"**And we are not going to use it as a return.**"

---

Owen Rusk did not move at the back of that hall, and what he was doing for about four seconds was a thing he has caught himself at three times this year, and this time he did not say *stop me* out loud, because there was nobody to say it to, and being right about catching it is not the same as being right about anything.

Ada Rusk did not turn round.

"**A return is a thing a person signs about their own premises. That is the whole of what a return is, and it is the only place in this county where a person is on a piece of paper saying *this is what I am and this is what I have* without somebody else choosing it.**"

"**And on the fourth of this month the answer this ward gives to a count sheet became a document that a man from the district office wrote, and the moment it did that it stopped being our answer and it became the district office's account of us, and I have looked for an argument against that for nine days and I have not got one. And I want the hall to hear what that is, because it is the fourth of the eight and I have been round the other way to say it.** It is Marl Dykes. It is a ward that has an officer. It is a place that has got a man in a building who keeps facts about it, and the man is a good man, and he did it because his sister asked him to in a hall and she had the objection ready for nine days and asked anyway, and **none of that matters, and none of it is the point, and the point is that in about four years the district office's account of Lower Lock will be the truest document anybody has, and it will be a document about us, and the person who wrote it is a person with a file on himself.**"

"**So it stays in the drawer. It is correct. It is the right four paragraphs. I am not going to pretend it is not.**"

---

Then she turned round, and she looked at the back of the hall, and this is the part of the night the ward had actually been doing for a fortnight.

"**And I am going to say the other thing now, and it is the reason I asked him to be at the back, and I want Marguerite to have her pen out.**"

Marguerite Bray had her pen out before the sentence finished, and she did not write anything, and everybody in that hall knew the rule they work by, which is that a thing is read back twice before it goes in.

"**This ward is not going to ask Owen Rusk for anything.**"

Cobb Rennie put his head up. "**Now?**"

"**Not tonight, and not in this season, and not in any season I am standing in this hall for.**" Ada Rusk said it to about thirty people and she did not use the wall voice, because this was not the wall. "**And I am saying the reason out loud now while he is in the room, because the reason is not a slight, and I will not have it ruled as one, and I would like it written in this book in my words.**"

"**The first of the eight is that we will not accept a return made by another office. And he is another office. And he wrote the best document anybody in this county has ever written about us, and he is not on the side of this ward and he has never once been on the side of this ward, and if I asked him for one thing in this hall tonight he would do it.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And then in about four years there would be two documents in a district file in one season with a man's name at the foot of them, and one of them would be about this ward and one of them would be for this ward. And a ward that has had a document done for it has got an officer. And I am not going to be the reason a place in Fetter Lane has an officer, and I do not say that about him, and I have not said one word about him to this hall tonight except what is in the eight.**"

She put both hands flat on the table.

"**So there is nothing. That is what this ward has for him. It is nothing and it is said out loud and it is in the book tonight and it is not a punishment and it is not a slight and I would like the hall to notice that I have used the word *nothing*, because a steward used it about a granary on the second of this month and it is a real word and it is not the same word twice.**"

---

Marguerite Bray read it back twice and ruled it in the delivery book in her own hand with the day and the hour, and about thirty people watched her do it, and she read the ruling out.

**Ruled the twentieth day of the third month, in the hall, with about thirty persons present. That the ward of Lower Lock has set out what it will not accept in exchange for being reconciled, being eight things, and what it will accept, being three, and that the second of the four terms of this district stands among the eight and is not performed and is not withdrawn. That the officer of the record of the district returns was in this hall and at the back and was not asked for anything. That the ward will not ask him for anything, and has said so out loud, and that this is not a slight and is not to be ruled as one. And that no person in this hall is to be thanked, because a thank you is a receipt and there is nothing to receipt.**

And then she ruled a second line, which she had not been asked for, which is the seventh time in about fifteen weeks.

**And that a ward which has an officer has a document about it that it did not write, and that the ward has not got one, and that the person who wrote the document did not do it for the ward and was not asked to, and that this is recorded here so that nobody ever has to guess whether it was offered or asked for.**

Nobody thanked anybody. Owen Rusk sat at the back of a ward hall for about forty minutes while about thirty people who had never once been asked anything by a building worked out that they were not going to ask him for anything either, and he had three arguments ready and all three of them were correct, and he did not make any of them, and the only one he has been able to stop thinking about is the last one: that in about four years the file would have three documents in it with his name at the foot of them and the ward would have none, and that a ward with none of its own and an officer with three is a place that has been administered.

**He is not the person who speaks for the ward. He has been in nine halls this year and has been asked for a document once and has been told out loud by his sister in front of twenty-two people that the document is not theirs. That is not a failure at the thing. That is the thing.**

On his way out Marguerite Bray caught him at the door with the delivery book shut under her arm, which she does not do, because the book is not a thing she carries while people are still in a room.

"**You did not say one word,**" she said.

"No."

"**I have ruled that a steward reads anything read back to her twice, and it is a stricter rule than the third clause, and I have read back the words *the officer was not asked for anything* three times in my own head tonight and it is the same three times and it is going to stay that way.**" She shifted the book. "**I am going to say one thing to you and I am not going to put it in the book, because it is mine and not the ward's, and if I put it in the book it is the ward's.**"

"All right."

"**In about two years you are going to be the best-informed man in this county,**" said Marguerite Bray, "**and you are going to be a stranger in every room in it, and I have watched two men get there in my time and one of them died of it and one of them moved away, and neither of them was a bad man and neither of them was thanked and neither of them was wrong.**" She opened the door. "**And in about four years somebody in a building is going to ask you why a ward of eleven doors has not been reconciled, and you are going to have to say that the ward would not accept anything, and the ward will be right, and you will be right, and neither of those is going to be a reason, and there is no column.**"

---

The Friday morning of the twenty-first day of the third month he was at the end of the second desk at about a quarter to nine with the ward's sheet in front of him, and it was addressed to the district office of Docketford and not to a person, and it had the eight and the three on it in Marguerite Bray's hand with the day and the hour under them, and it had come down the lane in Ruth Mapp's bag at about eleven the night before, and Ada Rusk had sent it.

"You are going to do the ordinary thing with that," she had said, at the door, in about nine words. "**It is addressed to your office. It is not addressed to you. I am not asking you for anything and you are not doing anything for us and you are entering a paper that arrived.**"

"What is the ordinary thing."

"Enter it in the record of your office, in your hand, with the day on it, because that is what happens to a paper that is addressed to an office and arrives, and then it goes in a drawer with two other things you have put there this season, and I am not going to be thanked for it and neither are you."

He entered it in the record of the district office of Docketford on the Friday morning of the twenty-first day of the third month, in his own hand, dated, signed *Owen Rusk, of Fetter Lane*, and it took him about eleven minutes and he read it back twice before he signed it because Marguerite Bray reads anything read back twice and he has been in her hall for nine years.

**Three documents in a district file in one season with a man's name at the foot of them.** The reason column granted in pencil on the ninth day of the first month, with no authority behind it, which nobody took back. The statement of the district office concerning the district of Lower Lock, entered on the fourth day of this month, four paragraphs, correct. And now a ward's paper with eight refusals and three acceptances on it, addressed to an office, entered by the officer of the record of that office's returns.

**And not one of the three was asked for by a person in Lower Lock, and two of the three would not exist but for a woman who said the word *anyway* out loud in a hall on the Monday morning of the third of this month in front of about twenty-two people, having had the objection ready in her hand for nine days and having said the word so that the hall would notice.**

He put the tray on at a quarter past six that evening and there was not one sheet on it, and on the Friday the twenty-first he did not go down the lane at all, and that was the first Friday in about four months he had not gone down the lane, and **nobody noticed, and the ward held the roster at ten past six on the Thursday and the channel was done on the Wednesday and the pump was primed at four by two people, one of whom is a household with a girl of twelve in it, and the bins are empty, and the marker went up at seven forty and the crossing is shut to loads, and there is a sheet of the ward's own paper in a drawer in a building two miles up the hill with a man's name at the foot of it and nothing at the top of it from the ward.**

He did go down the lane on the Saturday morning, at about eleven, on his own, and Ada Rusk was outside the hall with a bucket and he helped her carry it to the tap and neither of them said anything about the Friday night, and then she said one thing and it was the thing she had not said in the hall and she said it to the bucket.

"**The statement is the best document anybody has written about this ward and I am not going to use it, and I want to be exact with you about why, because in a hall it was the room, and in a lane it is me.**"

"Go on."

"**In a hall it was a room and about thirty people and a book. In a lane it is this. If that paper goes into a return of this ward, then in about four years the district office's account of us is the only account of us there is, and the account is true, and it is true because a man in a building wrote it, and a ward that has a true account of itself written by somebody else has been given an officer and has not been asked.**" She set the bucket down in the yard. "**And there is another thing and it is smaller and I am only going to say it once. Every one of the eight on that sheet is something I want. Every one. I want the pump grant back and I want the crossing open and I want a figure about this district on a board in this county before I see it anywhere else, and I have wanted all eight since the month of rain. I have said in a hall that we will not accept them in exchange, and that is true, and it is also true that we would take all eight of them for nothing, tonight, from a person who did not want anything, and I have not said that in a hall because in a hall it sounds like saying the ward wants a thing.**"

"And in a lane?"

"**In a lane it is the truth and it is mine and you may do what you like with it,**" said Ada Rusk, "**and you will do nothing with it, and I know that, and I have known it since the first month, and I am telling you a thing I do not need to tell you, and you may put that in a file of your own if you have a file.**"
