## Where the story stands at the hour you start

**The twelfth day of the second month of the Concord Year 488, a Monday**, forty-one days into a counting season. Day 1 of C.Y. 488 was a Tuesday. **The first month ran to thirty days, so its Day 30 was a Wednesday and the second month opened on a Thursday.** Second month: **1 Thu · 2 Fri · 4 Sun · 5 Mon · 6 Tue · 7 Wed · 8 Thu · 9 Fri · 12 Mon · 13 Tue · 16 Fri · 20 Tue · 24 Sat · 27 Tue · 28 Wed.** **Keep the calendar exact, never let a chapter's weekday drift, and treat this line as binding over any card.** Weeks of the season are seven days from Day 1, so week 6 runs Days 36–42 and **the season is over: the day-book closed at nine of the quota of eleven on Day 39 and the count of C.Y. 488 for Docketford district was certified and is qualified.**

Owen Rusk is a **Stage 2 Continuance Examiner of the Count, Docketford district**, entered on the fourth day of the month of the first month, holding **three active holds and three named claimants**: Ivy Sarr's household of Fetter Lane, a person who cannot be asked; Mavis Rudge of Witherow, who has her file; Ned Callow of Quill Bank, who asked for a person in the room. **A hold is an answerability and preserves nothing.** A hold may not be transferred without the claimant's written agreement.

**The season's public account is on a board and it is not a remedy.** The count is qualified, nine of eleven, Docketford district is reported short by seven and was also short in 484 and 486, **and there is no row for a settlement that was not reconciled, so two places came off the board by doing nothing at all. Nothing in this batch may undo that.**

**Warden Oress Kade suspended the reconciliation of Docketford district in her own hand at the west gate at noon on the ninth of the second month**, in the field, on a sheet the size of a large envelope, with a reason that is not a figure and is a page long: *I would rather leave two settlements unreconciled and short of a quota than carry a figure I did not make.* **The suspension is an act of operations and not a finding. She is not relieved of the Guard, not disgraced, and not thanked, and she is not going to be before Chapter 90. Her full-season strike of the district schedule is still owed at Chapter 98 with a reason that is not a figure and this batch must not pre-empt it.**

**The two that were not reconciled are Lower Lock and Ossery.** Lower Lock's recitals bar was lifted on the twenty-fourth of the first month and **the Warden has named no day and has said she will not write one down.** Ossery is twelve households and a lime kiln forty minutes off the nearest water, and **Kade has not been into it and would not say why and said the reason is a thing about her and not about Ossery.** **Ossery is this batch.**

## The batch's job

**Written Before Anybody Looked.** The settlements that were removed from the count were not all failing. Some of them were struck to protect Docketford's water ledger — struck when their recorded draw was inconvenient and the district's balance was short, by a schedule written in the last week of the Concord Year 487 by a department that had not surveyed anything, **because it already had their numbers.** The Census Guard reconciles what it is given. **The schedule is not the Guard's.**

Two facts this batch runs on, both established in Batch 0002 and both load-bearing:

1. **The figure that chose the eleven is not on any board in this county.** The eleven settlements named on the printed schedule draw **nine thousand four hundred** a day between them, authorised, and Docketford district is **over its allocation by nine thousand four hundred**, and the two figures are the same to the unit. A shortage is a round thing and that is not round: **that is a sum somebody put together.** The draw is in a yard-wide frame against a wall in the Water Office across the cut, one hundred and ninety-four lines, revised every October, about four readers a year.
2. **A copy of that return comes on the sixteenth of the second month.** Applied for on the Friday of the second, stamped unasked by a man of about fifty-five with twenty-two years in the room, made and delivered within fourteen days. It has the eleven on it and their authorised draws.

**And the trap this batch walks into, which must not be written as a good thing.** On the Monday of the twelfth a form came down for the season of 489 with a **new fifth column headed YEAR OF LAST SURVEY**, to be completed by the district office, due on or before the **twenty-eighth of the second month**. **There is no survey in Docketford district to put a year in any of the ten cells.** Owen wrote *no survey in this district in the season of the Concord Year 489* across all ten in his own hand — not a figure, not a year, a sentence in a form that wants a year — sent it up the stairs, and was told it would not be read. **A blank in a machine is a zero; a zero in a settlement's row is a figure; a figure in a settlement's row is a reason somebody will put in a box in a hand the size of a hand.** His own fourth column cost the county nothing and was the truth. **This one cost nothing and is not, and it is not his, and it is the answer to a challenge nobody has made yet. The Office has got carefuler and not kinder.**

**The batch's two hard turns:**

1. **Owen's first successful challenge against a document succeeds, and it succeeds too well, and somebody pays for it in a place he did not know was in the file.** He may examine arithmetic, procedure, and the currency of any paper a reconciliation rests upon. **A schedule nobody may examine, a choice of places he may not examine, and a reason column he may not put a word in.** What he *can* do is ask whether a paper is current. **A survey column that is blank on ten rows is currency, and the survey is the paper, and it does not exist, and that is examinable — and the moment he says so out loud the Office stops surveying anything at all, which is the correct response to a challenge about surveys and is also the death of the only mechanism by which a place could have been looked at.** He wins the point and the win is the harm. **He is right about the schedule for the first time in the volume and it costs a person he never met.**
2. **The Water Office is a set of hands and not a villain, and the man of fifty-five asks Owen for one thing**, and Owen has to decide whether a document is a weapon or a letter, **and the man is right and Owen is right and they cannot both be served.** The Office gets carefuler and not kinder. **Nineteen twenty-two years of not asking is not redeemed and the man does not become his instrument and is not written as grateful.**

**And the whole batch runs on the standing contradiction:** a schedule is a list of places somebody decided to look at before they looked, and the people on it were not asked, and now a man with a pencil is going to hold the list up in public and somebody is going to be on it who is not a settlement.

## Verified canon this batch inherits — check every line of it against the prose before you write

### The four instruments of this volume, kept apart

1. **The Count** — a count of **draws, premises of record, and works maintained**. Published. **It is not a census of people and no chapter may let it become one.**
2. **The schedule of settlements to be reconciled** — eleven settlements printed on the twenty-eighth before the season, posted in advance, **written before anybody surveyed anything.** The Warden **may return it and may not amend it.** Nobody knows who made it.
3. **The emergency suspension of fourteen days** — person-level, per person, with a date and a care act named. **Thirty days** before no supported living restoration is possible.
4. **The count correction** — made by the Provincial Counting Office, attested by the Warden, with a **schedule of premises attached**, a **date**, and **a reason, and the reason is a figure.**

**And keep them apart from Volume 01's four:** the spring engineering notice, the C.Y. 486 transfer receipt, the Day 10 load-restriction suspension, **Stability Order 114** — plus Redaction Batch 471/Q4/19 and Precedent 37/C. **Stability Order 114 is a local instrument on sixteen named returns, in public appeal, not withdrawn, the sixteen are sixteen, and the Count is a regional act on draws. Neither can move a line on the other.**

Two, year of last reconciliation. Three, premises of record. Four, reason. **Hark Holm is not on the eleven** — it was struck in C.Y. 484 — and it is a ferry hamlet eleven miles up, four households, and **one of the four is alive: Elda Varn, seventy-nine, the fifth house at Wexford Landing, not one of the four premises there, keeps the book at the hall.** She wanted the **eleven** in a column that is kept, beside the four, and not the four restored. **Nobody refused her and nobody gave her anything, and the eleven is not on any document produced so far. ### The limits of the qualification, and the one door in it

An examiner may challenge **arithmetic, procedure, and the currency of any paper a reconciliation rests upon.** An examiner **may not examine the schedule, may not examine a choice of places, and may not put a word in the reason column.** He may enter a challenge and be heard. **He holds up to three, each a real responsibility toward a named claimant.** His review record is two entries — the Gallants Reach carried sheet, and the fourth-limb determination at the Glass Stair — and **it changed no person's water, restored no household, and ended a practice in one district.** The office called the first record thin and he wrote *thin* about it himself.

**The one door: the currency of a paper.** A survey that does not exist is not currency. A column asking for a year of last survey, blank on ten rows, is a paper that does not exist, and asking whether it exists is asking about currency and not about the schedule. **That is legitimate and it is narrow and it is the whole of his standing. It is also the reason he can lose this batch by being right.**
