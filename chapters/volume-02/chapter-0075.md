# Chapter 75 — The Third Limb

He wrote it on the Friday night of the twenty-third day of the second month of the Concord Year 488, at the table in two rooms in Fetter Lane, with the lamp out, because the lamp made him think he was working. The form is a single sheet printed one side and it is item fourteen and it asks four things, and the four things are the same four things they were in the first month of the year, and the second of them is the one he had to think about for about ten minutes before he could put anything on it, because the second line asks for the premises at which the objector may be asked, and a person of Fetter Lane has no premises, and a person who is the officer of the record of the district returns of the district out of which a settlement is taken is a person whose premises are a building that a settlement might one day be taken out of.

*One. The objector. Name, and the premises at which the objector may be asked.*

*Two. The settlement. Name, and the year of the last reconciliation.*

*Three. The ground of the challenge. State it as a ground and not as a grievance.*

*Four. The figure you say should stand instead.*

He wrote the first line in and stopped on the second one, and then wrote it, and the writing is the whole of the evening and it took about two hours.

*One. Owen Rusk, Continuance Examiner of the Count, Docketford district. The office of the district returns, Docketford. I am the officer of the record of the district returns out of which the settlements named in this paper are taken and I have no other premises.*

*Two. Docketford district. The schedule of settlements to be reconciled for the season of the Concord Year 489, printed in advance and not to be amended after printing, together with the district return of that schedule, to be completed and returned on or before the twenty-eighth day of the second month of the Concord Year 488.*

*Three. The ground of the challenge, and the ground is that a paper upon which a reconciliation rests is not current. The district return for the season of 489 carries a fifth column headed year of last survey. That column is not completed. There is no survey of any settlement of record in this district, in this season or in any other season that anybody in this county can produce, and a water office in this county has answered a request for that year, in this month, with the word not known against about ninety lines.*

*Four. No figure. I am asking that no reconciliation in this district be conducted in reliance upon a return that asks this district for a year it has never had.*

And then, because a form that carries a ground a man will not argue is a better form than one that does not, he wrote four grounds under the third line and numbered them, and the numbering is what took the two hours.

---

**Ground the first.** That the schedule for the season of 489 is founded upon a figure, and that a schedule founded upon a figure is not a schedule of places anybody is going to go to.

**He struck that one out before the ink was dry on it.** Not on the third ground. Not on a technicality. Because an examiner may not examine the schedule and may not examine a choice of places, and that is the first line of the card he has carried since the fourth day of the month, and he is not going to stand in a room with high windows and hold a schedule up by a figure he is not permitted to look at and call it a ground of challenge, and **a challenge that begins by pretending is a challenge a person can be asked whether they are ashamed of, and the answer would be yes, and then there is no third ground left.**

Underneath it, in the same hand and not smaller: *I do not argue this and I am not permitted to argue it and I would be ashamed of arguing it.*

**Ground the second.** That the Count has no word for a settlement, and that a settlement is a line in a return of premises of record, and that the line is a line because a quantity is authorised against it, and that a building where the count is administered is a line.

**He struck that one out too, and it took him about nine minutes to decide to, and deciding it was the hardest thing on the page.** It is true. It is the truest thing he has found in a season. It is also an argument about the business of an office in another city, and the answer to it is a form in five clauses that came back on the Monday afternoon and is correct, and the man who wrote it is right, and the form will be the answer in every quarter, and **a ground of challenge is not a place to be right about a thing that has already been answered at you properly by somebody who does not know you and is not thinking about you.**

Underneath it: *I do not argue this. It is not currency. It has been answered and the answer is correct and I have no standing to say it is not sufficient.*

**Ground the third.** That a reconciliation in Docketford district in the season of the Concord Year 489 will be conducted, if conducted at all, in reliance upon the district return for that season, made by this district office and received by the count; that the return asks in its fifth column for the year of the last survey of each of the ten settlements named in it; that the return has been made and the column is not completed, and the district office has written a sentence across the ten cells and been told, correctly, that a sentence is not a year; and **that a paper upon which a reconciliation rests, which asks for the year of the last survey of the place, is a paper that is not current, and the currency of a paper a reconciliation rests upon is the third limb of what I am permitted to do, and I have not used it before.**

And then, because a form asks for it and he knows it does:

**Ground the fourth.** *That I am aware the Office may say the fifth column is not a function of the count and that the completion of a column in a district return is the business of the district office and of nobody else, and that this is correct, and I have put it on the page so that it is on the page before somebody else puts it there.*

**And line four, which is the box the form was built for, and which wants a figure, and there is no figure in it and there has not been a figure in it since the first month of the year:**

*The figure you say should stand instead.*

*No figure. There is nothing in this district to put in that box. I am asking that no reconciliation be conducted in reliance upon a return that asks this district for a year it has never had, and that is not a figure and I have put a sentence in a figure box for the second time in my working life and I would like the officer to know that I know what a box is.*

---

Junia Pell read the whole of it at the end of the second desk on the Friday night, standing, and she is twenty-two, and she is the fourth name on a roll of nineteen that has had no vacancy since the spring, and she read it in about a minute and a half and put it down and said two things.

"The first is that you have struck out the one that would have won for the second time in a season, and I am not going to congratulate you on it, because you do it because it is easier and you both know that it is easier."

"Yes."

"The second is that **you have never used the third limb.** Nine years on the Fourth River Desk and a supervised case and a review the office called thin, and there is no record in this county of anybody ever challenging the currency of a paper, and you have just written one in a room in Fetter Lane at about ten o'clock at night with the lamp out, and in nine years the only two people who will remember it will be you and the woman who determined it."

"She is not the only one. The man in the Water Office read the fifth column on the tenth of this month and answered it with the word not known ninety times and told me about it, and he has not got a card and he is not a party and he cannot be one."

"**Then he is a person the determination will be about and he does not know it yet, and that is a worse arrangement than him being on a file.**" She squared the sheet. "And here is the thing you have written on the page and have not seen you see. Read me the fourth ground out loud."

Owen Rusk read it out.

"That is not a ground of challenge," said Junia Pell. "That is a note you have left for a room in about three days' time. **You have told an officer of the count, in advance, in writing, that the only answer to your challenge is a survey, and the only way anybody in this district can produce a survey is to go to every one of the ten places on that sheet and look at them.** You have handed a body a written instruction to be cured and you have not read it, because you are a man who writes a sentence across a form and sends it up anyway and finds out on the twenty-eighth."

"I read it about an hour ago."

"And?"

"And I am going to send it up anyway and I am going to say that sentence in a room if I am asked to, and I have not worked out the whole of it and I am not going to work it out tonight."

---

He lodged it at the Glass Stair at ten past ten on the Saturday morning of the twenty-fourth, at the count room on the ground floor at the back, at the counter with the rail round three sides and the public stand on it, and the counting clerk at the second counter read the four lines and then read the fourth line twice and put her hand flat on the sheet, which is not a thing anybody does to a document in a public room and which she did anyway.

"You have written a challenge about a return that has not been used for anything," she said. "It has not been read by anybody except you and it has come back once with a note saying the column is not completed, and you have challenged the currency of a document that has never been relied upon by anybody, and that is either very good or it is nothing at all and I have been doing this for long enough to know that I will not find out which for about a season."

"Stamp it."

"I am going to stamp it. **And I am going to tell you the thing that will decide it and I am telling you in advance because nobody ever tells anybody in advance in this room and I have decided to be the first.**" She turned the sheet round. "A hearing is before an officer of the count having no district in the matter, and there is one of those in this city and she is the same one that examined your application and heard you in a room with high windows in the first month, and she has been in this county four times this year, twice on your file. There is a date on the list of rooms and the date is the twenty-fifth, and the twenty-fifth is **a Sunday**, and she will be there, and the room is free on a Sunday, and I have never once in nine years seen anybody object to a date on the ground of the day of the week and neither will she."

---

On the Sunday morning of the twenty-fifth at ten of the clock the long room was about a third full, which is nine people, and there was no queue at the door, and there was a plain flat stone on the table with a Registry punch on the side of it, and the officer was the same woman of about fifty with a slate and no coat, and Warden Oress Kade was not in the room and had not been in the room since the first month and sent nothing. Junia Pell was against the wall. Ada Rusk was against the other wall. Cobb Rennie was at the back on his own because Cobb Rennie is at the back of everything now and has been since the ninth day of the first month and nobody has told him he may sit at the front. The counting clerk from the second counter was in it. A woman of about thirty with a slate was in it. The man of about forty from Concord Hall was in it with a folder and no coat, and gave a name that was not a name so much as a thing that sounds like a name, and Owen Rusk did not write it down and would not have been able to swear to it in a room.

The officer read all four grounds out, including the two he had struck out, including the sentences underneath them, including *I do not argue this and I am not permitted to argue it and I would be ashamed of arguing it*, and it took her about three minutes.

"You struck out two and told me why on the form, the last time," she said. "I would like the room to understand that this is the second time and that I have now done this twice in a season and that on the first occasion I wrote a word into the third box myself. It is becoming a habit in this district and habits in forms are how a thing stops being examined, and I am not going to pretend that I have not noticed it." She put her hand on the sheet. "Ground three. Go on."

---

The office took three minutes and it did not resist, and Owen Rusk had been in a room like this one in the first month of the year and had watched the same man refuse nothing and win anyway, and had not understood it, and understood it now.

"The Office does not dispute that there has been no survey of any settlement of record in this district. The Office has no evidence to dispute it with and would not have put it in dispute if it had. A survey is not a function of the count. The count reconciles what it is given. **The schedule of premises of record of a district is the district's, and it is the district's book, and a district may survey its own book at any time it likes, and nothing in any instrument in force prevents it, and the Office has never had any view on the matter.**"

"Then the return is not current," said Owen Rusk.

"**The return is not current, and it can be made current, and the district office may make it current at any time before the season opens.**" The man of about forty closed the folder about halfway, which is a thing a person does when they have said the part they came for. "The Office would like the applicant to understand, and would like it to be on the record, that it is not the Office's view that a return ought to stand in a tray unanswered while a form is corrected. The column is new. The column was made in this building on the ninth day of this month. A district that has not had a survey does not have a year to give and the Office knows that, and the form is the Office's and the form is correct, and **the cure for a paper that is not current is that somebody make it current.**"

The room was very quiet.

The officer wrote for about ninety seconds and then read back what she had written, which she does.

"I am going to add two things to this determination before I make it, and I am going to read them out, and they are not the determination and they are going in it anyway because the rules of this office allow an officer to note what she has heard and I have never once in eleven years used that and I have been waiting for something to use it on."

She read the first one out.

"**The applicant is right.** The return for the season of 489 asks for the year of the last survey of each of ten settlements of record. There is no such year in this district. A paper which asks for a fact that does not exist, and does not know that it does not exist, is not a current paper, and a reconciliation may not be conducted in reliance upon it. That is the third limb and it is the whole of it, and I have determined eleven matters in this county in eleven years and I have determined one other thing on a third limb and it was a season, and this one is also a season, and I am beginning to think that a third limb is a season-shaped thing and always was."

Then she read the second one out, and it took longer, and the room did not move.

"**And the applicant's fourth ground was correct and is on the page before the Office did anything about it, and I want it on this record in these words: a determination that a paper is not current is answered by making the paper current, and the making of that paper current in this district would require somebody to go to each of the ten settlements named in it and look at them. The applicant said that in writing, in advance, in a form, and I do not know whether he knew, and I have not asked him, and I am not going to.** A body that has been asked whether a paper is current will answer that question by suspending the making of the paper, and the making of the paper is the only mechanism this county has by which a place can be looked at, and I am obliged to record that in a determination I am about to make, and I am not going to pretend I have not seen it, and **both of these are true and neither of them cancels the other and I have been a member of this service for twenty-two years and this is the first time I have had to write both of them down.**"

---

**Sustained on the third ground only.**

A paper upon which a reconciliation rests must be current. The district return for the season of the Concord Year 489, received by this office, carries a column headed year of last survey, in respect of each of the ten settlements named in it. The column is not completed. The Office notes that the completion of the fifth column is within the power of the district office and is not a matter for the officer, and the Office notes that the district office has stated in writing that it is unable.

**It is determined that no reconciliation shall be conducted in Docketford district in the season of the Concord Year 489 in reliance upon the district return for that season, unless and until that return is completed and current. The third limb of a Continuance Examiner, in the currency of a paper a reconciliation rests upon, is sustained in this matter and no other limb is.**

The officer read it out and signed it and did not read it back twice, and then she said, to Owen Rusk, not to the room:

"**You are about to find out what it costs, and I want to tell you the part that is going to hurt, and then I am going to stop talking.** In about four years every officer of the count in this county will know that currency is the third limb. That is what you have done and it is not a small thing and I am not going to pretend it was a small thing. And the way to be safe on the third limb is to have nothing to be current about. A district with no papers has no currency to challenge. **The finding you have made will not be used to make people look at things. It will be used to make sure that nobody ever has to.** That is not what you meant and it is what a determination is for, and I have written it down, and I would like you to go out of this room and understand that I wrote it down."

Owen Rusk said, "You are right about all of it."

"I know," said the officer. "That has been your problem for a season and it is going to keep being your problem and I am the only person in this county who will say so to your face and I have just said it."

---

He was in the corridor for about a quarter of an hour and then he went out into the street and down the hill, and the room emptied behind him at its own pace, and Junia Pell came out last and stood about three feet off him and did not say anything at all, which she has never done before, and which was the correct thing. At the top of Fetter Lane, on the post where the repair notice has been in the weather since the flood, there were two notices, and the second one was the counting season's notice with eleven names on it that had not changed all season, and he read the first one because he always reads the first one, and then, for the first time in a season, he read the second one as well.

**The schedule of settlements to be reconciled for the season of the Concord Year 489. Ten settlements. To be reconciled in the weeks given in the fifth column. Printed in advance. Not to be amended after printing. A Warden may return this schedule and may not amend it.**

The lower part of the notice said the season opens on the first working day of the month and that the district return was to be completed and returned on or before the twenty-eighth, and the twenty-eighth was three days away, and the fifth column on the return he sent up the stairs on the Monday had ten empty cells in it, and he had written a sentence across them, and it had come back with a note saying a sentence is not a year, and on Monday afternoon a building in another city had told him that a line against which a quantity is authorised is a line. He stood at the post for about five minutes and worked out that in four days the schedule for the season of 489 would be printed and posted in a field behind the tanneries, and that it would have a column on it that nobody in this county could fill, and that he had just spent a season and a review record and a determination in a room with high windows making sure that it stayed that way, and that it would stay that way at least until somebody with a chain and a rule and a book is allowed to go and look.

Then he went down the lane and did the tray and was on it at a quarter past six, and there was nothing on it from anywhere, and the twenty-eighth was still three days off, and he had told a woman of about fifty with a slate that a determination of hers was the best thing that had happened to the Count in Docketford district in a year, and she had read it out, and he had sat there and agreed with her, and both of those things were now on a piece of paper in a file in Concord Hall with both their names on it.
