# Chapter 90 — The First List in the City's Record

The count of the season of 489 for Docketford district was certified on the Friday at about ten in the morning at the west gate of Cutter's Fields and on the Saturday morning of the twenty-ninth day of the third month there were about thirty people standing about in the cold in nine flat acres behind the tanneries reading the bottom of a frame, which is where it had gone, and they were reading it from the ground up because there was a certification at the bottom of it with a schedule attached and the woman who maintains the board had put it there and would not have it any other way.

Owen Rusk got down there at about half past nine and stood at the rail with his hands behind his back and did not go in front of anybody, and there is no form in this county on which a person may be told to stand somewhere else.

---

Marguerite Bray was in the ward hall on the Saturday at about half past eight and she had counted the granary on the Wednesday night and written it up on the Thursday bin by bin in a lamp, and she read the figure out in the hall before anybody else said anything, because that is the order they work in.

"**Nothing,**" she said. "**The eleventh bin is nothing and the tenth is nothing and the fourth is nothing and the figure is nothing for the seventh week running and I have written the day and the hour against it and I have not shortened it and the writing up took about two hours and six minutes and I am sixty-one and I did it in a lamp because I do it in a lamp.**"

Nobody said anything. There have been no meals into that building since the seventh day of the second month and there is not going to be one, and the figure is nothing, and the decision not to buy was made on the first of the second month by a woman in this hall and it was correct.

Then she read the six lines out of the register, slowly, and she read the six lines and not the six names, and there was not one person in that hall who did not know which six names were on them, and not one of them said so, and there were about eleven people in that hall on a Saturday morning at half past eight and one of the six of them was in it and she was not asked anything and she did not have to be.

"**And the seventh week running is a figure and I am going to say the other figure in this hall this morning because there are two of them and people only ever hear one.** Five hours a week. That is what the gap costs. That is a figure of hours and it is not a person, and the hours of the person who is there do not stop, and it has been five hours a week since the month of rain and it will be five hours a week for as long as the care situation lasts and nobody has been put in the place and the word is not written beside anybody's name and it is not going to be."

Cobb Rennie said, "**Two years and one month,**" and did not say anything else, and Marguerite Bray said, "**I know,**" and that was the whole of the exchange and it was in front of about eleven people and neither of them said thank you and neither of them was thanked.

---

Then they went up the hill in about four groups and Ada Rusk came last and walked with Owen Rusk and said one thing and then did not say another thing for a quarter of a mile.

"**The second of my four terms is not performed and I am not going to say it at the gate this morning, and I have said it twelve times in a hall, and it is on a board behind a tannery as a list of ten places and there is no figure about this district on it, and that is the ninth time that has happened to a term of mine and it is going to happen again next season and the one after.**"

"I know."

"**You are not going to write to her.**"

"No."

"**Good,**" said Ada Rusk. "**I am not asking you to. I want it recorded that I was not asking you and that I have thought about it and that I decided not to, and the reason I decided not to is that if I ask a man not to write a letter, then the not-writing is mine, and I do not want it in a book of mine that I told my brother not to do a thing that a woman in a field told him not to do.**" She quickened up about four steps and then let him catch up, which is a thing their mother did. "**And that is all I have. Come and stand where you can see the bottom of it.**"

---

The frame at the west gate of Cutter's Fields, on the Saturday morning of the twenty-ninth day of the third month of the Concord Year 488, from the top of the frame down, was this.

*The schedule of settlements to be reconciled for the season of 489, up since the twenty-eighth day of the second month. Ten settlements. Five columns. The fourth, headed authorised draw, empty on all ten. The fifth, headed year of last survey, empty on all ten. The tenth row is a building.*

*The count of the ninth day of the second month. Qualified. Nine of eleven. Not reconciled: Lower Lock. Ossery.*

*The suspension of the Warden. One loop of tarred cord. A page long, and the reason on it is not a figure.*

*The notice of the twenty-sixth day of the second month. Survey of settlements of record. Suspended. Nine persons affected. The reason is not given and no form in this county provides for one. At the foot of it, in a smaller type, the survey round as it stood on the twenty-third, five places, four of them carrying a figure somebody looked at; and under the round, printed entire and filled in in nothing, a form headed SUSPENSION BY A WARDEN OF THE COUNT. Four lines and a date. The twenty-sixth. Not signed. The four lines are still empty.*

*Then the two sheets wired up on the seventh without being asked: a strip the size of a receipt saying that the suspension continues, that figures are carried forward, and that the fifth column will be completed in the season of 490 from the returns of district offices and of water offices; and a single sheet with a number and a month and initials and four words on it and five clauses under the four words, being the district's own answer to a woman of sixty-one.*

*And at the bottom, wired on on the Friday morning and put under everything because the woman who maintains the board will not put a sheet over a sheet: the certification of the count of the season of 489 for Docketford district, qualified, with no figure of premises of record on it for the first time in the record of this county, and the schedule of the settlements not reconciled in this district in this season and unresolved at the certification, ten rows, five columns, and not one word upon it about why.*

Cobb Rennie read the certification out, from the top, in about two minutes, the way he reads a price, and he read the second line twice.

"**Premises of record certified for this district for this season: none. No person was in any settlement of record in this district in the season of 489 and no figure of this season is certified.**" He put his hand flat on the frame an inch off the paper. "**And that is the first time in this county that a count has said out loud that it did not go anywhere, and it took an officer of the count in this city nine years in this office and a settlement with eleven doors on a printed list to make a body admit it in its own voice, and it is correct, and I am not going to be thanked, and I would like it noticed that I have read that line four times and I am going to read it on Monday as well.**"

Then he read the schedule, and he read the fourth column and the fifth column together, because the fourth and the fifth are the same two words in a different order.

"**No. No. No. No. No. No. No. No. No. No.**" He counted them. "**And then yes. Yes. Yes. Yes. Yes. Yes. Yes. Yes. Yes. Yes.** Ten of one and ten of the other, in a field, on a board, in the cold, and it is the first schedule anybody in this city has ever printed about places that were not looked at, and there are a hundred and forty years of count records behind me in a building two miles up the hill and not one of them has a column in it that says no.

"**And there is not one word of why on it, and the Warden has told us in a field yesterday that she is not going to write one on the back and not going to write one in a second hand and not going to write one at the foot in a smaller type, and I asked her for one yesterday and she said no and I told her I was not going to press it, and I am not going to press it, and I have been owed a pension for two years and one month and I know exactly what a reason that is and I am not going to stand here and tell anybody it is like mine.**"

Somebody at the back said, "**What is it then.**"

"**It is a village, in this county, with a chapel, and I have not been to it and I am not going to in this season,**" said Cobb Rennie, "**and I would like that to be the end of it.**"

---

Ada Rusk was about ten feet off with her hands behind her back and she had been reading the tenth row for about a minute before she said anything at all.

"**There is now a row for a settlement that was not reconciled.**"

The field got about two seconds quieter. It is the only thing anybody in this county has been saying all season and about nine people in this field have said it and it has meant the same thing every time, and it has meant the same thing every time until this morning.

"**I have said it thirteen times in a hall and twice at this gate and Cobb has given it away twice and a warden said it to him in a room with high windows in the summer, and every one of us meant that a place that was not looked at is a place with no row and no door and nothing to appeal against. And this morning there is a row. There are ten rows. They are printed. They are dated and they are signed and the fourth column says no ten times.**"

"**And I want the field to hear the rest of it, because the rest of it is the whole of what this morning is.** There is a row for a settlement that was not reconciled **and it is a list of the ten they printed in the last week of the month before last, and Marl Dykes is not on it.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Marl Dykes. Two doors. Nine households. Last looked at in the Concord Year four hundred and eighty-one. Gone from a printed list by an arithmetic, with no correction and no day-book line and no form on which anybody may ask. And it is not on this one either, and the reason it is not on this one is that a list of places that were not reconciled is a list of the places somebody else chose, and I am not allowed to choose places and neither is the warden and neither is the man standing at this frame with his name on the preparation line.**"

"So it is a list of places nobody went to look at," said Owen Rusk, from about eight feet off, and it is a sentence and not a fact about a document, and it is the first time in about a year that he has said a whole sentence in a field that was not a reading, and Ada Rusk turned round and looked at him for it.

"**Yes,**" she said, "**and it is the first list in this city's record of places nobody went to look at, and it is itself a list of places nobody went to look at. That is not a thing that has been fixed. That is a thing that has been printed.**"

She put her hands down at her sides.

"**And I am the eleventh door on it. This ward performed on the Thursday and the Friday and the Wednesday night and at four in the morning and it is not counted, and we are on the list, and I have been on a list about which nobody in this county can do anything since the twenty-eighth of the month before last. The list is printed. The list is legible. Anybody who can read a column can read it, and about thirty of us are reading it, and a man in a building in another city two hundred miles up a river is going to read it in October, and he made the ten rows out of a pencil, and there is no column on any of it that says this ward asked to be reconciled on the tenth day of this month and was told, in front of about twenty-two people in its own hall, that there is no form in this county on which a ward may ask for anything.**"

"**And I am not going to stand in this field and ask anybody to make one.**"

---

Warden Kade was at the frame. She had come down the hill at about a quarter to ten on the Saturday, alone, without a case and without a slate, and she had not said a word to anybody in about twenty minutes, and she is a person who has stood in front of a public board in a field in this county for nineteen seasons and about four of those seasons she has stood at one for two days.

"**Ma’am. I asked you that yesterday in this field in front of nine people and you said no, and I am asking you again this morning in front of about thirty, and I would like the answer to be the same one.**"

"**No,**" said Warden Kade. "**Not that I have been told, and nobody is required to tell me. It is an act of operations and is not a finding, and it may be reviewed by the office, and it ends when the office ends it and not before, and the office has not ended it, and I am going to be standing in exactly this state in June.**"

"You could end it yourself."

"**I could end it in about four seconds and I have thought about it on a boat twice this month, and the reason I do not is on that frame above your head and it was there on the ninth, and it is a page long and it is not a figure, and the day I put it back the way it was, is the day nothing anybody ever puts on a suspension again will be a reason that is not a figure.**" She looked at the certification at the bottom of the frame. "**And I have signed a count this season with nothing on it but arithmetic and a list, and I have written no reason on the back of it and I am not going to, and that is the same discipline and it is nineteen seasons old and it is the only thing I have that anybody in Concord Hall cannot take off me in four years.**"

Then she said one thing to Owen Rusk and it was about forty words and it was in a field in front of about thirty people and there was no letter in it and it was not an alliance and it was not the beginning of one.

"**Your determination was correct and it was the correct answer to the question you were asked and it is going to cost you more than it has cost me, and I told you that in a field on the twenty-ninth of the month before last and I would say it again to anybody who asked me this morning. And I have not thanked you, and I am not going to, and you have not asked me for anything, and I have noticed that, and noticing it is not the same as anything and I would like it noticed that I have said so.**"

"Yes," said Owen Rusk.

"**Then we are two people who have said *yes* in a field,**" said Warden Kade, "**and somebody is going to read one day that this district and this service agreed about a piece of paper, and I would like it on the record that what we agreed about was a piece of paper and that neither of us has looked at a place.**"

---

Then, at about eleven, the thing that happens in a field behind a tannery on the last morning of a season happened, which is that about six people found that they had opinions about the schedule and had had them for eleven days, and Ada Rusk said *you are going to do it now and I am not going to stop you and I am going to stand over there*, and went and stood about eleven feet off with Marguerite Bray, and let them have it.

And what they said, in front of about thirty people, was this.

A man who keeps a boat at Rush Halt said that the suspension of the survey was the best thing that had happened to his reach in nine years, because a surveyor coming down a lane with a chain and a rule is a thing that stops a settlement saying what it actually is, and he had watched a man do it on the fourth and had not known what he was for.

A woman from Ashlocks said that the surveyor on the fifteenth was extremely polite and had asked her whether the second door was still a door and that she had said no and that he had written it down, and that she had told her mother and her mother had said *that is the first person in four years who has asked me a question about that door*, and that neither of them had any idea what to do with it.

Pell said nothing and had not come down the hill and had sent nothing.

A man from Ossery who had come up the hill on his own two feet said, at about a quarter to twelve, in the ordinary voice, that he had heard a clerk of the guard say yesterday in this field that the ten places on the list were the ten places the district office named, and that that was a different sentence, and that he did not know what to do with it either and would not mind if somebody told him.

And nobody told him, and it went in a case.

---

**And here is what the season cost, and it is not a person, and everybody who was in that field on the Saturday morning of the twenty-ninth day of the third month of the Concord Year 488 could have told you what it was, and about nine of them said so and none of them said it to him.**

The certification says, in the second line, that no figure of premises of record is certified for this district for this season, and the return it was certified from is the district office's own return, and it is in the district office's own record, and it was made in a room two miles up a hill by a person whose initials are on the corner of it. **The Office's own strip of paper, the one the size of a receipt that has been on that frame since the seventh, says the fifth column will be completed in the season of 490 from the returns of district offices and of water offices.** So the work of ten surveys is now the district office's, and it is the district's, and it is unfunded, and nobody has been asked whether it can be done, and there is no form in this county on which anybody is asked whether a district can do a thing.

**And not one person in Docketford district is any worse off than they were on the seventh of the second month: nobody struck, nothing off a household, a draw to no main. Not one person's water moved in the season of 489, and the reason is not that it is a good thing, and the reason is that the count is a count of draws and premises of record and works maintained and not a count of people, and nothing on that frame is a count of people.**

**The crossing at Fetter Lane is shut to loads. The repair is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and is unfunded and the notice at the weir path is in the weather and has brought no district. The crew is nine and their wage is four months in arrears and is the crew's wage and is not the district's. The granary has been open and empty since the seventh day of the second month and the figure is nothing for the fifth week running. The Fetter Lane pump grant is suspended and not extinguished and the pump is primed by hand at four in the morning on the Thursday by two people. The six persons in this ward whom it cannot ask are six persons and are at three of five parts, and the word is not written beside anybody's name and is not going to be. Cobb Rennie's pension is two years and one month and is uncured. Stability Order 114 is on a docket at the Glass Stair in public appeal and has not been withdrawn and the sixteen are sixteen and Lower Lock is sixth and none of it moved this season. The field verification of premises of record in this district is suspended and the reason is not given and no form in this county provides for one and the four ruled lines under the survey round are still empty and nobody has signed them.**

**And it is not the man who wrote the figures who is paying. He is in a room across the cut with a bad knee and a pigeonhole wall with forms in eleven colours in it, and about a hundred and ninety-four lines on a form he answered honestly in good faith in a fortnight he did not charge anybody for, and he put not known against about ninety of them, and he is going to find out in October, on the twenty-eighth, when a form comes back with a year in it that he put there himself, and nobody is going to tell him in advance, and the reason nobody is going to tell him in advance is that a kindness and a conversation are the same act and the only difference is whether anybody writes it down afterwards, and Owen Rusk worked that out in a corridor in the last week of the second month and decided not to, and it was the correct decision and it cost him the thing he is not going to get back.**

**The district office is paying. The district office made the figures and the district office is going to have to look, and the amount is ten places, and nobody has cost it, and nobody is going to. The crossing at Fetter Lane has a price on a post and the price is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and it is a figure of timber and piles and hands and it is correct and it is unfunded. Ten surveys of ten settlements of record in a district of four hundred people is a bigger job than one shut crossing and it is going to be done out of a room with a bulb on a flex, and the only difference between the two is not the money. It is that there is a notice nailed to a post in the weather at the head of Fetter Lane, inviting any district able to perform the repair to perform it, and there is no post anywhere in this county with a figure on it for the other, and there is not going to be one, because a district's own arithmetic is not a work and there is no form in this county that puts a price on a district accounting for itself.**

---

At about twenty past twelve on the Saturday Owen Rusk walked back down Fetter Lane to the post at the head of it, where there are three notices, and the first is the repair and the words *any district able to perform the repair is invited to perform it*, and the ink has run in one line, and the second is the season's notice with ten names on it and a column nobody can fill, and the third is last season's with eleven names on it and the weeks gone. None of the three had changed and he did not take any of them down.

Down at the bottom of the lane the granary is open and empty and the crossing is shut, and the marker board was up, it having gone up on the Friday, and about nine people had stood at it at half past seven that morning and read a figure about the depth of water on the flats that a person in this ward had written in chalk because they had been at the flats.

**On the Sunday morning of the thirtieth day of the third month he did the tray and it was on at a quarter past nine and there was nothing on it from anywhere, and there is going to be a return for the season of 490 to be made in that building with a fifth column in it that can be completed, and he is the officer of the record of it, and in about four years somebody is going to ask the district office of Docketford for the reasoning behind four documents in a drawer and a list in a book on a shelf, and the district is going to produce all five, and he will not be in the building when it does.**

**Three holds. Ivy Sarr's household, of two rooms in Fetter Lane, a person who cannot be asked, whose household primes a pump at four in the morning on a Thursday. Mavis Rudge, of Witherow, who asked for a document in which somebody asked her and the answer was no, and got one. Ned Callow, of Quill Bank, who asked for a person in a room and did not get one and will not ask again. Three is a card and the card is the answer to a question he asked himself in a hall two months ago and the answer has not changed and there is no fourth.**

The season of 489 is over in Docketford district. Ten settlements are named on a printed list and ten were not looked at and the not looking is printed and dated and signed and on a board behind a tannery where about thirty people read it in the cold on a Saturday in the third month, and the practice did not stop, and it is now legible, and legibility is not a remedy, and Ada Rusk has said so twice already and said in the last week that she was not going to say it a third time because saying it a third time is the way a thing stops being true.

And a ward in Fetter Lane performed on the Thursday and the Friday and the Wednesday night and at four in the morning, and is not counted, and says so, in a field, out loud, in a voice about four hundred yards from the door of its own hall, and there is a row for it now, on a board, with *no* and *yes* in the two columns that are new in this county, and there is no column anywhere in this county that says it was looked at, and it was.
