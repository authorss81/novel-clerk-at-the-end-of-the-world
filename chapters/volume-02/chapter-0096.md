# Chapter 96 — The Custody Limit

A room with a rail round three sides of it, at the count room at the Glass Stair, on the Tuesday of the sixteenth day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488 at about eleven in the morning, and about fourteen people in it, and the rail is there so that a person may say a thing in front of nine other people, and that is the whole reason for the rail and it has worked every time anybody has used it.

The return of the settlement of Slackwater was received on the Tuesday of the sixteenth and recognized on the Wednesday of the seventeenth by an officer of the count having no district in the matter, a woman of about fifty with a slate and no coat, in her sixth appearance in this county this year and the third on his file.

It is narrow. It is eleven pages and a cover and it is made of **ten branches, one adult to a page, separately consenting, no shared line anywhere, no proxy clause, no witness line a householder could fill, and a right of withdrawal on every sheet printed at the top in eleven-point type before a word of the body of it.** It was drawn on the tank house table by Owen Rusk in about four hours on the Sunday of the fourteenth and it took four hours because he was made to copy out the tank book in full twice, and the Registry's own precedent is the precedent, being two hundred and nine words in a room in a building in this city, and the ward's paper is not a precedent and he was not permitted to use it and did not.

**Scope, and it is one sentence and it is the whole of the instrument:** *the named persons keep the named tank at the named place, and are responsible for the upkeep of the sluice at the head of the named beck, and claim nothing as to the condition of either, and claim no status.*

Four words of that are not the ward's. He wrote the other twenty and did not use the ward's four, and Ada Rusk has not been asked and is not going to be asked and has said, in a lane and in a hall and in a book, that this ward will not accept a return made by another office, and the sentence about that is in a delivery book in Fetter Lane with the day and the hour, and neither of those things was said to him at the tank and both of them are true.

---

**The two witnesses of it are a woman of fifty-two named Skell and a man of forty-one named Harrow, and they have not spoken to each other since a Sunday in the spring of the Concord Year 484, and they gave their statements in a cart house about nine feet apart on the Monday of the fifteenth, and neither of them would be in the room while the other one was in it, and working that out took about forty minutes and it is the correct arrangement and it is a hundred and forty years old.**

He asked, on the Sunday, why. It was the oldest man in that yard who answered and he was seventy-one and he said: "**Because they were not asked, and because somebody said a thing in a room in 484 and neither of them has forgotten it and neither of them has been asked, and you are not going to ask them, and if you do ask them then in about four years there is a file with a question in it that came from a building.**"

"So the record says nothing about why."

"**The record says what they did and the day they did it and it says that the two statements were taken apart. The record does not say why, and there is no column for why, and a column for why would be a reason, and the reason column on a count instrument is always a figure, and I would like this one to be nothing at all.**"

"Write that down."

"**No,**" said the old man. "**That is yours.**"

So Owen Rusk wrote it down himself, in the longhand book, in his own hand, with the day and the hour, and the return says only what was done and when, and the tank book says nothing, and the two of them do not know that it is in a book two miles down a hill and never will.

And the ten sheets were carried to the Glass Stair on the Monday afternoon in a tin by the man of nineteen on a bus that does not go there and then on his legs, and it took him from about two in the afternoon until about eight in the evening, and the fare was the cost of the bus and the legs were his, and Owen Rusk offered him the fare back and the man of nineteen said no and then said what he would take, which was a copy of the return in about four weeks' time, in a month, when the office has made it, and Owen Rusk said that would be a copy of a document about eleven people's premises handed to a person in a yard, and the man of nineteen said, "**Then I will take it in a year, or I will not take it, and you may write down that I said both of those.**"

Both are in the longhand book, in order, with the day and the hour.

---

**And the return carries a custody limit, and the limit was written by the households, and it is the fourth clause of the second page, and it is the only clause anybody in this county has been able to write for themselves in about eleven months, and it is a limit, and not a protection that fixes anybody.**

*That the return, and the book of the tank, and the fortnight's list, are kept at the tank house at the named place, and are not to be removed by any officer of any office; and that the person who keeps them is P. Naylor of the tank house; and that she may be replaced by no officer of any office, and only by the households at the tank house acting together; and that the keeping is five hours a week; and that the keeping is a figure of hours and not a person; and that the hours of the person who is there do not stop while the return is in being; and that the whole of this clause ends when the return ends and does not survive it.*

Seven clauses. **The five hours a week is a figure and it is not a person, and the hours of the person who is there do not stop, and the cost of the gap in Lower Lock has been five hours a week since the month of rain and the cost of the gap here is the same figure in a different place, and the two five hours are not the same five hours and are not added together anywhere, and they are not going to be.**

"You have written a limit that stops an officer of this office taking a book out of a house," said the officer of the count, on the Wednesday morning, reading the second page.

"Yes."

"**And you have written that a woman of sixty-one cannot be replaced by this office without the agreement of the households in a yard, and the fifth of the five parts in a protective return is a named person who keeps it, and the Registry may put somebody else in that place, and you have taken that out of it. Do you know what you have taken out of it.**"

"The office's right to replace her."

"**The office's right to replace her, and the reason for it, and the reason for it is about what happens if she dies, and she is sixty-one, and there is a man of forty-six in that yard and a man of nineteen and a woman of thirty-four, and any one of them could be named in an hour, and the office has never been able to do it in four years and has never said why.**" She put the sheet down. "**A custody limit is a limit and it is not a protection, and I am going to say the rest of this in a room and not in a corridor, and there are about fourteen people in it, and you will not like it, and you may not answer it.**"

"Go on."

"**That is the only clause in this instrument that puts a person in charge of a document about herself, and there is a form in this county for that and the form is a Registry function, and it is unfunded, and the hour it costs is five a week, and I have signed six of them in eleven years and every one of them is still in force and none of them is a person anybody in this county could name.**" She turned a page. "**And that the hours of the person who is there do not stop. That is your sentence and not mine and I have read it three times and I am not going to argue with it, and I am going to record it, and in about four years there is going to be a file in this county with a limit in it and somebody is going to read that sentence and work out that the person who wrote it lives two miles from a ward of eleven doors and that the ward has three of the five parts and has not got a fourth, and they are going to think it is a lesson, and it is not a lesson, it is a fact about a yard.**"

"Then what is it," said Owen Rusk.

"**It is the last legal fact this county has produced this year and it is a limit and not a cure, and there is no custodian behind it, and I am not going to sign my name to a cure.**"

She wrote *recognized* on the cover, and the date, and the hour, and the fact that it was made on ten separately consenting adult branches out of twenty-two households, and that eleven households declined and that their declining is recorded and that no sheet of theirs is in the file and no reason of theirs is in the file, and she read all of that out before she wrote it.

**A strip of paper the size of a receipt came down about an hour after that, and it is unsigned, and it is the answer of a building about itself, and the counting clerk at the second counter gave it to Owen Rusk face up and read it to him standing at the counter because she has read four or five of these out in nine years and has never once read one for anybody who did not ask.**

**COUNT ROOM, PROVINCIAL COUNTING OFFICE. Upon the recognition of a local return made by the households of a place with nine premises of record. The custody clause is received and is not objected to. A custodian is a Registry function and the Office does not provide one. The Office does not maintain a list of the settlements of this district. A local return is not a settlement of record. Nothing in this instrument is a determination as to the condition of any work. The Office has no view.**

"That is correct in all five clauses," said Owen Rusk.

"**It is correct in five clauses and the second one is the answer and the answer is a refusal with a noun in it, and a noun is the hardest thing in this city to move, and I have been at this counter nine years and I would like you to notice that the word in the second clause is the same word that is in your custody clause, and that the Office has just told you it does not do that, in a strip of paper, in a form, in about four minutes, and it is the truth, and it is going to be the answer in every quarter for as long as I am on this counter.**"

"And nobody at the district office signed the return."

"**Nobody at the district office signed the return, and there is not going to be a document about that in this building, and the reason there is not going to be a document about that is a thing I have worked out in nine years and it is not a thing I am going to say at a counter.**"

---

**The eleven households that did not sign are not in the room, and nobody sent for them, and about nine people noticed and one of them asked.**

"Where are they."

"**They are in a yard four miles off,**" said the officer of the count, "**and they are not parties, and the only person in this county who can put them in this room is a person of the place, and the place has not sent for them, and I would like the person who asked that to understand that I have answered it out loud in front of fourteen people so that it is a record, and so that nobody in this building later on can say that nobody thought about it.**"

And Junia Pell, who had come down off the second landing and had not said anything for about fifty minutes because there was nothing for her to say and she has said so in this building before, said the only thing she said in that room, and she said it to Owen Rusk and not to the officer, and it was three sentences.

"That is a limit and you know what it is. Do not write it up."

He did not write it up. It is in the longhand book on a shelf in a room with no window, in his own hand, with the day and the hour, in a book that may not leave the building, and it is the fifth of the five documents and it is the only one of the five that is not in a file, and he has never worked out whether that is because it is the truest or because it is the most dangerous.

---

**And then Owen Rusk gave up the last thing he has, and he gave it up standing up, in a room with a rail round three sides of it, in front of about fourteen people, and it was not a speech and he had practised it on a walk and it did not improve on the walk.**

"I have four documents in a district file in one season with my name at the foot of them," he said, "and a fifth thing copied into a longhand book on a shelf in a room I have the key to, and I made the first of the four myself, and the second of the four nobody in a ward asked for, and the third of the four nobody in a ward asked for, and the fourth of the four is a preparation line on a sheet of ten places I am not permitted to examine. I have been the person who decides which places get looked at for about eleven weeks and I have not once been in a room where that was the question in front of the people in the room."

"Then say the other thing."

"**I am not the person who decides which settlement is reconciled,**" said Owen Rusk, "**and I have given it up, and I want it said in this room and not in a corridor, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it because saying it is a thing I have been doing since the second month and the thanks would be for a decision I made in a corridor and about four years ago.**"

The officer of the count wrote nothing down, and in eleven years she has never once not written down, and about fourteen people were in that room and two of them noticed and one of them was a clerk of the Census Guard and one of them was Junia Pell, and neither of them has said anything about it to anybody.

---

**The recognition is a procedural act and it is not a mercy and here is exactly what it does and does not do, in the order the officer of the count read it out, and she read it out herself and would not let a clerk read it.**

It recognizes a local return made by the households of a settlement with nine premises of record, a count correction dated the eighth of the fourth month, a suspension of fourteen days entered on the tenth, and a return for the season of 490 to be made by the district office.

It does **not** amend the count correction. The nine premises of record stay reinstated and the reason in the box stays nine, and the quantity stays authorised against the head, and the return does not say one word about the quantity, and the officer of the count said so and made him look at the fourth sheet of the correction while she said it.

It does **not** restore anything. Nobody at Slackwater has gained a draw, a rate, a fund, a writ, a market, a school place, a burial status or a door in any register, and there is no clause of this return that would give anybody one, and the return is not going to.

It does **not** amend the schedule of settlements to be reconciled for the season of 489, which was printed on the twenty-eighth of the second month and cannot be amended after printing, and Slackwater is not on it and never was, and no clause of this return puts it on it, and the Warden may return a schedule and may not amend it and nobody in this county has a power that will put a place on one.

It does **not** touch the certification of the count of the season of 489 for Docketford district, dated the twenty-eighth of the third month, qualified, with no figure of premises of record upon it and the schedule of the settlements not reconciled attached to it, ten rows, five columns, and not one word about why on it anywhere.

It does **not** touch the notice of the twenty-sixth of the second month suspending field verification of premises of record in Docketford district, nine persons affected, the reason not given, no form in this county provides for one, with the survey round at its foot and under the round four ruled lines and a date, the twenty-sixth, not signed, **and the four lines are still empty in the fourth month of the Concord Year 488, and nobody has filled them in since the twenty-sixth of the second month, and nobody in this county is going to.**

It does **not** touch Stability Order 114, which is on a docket at the Glass Stair in public appeal and has not been withdrawn, and the sixteen are sixteen, and Lower Lock is sixth.

And it does **not** do the one thing everybody in that room, including about four who work in offices in this city, came to look at it for, and the officer of the count said the last of it standing up with the folder shut, and it is the last sentence she said in this county this year.

"**A return is recognized. That is the whole of what has happened this morning and I would like the fourteen people in this room to understand what the word means, because it is a small word and it is the largest thing that has happened to a place in this county in about eleven weeks, and it moves no figure and restores no household and puts nobody's roof on and nobody's water back, and it is the first time in the record of this county that a place nobody had looked at has been described by its own households in a document that an office received, and I am not going to be thanked for recognizing it, because recognition is what offices do and I have done the easy half of it.**"
