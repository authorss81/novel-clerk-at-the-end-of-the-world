# Chapter 82 — What He Is Allowed to Ask Now

He went up the hill on the Wednesday morning of the twelfth day of the third month at about a quarter to nine with the folder under his arm, and he got to the count room at the Glass Stair at ten, and the counting clerk at the second counter had his application in a pigeonhole before he had taken his coat off, which told him it had been in since about eight, and told him something else, which was that she had put it there herself and not left it in the tray.

"There is a rail," she said, "and a chair on the wrong side of it, and I have not been asked to say what I am about to say, and I have not said it, and I am saying it now."

"All right."

"You may examine arithmetic, procedure, and the currency of any paper a reconciliation rests upon. You may not examine the schedule. You may not examine a choice of places. You may not put a word in the reason column. That is the standing and it has not moved and it is not going to move because of what you did on the twenty-fifth." She squared the sheet on the counter. "And there is a new thing and it came down on Sunday the ninth, three days ago, and it is a note and not a rule, and you are going to be annoyed by it and you are entitled to be."

"Go on."

"**A challenge that says a figure is the wrong figure will be answered by a form, in the ordinary way, in about nine minutes, and you will be handed the form, and the form will be correct, and you will go away. A challenge that says a figure is not a figure of the season written on it will be heard.**"

Owen Rusk did not move for about two seconds.

"Say the second one again."

"A challenge that says a figure is not a figure of the season written on it will be heard. That is what the note says. That is the whole of the note and it is four lines and it is unsigned and it does not need to be, because a note is a thing a building says about itself." She put her hand flat on it. "**And I want it said that I have not got a view about it and I have not been told to have one, and that it went down on Sunday the ninth of this month and that it is the shape of the thing you said in a room in another city two days before, and that a building has taken the shape of a thing a man said and printed it and sent it down inside three days, and that is not a kindness and it is not a threat. It is the fourth time this season that this has happened and if you have not worked out yet what it is then you have not been listening.**"

He read the first note again, which was the one that had gone down in the first month of the year, and then he read the second one again, and then he put the two of them side by side on the counter, which he is not permitted to do with anything, and which nobody stopped him doing.

"**The first one asked a water office in this district for the year of the last survey of every settlement of record in the district, and a man of about fifty-five with twenty-two years in that room answered it honestly and wrote not known against about ninety of a hundred and ninety-four lines, and it cost a man with a chain and a rule his whole season's work, and that man has a name on a painted board four feet above the second counter and I have not read it.**"

"I know that," said the counting clerk.

"**And nobody has told the man of fifty-five to this day that his answer is going into a column as a year somebody looked at something, and he is going to find out in October, on the twenty-eighth, when a form comes back with a year in it that he put there himself, and I worked out in about ninety seconds on the Monday morning in the last week of the month before last why I was not going to tell him, and the reason is in his own head and it is one sentence and I am not going to say it at a counter.**" He put his hand flat on the counter, about four inches from hers, and did not go closer. "And the second note came down on the Monday after I was sustained, and it is correct, and it is going to hand me the best procedural win of my working life, and it is four lines and it is unsigned."

"Then say what it is," said the counting clerk. "You have had it in your mouth for about a minute."

"It is the same building being careful, twice, about two different things. And I want it noticed that I have said *the same building* twice in about ninety seconds and that I have not called it anything at all."

"Noticed," said the counting clerk. "And I am not going to write it down, because a thing said at a counter is a record and a thing written down is a document, and there is a difference, and this counter is the whole of it."

"I have worked out what it is."

"Then you have worked out that in about four years every officer of the count in this county is going to know that a challenge may be brought about the season a figure belongs to, and that the answer to it is that a place is not looked at." She turned the sheet round and squared it again. "**And that the safe course in this county, from about now on, is to have nothing to be current about. There are going to be officers in offices in this city who never have anything to be current about and they are not going to be the bad ones, and they are not going to be asked a single question about it, and nobody is going to tell the first one of them either, and they will find it out on their own in about two years and they will not be unhappy.**"

"Yes," said Owen Rusk.

"**Well. I would like it noticed that I have said that to you and that you did not look pleased, and that I have said it at a counter and not in a room with a door, because a question asked in a room with a door is a conversation and a question asked at a counter is a record, and this one is a record and I have made it into one by saying it out loud to you and that is the only thing I have done with it.**"

He thanked her for the sheet and she said, "It is a sheet and it is not a favour and do not put it in the file that leaves the building."

---

He did not go to the room with high windows. He went and stood in the corridor outside the records room on the second landing for about eleven minutes, and the corridor is not a place anybody stands in, and the window at the end of it does not shut and has not shut since the spring, and a form maintainer from the district office, up here on the circuit for a week with a tray, went past him twice and did not look at him and he did not look at her, and that is the whole of what has ever happened between those two people in nine years and it is going to remain the whole of it.

What he was doing in the corridor was the thing he does.

The determination of the twenty-fifth had said that no reconciliation shall be conducted in Docketford district in reliance upon the district return unless and until that return is completed and current. He had won it. It had cost a man with a chain and a rule his season's work, and it had cost the county a survey, and the woman who determined it had written down in her own hand that the correct answer to a challenge about a paper is the wrong answer for a county, and both of those were true and neither cancelled the other.

**And now, standing on a landing, he worked out the thing the counting clerk had put on the counter in front of him in about nine seconds, which is that a determination about a paper is the only thing in this county that can stop a challenge about a paper without anybody having to look at anything.**

A district with a return that is completed and current is a district that can be stopped, by anybody, in any season, for as long as anybody cares to, and the stopping is free and the stopping is correct, and the fourteen days run on a place because a man in a building noticed a date.

A district with a return that is not completed and current is a district that cannot be stopped at all.

**Which is what he had. He had it in a drawer in his own building with a date on it and a woman's name on it and a finding in it, and he had it because he had been right in a room, and the price of it is a survey round that stopped four settlements short and one settlement's whole season of work, and he had put that price in his own hand and underlined it, and it was correct, and it had cost him nothing to be correct about.**

He stood in the corridor and got about as far as the shape of it and then made himself finish the sentence, because the sentence is where the useful part is and he had learned that in the month of rain, in a lane, in the wet.

**He is not going to be one of the safe ones by accident. He is going to be one of the safe ones on purpose, in about four years, and by then there will be no form anywhere on which a person can ask him whether he had it on purpose or whether he had it because he was frightened, and he will not remember which, and that is the whole of what the third limb is and it was given to him for nothing by a woman with a slate who told him to his face that she was the only person in this county who would say it and had just said it.**

None of that was a comfort. He had gone into that corridor expecting to be told he was safe and what he was told was that he was going to be safe, and that the price of being safe is a number of people in a county who are not looked at, and that he had chosen the number by being right, and that he would never afterwards be able to tell anybody which number he had chosen.

He went and did the tray at a quarter past six and there was nothing on it.

---

On the Friday morning of the fourteenth he went down to the public stand at the Glass Stair, which is a shelf of blue books that anybody may take out and read and which four of the people in this county have ever done for the purpose of a column.

The counts of the season are three books per season, one for the settlements, one for the works, and one for the district. He took out the district book for the season of 486 and the district book for the season of 488 and put them side by side on the sloping shelf with a weight on each because the shelf has a lip and the books go.

The district book is a good book. It is bound in a buckram that has gone the colour of a wet slate, and it is a summary, and the whole of the summary for the district is a page and a half: the settlements reconciled, out of the settlements named; the premises of record certified, in a line with a total against it; the works maintained; the draw authorised, in the district's own column, which is the column Docketford is short in and has been since he came; and the qualifications.

He wrote the two years out on the back of a sheet from a tide table, standing up, in the way he reads.

Then he opened the settlement book for 488, which is the book the board at Cutter's Fields is printed from, and read the third column down, and read the second column down beside it, and he did it for 484 and 486 as well, and it took him about fifty minutes in all and the counting clerk let him stand at the shelf for as long as he wanted without saying anything, which she has done about four times in nine years.

**The second column is the year of the last reconciliation. The third column is the premises of record.**

**Wexford Landing is 479 and 4. Gallants Reach is 479 and 6. Rush Halt is 486 and 7. Quill Bank is 486 and 9. Ashlocks is 486 and 6. Otter Stair is 486 and 9. Witherow is 479 and 3. Ossery is 486 and 11. Marl Dykes is 481 and 2. Lower Lock is 486 and 11.**

There is an eleventh row and he read it and then read it again, and then he put his finger on it and stood there for a while.

**Nether Sill. The year in the second column is struck and so is the figure in the third, and both of them have a clerk's stroke through them and nothing after the stroke, and it is the same stroke in both cells, and it is the stroke that took the row off the board, and the board has not carried the row since.** There is no row anywhere in the county with a reason on it and there is no form on which anybody may ask for one, and there is no season in which a place that is struck is put back because somebody went and looked at it, because there is nothing to put back.

He copied the rest of it out and then he copied out the third column for 484 and 486 and set the three years in a line, and he did it in pencil, and he was careful, because this is the only arithmetic he is going to be permitted to bring into a room in his life.

**Nine of the ten rows on that board have not moved. Four, six, seven, nine, six, nine, three, eleven, eleven. The same figure, in the same row, on the board of the season before last, and on the board of the season before that, and on the printed schedule that went up in the last week of the month before last, and on the district return in the drawer behind him, which is his.**

**The one that moved is Marl Dykes, and it did not move in a room, it moved in an arithmetic.**

And then he stood at the shelf and did the other sum, which took him about four minutes and which he has never brought into a room and never will.

It is not a scandal. It is the plainest arithmetic in the county and it is the reason the count is cheap.

**The second column moves and the third column does not.** A settlement is put on a schedule when its year of last reconciliation goes stale, and the year is the only thing that moves, and it moves by arithmetic, at a desk, once a year, in the ordinary way. The figure of premises of record is what a person would have to go and look at in order to change, and nobody goes, and so the figure stands, and stands, and stands, and a count of a settlement nobody goes to costs nothing at all.

**And the count of this district is a count of ten figures, and it is not the ten he has just been reading, because one of those is not on the list and the list carries one that was never on the board, and of the ten on the return nine have not changed since the season before last and the tenth is a building with six doors against it, and the year beside each of them is what puts them on a list, and the two columns are printed eleven inches apart on the same sheet and they are about the same size and one of them is a life and the other one is a stamp, and the county has never once had to decide which one it was counting.**

Then he went and got the schedule of the survey round, which is printed at the foot of the notice of the twenty-sixth in a smaller type and a different hand and is in the district file as well, and he laid it out beside the third column of the district's own book, which is the column the schedule of the twenty-eighth of the second month was printed out of and is the only column in this building that Marl Dykes is still in, and it took him about six minutes, and it is the sum of the season.

**Rush Halt. The fourth. Seven on the round. Seven in the book.**
**Otter Stair. The ninth. Nine on the round. Nine in the book.**
**Ashlocks. The fifteenth. Six on the round. Six in the book.**
**Marl Dykes. The nineteenth. Two on the round. Two in the book, and Marl Dykes is not on the schedule and the round is not a list of the places on the schedule, and a man walked into a place on the nineteenth of a month that is not on the printed list and nobody sent him.**
**Ossery. No date. No figure on the round. Eleven in the book.**

He stood at the shelf and looked at five lines of arithmetic for a while.

**Four figures that a person looked at, and four figures that a book already had, and they are the same figures.** Not approximately. The same. A man with a chain and a rule and a book walked into four yards in this district in the second month of this year and wrote down, at four gates, four numbers, and every one of the four numbers is the number that was in the district's book before he arrived, and there is no column in this county that will ever say that, and there never will be, because the column would have to be headed *did the looking change anything*, and the answer is no, four times, and a man who spent his season's work confirming a book in four yards is the best vindication of a practice anybody in this county could want and the worst possible news about the practice, and both of those are the same fact and the fact is true. **And the fourth of those four is not on the schedule, and the round has a line against it anyway, and the column the round was kept in does not ask whether the place is one anybody has to go to.**

**And the fifth line says eleven, and eleven is a figure from the Concord Year 486 that a clerk typed out of a book, and it is in the same ink on the same form, four lines away from four numbers a person put his hand on.**

He wrote three things on the back of the tide table and they are the whole of the ground and he has them still:

**One. That the district return for the season of the Concord Year 489 was received in the state in which it was made, figures carried forward, no verification of premises in this district in the season of 489, and that the ten rows of premises of record upon it are therefore not figures of the season of 489.**

**Two. That a count is a count of a season named on it, and that where no person was in any of the ten settlements of record in this district in the season of 489, then no figure upon the return is a figure of that season, and the arithmetic of a certification that describes them as such does not add up to a statement of what the count is a count of.**

**Three. That there are exactly two things the Office can do about that, and both of them are true, and the Office may choose which one it does, and the choice is not mine.**

And then, because he writes things down and because he has been caught twice in a season about the difference between an answer and a question:

**I do not say the figures are wrong. I say they are not of this season. I do not say the schedule was made wrongly. I do not say any settlement was chosen and I have nothing to say about the tenth row and I am not going to say it. I do not put a word in the reason column and there is no reason column in what I have written and I have checked.**

He put the sheet away and asked the counting clerk one question, and it was a real question and it was the only one he had come for.

"If I put that in, and it is heard, and the Office answers it — which of the two answers is the one they have already decided to give."

She did not pretend to think about it.

"**They will print that they counted none.** They will not produce the figures, because there are no figures, and because the return is on your own file with a note on it in my building's hand that says no verification took place, and because a body that has been asked to produce figures it does not have and then produces them anyway is a body that has been asked the wrong question by a man who did not think about it." She put the pencil down. "And they are right to. And you know they are right to, and you are going to go and tell somebody you knew, and I would like it noticed that you worked out which answer was coming before you had finished your second cup of water."

"Yes," said Owen Rusk.

"**Then the whole of what you are going to get is the second of the two, and I want you to sit with it for about a minute before you go down the hill, because I have watched four people do this in nine years and every single one of them got the thing they asked for and went out of this room happy and came back inside a season and could not work out why.**"

He sat with it for about a minute.

Then he said, "**I want the print.**"

"You want the print."

"If they print that they counted none, then this district is not counted this season, and it is on a printed list, and there is no place on the list that says the district was not counted, because the list is of settlements, and a district is not a settlement, and nobody has ever made a list that says that."

"That is not an instrument," said the counting clerk.

"No," said Owen Rusk. "It is not an instrument. It is the fourth thing."

---

He went down the hill at about four and he did not go to the frame, and he did not go up the lane to the ward, and he did the tray, and it was on at a quarter past six, and it was as empty as it had been on the Friday.

**Three holds and three claimants and no fourth.** Ivy Sarr's household, of two rooms in Fetter Lane, a person who cannot be asked. Mavis Rudge, of Witherow, who asked for a document in which somebody asked her and the answer was no, and got it, and did not enjoy one minute of it. Ned Callow, of Quill Bank, who asked for a person in a room and did not get one and will not ask again.

**And the only thing he has found in a fortnight that is his to do is arithmetic, about a figure that is the right figure and is simply not of this season, and the Office has already told him in writing that it will hear that, and it did not do him a favour, and it is not a favour, and there is no form in this county on which a person may be given a thing without it being a decision by somebody.**
