# Character State

Where each active character stands, what they want, and what they are carrying. Updated after every batch. This is state, not biography; fixed background lives in `bible/characters.md`.

Positions below are as of the end of **Chapter 10, batch Day 14**.

## Volume 01 — The Unfiled Ward

### Owen Rusk, 32 — Copy Clerk, Fourth River Desk, **posted to the tray of unfiled correspondence**

Wants: to enter the ward's return, and to stop being the only person carrying it. Holds: a private notebook that leaves the building, a co-petitioner's place on the Glass Stair docket, one signed statement against himself, nine pages nobody may enter. Fears, and now has evidence for: becoming the hand inside a machine that closes crossings. Flaw, named by other people before he could name it: he writes the trap and then defends his own accuracy. Stage 0. He signs nothing that carries authority. He has lost his quarterly run, his standing in his own ward, and the belief that being right is a defence. He is frightened of himself in a specific, useful way and does not yet have a method for the difference between being right and being dangerous. He has one brass number in his notebook and a forgery's exact wording, and he has not priced either.

### Nell Arbour, 35 — Claimant Advocate, Arbour Kitchen

Wants: people able to answer for themselves before the rule finishes answering for them, and she has just given Owen the terms of the only instrument that can do it. Has reestablished contact as an adversary who recognises his work. Refused to stop representing Lower Lock, refused to be his witness, and has now offered to help with the return's **appeal route** and nothing else. Read his Chapter 9 statement on Saturday and told him it was the first thing he had written in ten years that was not a way of not saying something. Does not know his ledger, does not know about the continuity statement, and has not asked. Still carries the hearing room. **Owes him nothing and has not said so kindly.**

### Ada Rusk, 24 — Bridge Mechanic and crew leader, Lower Lock

Wants: her ward to have a lawful return of its own, on terms she did not have to be rescued into. She refused Owen's money and his plan at the pump house door, and told him the return's scope in a two-hour argument she won. She voted **no** at the ratification and is one of the four named forever in the ward's own book. She has said, in front of the two witnesses, that the nine of them are now the people who keep that crossing up and that when the water comes up in the winter it is us who go down there with a line. She was the one who wrote the disclaimer about safety, condition and use into the return. She calls him *copyist who stayed* and means it as an accounting, not affection. Wants, next: to be asked what she wants rather than what she is owed.

### Junia Pell, 22 — Junior Copy Clerk, second desk, supervising the assisted tray

Wants: to be competent without becoming a custodian of other people's lives, and has now demonstrated it three times — she refused the short-form attestation's witness line, she signed the return's observation as an outsider while recording her own office's interest against herself, and she read the ward's copy of the transfer and got nothing and did not pretend otherwise. She dated Owen's statement and refused the entry, because the date is what she is on it for. She is the only living witness to the destruction of the continuity statement and she has not called it a debt, has not told anyone, and owes him nothing. She has a slip with six names and four missing parts on it, and she told him plainly that nobody will solve it in the month he has. She is carrying a case on her tray waiting for an assisting clerk who does not exist.

### Halden Pike, 47 — Deputy Registrar, Docketford

Wants: Docketford solvent and a decision actionable by Friday. He has said, out loud, that every district he stabilises is a district that does not drown somewhere else, and meant it, and offered Owen a better-paid posting which Owen refused. He issued a technically correct load-restriction suspension on the eleventh and it is in force. He has not yet signed a stability order and cannot until Chapters 17–18. He has read the sixteen names into a column on the morning of the twelfth and has begun to think of them as a schedule. He has stopped managing Owen and started routing documents through him, and has not said so.

### Marguerite Bray, 61 — Ward Steward, Lower Lock

Keeps the ward's roll and, in its back half, the delivery book. Filed the petition in her own name and her own words and asked only for a hearing. Told Owen that the four dissenters matter more than the nine. Told him, to his face, that she is not his ally and never thanked him for the thing he proved. She is the filer and Owen is the co-petitioner. She wants the ward to be able to exist without the Office and she does not believe Owen is the person who will get it there.

### Wend Voss, 51 — Counter clerk, Mill Ward annex

The man whose counter received the transfer. Signed as the second outside witness to the ward's return, having personally witnessed the marker and two pump starts, and separated his interest in his own words as a cost his district has carried for eleven months without a district behind it. He came down to the weir at dusk on his own time and did not announce it. He is not a convert. He wants a district that performs what his district cannot afford to keep, and he wants it named.

### Cobb Rennie, 58 — Lower Lock

Nineteen years at the same two rooms and off the Registry roll for two of them. Outside the thirteen households and outside the thirteen erased entries; nothing in this case reaches him. Got his name written once, in pencil, in Bray's book, in Ferris's and Tillet's own hands, and said he liked being written in the one time. Still cannot draw a pension and will not be able to at the end of the volume. Wants: to be locatable. Is not going to be.

### Dory Anstruther, 19 — Bridge-crew apprentice, Lower Lock

First of the seven adults on the no-local-record schedule, and now a man with a broken collarbone, a lung full of river, and two descriptions of himself that do not match. Owen told him to his face, at four in the morning on a pump step, that his name is on a schedule and that Owen had read it, and he worked harder for four hours afterwards. He came out of the spillway. He has not been asked to sign anything and is not going to be asked by Owen.

### Ivy Sarr, 12 — Lower Lock, lives with her aunt Wenna Sarr

First line of the no-local-record schedule, one of the six who cannot answer. Tal's granddaughter; he had no authority over her and there is now nobody left who ever had any authority over her. Her aunt is one of the four who voted no and said the reason out loud in a hall: *I am not a witness. I am the only grown person that child has got.* Needs a protective return. There is no instrument in Docketford that can hold her, and nobody has the four parts to build one. Unresolved at the end of the batch and unresolved at the end of the volume.

### Marta Rusk — deceased

Fifteen years dead of fever after a medicine shortage in a flood-year closure. The Registry's delay in revising her residence status did not cause the fever; it delayed her priority medicine and left her **burial disputed for eight months**. Owen used that fact at the pump house door to argue for money and was refused. Her record is not amended in this batch and is not amended until Chapters 46–50. The correct record says she is dead and says the Office delayed.
