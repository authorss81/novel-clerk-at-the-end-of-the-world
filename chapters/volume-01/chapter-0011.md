# Chapter 11 — Entry

The tray has no schedule, and that turned out to be the single most useful fact Owen learned about it in his first fortnight, because a thing with no schedule is never late and never early, and a man posted to a thing that is never late is a man who is there on a Tuesday morning at seven with nowhere he has to be for an hour.

He came up off the ground floor at ten to seven with his coat still dark at the shoulders and put his case on the rail of the second desk, where the supervised tray lived, and Junia Pell was already in it.

The tray was a shallow tray. He had spent fourteen days at a deep drawer under a lock and the change was like stepping out of a river into a room: a board on two trestles, a ledger, a stamp, a cup for a wet pen, and seven things in it, none of which had a schedule either.

"Thursday's still sitting there," Pell said.

"Whose?"

"Twice. A parcel for Stonecross that needs a district return to be of anywhere, and a notice for Havelock Reach, and I have not opened the parcel because the parcel is not mine to open and there is a difference and nobody ever believes it." She dipped her pen. "You are going to ask me for something."

"I am going to ask you for a supervised tray."

"You are on the tray."

"I'm on the tray that you supervise and nobody enters on, which is a posting of a person to a piece of furniture. I want to be appointed to it."

Pell put the pen down. It was the first time in a month she had stopped writing when somebody spoke to her.

"Say the rest of it."

"If I ask to be appointed and you ask me why, I am going to tell you that a case is sitting on it with a date on it, and the date is the fourteenth, and if somebody reads that date on the docket in a year they will find out how long a ward's return lay in a Registry clerk's tray while that clerk was in the same building." He put the covering slip on the board. "That is the whole reason. I am not asking because I think I am the right man. I am asking because the delay has to be somebody's and I would rather it was on the record than in a drawer."

Pell looked at the slip. On it, in her square hand, was Monday's date, and under it, in his, the words *delivered by O. Rusk, copy clerk*. One day.

"Sit down," she said.

---

It took most of a day, and it took most of a day because an entry is not a signature. He had spent nine years believing that a filing was a thing a man wrote and stamped, and he had been wrong about that in a way that was going to cost him an afternoon and, permanently, a certain kind of confidence.

A return is entered by a set of checks, and the checks are performed in an order, and the order is not a matter of taste. The first check is that a filing must be complete on its face. Completeness on the face is not accuracy. It is only the presence of the six things a filing is made of: a subject, a term with a date in it, a jurisdiction, an effect, an appeal route, and two observers.

He laid the nine pages out on the tray in the order they were bound and went down the list with the flat attention he had trained into himself for batch days.

Subject: the maintenance of the Ninth Weir public crossing, a named work at the named place. Present, specific, and narrow enough that he could not, if he wanted to, hide a whole ward inside it.

Term: seven days from the day of entry. Present. The date would be today's, whatever today's was, and the interval would run from it, and he had spent a fortnight being told this by a woman in a kitchen and had not understood until the trestle table that the return was the only document in this business that started its own clock by being received.

Jurisdiction: Lower Lock, and the river path, and the pump house, and the granary door. Present.

Appeal route: named, on the first page, and open. Present, and — this was the one he stopped on — the route ended at an address in Mill Ward and a name he had never met, and there was a space under the name for a person to say whether they would stand at the far end of it, and the space was empty, and the route was nonetheless open, and open was the legal word, and open with nobody behind it was a thing he filed away and did not like.

Observers: two. Junia Pell, Registry of Continuant Works, River Desk, second desk. Wend Voss, counter clerk, Mill Ward annex. Both present, both with their own words in the margin, and both of those margins were longer than anything the form asked for, which is what happens when people are honest on a form designed to be dishonest with.

"Complete on its face," Pell said. "Sign the box."

He signed the box. It was the first box he had signed in nine years that meant anything, and he found that he had no feeling about it at all, and that this was itself a feeling, and that he was going to have to look at it later when he had the time.

The second check was the supervising officer's countersignature. The second check was not a formality and it was not a rubber stamp, and the reason it was not a rubber stamp was that a supervisory countersignature is a statement that the entry was made within the filer's authority and in the ordinary form, and a man who countersigns an entry made outside the filer's authority does not get a bad morning, he gets a quarter of an inquiry and a career.

"Sit down, Mr Rusk," said the seal-stair clerk, and Owen discovered that he had followed Pell up without deciding to, and that they were on the stair.

She was about fifty and had been on that stair twenty years, and she had the economy of a person who has watched nine thousand filings arrive and has never once been surprised by one. She read the covering slip and the appointment slot on the tray card and the tray itself, in that order, and then she said the thing that was going to cost the day.

"A return may not be entered by a clerk named in the case, or living in the district to which the case relates."

"Yes," said Pell. "I know. I found it last night."

"You found it last night," said the seal-stair clerk, "and you brought him up a stair at ten to eight in the morning to tell him."

"I brought him up a stair," said Pell, "because you are the only person in this building who can appoint an assisting clerk and I am not."

That was a very small sentence and it was perfectly correct, and Owen understood in the four seconds it took him to hear it that she had decided the night before to go and get the appointment, and had gone this morning at seven, and that she had not mentioned it and was not going to, and that this was the third thing she had done for him in a month without letting him thank her for it and the first two had cost her something and this one would as well.

"Right," said the clerk. "Let us look at what is actually wrong with that."

Owen said it, because it was his to say and Pell had brought him up a stair to let him say it. He said: his name is on the co-petition in the Glass Stair docket. His name is on a statement of his own part in the same file. He is the person who delivered the return to the tray and the person named on the covering slip. He copied the ward's nine pages, which is not in itself a bar and which he wanted on the record, because somebody would use it later. And he lives at Fetter Lane, in Lower Lock, which is a bar, and which is not going to change, and which he had spent nine years declining to treat as a professional fact.

"And the supervisor?" said the clerk. "Named in the instrument?"

"As an observer."

"Then the supervisor is named in the instrument." She said it as though she were counting sheets. "And?"

"The supervisor has separated her interest in the margin in her own words, and the separation is against herself, and it is dated," said Owen, "and it is the third sentence in it, and I did not write it and could not have written it better."

"I am not asking whether you could have written it better. I am asking me." The clerk looked at Pell. "Are you a party to the drafting of this return?"

"No."

"Have you read the scope?"

"Yes."

"Are you the person who may be asked, in a room, whether the ward consented to the crossing being in it?"

Pell did not answer for a moment, and Owen understood that she was not hesitating but choosing, and that a man who interrupts a choice is a man who thinks his own is better.

"No," she said. "If I am asked that, the answer is that I saw four acts on a riverbank and a lamp in a pump house and did not know what they were for, and that I have not read the second leaf of the annex, and that anything else I say about the ward's consent is a thing you are asking me to have an opinion about so that you can quote it."

"That," said the clerk, "is the correct answer and it is the correct shape. Very well." She wrote for about two minutes, in a small hand, on a form about the size of a card. "Assisting clerk, supervised tray, second desk. It is a posting and it is not a qualification and it will be on your record as a posting. It names the tray. It names the case. It does not travel with you if you are moved off the tray, and it is not in your own hand to keep."

"And what may I sign on it?"

She turned the card round and put her finger on the four lines at the foot of it.

"Entry of a filing on this tray, and the covering of papers going to the stair, and a comparison of copy against record. Nothing else. You may not sign a continuation. You may not sign a certification. You may not sign a suspension. You may not sign a hold. If a filing on this tray requires any of those four, it does not go up, and you do not have a discretion about it, and neither does I, and there is no form on which either of us could record an opinion." She took her finger off the card. "Sign that you understand it, and then sign your name under it, and then the card goes in the tray and not in your pocket."

Owen read it twice. He read it a third time with his finger on the third line, and read the third line aloud in the flat voice.

"May not sign a hold."

"That is the one everybody reads past," said the clerk. "In nine years I have watched two men read past that line and neither of them is still in this building. Are you going to be all right with it?"

"I am going to be careful with it," said Owen.

"That is not the same and it is not what I asked." She put the pen down. "Everybody is careful with it. Careful is not what anybody can do. What you are going to do is go back down that stair and want something you cannot sign, and on the day you want it you are going to remember that there is a line, and if the line is in your handwriting you will go and find somebody else to want it from. Sign."

He signed the card and signed his name under it and the clerk took it away and put it in the tray, and it was, he understood walking back down, the first document of his working life that had been written about what he was not allowed to do.

---

They went back down at ten past eight with the appointment made, and nothing happened for three hours, and the three hours were the most instructive of his life in this Office.

Because the checks have an order, and the order is not the order you would choose. The completeness check has to be recorded before anybody may write on the effect, and the supervising countersignature has to be *asked for* — it is not given, it is requested, and it is granted or it is not, and a tray waits, and a tray that waits is visibly waiting, and every clerk on the copying floor can see a tray waiting from the end of the hall. And Owen, who had spent ten weeks producing a batch that came up the stair on the day it was due, discovered that a tray waiting for a countersignature is a public act.

So at eleven he went and asked for it, at the second desk, in front of the hall.

Pell read the nine pages. Not the acts — she had been there for the acts; she had stood on the weir path in the dark while a boy was in the water. She read the *instrument*: the subject, the term, the jurisdiction, the route, the observer lines, the withdrawal note, the four recorded dissents, and the scope, word by word, and she took four minutes over it and stopped twice.

"Stop at 'performed by the household branches of this district,'" she said at the second stop.

"Go on."

"I am not objecting. I am asking you to hear it. Nine people, one page each, and every one of them is a *household branch*, and this is a district return, and the form does not have a word on it for a household in it." She put a finger on the margin. "It has a word for a household, and a word for a district, and it has no word for a district made of households who each did a thing. I am not going to be the officer who returns this. Somebody in the district office is going to read *household branches* in a district return and decide it is loose, and he will be right, and he will write a memorandum, and the memorandum will say the return is irregular in its composition and will not say what to do about it, and that memorandum will be in this file in a year and it will be the only thing anybody reads."

"Then what do I do?"

"You have two options and they are both bad." She turned the sheet round. "You can leave the words. And then you defend a phrase you invented to the first man in the district office who is paid to dislike invented phrases. Or you can narrow it, and narrow it to *the nine persons whose acts are recorded*, and then it is a district return with nine performed acts in it, which is what it is, and which nobody can argue is loose because there is nothing in it to be loose about." She squared the sheet. "I would narrow it. Narrowing is not a defeat. A filing that says less is a filing that cannot be wrong about more."

Owen took the sharp pencil out of his case for the first time in a month. It was the pencil he had not sharpened in ten weeks because he had not needed it, and it was blunt, and he had to take it back to the tray desk and borrow a knife, and Pell watched him do that and said nothing whatever about it.

He wrote, in the scope, after *maintenance of the Ninth Weir public crossing, a named work at the named place, is performed by*: *the nine persons whose acts are recorded in this return, at the river path and the pump house, on the days recorded, before the observers recorded; and by no other person; and no other person is included in this return.*

It was a loss. Every word of it was a loss, and it took four minutes, and when he had done it he read it out loud in the copying hall to nobody in particular because it was the only way he had of hearing whether a sentence was true.

Nobody said anything. The hall went on copying.

Then Pell countersigned, in her own hand, in the box, and the box said what a supervisory countersignature says, and she did not add a word to it and did not need to.

---

The third check was the effect, and the effect was a menu.

Owen had known, in the way a man knows the shape of his own desk, that an entry recorded four things: a subject, a term, an effect, and a jurisdiction. He had never seen the effect field filled by a person who was thinking about it, because the effects he had copied had all been the same six words in the same place, and it had never occurred to him that the effect was a *choice* — that the form printed a list and a list is a set of doors and a clerk standing at a list at half past two in the afternoon is the most powerful person in the room.

The effect field for a community return printed five clauses. He read all five out loud, twice, the first time to the hall and the second time to himself, because the second time was for him and the hall had not asked.

*i. That the district is restored to supported standing for the purposes of district acts, the named work included.*
*ii. That the last supported status of the named work is supported by the district's performance of its maintenance.*
*iii. That the performance recorded in this return is received and is subject to review at the interval stated.*
*iv. That the district's performance may be relied upon by any district having jurisdiction over the named work.*
*v. That the repair of the named work is a duty of the district filing this return.*

Owen read them out and then sat quite still for a moment, and the thing he felt was not a thought. It was the sensation of standing in a room in which several doors had just opened, all of them at once, in a building in which a man had spent nine years believing there was one door and a rule about which side of it you stood on.

Clause i was the freeze. The Day 14 freeze — no household continued, licensed or witnessed on a district act — lifted on i, and not on ii, and not on iv, and the difference between those two words was the whole of the difference between a ward that had its own name back and a ward that had been given something in the name of a bridge.

Clause ii was a status claim about the work. That was the one the Office had used on the transfer. That was the sentence that said a crossing had a status because somebody had signed something about it, and it was the sentence that had put thirteen names against a piece of river masonry. And it was available, and it was on the list, and it would have been so much easier, and the reason it was easier is that it would have let the return be copied into a claim by anybody who ever needed one, and the return had been built by nine people in nine days specifically so that it could not.

Clause iv was the one that frightened him. *May be relied upon by any district having jurisdiction over the named work.* That was a sentence about the future, written by a clerk at a trestle table on a Tuesday afternoon, and it would have handed the repair of a bridge to whoever claimed jurisdiction over the bridge, which was exactly the door the transfer had come through eleven months ago with a tired man's signature on it.

Clause v was a lie the ward had explicitly declined to tell. Ada had beaten him with a wet marker boot over that sentence and the sentence she had beaten him with was in the return, four paragraphs up, in her words, and it was still there: *this district does not perform, and does not claim to perform, the repair of the west abutment.*

Owen read the five clauses a third time and then he said them out loud with the marks on, which is a thing a man does when he has just learned not to assume.

"I. That the district is restored to supported standing for the purposes of district acts, the named work included. Yes. That is what the ward is claiming and it is what the ward has performed. **Entered.**"

"II. That the last supported status of the named work is supported by the district's performance. No. That is a claim about a bridge and not about a duty, and the ward has expressly declined to make it, and I am not going to put it in for them at a trestle table because it would be convenient. **Not entered.**"

"III. That the performance recorded in this return is received and is subject to review at the interval stated. Yes. That is the return itself. **Entered.**"

"IV. That the district's performance may be relied upon by any district having jurisdiction over the named work. No. No such person has said that and no such person is in the ward. **Not entered.**"

"V. That the repair of the named work is a duty of the district filing this return. No. The ward wrote the opposite and Ada Rusk said it out loud in a hall in front of thirty people. **Not entered.**"

Pell listened to the whole of it without moving.

"You have narrowed it twice in a day," she said. "Once in the composition and once in the effect."

"I have."

"Do you know what a narrow effect does to a document?"

"It makes it harder to attack."

"It makes it harder to *use*," she said. "The Office will be able to say in six weeks that you did not claim the crossing's status, and that will be true, and it will be a sentence in a memorandum, and it will be a sentence that is technically correct and completely beside the point." She capped her pen. "You have just made your instrument unimpeachable. Do not mistake that for making it strong. They are different properties and this Office has spent four hundred and eighty-seven years confusing them, and so have you, in the last ten minutes, a little."

Owen had no answer to that. He wrote it down instead, on the back of the tray card, in pencil, in the margin where a man puts a thing he is not yet able to argue with.

Then the tray went down to the ground-floor counter, because that is where a filing that is not lodged by a supervisor of the copying floor is initialled into the Front Desk register before it goes up the stair. And the counter is the Front Desk, and the Front Desk keeps a request register, and the request register is the book the entry clerk initials past.

He had been sitting in that room for a fortnight. He had never once turned a page back.

The clerk's finger went down the intake column to today's line, and stopped three lines above it, and Owen looked at the three lines above it because they were above his finger and he could not not look.

Three refusals. In the standard form. In the standard ink. Each one dated inside the two days between the ninth and the eleventh of a month in the autumn of last year, and each one initialled at the foot by an office and not by a man, and each one reading, in the whole of its words:

*Refused. No supported district at the named place.*

He had known they were there. He had read them on the first day of the month in a different volume of the same book and copied them into a notebook he bought at the ironmonger on Fetter Lane, and he had looked at them on his first morning at that desk and had thought they were probably about a place, a bridge, a ferry, water, a name, and had gone up to the seal stair and read a standard about a person who cannot answer, and had spent the rest of a fortnight proving that thirteen people existed.

And they were three lines above his finger, and he had been in the room every day, and nobody had said a word about them, because nobody was going to.

The price of the amendment in Marguerite Bray's hall on the sixth day of the month was that he would not look. He had set it himself. He had set it out loud to two people in a warm room and one of them had asked him whether he could live with it and he had said yes.

He initialed his own line and he did not stop, and he did not ask, and the not-asking went into him in a place he did not have a name for.

---

The return went up the stair at ten past four. Pell carried it to the press and Owen went with her because the tray goes with the filing. The seal is pressed onto a wet carbon and the carbon is folded and the fold is where the file lives for the next forty years, and the press has a catch tray of pressed steel, and the die is still hot at half past four in the afternoon in a wet season because it is banked and never goes properly cold.

"Take the sheet out flat," Pell said. "Not by the corner."

He took it out flat and put his right hand down on the lip of the catch tray to steady the carbon while he set it on the board, and the lip was hot, and the skin on the outside of his right middle finger opened along the crease in about half an inch, and it did not hurt for perhaps two seconds and then it hurt continuously, and he put his hand in his coat and stood there while the clerk entered the docket and initialled the seal and stamped the stub, and the docket number came back on a slip about the size of his hand.

**487-LL.**

District return. Lower Lock. Entered this day. Term: seven days. Review: this day in the next week.

He read the stub the way you read a thing you have just learned not to assume, and then he read the return's own first page, which was the ward's page, in the ward's words, and the sentence on it said that the return came back for renewal every seven days from the day it is entered and not before, and the day it was entered was the fifteenth, and so the day it came back was the twenty-second.

Underneath the docket number the clerk had written out, in the small print the seal stair uses for a thing an entry has done to the roll rather than to a person, what the entry had done. Owen read it standing up, and it took him a moment to understand that he was not going to be able to understand it standing up, and that the only thing to do with a thing like that was to sit down and read it.

*The standing of the Ninth Weir public crossing is restored as a duty which the district of Lower Lock performs, and as nothing else. The district is restored to supported standing for the purposes of district acts, and the suspension of district acts entered against the households of the district on the fourteenth day of the month is lifted from this hour. No grant is restored, and the grant of the Fetter Lane pump is not restored by this entry, and the suspension of that grant stands until its own review. The load-restriction suspension of the tenth day of the month is not affected, and is not superseded, and remains in force.*

He read the fourth line three times.

So the ward had its name back and could be licensed, continued and witnessed on a district act again as of ten past four on the fifteenth, which was nine days of frozen households ended by a piece of paper, and none of the three other lines said one word in its favour: the crossing was not safe, the crossing was not open, the crossing was not funded, and the crack in the west abutment was still five inches where a district engineer had measured it in the wet nine days ago with a rule and a lamp.

Owen Rusk sat on the arm of the stool at the bottom of the stair with a cut hand and a docket stub and read a paragraph of the Registry's own small print that gave his ward everything he had asked for and nothing he had wanted.

He wrote both dates in his own book. The fifteenth and the twenty-second. And then he sat there a while longer and found that nothing was happening to him.

That was the part. He had spent a fortnight being frightened of his own precision, and he had got a clean result, and the only thing available to him was the discovery that the file was now bigger than he was. Nine pages, and a docket number, and a supervisor's name, and a hand of his own in a box that meant something, and a date seven days out that would come back to ask him questions, and every one of the nine would be able to take their page out on a Tuesday and go and do something else with their life, and he would not be able to take a single word of it out, because it was not his document and never had been and never would be.

It was the best work he had ever done. There was no feeling in it at all. That was worse than anything that had happened to him in a month.

Pell came down past him with the tray and the ledger and said, "You are going to say something about how you feel."

"No," said Owen. "There is nothing in it."

"There never is," she said, and went up, and he understood about nine minutes later, on a stair he was not on, that this was the second time in three weeks she had said something that was not advice and had not charged him for it, and that he was keeping a ledger of them, and that keeping a ledger of them was a habit, and that a habit is a thing a man does instead of a thing a man says.

At the bottom of the stair a runner was waiting, which was not an ordinary thing at ten past five, and she had a slip in her hand with the district office's stamp on it.

"From the Deputy Registrar's office," she said. "They have asked for the return for the quarter's review."

"What review?"

"The Deputy Registrar's review." She said it the way a runner says a thing she has been told to say exactly. "It is not a hearing, sir. It is a review. I am told to tell you that it is not a hearing and I have told you."

Owen took the slip. It was a request for papers, in the correct form, in the district office's hand, and it named his file, and it named the docket number he had been holding for two hours, and it was dated the same afternoon the filing went up the stair, which meant it had not been written today, which meant somebody had known the return was coming before the return came.

He looked at the slip for a while.

"Then that is the next thing," he said, "and there is nothing in it for me yet, and I am going to go upriver and tell nine people that a piece of paper exists."

He walked out into the rain with his hand in his coat, and the river was up another two inches than it had been at seven that morning, and the stubs came off the docket at the counter in the ordinary way, in a numbered fan, one to the ward, one to the receiving annex, one to the district office, one to the file, and his was the last one, and he put it in the inside pocket on the left, because the right hand had stopped working properly somewhere between the press and the bottom of the stair.
