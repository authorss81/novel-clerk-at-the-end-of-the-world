# Chapter 80 — Which of These Is a Place

Marguerite Bray wrote to the district office on the Wednesday morning of the fifth day of the third month of the Concord Year 488, at the ward hall, on the ward's own paper, in her own name, and it is one side of a sheet and it is the first question that district has been asked by that ward in about two years and it is the first question it has been asked in a steward's own name about a document rather than about a person.

**To the officer of the record of the district returns of Docketford district. From Marguerite Bray, steward of the ward of Lower Lock. I am asking about a column.**

*I am told that the return your office made for the season of the Concord Year 489 carries a fifth column headed year of last survey, and that the column is not completed, and that your office has written a sentence across the ten cells and has been told that a sentence is not a year. I want three things and I am asking for all three at once and I am not going to come back for them one at a time.*

*One. I am asked to rule into this ward's book whatever this district office decides about its own record, and I would like to rule in a document and not a rumour. What is the fifth column for.*

*Two. There has been no survey of any place in this district in this season and the notice on the frame behind the tanneries says so, and it is dated the twenty-sixth day of last month, and I would like to know what your office proposes to do about the ten places named in the schedule that goes up in June if nobody goes to them.*

*Three. If the answer is that the district office is not competent, I would like that in writing with a day on it, so that it can be ruled into this book with the day and the hour, because this book has been kept fifty-one years and it does not have anybody's guess in it and it is not going to start.*

She read it back twice to herself at the table before it went, which is not the third clause of the rule they work by and is a stricter one, and then she put it in a bag and Ruth Mapp carried it up the hill at about eleven because Marguerite Bray is sixty-one and was not going to be seen on a road with a document. The disposition came back on the Thursday morning of the sixth at about half past ten, at the second desk, in a hand at a counter, and it is a numbered column and a month and an officer's initials and a disposition, and the disposition is four words long, and the four words are the four words that have been in the disposition column of that office about forty-one times in one quarter of the last year.

**DISPOSITION. The third month. Entry numbered. *NO DISTRICT OFFICE COMPENT.***

And underneath, in the ordinary form's voice, five clauses and a courtesy, and the fifth clause is a sentence Owen Rusk has read in about nine years in two other buildings and has never once enjoyed.

*A disposition is a decision. A decision is entered with the initials of the officer and the date of its making. A disposition is not a reason and is not a record of a reason. This office does not maintain a column of reasons and is not required by any instrument in force in this district to maintain one. "No district office competent" is a statement of the reach of this office at the date of its making and is not a finding upon any person, place or work.*

He read it standing at the desk, once, and then went and got Marguerite Bray's letter back out of the district tray, because a person who has sent a question is entitled to the answer in the hand, and carrying it up the hill in a bag is not the same as carrying it in a hand, and he has been the officer of the record of a thing that happened to a person in a lane about forty times in nine years and it has never once been worth it and he does it anyway.

---

He read it out in the ward hall on the Thursday evening at ten past six, after the roster, which is the order the ward works in and he did not ask to change it. About thirty people. The stove on. He read the four words and the five clauses and the number of the entry and the initials, which he does not spell out, because a steward reads out an entry number and not a set of initials, and there are about nine people in that hall who could not have told you what an initial is.

Then he said, in the ordinary voice, in about nine seconds, the two sentences he had prepared and had not shown to anybody, and Ada Rusk had not looked at them and had not asked to see them and had found out about them at about ten past six along with everybody else, which is the correct way for a sister to be treated by a brother who is not the person who speaks for the ward.

"**I entered the answer into the record on the Thursday morning at about eleven and it is a correct answer and I am not going to stand in this hall and pretend that it is not, and I did not write it, and I cannot improve it, and there is nobody in this county I could send it back to.** A disposition is a decision, a decision is entered with initials and a date, a disposition is not a reason, this office does not keep a column of reasons and is not required to, and *no district office competent* is a statement about the reach of this office and not a finding upon any person, place or work. **All five of those are true. I have put four of them in this hall myself in other months about other documents, in about the same words, and I was right to, and this is what an office that is careful and not kind looks like from the inside.**"

Marguerite Bray was at the table with the delivery book open in front of her and she had her pen out before he had finished the first clause, and she had not written a word, and she waited until he had finished all of it, and then she said the thing that was in the ward's book from the first month of the year, in about nine seconds, and it was not a thing anybody in that hall had said before.

"**I asked a question in my own name on a piece of this ward's own paper and I have been told that this office is not competent, and I am sixty-one years old, and I have asked a question in this ward about eleven times in about two years, and the answer to eight of them was those four words, and I have written the numbers down in this book and not the answers, because an answer is not a fact about a ward and a number is.** And there are eight numbers in this book now and a ninth, and a number is the only thing this county will give a person who asks properly, and I would like the hall to notice that I have just said a true thing about nine questions and that nobody in this hall can do anything about any of the nine, and that includes me, and I am not going to pretend it does not."

Nobody said anything. The stove ticked.

Then Cobb Rennie said, "**It has not been a year since I asked for one of those to be said by somebody who was not me, and I have now said it myself twice and I am still not going to be thanked, and the reason is that it does not pay anybody and I have been told what a number is not a number of by three people in this hall and I am the fourth.**"

---

The two new sheets went up on the frame at the west gate of Cutter's Fields on the Friday morning of the seventh day of the third month, and the woman who maintains the board had the ladder out by half past nine and wired both of them before the field filled, and neither of them had been sent to her, and she had decided on the Thursday night at about eleven, which she told Owen Rusk afterwards and which is the only thing she said about it. The first is a strip of paper the size of a receipt and it is from the count room and it is unsigned and it corrects nothing and adds nothing.

**SURVEY OF SETTLEMENTS OF RECORD, DOCKETFORD DISTRICT. The suspension continues. Figures are carried forward. The fifth column of the district return will be completed in the season of 490 from the returns of district offices and of water offices.**

About thirty people were standing about and reading it as it went up, which is the whole reason the count has ever been published, and about thirty is a different number from about nine, and she has never once asked them which they were. The second is a single sheet, and it is the district's own answer to a question asked by a steward in her own name, and it has a number on it and a month on it and initials on it and four words on it and five clauses under the four words, and it is the district's own document, and it is on a public board in a field behind the tanneries, and the woman who maintains the board wired it up with two loops of tarred cord and stood back twice.

**Two sheets went up in about forty minutes, and one of them is the answer to a column that cannot be filled, and the other one is the answer to a question, and between them they are the whole of what this season has produced, and not one person is on either of them.**

Then Cobb Rennie asked the warden whether she was going to do anything about the list, in a field, in front of about thirty people, in the ordinary voice, and Owen Rusk was about eight feet off with his hands behind his back and did not go closer. Warden Kade had come down the hill at about a quarter to ten on the Friday, alone again, without a case and without a slate, and she had been at the frame since the Thursday, when the schedule was a day old, and she had not said a word to anybody since — and on the Thursday she had said several, in that field, to nine people, and had shut a door with one of them in it — and it is in this county a considerable thing for a warden to stand at a public board for eight days and say nothing.

"**No,**" said Warden Kade.

"You are the Warden."

"I am the Warden of the Census Guard, and the schedule of settlements to be reconciled is printed in Concord Hall in the last week of a month, and it is not mine, and I may return it and I may not amend it, and I have returned one in nineteen seasons and it came back with the same names on it four days later." She did not raise her voice and she did not soften and she did not turn round. "**And the day I start returning schedules to say that a settlement should not be on one is the day the Guard is choosing places, and a Guard that chooses places is the exact opposite of the thing this service is, and I would rather be the woman who signed nine of them and wrote none of the reasons than be the woman who started picking.**

"Your colleague's determination is mine as much as anybody's and I would have sustained it, and it is the correct answer to the question he was asked, and it is going to cost him more than it has cost me, and I told him so in a field on Thursday and I would say it again to anybody who asked me this morning."

"What will it cost," said Cobb Rennie.

"**It will cost a settlement nobody has been to, and I do not know which one, and neither does he, and that is the answer and I have been giving it for nineteen seasons and it is the reason nobody trusts me and it is the only shape of it I am allowed to work in.** Ask me on Friday, in a room, in four years or five, when somebody has made a document out of it, and I will tell you then, and I will be wrong in a way that a woman of about fifty with a slate has already written down in a determination in a room with high windows, and neither of us will have said no."

---

Then Cobb Rennie read the schedule out loud, which nobody asked him to do and which the woman who maintains the board let him do because she has maintained a frame for nine years and has never once told anybody what they may stand in front of and read. He read it from the top. He read it the way he reads a price, and he reads prices.

"Wexford Landing. Gallants Reach. Lower Lock. Quill Bank. Rush Halt. Witherow. Ossery. Otter Stair. Ashlocks. **District Office, Fetter Bend.**" He stopped there and looked up. "**Which of these is a place.**"

Nobody answered for a few seconds, and a man at the back said something about a building being a building, and Cobb Rennie said, "It is, and it is the building this whole thing is done out of, and it is on the list of the places this county is going to look at in the season of 489, and the man who made the list is the officer of the record of the returns the list is made out of, and he is standing about eight feet off this frame and he is not going to be able to tell you why it is on it, and neither am I, and neither is anybody in Concord Hall, because I have read the second column on that form and there is no second column for a place being a place."

Then he read the fourth column, and then he read the fifth column, and the fourth one took him longer.

"**The fourth column wants a draw.** Authorised draw. And there is nothing in it. Not one figure in ten rows." He put his hand flat on the frame an inch off the paper. "And the fourth column on this board last season had a heading on it and a figure in it against every place, and the figures were the doors written twice, and it went up in a person's own pencil on a word *granted* with no authority behind it, and I have read that column about nine times and I did not know then and I know now and I would like it said that I know now.

"**And the reason that column is not on the board is not that anybody took it off.** It is that the fourth position on a sheet like this has whatever heading the printing gives it, and this season the printing says *authorised draw*, and the figures for that are not on this board, they have never been on this board, and they are in a frame against a wall in a room across the cut that is a yard wide and that about four people a year go and read. **That is the column the eleven were chosen out of, and the eleven is not the ten, and there are two of the eleven that are not on this sheet and one on this sheet that was never on it, and the choosing was done in a room two hundred miles up a river with a pencil and a schedule of a hundred and ninety-four lines in front of it, and there is no column on any of them that says a place was chosen. The whole water the district is allowed and the water the eleven draw are the same figure, and I do not know what the figure is, and he does, and he is stood eight feet off this frame and he cannot put it on a board either, and nobody in this county may ask him where a schedule came from, because there is nothing to ask it on. And none of it is on a board anybody in this county walks to, and nobody was ever going to print it, and the reason nobody was ever going to print it is that a number in a field is a thing you can be asked about and a number in a room is a thing you can be.**"

Then he read the fifth column, which is ten cells with nothing in them either.

"**And the fifth column wants a year,**" said Cobb Rennie, "and there is not one year in it. Not one. And I have stood in front of this board about nine times this season and I never knew that column was on it until a fortnight ago, and I have never known what it was for, and there is not one person in this field who knows, and the man who has been in that building every day since the first day of the month could not fill it in either — he wrote a sentence in it, and the sentence came back with a note, and the note said a sentence is not a year, and the note was correct, and that is the whole of what has happened in this county in a season, and I have been standing here reading it for three months."

And then, at the end, in the ordinary voice, the thing he had wanted said by somebody who was not him since the end of June:

"**And Marl Dykes is not on it.**"

The field got about two seconds quieter.

"Two doors. Nine households. Last looked at in the Concord Year four hundred and eighty-one, and I have not been to Marl Dykes in nineteen years and I did not know it had come off a list, and nobody told me and nobody was going to tell me, and there is nothing anywhere in this county that says it is not on it, because there is no row for a settlement that was not reconciled and there is not going to be one for a settlement that was. **I have been owed a pension for two years and one month and I know exactly what that is and I am not going to compare myself to a village. But I read a column for three months and this is the first thing I have found on one that nobody is going to be able to put back, and I would like it said out loud by somebody who is not me, and I am saying it anyway, and I am not going to be thanked, because I have now asked for that twice and I have got it twice and it has never once been worth anything.**"

Ada Rusk, at the frame, ten feet off, said, "Correct," which from her is a decoration, and Marguerite Bray, who was at the back with the delivery book under her arm because it was a Friday and the roster had been read the night before and she had come up the hill afterwards with about four people, said the only sentence she said in that field all morning.

"**It goes in the book tonight, and not on the day, and the day it goes in the book is not the day it was read out, and both days are in the book, and that is the whole of what a ward's book is for.**"

---

The woman who maintains the board said one thing about the fourth position before she came down off the ladder, and she said it to about twenty people, and she said it in the voice she uses for a price, and she was not looking at anybody.

"**I have maintained that frame for nine years and I have never once been asked to put anything on it or take anything off it, and the one time I did a thing to it I did it on my own in the first month of the year and I was right and nobody asked me to.** And there is a position on that sheet the fourth one where a person in this district wrote figures in a pencil about ten weeks ago on a word *granted*, and it is empty again, and it is not empty because anybody took anything off it, and I am not going to write in it and I would like it noticed that I am a person who maintains a board and not an officer of anything, and there is no form on which anybody could have asked me to and there is no form on which anybody could have told me no."

Ada Rusk, at the frame, said the thing that was in the ward's book from the first month of the year, in about four seconds.

"**He has been right about a document.** And a strip of paper the size of a receipt has come down and it has moved the work of two offices in this county onto the two offices that can least afford it, and it has done it in the form's own voice, and the strip is correct. Visibility is not remedy and I said that in this hall in the first month and I am not going to say it again today because everybody in this field has said it twice this season and saying it a third time is the way a thing stops being true."

And Owen Rusk stood about eight feet off and did not say one word, and about thirty people watched a column of a board stay empty because nobody had ever given anybody anything, and it was correct, and the office had granted it in pencil with no authority behind it on the ninth day of the first month, and there was nobody in the county to take it back and there had never been anybody.

---

The frame at the west gate of Cutter's Fields, on the Friday morning of the seventh day of the third month of the Concord Year 488, from the top of it down, was this.

*The schedule for the season of 489, up since the twenty-eighth of the second month. Ten settlements. Five columns. The fourth column, headed authorised draw, empty on all ten. The fifth column, headed year of last survey, empty on all ten. The tenth row is a building.*

*The count of the ninth day of the second month. Qualified. Nine of eleven. Not reconciled: Lower Lock. Ossery.*

*The suspension of the Warden. One loop of cord. A page long, and the reason on it is not a figure.*

*The notice of the twenty-sixth, at the bottom. Survey of settlements of record. Suspended. Nine persons affected. The reason is not given and no form in this county provides for one. And at the foot of it, in a smaller type, the survey round as it stood on the twenty-third, five places, four of them with figures somebody looked at, and under the round, printed entire and filled in in nothing, a form headed SUSPENSION BY A WARDEN OF THE COUNT. Four lines and a date. The twenty-sixth. Not signed.*

*And, wired up that morning by the woman who maintains the board: a strip the size of a receipt saying that the suspension continues, that figures are carried forward, and that the fifth column will be completed in the season of 490 from the returns of district offices and of water offices. And a single sheet with a number and a month and initials and four words on it, and five clauses under the four words, being the district's own answer to a question a woman of sixty-one asked in her own name about a column.*

**The first working day of this month was the Sunday, and the season of 489 opened on it, and the schedule will not be amended after printing, and a Warden may return it and may not amend it, and there is no row on that frame for the two that were not reconciled, and there is no row on that frame for Marl Dykes, and there is no row on that frame for eleven households off a reach in the Concord Year 484, and there is no row on that frame for a person who cannot be asked, and every one of those four is a different door and not one of them has a handle.**

He went up the hill at about half past ten and did the tray and was on it at ten past one, and the tray was the same tray it had been on every evening since the first of the month.

Then he walked down Fetter Lane to the post at the head of it, where there are three notices, and the first is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and the words *any district able to perform the repair is invited to perform it*, and it has been in the weather since the flood and the ink has run in one line, and the second is last season's, with eleven names on it and every printed week on it gone or nearly gone, and it has been on that post since the twenty-eighth of the first month, and the third is the counting season's notice with ten names on it and a column nobody can fill, and it went up on the twenty-eighth of last month, and none of the three had changed.

He stood at the post for about a minute and a half and did not take the third one down, and did not think about taking it down, and did not write anybody a letter, and did not go up the reach, and did not open a drawer, and did not read a column.

**Three holds. Ivy Sarr's household, of two rooms in Fetter Lane, a person who cannot be asked. Mavis Rudge, of Witherow, who asked for a document in which somebody asked her and the answer was no. Ned Callow, of Quill Bank, who asked for a person in a room and did not get one and will not ask again. Three is a card and the card is the answer to a question he asked himself in a hall a month ago and the answer has not changed.**

A season is over. A list of places somebody chose before they looked is on a board in a field, and the practice that made it is not stopped and is not amended, and a column that wanted a year is going to be full in June of a year in which a man in a room across the cut corrected a piece of paper, and **the price of the whole of it has been paid by two offices in a strip of paper the size of a receipt, and neither of them has been asked whether it can pay it, and one of them is his own, and he is the officer of the record of it, and there is no form in this county on which a person may hand a piece of work back once it has been given away in a sentence somebody else wrote,** and a woman of sixty-one has eight numbers in a book and a ninth, and a man of fifty-eight has read a column for three months and found a village on the wrong side of it, and a man in a cart with a bootlace in a book has been asked nothing and cannot be named, and a man with a chain and a rule has been stood down with a polite form of words, and a Warden is suspended in her own hand in a field with a reason a page long and is not relieved and is not disgraced and is not thanked, and has said in public that she would rather sign nine of them and write none of the reasons than be the woman who started picking.

**And a district in Fetter Lane performs the crossing, the pump, the marker and the channel every Thursday and every Friday and every Wednesday night, out of a return that has been renewed six times for review and has never bound anybody, at four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds, with a granary empty since the seventh day of the second month, and a wage four months in arrears, and a girl of twelve who did the weights in a shed on the Sunday and asked whether a piece of paper opened the crossing and was told no and said right, and went back to her trays.**
