# Chapter 98 — The First Reason in Nineteen Seasons

The Guard's counting tent was up the reach at Ravensmoor on the Monday morning of the twenty-second day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488, and Owen Rusk went up there on the Monday because the correction of the eighth had to reach the Warden in her own hand and she was a hundred and sixty miles from the count room and forty from where the correction was, and because there was a form in this county on which a person may attend at the place of the count, and he had used it twice this year and both times it had been a formality.

He went up there to hand over four sheets, and he stayed two nights, and he came down on the Wednesday afternoon with a piece of paper in his coat that she had given him back unread at about eleven o'clock in the morning, and it is in a longhand book on a shelf in a room he has the key to, and the room has no window and a bulb on a flex taped twice.

**He had given her a sheet on the Tuesday morning of the twenty-third with the things he did not tell her in order, with the days on them, which is what she told him to do in a field on the twenty-ninth of the second month of this year, on the understanding that he would not write to her for a season, and the season was over, and the door was not shut any more, and he wrote the sheet in a room with a bulb on a flex and it took him about two hours and it is eleven lines and it is the only document in this county that anybody has ever written for her and nobody else.**

She read it standing up, at a table, with the tent flap open about four inches, in about ninety seconds.

"That is eleven lines and there are nine things in it and I am not going to ask you about any of them," she said. "**I said write them down in order with the days on them and you have done that and I have read them and I am putting it in the case and I am not going to say one word about it now or later, and I will not tell you what I thought of it, and I want that to be a rule between us and not a favour.**"

She gave it back and he put it in his coat and it is in the book and it is not in a file.

---

**Here is the Monday, and it is the reason for the Wednesday, and the reason is a thing she signed on the Monday morning without any difficulty at all.**

The counting tent at Ravensmoor is the same canvas room as at Cutter's Fields with a table and a lamp and a wooden case with a stone in it, and there were about six people in it on the Monday morning and one of them was the officer of the Ravensmoor district, who is a man of about forty-four who has been eleven years in that office and who is a set of hands and not a face and who is owed nothing by anybody.

A count correction came in on the Monday from the Provincial Counting Office at about ten, for a settlement of four premises of record in Ravensmoor district, and it was in four sheets and it was dated the fourth day of the fourth month, and it was a week late on a settlement that had been on a printed schedule since the twenty-eighth of the second month, and it was correct, and the reason in the box was **4**.

Kade read it standing up. She read every word twice and the second time slower, which she does.

"**Four,**" she said. "**Four premises. The reason is four. And the four is the number of premises reinstated, and it agrees with the schedule attached, and it is the right figure, and I have attested nine of these in a season and I will attest this one, and I would like the two of you to notice that I said all of that in about nine seconds and that I did not notice myself doing it.**"

She attested it. It took about a minute. She did not go to the place and there was nobody going to ask her about that, and the officer of the district said nothing, and a clerk wrote it down, and the tent got on with the day.

**And at about half past eleven on the Monday morning a clerk said a sentence out loud in that tent that was not addressed to anybody, and Warden Kade heard it, and Owen Rusk was about four feet away and heard it too, and it is the sentence that put the whole week where it went.**

"The district returns for four settlements this district have not gone up since 486," the clerk said, to the other clerk. "**And it is a good year for us. Four is easy. Four is four doors.**"

And Warden Kade put down the pen.

---

**On the Tuesday morning of the twenty-third they walked four miles to a place of four premises of record, and it took about an hour and a half, and Owen Rusk went because he was the person in the tent who could read a count sheet and not because anybody asked him to be there, and because she said *come* and did not give a reason and he did not ask for one.**

Marden Fell. Four premises of record against it, in the district's own book, entered in the season of 471, not struck, never struck, and about eleven households in it, and a chapel with a stove in it, and a lane of about four hundred yards off a road, and a woman of about sixty standing at the end of the lane with a dog that did not come forward, and there was nobody else on the lane and nobody had been on the lane.

**The warden of the Census Guard stood in a lane at about half past twelve on the twenty-third day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488, and she was on a printed list, and it was the first time in nineteen seasons that a person had asked her a question about a list on which she was the signatory, and it took about four minutes, and Owen Rusk wrote none of it down, and she has not asked him to and he has not offered.**

"You are on the schedule," said the woman. "**I can see it. I have read it. I can read.**"

"Yes."

"Then I am on it. Then I have been on it since the twenty-eighth of the month before last."

"You are on the schedule of the settlements to be reconciled. Your name is not on it and never has been. **The count is a count of draws and premises of record and works maintained and it is not a count of people, and I have been attesting that sentence in nine districts this season and this is the first time I have had to say it standing in front of the place.**"

The woman of about sixty looked at her for about four seconds.

"**Then I am not on it and I am on it,**" she said, "**and I have been waiting since the twenty-eighth of the month before last for somebody to come up this lane, and not one of you has come, and I have not asked anybody in nine years, and the last time I asked anybody anything at all I was told the answer in a room and I was not in the room.**"

"Who did you ask," said Warden Kade.

"**I asked the district office, nine years ago, whether my mother's house was still a house, because there has been nobody living in it for a long time and it is still in a book somewhere, and I wanted to know if a person is allowed to be a person in a place that has stopped being houses.**" She did not raise her voice. "**And I got a form, and the form is correct, and it said four clauses, and none of the four clauses was about my mother.**"

There were about nine people in that lane by then and the dog had gone to the far end of it.

Kade said: "**That is the answer in every quarter and I have said it nine times this season and I have never once said it standing in the place. I am not going to improve on it now. I am going to write down two facts and one of them is that you have not been asked in nine years and one of them is that I was in this lane at half past twelve today, and I am going to write them both in my own hand, and neither of them is going to be on the front of anything.**"

"And you cannot put them on the front," said the woman. "**There is no box. I have seen the form. It is a box for a number.**"

"No," said Warden Kade. "**There is no box, and I have been the Warden of this service for nineteen seasons and I have signed nine schedules and written nine reasons and every one of the nine reasons was a number, because a number is a thing you can say the meaning of in a room without deciding what it means, and I did not decide what any of them meant and I would have had to.**"

She looked up the lane at the four doors of the four premises of record and then at the eleven houses and did not finish the sentence.

"You have been on a list for two months and you have not been asked," she said, "**and the reason there is no box for me to write that on is that this county has never had a document that a person could read, and now it has one, and the document is a list and a list is not a person, and that is what we have instead and I have signed it nine times.**"

---

**At about ten o'clock on the Wednesday morning of the twenty-fourth day of the fourth month, at a gate on the Fetter Bend road where the Ravensmoor board is, Warden Kade struck the schedule of settlements to be reconciled for the season of 489 for Ravensmoor district, in her own hand, with a reason that is not a figure.**

She did not argue first. There is a thing people expect a person to do and she did not do it. She did not ask to see anybody's returns. She did not ask the district office for anything. She did not ask a clerk whether the office had a view. There were about nine people at the gate and about four of them work in offices in this city and two of them had come up the reach in a cart to be there and none of them had been told what was going to happen at the gate.

**She did not have a case and she did not have a slate. She had the schedule itself, a single sheet, printed on the twenty-eighth day of the second month of the Concord Year 488 by a building two hundred miles up a river, five columns, eleven settlements named, the fourth and fifth empty on all eleven, and it has been hanging on that frame since the twenty-eighth of the second month. **There is no row on a schedule for a settlement that was not reconciled, and there is not going to be one for a settlement that was, and that is a fact about the design of the form and it is a hundred and forty years old and it is the reason a list of places nobody went to has never had anywhere to go.** And a schedule is not to be amended after printing, and the Warden may return it and may not amend it.**

She wrote on the back. That is the whole of it, and she has said since that she chose the back deliberately, and that the back is where a person puts the thing they have to say when there is no box for it, and that the front of that sheet is a thing a building printed and the back is hers.

**STRUCK. The schedule of settlements to be reconciled for Ravensmoor district for the season of 489 is struck. The reason is that on the twenty-second day of this month I sat at a table in the counting tent and attested a correction for a settlement I had not been to, and the reason in the box was a figure, and it was the right figure, and on the twenty-third I went to the settlement, and there were eleven households in it, and nine of them have not been asked anything for two years and one of them has not been asked anything for nine. I have written reasons in a box nine times and every one of them was a number, and a number is a thing you can say the meaning of in a room without deciding what it means, and I did not decide. I cannot tell you what a settlement is until somebody has gone to it and asked. I went to one yesterday. I have not been to the other ten on this sheet, and this sheet is going out without me. — O. Kade, Warden of the Census Guard, the twenty-fourth day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488, at a gate on the Fetter Bend road.**

**And there is nothing on it that she could defend in a room, and that is why it is the first one.**

"Nine seasons," said Owen Rusk.

"Nineteen," said Warden Kade.

"You have returned one schedule in nineteen seasons and it came back with the same names on it four days later."

"**I have returned one and struck none, and those are not the same act, and the difference between them took me nineteen seasons and a sluice wheel, and I want to be exact about it in front of nine people, because everybody who has ever done this thinks the two are the same thing and they are opposites.**" She had the pen uncapped. "**Returning a schedule is a thing I do about a list. It says: I disagree with this list, here is my objection, send it back. And it comes back with the same names on it four days later, because the list was not mine and the office that made the list is not going to take a warden's word on a list of places, and the four days are the whole of what I got for it. Returning a list is a warden asking a building a question.**"

"And striking one."

"**Striking one is a warden saying: the list is not going out. Not: these places should not be on it. Not: take this one off. The list does not go out, and no place has been chosen, and no place has been removed, and there is no place on that piece of paper that I have said anything about, and if a person in Concord Hall wants to know why eleven settlements in Ravensmoor are not going to be reconciled this season then they may come down here and read the back of a sheet in a gate in the rain, and that is the whole of what they will get, and it is words.**"

---

**The woman of about thirty with a slate was at the rail on the Wednesday afternoon at about half past four and she had the season's tally on her round sheet, and she read it out once, in the ordinary voice, at a gate, to about nine people, and it is the first time in nineteen seasons that the Guard's arithmetic has been said out loud in a field, and it is a figure.**

"**The quota for the season is a hundred settlements in the county of the eleven districts. The Guard has closed eighty-nine of them reconciled or accounted. The season ends eleven short of the quota, and eleven of them are the eleven named on the sheet I have just struck, and neither figure is going into the same sentence.**" She put the slate under her arm. "**Ravensmoor was eleven and I have a mark against every one of them and I have been to all of them and the number is not a thing I did alone and the number is not a thing anybody is going to give me back by writing a word on the back of a sheet.**"

Then somebody at the back said, "**Ma'am.**"

"Yes."

"They're going to say you missed it."

"**They are going to say I missed it and they are going to be right, and I have not been relieved of the Guard and I am not going to be and nobody has reviewed the suspension I put in my own hand in a field on the ninth of the second month and I am going to be standing in exactly this state in June, and I would like about nine people at this gate to hear me say the next part.**"

"Go on."

"**I am not going to be thanked. A warden who has been thanked is a warden somebody has paid, and I have been paid twice this year and one of them was a strip of paper the size of a receipt from a building that cannot issue a reason, and the other one is a man in a corridor who is not going to be thanked either, and I do not know his name and I have not asked, and a thank you is a receipt and there is nothing to receipt.**"

Somebody said, "**Then why did you do it.**"

"**Because a warden who writes reasons in words has to be able to say what the words mean, and I have never been able to do that, and this morning I have written one anyway and there is nothing on it I could defend in a room.** And then she said the other half of it, and she said it flatly and to about nine people: "**And I swore in front of about thirty people behind a tannery on the twenty-ninth of the third month that I would not write a reason on the back of a count, and I did not, and I have not, and what is on the back of this is not a count. It is a list of places I have not been to. If somebody in this county is keeping a list of the things I have said, then the one I am keeping is: on a count, no; on a schedule, this morning, and I will not do it twice.**"

And then, because she was aware that nine people were going to say afterwards that they had heard something about courage: "**And I want the rest of it said, and it is the whole of the rest of it. It is four sentences and it is on the back of a cheap sheet, and in nineteen seasons I have never written less than a page. In about four years somebody will read that in a file and will decide it was a strong thing, and it is not strong, it is short. A short reason is the one that cannot be argued with, because there is nothing in it to argue with.**""

She put the sheet on the frame and wired it up herself, at the back of the board, with a loop of tarred cord, in about four minutes, and she did not ask anybody to hold the frame and she did not ask anybody to hand her the cord, and it is the only thing in nineteen seasons she has put on a public board that is hers.

**It took her about ten minutes to write the words and the ten minutes were the whole of the cost. She stood with her back to about nine people at a gate in the rain with a fountain pen and a sheet of cheap paper and she did not read it twice and the second time slower, which is what she does with everything, and Owen Rusk noticed that she did not, and has never asked her about it, and it is the only question he wanted to ask her this month and did not.**

A man of about fifty from a settlement in the district came up at about ten past ten and said that the Guard had done a great deal for his reach and that everybody up the road knew it, and that he was obliged.

"You are not," said Warden Kade.

"I am not, ma'am."

"**Then say nothing,**" she said, "**because a warden who has been thanked is a warden somebody has paid, and if you thank me on this morning then in about four years there is a file in Concord Hall with your settlement's name in it and mine beside it, and somebody will read the two names and work out that the two of us agreed about something, and what we agreed about is a wet piece of paper at a gate, and that is the whole of it, and it is not worth a man's morning.**"

He stood there for a bit and then took his hat off, which was not the same thing and which she did not correct.

**And the sheet at the Ravensmoor board is not a schedule of places anybody chose. That is the point of it and it is the reason it cost what it cost, and she said so at the gate, once, before she got on the cart: the eleven settlements on that sheet are not Slackwater and they are not Ossery and they are not Lower Lock and she has not been asked to say anything about any one of them, and if anybody in this county writes that a Warden struck eleven places then that person is telling it exactly backwards, and the sheet behind the reason says so in her own hand.**

---

**They came down the reach on the Wednesday afternoon and Owen Rusk did the tray at about a quarter to seven in the evening and it was on at ten past five, and it was empty, and it has been empty since the first of the month, and there is going to be a return made in that building for the season of 490 with a fifth column in it that can be completed, and he is the officer of the record of it, and it is not written yet.**

He wrote nothing down that night about the gate. He wrote the eleven lines back into the longhand book, in order, with the days, in his own hand, and under them he wrote two things and the two things are the only sentences in that book that are not a record of what somebody else said.

*The twenty-fourth day of the fourth month. Ravensmoor. She struck it and she did not argue first and she was not thanked and she was not relieved and she was not disgraced and she did not win.*

*And a county has now printed a column of reasons and every reason in it is a figure, and a Warden has written a reason in words and could not defend one word of it in a room, and both of those happened in the same week, and I do not know which of them I would rather had not.*
