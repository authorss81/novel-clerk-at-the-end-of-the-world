# World Bible

## 1. The Ordinary World Before the Crisis

Vespera is an industrial, river-fed civilization with roughly equivalent late-nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century technology: steam ferries, electric lighting in wealthy districts, mechanical pumps, printed forms, wax seals, pencil copying rooms, rail carts, and hand-cut sealing stones. It has no global electronic network. Communication moves by courier, semaphore, river post, and witnessed public notice.

Most people do not think every morning about the end of the world. They think about wages, flood damage, inheritance, school places, ferry schedules, missing relatives, and whether a neighborhood can prove that its market, clinic, or bridge still exists.

That normality is the point. Vespera is not a wasteland waiting for a hero. It is a crowded society whose ordinary infrastructure depends on continuous public acts such as:

- A road crew repairs a span and travelers use it.
- A midwife witnesses a birth and the family accepts the child.
- A market weighs flour, buyers accept it, and the stall renews its claim.
- A bereaved household names the dead without pretending the dead still make choices.
- A bridge ward maintains a crossing, and the city agrees to fund the next repair.

Vespera’s unusual law is not that belief creates reality. It is that sustained, witnessed commitment gives social fact unusual durability.

## 2. Calendar and Present Moment

The official calendar begins with the **Concord Year** after the **Blanking**, the disaster that destroyed the old central archive network and caused thousands of people and places to lose supported existence.

The novel begins in **Concord Year 487 (C.Y. 487)**. The provisional emergency government expires at the end of **C.Y. 500**, an event the Registry calls **Civic Zero**. At that moment:

1. The Provisional Mandate loses legal force.
2. Its shared continuation layer is withdrawn: acts that are not independently maintained lose their common recognition, while isolated local acts may continue for a time.
3. Existing facts begin failing faster than any local registry can repair them.
4. Vespera has no shared supported future. The world does not vanish in one blast; it fragments into locally supported islands whose ability to recognize and coordinate with one another collapses.

The distinction matters. A family can keep a meal, a clinic can keep a ward, and a bridge crew can keep a crossing if the living acts continue. What the emergency government supplied was the **common future**: the shared temporal authority that let separate communities recognize one another’s names, inherit obligations, trade across a river, and plan beyond the next local duty. Civic Zero withdraws that common layer, not every local act at once. Without a valid Common Return, isolated supports can endure, but they cannot reliably remain one Vespera.

Civic Zero is thirteen years away at the opening. The far deadline prevents nonstop panic while local closures, missing returns, and redacted wards create immediate cases. The countdown becomes politically active as the series proceeds; it is not repeatedly extended by arbitrary tricks.

## 3. How Physical Reality Is Stabilized

A fact does not become real because a clerk writes it down. A document can misrepresent, delay, or preserve. Reality strengthens a fact when the following are present:

1. **Identity:** the subject can be named with reasonable specificity.
2. **Act:** something has actually happened, been maintained, or been mutually promised.
3. **Witness:** living people with separable interests acknowledge what they personally observed or performed.
4. **Renewal:** the act is repeated, repaired, disputed, or otherwise kept socially active before its support expires.
5. **Scope:** the community understands which part of the claim is being maintained. A claim to “the north road” does not secretly guarantee every traveler’s safety.

Supported facts have stable edges. When support fails, the result is called **unfiling**:

- A room may remain physically intact while losing its recognized address.
- A person may become difficult to remember, absent from documents, and unable to sustain new reciprocal acts.
- A road can become unreachable even to people standing on it.
- A repeated obligation with no living performer may eventually become a **surrogate**, an imitation assembled from old records and compulsively repeats the abandoned duty.

Unfiling is not natural death. It is the failure of a social fact to remain supported. A person can be medically alive and already unfiling. A district can be geographically present and legally absent from the world.

## 4. Why the Blanking Happened

Before C.Y. 1, multiple river cantons kept local proof traditions. The central **Glasshall Archive** attempted to replace them with one comprehensive index, partly to prepare for war and partly to strengthen the central government. During the archive fire and fighting, the master roll was damaged. Local groups assumed that Glasshall had the only surviving proof and stopped maintaining their own claims.

Within nine days, bridges vanished from maps, workers could not be shown to have worked there, wards of the dead folded into undocumented ground, and hundreds of thousands of people became unfiling. The survivors could not reliably determine which version of events was valid. The disaster became the **Blanking**.

The new Provisional Mandate created the **Registry of Continuant Works** and made the Registry custodian of emergency filings. In exchange, every region had the right to maintain a local return and to appeal an erasure order. Those rights were real. They were also systematically narrowed over the following centuries.

## 5. The Common Return and Civic Zero

**Form Zero: The Common Return** was drafted in C.Y. 9. It is not a request for one ruler to remain in power. Its purpose is to end emergency rule and return authority to the people and communities who will be governed.

A valid completion uses a **bounded completeness procedure**, not an impossible universal census. The form's duty is to offer a return to every functioning community and person who can be reached or who chooses to enter the civic network; it may never treat an unreachable place or a silent person as consenting.

Before final activation, the central clerk must:

1. Publish the received branch list and a public challenge notice through Registry offices, ward meetings, river post, and recognized courier routes. The notice states that silence, absence, and an unreachable place are not consent.
2. Classify each community or person presented to the network as an authorized branch—a local return, personal or refusal return, protective return, or community refusal—or as an explicitly unresolved branch. A household return is a set of separately consenting adult branches, each with an individual withdrawal and departure right; no adult signs for another.
3. Receive confirmation from two independent local witness networks, each including at least one witness who did not carry the return, that no authorized branch inside the declared network has been knowingly omitted. This confirms the network's record; it does not prove the existence of every hidden person.
4. Record the boundary of the network, every unresolved branch, and a live appeal route that remains open after activation. An unresolved branch is never filled by a proxy. It remains outside the aggregate and keeps whatever local support it can maintain.

Protective coverage for children, disabled people, displaced people, and anyone unable to answer must be recorded as immediate care with a named custodian and appeal route. It is never a transfer of the person’s future consent, labor, identity, or vote.

At the final stroke, a valid Common Return can activate when every branch included in the aggregate has an independently authorized return, refusal, or protective status; every other received branch is explicitly unresolved and outside the aggregate; no proxy is used; and the challenge and appeal routes remain open. The central seal is a **ministerial actuator**, not an all-knowing judge: it checks the recorded shape of the filing—branch marks, witness confirmations, public notice, declared boundary, and the declared representative line with its supporting marks—then actuates the common future if the recorded conditions are met. It cannot prove that an absent person secretly consented, and it cannot certify universal completeness. A concealed proxy can pass a first structural check and create a temporary hold, but a supported counter-entry can expose and narrow it.

A hidden Free Counter that never reaches the network is a final cost: it may keep itself supported, be lost, or later file without being claimed by the old order. A branch outside the aggregate is not presumed to support the activated network, and its absence is part of the published boundary.

This bounded procedure preserves the no-proxy rule while acknowledging that the Registry cannot safely inspect every person or mobile community. It does not turn silence into a return, make a central clerk omniscient, or assign a speaker to a community that refuses the aggregate.

### Last-Mile Return

Vespera has no instant worldwide signal. The final filing travels through a human relay chain of regional clerks, couriers, semaphore stations, river post, and rail carts. A branch must be sealed, witnessed, and physically delivered to a regional receiving table before the ordinary final window closes three days before Civic Zero. After that, a branch can still arrive as an unresolved notice, but it cannot be silently folded into the final aggregate. Concord Hall’s receiving tables hold pre-positioned bundles; Owen’s Custodian role reconciles and transmits those bundles, not thoughts, remote consent, or unrestricted truth.

Form Zero may be assembled and reviewed before the deadline. It becomes the governing Common Return only at the last stroke of C.Y. 500, when the Provisional Mandate expires. The Mandate’s one-time transition clause can convert an accepted coercive emergency filing into a **30-day succession hold**: it can keep central roads, hospitals, waterworks, and archive shells at their last supported status while officials repeat emergency acts. The hold is a bridge, not a new mandate. A succession hold has a separate ledger status and cannot be promoted to a final return merely by adding Owen’s transmission mark; it would need a new filing with independently authorized branches. The hold does not create the common future, cannot answer for people, and cannot remain the final authority. Lound’s plan is built on the claim that a centralized succession can turn this finite hold into a permanent central return; the original Form Zero rejects that claim.

When Civic Zero arrives without a valid Common Return, Vespera does not transfer to another realm. Its common future ends, and the remaining local supports become isolated, contested, and increasingly unable to renew one another.

## 6. Geography

### The River Delta

The opening region. Docketford, a river city of roughly 80,000 people, is built around locks, warehouses, ferry slips, tanneries, printworks, and flood-prone neighborhoods. The Registry’s River Desk handles mundane changes of name, inheritance, land use, apprenticeships, and local continuance. Docketford is proud, politically split, and vulnerable because several lower wards are built on marginal land with weak historical rolls.

Major locations include:

- **Registry River Desk:** Owen’s workplace, a long copying hall with numbered desks and a central seal stair.
- **Lower Lock:** Ada Rusk’s bridge ward, including informal homes, repair sheds, and residents missing from recent rolls.
- **Glass Stair:** the Registry’s public appeal hall and entrance to the surviving Docketford docket rooms. It is not the destroyed Glasshall Archive.
- **Arbour Kitchen:** Nell Arbour’s claimant kitchen and neighborhood meeting place.
- **The Ninth Weir:** a flood-damaged crossing whose condition becomes Owen’s first open case.

### The Upland Commons

Farm terraces, cooperative granaries, and towns that use rotating household proof rather than one permanent seal. Their rolls are socially demanding: every household keeps visible duties to common stores, roads, schools, and neighbors. A dishonest member can damage an entire district’s standing, so the Upland Commons is both cooperative and intensely suspicious of outsiders.

### The Salt Verge

Dry former archive provinces where roads and settlements were removed during earlier redaction campaigns. Stable “holes” in travel geography lie between them. Some erased communities left physical structures, contradictory histories, and records that support no surviving claimant. Salvagers, family hunters, and blank-road guides live there.

### The Claim Market

A river-and-rail commercial zone where legal sponsorship, witness fees, inheritance continuances, and emergency custody can be bought. The market is not a magical shadow realm. It is banks, brokers, corrupt notaries, court houses, warehouses, and shipping contracts that exploit the fact that some unclaimed people cannot afford the time required to maintain a claim.

### The Free Counters

Mobile and lightly settled regions that reject central rolls. They use route logs, meal debts, skill tallies, temporary household marks, and witnessed declarations that can be retired. A place may vanish from their map without its inhabitants ceasing to exist. Conversely, the Registry cannot safely summon a Free Counter community because it does not know who may speak for it.

### The Provisional Seat

The old administrative heart called **Concord Hall**, a terraced city of hearings, seals, banks, and elite residences. Chancellor Verity Lound governs from here. Beneath its lower terraces, the **First Seal Subvault** preserves a small cache assembled in C.Y. 9: the first surviving seal of Form Zero, its witness schedules, and selected pre-Blanking source fragments. It is distinct from the destroyed Glasshall Archive and contains no private override. The Hall’s polished procedures conceal the provisional government’s dependence on old emergency powers.

## 7. Major Institutions and Factions

### The Registry of Continuant Works

A mixed institution: archivists, clerks, field examiners, bridge recorders, censors, judges, and ward stewards. Many employees do necessary work and sincerely protect people. Its leadership has also benefited from emergency rule and controls the official map of valid communities.

### The Local Wardens

Neighborhood institutions that maintain rolls, shelters, disputes, and small infrastructure. They are neither sovereigns nor automatic heroes. Some are competent and generous; others exclude migrants or hoard witness capacity.

### The Upland Commons

A federation of reciprocal settlements that want recognition without central control. They offer a working model for Form Zero but impose demanding communal duties and struggle with internal dissent.

### The Free Counters

A loose network of mobile households, caravans, guides, and temporary camps. They are the chief obstacle to any clean “representative list” because their communities can be created and dissolved deliberately. Many distrust the Registry because central maps have preserved false versions of their routes.

### The Census Guard

A field service under the Provisional Mandate that checks distant returns and removes settlements whose facts cannot be reconciled. Its members believe deletion is kinder than indefinite instability. They are disciplined, dangerous, and sometimes the only people preventing a delayed collapse.

### The Claim Brokers

Private intermediaries who sell sponsorship, urgent filings, and emergency custody. They turn a human right into a balance that can be transferred. Some brokers exploit poor people; others are constrained by legal gaps and attempt genuine rescues.

### The Provisional Council

The central government under Chancellor Verity Lound. It claims the Council exists only until Civic Zero and that the Assumption Plan is the last humane way to prevent a world-ending gap. It has governed so long that temporary authority, private privilege, and genuine protection are difficult to separate.

## 8. Technology, Objects, and Visual Texture

Common tools include:

- Pencils that are sharpened but never sharpened during court hours.
- Numbered carbon copies with pressure-sensitive colored seals.
- Mechanical counters that show which community has voted on a motion.
- Clay “edge stones” laid beside disputed roads so the route can be inspected in daylight.
- Personal proof rings, carved bread boards, boat tallies, family knots, and shared meal bowls that renew claims through use.
- Ward lanterns joined by copper wire; each lamp can hear a witnessed declaration nearby, but cannot carry it farther than the wire.
- Archive drawers sealed with braided black cord, the color used for records under a redaction dispute.

The world should feel worn, wet, busy, and specific. Offices smell of ink, damp wool, lamp oil, river mud, and hot metal seals. Homes are not ruined by default. The apocalypse appears first in administrative and social details: a neighbor absent from the roll, a shop unable to open, a bridge that only outsiders can see.

## 9. What Society Gains and Loses

Vespera’s system produces real benefits:

- Adoption and emancipation do not depend entirely on biological parentage.
- Abandoned infrastructure can retain a public claim while crews repair it.
- Testimony and dispute can outlast a corrupt official.
- Shared labor can preserve a road, clinic, or school that no private owner could afford.
- A community can recognize a newcomer without pretending to own their past.

It also produces concrete abuses:

- Wealthy districts can buy faster and stronger proof.
- Literal interpretation can exclude people whose identities do not fit forms.
- Bureaucrats can delay a status change until someone dies or leaves.
- Claim brokers can bundle a person’s legal presence with debt.
- Central government can call erasure “stabilization” when the truth is political.
- A frightened person can choose a coercive custodian rather than become unfiling.

These are not side effects to be removed by a kinder clerk. They are the reason authority must be shared and reviewable after the final settlement.

## 10. Revelation Ladder

- **Volume 1:** Local unfiling is real; Form Zero exists; the Registry knows more than it says.
- **Volumes 2–3:** The Blanking, local returns, and the intended refusal rights become public.
- **Volumes 4–5:** Redaction often delays collapse, and coercive proof can be sold or transferred.
- **Volume 6:** Independent filing traditions outside the Registry are valid and numerous.
- **Volume 7:** The distributed design of Form Zero, the forged proxy condition, and Owen’s jointly sponsored childhood are revealed.
- **Volumes 8–9:** The real conflict is between voluntary returns and an emergency Assumption.
- **Volumes 10–12:** The Assumption mechanism, cached absences, and the final collective filing are exposed and confronted.

The mystery should be answered before the final climax. The ending tests the answer under pressure rather than inventing a new cause for the apocalypse.

## 11. Canon Guardrails

- The world is not a simulation and has no players or game designer.
- Vespera is a real world with a real history, not a bureaucratic afterlife.
- The Blanking was a human administrative failure amplified by an existing law of witnessed fact, not an alien invasion.
- No natural cycle automatically resets time or offers a second chance.
- No technology can scan all people or transmit unrestricted truth.
- Local proof is weaker than a central synchronized record but cannot simply be ignored.
- The Registry’s methods are often useful; the ending reforms power rather than abolishing recordkeeping.
