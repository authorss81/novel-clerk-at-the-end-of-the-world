# Chapter 94 — Fourteen Days, and a Wheel Twice a Day

The emergency suspension of fourteen days is the law and it is humane and it is the only thing in this world that will stand in front of a person who cannot answer for themselves, and it was entered on the Wednesday morning of the tenth day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488 at about eleven o'clock at the tank house at Slackwater, by the Warden of the Census Guard, alone, without a case and without a slate, reading every word out loud twice and the second time slower.

Six persons. Four of them cannot be asked. Two of them can be, and asked, and are in it because they asked to be.

**SUSPENSION BY A WARDEN OF THE COUNT. Emergency, fourteen days. Place: Slackwater. Date of entry: the tenth day of the fourth month. Six persons. For each person, a date and a care act. Terms: the care act is performed by the named persons for the fourteen days and the person is under an emergency suspension and may not be asked a question about their premises for the term. At the end of the term the matter is reported to the Provincial Counting Office and a count correction may be made. At thirty days from the date of entry no supported living restoration of the person is possible. Signed: Oress Kade, Warden.**

"**The thirty is on the form,**" she said, before anybody had asked her anything. "**It is in the middle of the form and it is the second of the two sentences that matter and there is a rule that says no part of this instrument may be left out, and the thirty is the part they would leave out if they could, and I am going to read it four times a season in this county and I have read it in about nine places and I have never once read it to a room that could not answer me.**"

The woman of eighty-four is the first name in the list and the care act against her is *carried to and from the tank on the Tuesday and the Friday, and at seven and at six, by J. Harker and by P. Naylor, and the water is brought to the door in a two-gallon can on the days she does not go out.*

"She has not asked for this."

"**No, and the form does not have a box for asking, and there are two persons on this list who did ask and the form has no box for that either, and I have written it in the margin of my own copy in my own hand and a margin is not a column, and I would like about eleven people in this room to know that I have done that and that it is the fourth time this season and that I have no intention of doing it a fifth.**"

---

**And Owen Rusk tried to enter a hold on the Wednesday morning of the tenth day at about four in the afternoon and could not, and finding out that he could not is what the rest of the afternoon was.**

The form is a sheet of printed paper about the size of a receipt. It asks for a claimant. It asks for a place of record. It asks for a date of entry and a term and a care act and it asks for the name and address of the person the claim is made for, and a hold is an answerability, and an answerability has somebody at the end of it, and Slackwater has nine premises of record as of yesterday and nine doors, and the nine are not anybody in particular, and there is no name at the end of a hold that would mean anything to anybody in this county.

He tried it four times. He got as far as the third column and could not get past the fact that he could not name a person and address at Slackwater in any form that he could send up a stair.

"A hold is a claim and not a duty," he said, out loud, in a yard, to nobody, on the Wednesday of the tenth day of the fourth month, and a woman of about thirty with a slate, who was in the yard because she is the clerk of the Census Guard and she was keeping the sheet she keeps, said:

"**Then it is not a hold.**

"**A hold is a duty that somebody has agreed to answer for, and you have three of them and I have seen the card and you have told me the number yourself twice, and there is no fourth, and the card is the answer to the question and the answer has not changed. What you have just said out loud is a claim, and a claim is not a duty, and a claim is not on the card, and a claim has no term and no renewal and no interval and nobody can transfer it and nobody can take it off you, and there is no instrument in this county for a claim.**" She put the slate under her arm. "**So you have claimed it, out loud, in a yard, in front of about nine people, and I have heard you, and I am a clerk of the Census Guard and what I have heard goes in the case and is not a finding. Write it down somewhere. And do not expect anybody to be told.**"

"I do not expect anybody to be told," said Owen Rusk. "**That is the part I have worked out about three days ago and it took me nine years to get to it. There is no form on which a person can ask me whether I claimed it on purpose or whether I was frightened, and I worked that out alone in a corridor in Concord Hall in the second month of this year and wrote it down, and I am not going to be a man who discovers it twice.**"

---

**The wheel would not turn a full turn, and the frame is split, and there is nobody in this county whose duty it is, and the ward at the bottom of Fetter Lane carts four hundred gallons of silt back onto the flats once a week in a cart that belongs to a crew of nine whose wage is four months and a bit in arrears and may not be migrated to the district office.**

They took the frame apart on the Wednesday afternoon and put it back together with four timbers off the cart house and two wedges cut out of the pump house floor, and about eleven people did it in about four hours, and the water came up the beck while they did it and stopped about a foot below the stone lip of the tank, which is where it is supposed to be, and stayed there, and is still there.

Then somebody said the thing that everybody at Slackwater had been not-saying since the eighth of October, which is that a hand-worked sluice is not a sluice for a fortnight.

A fortnight is fourteen days.

"It is exactly fourteen days," said Jos Harker. "**The day the suspension runs out is the day I have to have either a new screw or somebody who can cut one, and there is no new screw and there is nobody in eleven miles who can cut one before the frost, and you have all worked that out, and I want to say it out loud so that it is in somebody's mouth besides mine, and it is in yours now.**"

---

**The man with the cart came up the track on the Thursday morning of the eleventh day of the fourth month, unasked, with his own cart and his own gear, and about four miles of track, and he brought a block and tackle that came off a kiln, and he did not come up the track for a week of water and he did not say that he had.**

He is a man of about sixty-one. He is not named on the page and he did not give his name and Owen Rusk did not ask him for it, and Owen Rusk has known since a Monday in the third month that this man holds him to an exact promise, and the promise stands and has not been renegotiated and has not been broken, and it is three things and the third one is the one that is going to cost.

The three things are these. Not in a field. Not in a hall. Not to the woman who maintains a board. **And if the Warden asks him in a room with a day named, tell her the true thing; and no promise to lie to her and no promise to protect a cart.**

He worked the block and tackle for two days and then went back down his own track with his own cart and his own gear, and before he went he said one thing to Owen Rusk on the Friday, and Owen Rusk wrote down what it was and the day and the hour, and Owen Rusk wrote them in a longhand book on a shelf in a room he has the key to, in a room with no window and a bulb on a flex taped twice.

He said: **A cart is the only reason twelve houses have water, and it is not in a column, and it cannot be, and if it ever were somebody would strike it.**

---

**On the Wednesday night of the tenth day there were about fourteen people in a channel two miles down the hill at Fetter Lane and about four hundred gallons of silt came up in about eleven minutes, and the ward carted it back onto the flats, and the crew is nine and their wage is four months and a bit in arrears and is the crew's wage and may not be migrated to the district office, and it is the single most expensive thing anybody in that ward has ever done and nobody has ever costed it.**

At about nine that night Owen Rusk walked up past the pump house and up the lane to the top of the track because somebody in that yard had told him there was a light on at the tank and there was, and about nine people were up at nine o'clock at night with a wheel, because the wheel is worked twice a day and the second time of the day is six in the evening and it had been nine.

He stood at the gate for about a minute and a half and did not go in, and the reason he did not go in is not a good one and he has never been able to improve it: **it is not his wheel, and on the tenth day of the fourth month he had worked out that a claim is not a duty, and the difference between the two, in a yard, at nine o'clock at night, is whether you go in and take hold of it.**

"There is a thing you should know," said Prud Naylor, from the top of the steps of the tank house, to nobody in particular, when she saw him at the gate.

"Yes."

"**The woman at the end house was carried to the tank at seven and put back at about ten past seven and she asked the man carrying her where he had been working and he said on the wheel, and she said you will be tired, and that is the whole of what happened, and I am telling you because in about four years somebody is going to ask whether the care acts were performed and I would like there to be a person in this county who has heard it from somebody who was standing at the top of the steps.**"

"I will write it down."

"**Write down that she asked. That is what is worth writing down. Not the carrying. Anybody can carry somebody.**"

He wrote it down in the longhand book with the day and the hour and the words as they were given, which is the only way he knows to write anything, and he wrote that she asked the man carrying her where he had been working, and that she said you will be tired, and that the man had answered that he had been on the wheel.

Then he went down the track and slept for four hours on two sacks and a coat in the cart house at Slackwater, and the granary at the bottom of Fetter Lane is open and empty and has been since the seventh day of the second month, and the figure is nothing for the ninth week running, and there is not going to be anything in it on Thursday either, and the trays are not stopped.

Somebody turned the wheel eleven times before he went and the sound of it went out over the yard and stopped, and that was the second turn of the day, and there has been a second turn of the day at that tank at six in the evening every evening for nineteen years, and on the tenth day of the fourth month it was nine, and it was the fourteenth time in three days that it had been late. **The fortnight's list on the inside of the door of the tank house has a day against each household and has never once had a time against it, and there is no column anywhere in this county for the hour at which a household got to its own water, and there is not going to be one, and the fourteen days run out on a day and not at an hour, and there is nobody at that tank who will be told which day.**

---

**On the Thursday of the eleventh day the pump at Fetter Lane was primed at four in the morning by Bram Tillet and the household in the two rooms in Fetter Lane, eleven minutes, and the first stroke brought up the water that was standing in the pipe and it was brown, and the marker went up on the flats at seven forty with the depth of water in chalk, and Ruth Mapp read the roster out at ten past six in the ward hall to about nine people, and the crossing at the bottom of the lane was shut to loads and to vehicles above four tons and the barrier has an addition in Marguerite Bray's hand on it, and the repair is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and is unfunded and the notice at the weir path is in the weather and has brought no district and is going to be there in June.**

Owen Rusk was not at any of it. He was on the top of a track four miles off with about nine people and a wheel and a bag of oak wedges, and he slept on the Wednesday night in the cart house at Slackwater on two sacks and a coat and was up at five, and what he did that day was carry wedges and hold a frame and write down the number of turns it took to shut the wheel, which was eleven, and which is the same eleven that is the number of premises of record, and he noticed that on the Friday and said nothing about it, and it is in the book with the day and the hour.

**And the eleven was the number of turns and it was the number of premises and it was not the same eleven and he wrote the two of them on the same line of a book with a rule between them, and the rule is his, and he is the only person in this county who knows that both of those numbers are eleven, and about four years from now somebody is going to want to know why.**

---

**And on the Friday morning of the twelfth day at about ten, at the count room at the Glass Stair, Warden Kade was doing two jobs at the same time for the first time in nineteen seasons, and both of them are her job, and she did them about nine minutes apart.**

The first was the attestation of a count of drawings for the season, in Docketford district and in nine other districts, at a rail, with a clerk reading and herself saying the figures, and it took about two hours and it is the same act as the certification she did in a field on the twenty-eighth of the third month and she has said out loud that it is the same act and that anybody who says otherwise has not watched it twice.

The second was a count correction for a settlement in a district three hundred miles up the river, and the reason in the box was a figure, and she attested it in about a minute, and she did not go to the place, and there is nobody going to ask her about that either.

The counting clerk at the second counter watched the first for about forty minutes and the second for about one and has thought about the two in a different way since than she thought about them before, and has not said so to anybody, and will not.

Kade said four things to Owen Rusk at the rail on the Friday, in about nine minutes, in front of about six people, and none of them was about the sluice.

"**The suspension is entered and it is the only instrument in this county that stands in front of a person who cannot answer, and I entered it myself, which means that on the twenty-fourth day of this month I report it to a counting office and a correction may be made, and I have known since the Wednesday morning that I put my own hand to a clock, and I would like somebody else to be the person who understands that, and you are the only one in this room who understands arithmetic and I would like you to understand it on purpose.**"

"That is fair."

"**It is not fair, it is the job, and I have done it nine times in nineteen seasons and I have never once done it in a place that is not on a schedule, and this one is not on a schedule, and the reason there is no schedule of settlements to be reconciled for Slackwater is that Slackwater was struck out of a book in the Concord Year 471, and the strike is a decision somebody took, and I cannot appeal a decision, I can only return a schedule, and there is no schedule to return.**"

"You could enter a suspension of the count."

"**I could enter a suspension of the count on the four ruled lines, and there are four ruled lines printed under a survey round on a notice in a field behind a tannery and they are empty, and they have been empty since the twenty-sixth of the second month, and I have not filled them in and I have not asked anybody to, and if I fill them in now then what I have written on them will be a reason that is not a figure and the office will suspend the count in this district and the suspension will expire on a day named on a form and I shall be a warden who put a place in a field in a document because of a sluice.**" She shut her folder. "**I will not do that. And you are going to go back up that track on Monday and you are going to want to.**"

"Yes."

"**Then here is what I have for you and it is not an instrument and I am not giving it to you as one. That frame at the west gate is a frame. There are six things on it and the sixth is at the bottom because a woman will not put a sheet over a sheet, and every one of the six is a piece of paper in a building about another piece of paper, and not one of the six is a person, and the seventh thing is going to be a person and it is going to be a column of figures, and it is going to be a column because that is what a person can read, and I have been doing this nineteen seasons and I have never once in my life written a word on a piece of paper that I could not say out loud in a room, and that is the whole of what I have, and it is not enough, and I am telling you that it is not enough because you are going to find out.**"
