# Chapter 97 — What It Cost, and Who Paid It

The long room at the Glass Stair holds about nine people and has four high windows and the two observers of a return are separated in writing at each renewal, and a separation made once does not carry forward, and the renewal of a local return is at an interval and not before.

Owen Rusk had asked for it, on the Friday morning of the nineteenth day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488, and he had asked for it in a room with a rail round three sides of it and about eleven people in it, and what he asked for was the first renewal of the return of the settlement of Slackwater with its two observers separated in writing, and it is not the ward's return and no renewal of the ward's return has been heard of in this county for four months.

The separation was taken at about ten in the morning at a rail in a room with high windows and about eleven people in it, and it is a formality, and it is the only formality anybody in that room does not need explained, and the reason it is taken at all is that a person cannot be a witness and a party and there is no column on any form in this county for the difference between a person who is asked about their own interest and a person who is not.

**And then, at about half past ten, in a room with high windows, the thing that happened was that people said what it had cost, in order, because the room is where things get written down and nobody in it had any idea at all that anybody was going to ask them.**

The form asks the observer to state their interest. Each of them did. And then each of them said a second thing, and nobody asked for the second thing, and the clerk at the counter wrote all of it down because that is what a clerk does, and it is in the file, and it is in the guard's case, and the two are separate files and the two are about two different subjects.

---

**The first was the counting clerk of the Census Guard, the woman of about thirty with a slate, and hers is the one that goes in the case and is not a finding and is not a document.**

"**I entered six persons under a suspension of fourteen days at a place that is not on a schedule of settlements to be reconciled, on the tenth day of this month, on a letter that six households at that place wrote, and I did not go to that place before I entered them and I had walked past the gate on the Sunday and I had been told the date the sluice went and I wrote it in my round sheet, and both of those are the same weekend, and if anybody ever asks me which of the two I did first I am going to have to say the round sheet, and I would like it in a file somewhere that the answer to that question is the round sheet and not the letter, and I am a clerk of the Census Guard and I am not a person anybody is going to take a view about and I would like it left that way.**"

She put the slate under her arm. "**And nobody is to write the second thing down anywhere. I said it at a rail and the rail is why there is a rail.**"

And nobody wrote it down, and it is not written down in a longhand book on a shelf in a room with a key on a board, and the clerk at the counter wrote it down in a fair copy of a form because that is the clerk's duty, and the fair copy is in the file, and the original is in the file, and the file is the guard's, and the guard is closing.

---

**The second was Cobb Rennie, and he came up the hill on his own two feet because nobody in that room could pay for the coach, and he is fifty-eight and he reads prices, and he is the eleventh door on a schedule attached to a certification and is not counted.**

"I have been owed a pension for two years and one month," he said, "**and I did not say that for the pension. I said that because I have been sitting in this room for half an hour and everybody in it has been talking about what things cost, and I have got four things to say about the cost and none of them is money and I am going to say them in the order they happened.**

"**The first is the ward. Nine crew and four months and a bit in arrears and it is the crew's wage and it is not the district's wage and nobody in that building can move it and there is a form about it and the form is correct.**"

"**The second is the crossing. Four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and it is for timber and piles and hands and it belongs to that crossing alone and there is a notice nailed to a post in the weather that says any district able to perform the repair is invited to perform it, and it has been in the weather since the flood and it has brought no district and it is going to be there in June, and the reason it is going to be there in June is that the day the notice comes down is the day this ward has stopped asking.**"

"**The third is the granary. It has been open and empty since the seventh day of the second month and the figure is nothing for the tenth week running and Marguerite Bray has written the day and the hour against it in her own hand every single week, and nobody is going to buy anything, and the decision not to buy was made on the first of the second month by Ada Rusk and it was correct and it is still correct and it will be correct in June.**"

"**And the fourth is me, and I will say it once and then it is finished, and nobody in this room thanks me for it, and I have said that three times this year and a fourth would be a habit.**" He put his thumb on the rail. "**What it cost me this year was a week of standing in a field in the cold reading a board out loud. It did not pay me and I am not going to pretend it did and it is more than I had on the Friday, and that is all it is, and it is worth less than money and more than nothing, and both of those are the same size.**"

Then he said the thing he has been saying in fields since the seventh of the third month, and he gave it away for the last time.

"**A count is right about a figure and wrong about a place and there is not one instrument in this county that will ever make those two things agree, and the eleven reasons on the board that goes up on Friday are going to be eleven figures, and a figure is the only thing this county has ever printed about a person, and I have been on a list since the seventh day of the third month and I read my own name off a board behind a tannery, and I am not on any other list in this county and I am not on this one either, and both of those are correct and one of them I would like back.**"

---

**The third was the man with the cart, and he was not in the room, because he is not a party, and he was in the yard behind the building with the book with the bootlace in the binding, and he is a man of about sixty-one and has driven water up four miles of unmade track twice a week for thirty-one years and has never been named in this county and Owen Rusk has not asked him and is not going to.**

He had come up the reach on the Friday morning because the tent was on the reach and the Warden was at the rail, and he had not come into the building.

"He is out there," said Owen Rusk.

"**Then I will go out,**" said the man with the cart, "**because she asked me for it in a room with a day named, in the spring, and I gave her the exact answer and I have carried it up and down four miles of track every day since, and it has not gone off, and I would like to be in this county when it is asked for.**"

---

**And she asked him for it, in a room, with a day named, on the Friday morning of the nineteenth day of the fourth month, at about ten past eleven, and there were about eleven people in the room and it was not a question she had prepared.**

"You drove water up to Ossery twice a week in the season of 489," said Warden Kade. "**The round was stopped. The survey was suspended. Nobody came to your gate from an office. And the ground Ossery sits on is under a tank because of it, and I have not been into Ossery this season and I have not read anybody's return from Ossery this season, and I am not going to pretend to you that I do not know what you are.**

"**So here is my question and it has a day on it: was your cart a current local act of the district in the season of 489.**"

"Yes," said the man with the cart.

"**Recorded, and I have written the word yes and the day, and I am not going to ask you why, because why is the word I have written nine reasons in this year and every one of them was a figure, and you are a person and there is no box for you.**"

"**There is a box for me,**" said the man with the cart, "**it is on the fourth column of the fifth page and it is headed what was done about it, and I saw it in a room in Concord Hall in 474 with about nine other men in it, and it is empty on every line, and there are three hundred and seventy lines, and I do not know that because anybody has ever said so and I have not been asked anything in thirty-one years.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**I am going to tell you the true thing, because you asked me for it in a room with a day named and I gave you the promise before I knew what it was going to be for, and here it is and it is about four sentences and I would like them in your own hand and not a clerk's.**" He did not raise his voice. "**My cart is the only reason twelve houses at Ossery have water. The book on the seat of the cart is the reason the figure goes up to a district in Concord Hall every season. The office sends the figure back every season and asks the kiln whether it is right, and the kiln says yes, and the figure comes up again, and the number in it is the number of barrels I took up that track and no more. And there are eleven doors on the sheet and fourteen roofs and four of the roofs are kilns and the kilns are works, and a work is in the count and a house is not, and the number that is in the count is the number of barrels and it is a correct number and I put it there.**"

"That is the truest thing anybody has said in this room this year," said Owen Rusk.

"**No it is not,**" said the man with the cart. "**It is the only thing anybody has said in this room this year, and the two of those are not the same, and a man who cannot tell the difference is a man who is about to be pleased with himself. Ask the woman on the flats what her figure cost her. It cost nothing. It is a number about a tank and she wrote it in chalk.**"

---

**And the fourth was Junia Pell, and she is twenty-two years old and has been in this building three months and a fortnight, and was put on the roll of district-exclusion at twenty-one because she was the only person free on a Tuesday, and she is the fourth name on it and it has had no vacancy since the spring, and she is owed nothing, and she was in that room for fifty minutes and said one thing at the rail in about nine seconds and it was not asked for.**

"**I want to say the thing I have said four times this year and I have said it in a corridor and at a counter and I am going to say it in a room with high windows in front of eleven people, and it is that I am the reason a document about a person is not in this building, and I am not a person, and there is no column anywhere for me, and if I were a settlement I would have nine premises of record and a reason against me and a place on a board, and I have none of those, and I have never asked for any of them, and that is not modesty and it is not modesty at all. I have a file with two names on it and one of them is mine and it is on a roll, and that is a figure, and a figure is the only thing this county has ever printed about a person.**"

"**You will not be thanked for that,**" said Owen Rusk.

"**Say that again,**" said Junia Pell, "**and I will deny that anybody said it, and I have told you four times this year not to thank me and you have done it four times and each time you have meant it and each time it has been a small insult, and I would like the fifth thing anybody says in this room to be a true one.**"

Somebody laughed, which had not happened in that room in a month, and the woman of about thirty with a slate wrote nothing down about it and neither did anybody else.

And the fifth was Ada Rusk, and she came up the hill on the Friday morning with a delivery book under her arm and about four other people, and she did not speak in the room with the high windows, because she is not a party and she has not been asked and she was not going to be, and she said so before anybody could have wondered.

She said it in the yard instead, at about half past twelve, standing with her back to a wall, to about nine people, and it is the first time in about a year that she has said any part of the second of her four terms to a person who did not come from Fetter Lane, and she said it once, and she is not going to say it a fourteenth time in a hall and she was asked by nobody to say it at all.

"**The figure that goes in the reason box goes on the public board with this district's name against it, whatever it is, and before we see it.**" She shut the book. "**That is the second of four. It has not been performed. It is not withdrawn. It is going on a board on Friday as a column of eleven reasons and none of them is ours and I am not going to stand here and say it is the fourteenth time because I have said that in a hall and I do not say a thing fourteen times so that it stops being true.**"

"Will you be at the frame."

"**I will be at the frame and I will be at the back of it, and there is a difference and it is the whole of what has happened to me this year, and my brother is at the back of everything and nobody asks him for anything and that is his work and not mine, and I have not said one word to him about any of this and neither of those is for him to hear from me.**"

Cobb Rennie said, "**You are going to read the reasons out.**"

"**I am going to read the reasons out, and every one of them is a number, and I have said the sentence about that twice this year and a third time is the way a thing stops being true, so I will not say it. If the eleven on that board are eleven figures then a county has printed why, and I do not care whose it is, and I will not stand in a yard pretending otherwise.**"

And the sixth voice was the clerk at the second counter, who is not a person in this story and said nothing, and who has been at that counter nine years, and who wrote all six of these things down in a fair copy because that is the clerk's duty and the fair copy is in the file, and who has never been thanked and has not asked to be, and who did not say so in the room.

**And the seventh voice, in the yard, at about twenty past one on the Friday afternoon, was a woman of sixty-one who had come down the track on the morning bus with a tin and had not gone into the building, and she was asked whether she wanted to come in and she said no, and this is what she said, and Owen Rusk wrote it down afterwards in the longhand book with the day and the hour, and she asked him to write it down, which is the first time anybody at Slackwater has ever asked him for anything.**

"**You are all in there saying what it cost. It cost us a sluice wheel, about nine backs, and a woman of thirty-four who could not read the small print. That is the cost. It is small and you may all have it. I would like to say the other one — I have given that one twice in a room at the tank already and a third time is how a thing stops being true, so I will say this instead, and it is a fact and not a sentence, and it is the only fact I have about that building: it has not stopped. It has got a name on it. There is a board going up on Friday with a column of reasons on it and every reason in it is a number, and that is the first time in this county that anybody can check. I have been at that tank nineteen years and nobody out of a building has ever asked me a question, and I have told that man so eleven times this week and he wrote it down every time, and it has not stopped.**"

---

**On the Sunday evening of the twenty-first day of the fourth month Owen Rusk cut his right middle finger open again on an oak wedge in a cart house at Slackwater, and it was a small thing and it took nine minutes and it did not need anybody's attention, and it is the cheapest thing anybody paid for in this month of the Concord Year 488, and he is not going to have it put beside the other things.**

He had been in the county nine days and he had not been in his own building once, and there was a tray in the district office two miles down the hill and it had been empty on the Sunday morning of the seventh day of the month and there is going to be a return made in that building for the season of 490 with a fifth column in it that can be completed, and he is the officer of the record of it, and it is not written yet, and he has not begun it, and there is no form on which anybody is asked whether a district can do a thing.

There was a form in this county for the thing he had actually done for nine days and it is a sheet of printed paper about the size of a receipt that he never got past the third column of, and what he had instead of it is a claim with no term and no renewal and no interval, and nobody can transfer it and nobody can take it off him and nobody will ever ask him whether he had it on purpose or whether he was frightened.

**He wrote nothing about the nine days in the longhand book. He wrote the number of turns on the wheel and the day and the hour and the fact that a woman of eighty-four asked a man where he had been working, and he wrote the second of Ada Rusk's terms in a room with high windows because somebody in a yard asked him to and that was a request, and he wrote the reason the man with the cart gave in about four sentences because the man with the cart asked him to and there were no witnesses and it was the only one of the seven in that building that anybody had asked him for.**

He did not write that he had slept on two sacks in a cart house for five nights, and there was nobody in this county he could have written it to, and that is the thing he has never been able to put in a form and has never once been able to say out loud in a room, and he has been in this building nine years and it is the fourth time in a year that the whole difficulty has been the absence of a place to write a true sentence.

**The suspension of fourteen days ran out on the twenty-fourth day of the fourth month, and the thirty days from the tenth day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488 falls in the second week of the fifth month, and at thirty days no supported living restoration of any of the six persons is possible, and that is on the form, and the form is correct, and nobody at Slackwater knows the day it falls and three of the six cannot be told.**
