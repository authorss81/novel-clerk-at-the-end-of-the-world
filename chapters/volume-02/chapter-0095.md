# Chapter 95 — Eleven and Eleven

The question was put at the tank house at Slackwater at about eight in the evening on the Saturday of the thirteenth day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488, and the chapel was locked on the Sunday morning at about ten, and nobody put anything in a column about the closing of it, because a chapel is not premises of record and the district's book has never carried it and a book that has never carried a thing cannot have a thing struck out of it.

There were about thirty-one people in a room eight feet by eleven. Nine doors are on the district's book against this place and none of the nine is anybody in that room and nobody in that room has ever been able to find out which nine.

Warden Kade came down the track on the Saturday afternoon and did not have a case and did not have a slate and stood at the back with her arms folded for about forty minutes while the twenty-two households argued for an hour and eleven minutes, and then she said one sentence, and the sentence is the reason the room got anywhere, and she has never claimed it.

"**You are all going to want to know what the form says and I am going to tell you now so that nobody in this room spends tonight on it. A return is a set of branches. One adult to a branch. A branch is a person answering about their own premises. No adult signs for another adult. Nobody signs for a person who cannot answer, and if you do that it is not a return, it is a lie with a seal on it, and I have seen four of those in nineteen seasons and two of them were made by good people.**"

"And who do we sign to," said Tobin Ragg.

"Nobody. You sign it yourself. One sheet per person. And there is a right on every sheet to take it back, and taking it back takes the sheet out and does not take the return out, and I have never once in nineteen seasons seen that right printed on a sheet in advance and I am going to have it printed on yours in advance if the office will do it, and the office will do it, because it is the form."

---

**Owen Rusk did not bring the ward's paper.**

There is a paper in Fetter Lane of eight things a ward will not accept and three things it will, written in a bridge mechanic's capitals and read out in a hall of about thirty, and the second of the ward's four terms is on it, and it is the best of them, and it says that the figure which goes in the reason box goes on a public board with the district's name against it, whatever it is, and before the district sees it.

He had it in his coat for about two days. He took it out on the Friday evening at the top of the hill and looked at it in the lamplight and put it back.

The reasons are not good ones and he wrote all four of them down in the longhand book on the Saturday morning and only two of the four are reasons a person would give in a room.

One: **a ward at the bottom of a hill is not a settlement up a track.** Ada Rusk has said in a hall that this ward will not accept a return made by another office, and she has said it in a lane to a bucket that every one of the eight on that sheet is something she wants, and the lane and the hall are two different reasons and both of them are hers.

Two: **the woman who wrote those terms is not in that room and has not been asked and is not going to be asked, and if her eight refusals turn up in a chapel up a track on a Saturday night then a settlement has an officer, and a ward that has an officer has a document about it that it did not write, and the woman who wrote it knows that, and wrote it down, and ruled it into a steward's book with the day and the hour.**

Three: she has said that she will not say *visibility is not remedy* a third time because saying it a third time is the way a thing stops being true.

Four: he did not want to be in a room of thirty-one people at a tank house being the reason a place had a document.

---

**They split down the middle on the night, at about eleven, and it took about four minutes, and nobody shouted, and the better argument did not win, and there was no better argument, and there was one argument on each side and both of them were true and one of them had been true for a hundred and forty years.**

**One sheet per person. About thirty-one people were in the room, twenty-two of them adults and about nine of them under sixteen.** Eleven households put a branch in. Eleven households did not.

Prud Naylor went in first, at about half past nine, and gave a reason that took about forty seconds.

"**Nineteen years of a book in five hands. Two of us in it who have not spoken since a Sunday in the spring of 484 and we both turn the wheel on a Thursday and neither of us has said a word to the other. That is a return. That is the only kind there is. I want it in a book that is ours and I want it in a book that somebody can read, and there is a difference and I am sixty-one and I would like it in something.**"

Caws, who had said in the room on the Thursday that he wanted nothing, put a branch in and said, when somebody asked him why, that he had been forty-one years in this place and had never once had a question asked of him and was tired of it and wanted there to be a hundred and one.

**And the eleven that did not, and the first of them was Tobin Ragg, and he stood up and put the sheet face down on the table, which is not a thing a person does to a form.**

"**I have been a line in a ledger once,**" he said, "**and it was in 474 and it was my mother's line and it was an authorised draw and a dwelling at one address, and the two of them did not agree, and a woman came to the house and read out a column and my mother was a house that drew water and that was the whole of her, and the roof came off her that winter, and she was sixty-one and she did not get the roof, and I was twenty-three and I remember the shape of the column.**"

"You are not a person who cannot be answered for," said Owen Rusk.

"**I am not, and that is my point, and it is the only point I have and I am going to say it once.**" He did not raise his voice. "**If I sign a sheet tonight then I am a person who has answered for her own premises in front of a warden and a clerk and a man out of a building, and in about four years there is a file with my name on it and my mother's name is not on it and mine is, and there is no column on anything any of you have ever seen that says a person who answered did not answer for a person who could not. And I have watched what happens to people in this county who cannot answer. There are six of them in a ward at the bottom of this hill and the word is not written beside any of their names and the hours of the people who are there do not stop, and I have been here eleven years and I would like my daughter to be able to say no to somebody.**"

"**And the water, Jos, and the frame,**" said somebody.

"**And the water and the frame,**" said Tobin Ragg. "**I said on Thursday I would say the second thing in the room and not on the road, and this is the second thing: the sluice is going to fail before the frost and there are twenty-two of us and the county does not know this place exists. If we go down the road to Ossery or into Docketford we go somewhere that is on a list, and being on a list is how you get counted, and being counted is how they find out how many rooms we are in, and I have told this room what I think of that. I am not going down the road. I am staying here. I am not putting my name on a sheet.**"

Nobody argued with him. That is the thing about the eleven that did not: **nobody argued with them, and eleven people in that room wanted to say something and did not, and one of them wanted to say it very badly.**

---

**The second of the eleven who did not was the widow of a man who cut at the eleven in the Concord Year 481, and she gave a reason that was about a room and it is the best argument anybody made at Slackwater in the fourth month of the Concord Year 488 and it did not win either.**

"I have been asked one question in my life," she said, "**and it was in the front room and it was about a door and the door was not a door. And the man was very polite and he wrote it down, and I have been in this county sixty-one years and I have never seen that book and I do not know what is in it, and I am told there is a column now with the doors on it and I am told that somebody stood in a field and said it was the first time a person could check.**"

"Yes," said Owen Rusk.

"**Then check it, and leave us out of it,**" she said. "**I do not want to be checkable. I want to be left alone and I have never in sixty-one years had to say that to anybody and I am saying it tonight to about thirty people and to a warden and to a man out of a building, and I would like it written down that I said it, and I would like it written down that I did not say it kindly.**"

Prud Naylor wrote it down, in the tank book, on a page at the back, in the household's own book, with the day. **There is no form in this county on which a person at a tank house can rule a sentence into a book, and she did it, and the book is not an instrument and it may not leave the yard and she has said that and she means it.**

And the other nine of the eleven said nothing at all, and one of them was the man of nineteen whose daughter is eleven and whose name is not on the district's book anywhere, and he said afterwards, at the door, in the cold, to nobody, that he had wanted to sign and had not because the six on the list of persons under the suspension of fourteen days are on a piece of paper in a form and he did not want to be on the same piece of paper as his own daughter, whichever side of it she was on.

---

Kade said four things at about eleven and a half and then she left, and the last one is the one that is quoted.

"**I have been in a settlement in every season of nineteen and I have never been in one that asked me a question I could not answer, and I have never once been in one where the people who said no were on the wrong side of anything, and I have been in rather more than nine hundred and I have never written any of it down.**"

"You are not going to argue."

"**I am the Warden and there is no argument in this room and there has not been one since about nine o'clock. I am not a person you argue with. I am a person you hand things to.**" She was at the door. "**You have eleven branches out of twenty-two households and about thirty-one people in a room, and eleven is not a majority and it is not going to become one, and the return will be made on the eleven and it will be narrow, and a return narrows and does not collapse, and the eleven who did not sign have not said no to their neighbours and they are not obliged to and they are not going to be asked to.**"

"Nine of us still want the roof on the sluice," said Tobin Ragg.

"**I know, and there is no form for it, and I have been in this yard twice this week and I have watched about nine people turn a wheel at nine o'clock at night and I have written nothing about it, because a wheel is not a settlement and a settlement is not a wheel, and the only one of those two things that anybody in this county can put in a column is the second, and it is nine, and the Office put it there on Monday in four sheets, and I did not go to the place and I have not seen the book it went into.**" She said it flatly and it was the first time she had said anything about the correction out loud to a person at Slackwater. "**You are on a list now. You have been on a list since Monday and you were on a list before Monday and there was no list to be on, and that is what the four sheets are and I have read them and I attested them and I did not come up here before I attested them, and I would like about thirty people in that room to have that in their heads while they decide, and I am not going to decide it for you and I am not going to be thanked for it.**"

---

**The chapel was locked on the Sunday morning of the fourteenth day at about ten and there was a service in it on the Sunday morning of the fourteenth day at about ten, of about nine people, and it was the last one, and nobody has said since whether it was a service.**

They carried the bench out. Not the whole bench — two lengths of it, up the cart track to the cart house, and put it against the north wall, and the remaining two lengths stayed in the chapel and the door was locked and the key went to the tank house and hung on a nail beside the fortnight's list.

**And on the Sunday morning at about eleven a woman of about thirty-four who had signed a branch on the Saturday night came back up the track with the sheet in her hand and gave it to Owen Rusk in the yard and asked for it back off the return, and the right to do it was printed on the sheet in advance in eleven-point type at the top, because a clerk of the Census Guard asked for it and a clerk of the Census Guard was not thanked for asking for it.**

"It says a branch may be withdrawn by the person in it, and that withdrawal removes the branch and does not remove the return."

"I cannot read the small print and I can read the rest of it," she said. "**That is not a joke and I want it written down that it is not a joke. I have a child of six. The sheet asks me about my premises and my premises are two rooms and one of them is where she sleeps, and there is no box on that sheet for a room with a child asleep in it, and there is no column in this county that says what is to be written in a room like that, and I got to about the second of the small print on Saturday and then I did not get any further, and I have spent one night on it and I would like it out of the book.**"

Nobody in the yard said anything, which took about nine seconds.

"That is the right," said Owen Rusk, "and it is the first time anybody in this county has used a withdrawal right in the same week it was printed, and I am not going to stand in a yard and say that the instrument worked, because the instrument is a piece of paper and what happened in that room last night was not the paper."

**The return was made on ten branches and not eleven, and the return stands, and a return narrows and does not collapse, and the number of premises of record against the settlement in the district's own book is still nine, and the number of branches is now ten, and there is no column in this county that will ever hold both of those numbers and the fact that one of them is larger than the other.**

And the woman of thirty-four took her sheet home and did not give a reason a second time, and the man of nineteen carried the tin with the other nine sheets back down the track on the Sunday afternoon, and the tin is in the tank house and the tank house has one window, and the eleven sheets that are not in the tin are in a bag on a cart and a cart is not a place anybody will ever be able to look.

---

**On the Monday morning of the fifteenth day of the fourth month he wrote out the two lists, because the count room will want a list and a room of thirty-one people has produced two.**

*Branches in the return: ten. One adult to a sheet, separately signed, each sheet dated and each person naming their own premises.*

*Households that did not put a branch in: eleven.*

He wrote the second list and read it back out loud at the tank house at about nine in the morning to about nineteen people, and the second list is a list of places, and there is no form in this county for a person to be on a list of places they did not put themselves in, and he read it anyway, in a yard, in the ordinary voice, because the eleven who said no had said their reasons in a room and the only way a reason survives is being read back in front of the person who gave it.

**Caws asked for the two lists to go into the tank book and Prud Naylor said no, and he asked her why, and she said because the tank book is a book about a tank and a list of households is not a tank, and he wrote that down too, with the day, and it is the second time he has written down a sentence that is against his own convenience in a season.**

And the man of nineteen stopped him at the gate at about half past nine on the Monday with a question he had clearly been carrying since the Saturday night and had not asked in front of thirty-one people.

"You claimed it," he said. "On the Wednesday. In the yard. She heard you and it went in the case and it is not a finding."

"Yes."

"**Then what happens to you if we do not send it.**"

"Nothing," said Owen Rusk. "**Nothing happens to me, and that is the honest answer and it is the reason I do not like it. A claim is not a duty. There is no instrument for it, so there is no office that can dismiss it and no panel that can review it and no day it expires on, and I have been telling you since Wednesday that I cannot tell you what happens, and what happens is that I will still have said it.**"

"And if we do send it."

"**Then the same, plus a number in a file next to my name, plus the name of this place in a place I have put it in, and that is not a thing I can promise you is better.**"

The man of nineteen thought about it for about four seconds and then said, "**I am not signing and I want that written down as a thing about me and not about her, and I want to go and do the wheel.**"

He did both, on the Sunday, in a yard, in the household's own book, with the day.
