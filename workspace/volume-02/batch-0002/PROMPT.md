## Where the story stands at the hour you start

**The twelfth day of the first month of the Concord Year 488, a Saturday.** Twelve days into a counting season. Day 1 of C.Y. 487 was a Tuesday, so **488 Days 1 and 8 are Tuesdays, Day 15 a Monday, Day 19 a Friday, Day 22 a Monday, Day 26 a Saturday, Day 29 a Tuesday**, and Days 78, 85 and 91 of C.Y. 487 were Tuesdays. **Keep the calendar exact and never let a chapter's weekday drift.**

Owen Rusk is a **Stage 2 Continuance Examiner of the Count, Docketford district**, entered on the fourth day of the month, holding **three active holds and three named claimants**. He has a card that says *a hold may not be transferred to any other officer without the written agreement of the claimant*, and it is the first sentence in the Registry's instruments that says so, and it is not a cure of anything.

**Nether Sill is struck.** The correction is dated the twelfth, attested, the reason is the figure **4**, the draw is back in the district's main, the appeal lies to the Glass Stair and will take a season. **Nether Sill is not restored inside the volume.**

**The reason column is on a public board at Cutter's Fields.** It was granted in pencil, with no authority in the form and no condition. **Ten of the eleven reasons are the premises column written twice.** The practice is visible. **Visibility is not remedy**, and the sentence was said out loud by Ada Rusk and not by Owen.

**Docketford district is reported short by seven.** The day-book has four lines against a quota of eleven and an eleven-day notice that is on no form anywhere and is Owen's own arithmetic from a field. A qualified count goes up to the province.

**Seven settlements are still ahead in the season** — Gallants Reach (six premises, eleven households, back on the schedule because of Owen's own challenge), Marl Dykes (two premises, nine households), Quill Bank (nine, eleven), Rush Halt (seven, fourteen), Witherow (three, eleven), Ossery (eleven, twelve), Otter Stair (nine), Ashlocks (six).

**Lower Lock's own reconciliation is on the day-book for the third week of the season**, and the figure against Lower Lock on the public board is **eleven, which is a correct figure of its doors and a figure of nothing else, and Owen could not explain it and said so in a field.**

**The five lines.** Cobb Rennie's pension; six persons who cannot be asked at three of five parts; four pages; £4,160; and the receipt, **which was disposed of on the fourth of the last month under item nine and can now never be paid at all.** Nothing is funded. The granary was empty on the fourth and is at a fortnight on meal put in against a paper that is not money.

## The batch's job

**Four Households at Hark Holm.** A count correction, in the Concord Year 484, struck four households off the count on **Hark Holm** eleven days before the low bank came down. With those four off the count and the ferry's work not counted as maintained, there were fewer premises to protect and the slip could be rebuilt by the people who lived on it. **The deletion worked and it saved lives, and Kade made it, and it is in her own hand, and one of the four is alive.** Owen cannot hold both that fact and the eleven on the board without becoming a different man, and the volume does not let him.

This batch is about a **person who was one of the four** and about a **warden who will not be relieved of it**, and its two hard turns are:

1. **Kade's Hark Holm removal is read to her own detriment in a public room by a person it saved**, and she does not defend herself with the number of lives — because she does not know the number of lives and never counted them and the correction form has no place for them — **and she is not defended either.**
2. **A count correction is overturned on procedure for the first time in this city**, Kade **suspends one operation of her own**, and **is not thanked**. Owen takes his second and third holds. Nell and Owen work a case together and **disagree about it afterwards**.

**And the whole batch runs on the standing contradiction:** the settlement that wins is a settlement that was *deleted*, and the people who wanted to stay were not asked either.

## Verified canon this batch inherits — check every line of it against the prose before you write

### The four instruments of this volume, kept apart

1. **The Count** — a count of **draws, premises of record, and works maintained**. Published. Out of it come the water, the maintenance fund, and the right of a household to be witnessed at an address. **It is not a census of people and no chapter may let it become one.**
2. **The schedule of settlements to be reconciled** — eleven settlements, printed on the twenty-eighth before the season, posted in advance, **written before anybody surveyed anything.** The Warden **may return it and may not amend it.** Nobody knows who made it.
3. **The emergency suspension of fourteen days** — person-level, per person, with a date and a care act named. **Thirty days** before no supported living restoration is possible. **Nobody invents a worse rule than the one already in the bible.**
4. **The count correction** — made by the Provincial Counting Office, attested by the Warden, with a **schedule of premises attached**, a **date**, and **a reason, and the reason is a figure.**

**And keep them apart from Volume 01's four:** the spring engineering notice, the C.Y. 486 transfer receipt, the Day 10 load-restriction suspension, **Stability Order 114** — plus Redaction Batch 471/Q4/19 and Precedent 37/C. **Stability Order 114 is a local instrument on sixteen named returns, in public appeal, not withdrawn, the sixteen are sixteen, and the Count is a regional act on draws. Neither can move a line on the other. A ward on one list is not on the other.**

### The eleven settlements, in board order — do not renumber

**Nether Sill (9, 486) · Wexford Landing (4, 479) · Gallants Reach (6, 479) · Lower Lock (11, 486) · Marl Dykes (2, 481) · Quill Bank (9, 486) · Rush Halt (7, 486) · Witherow (3, 479) · Ossery (11, 486) · Otter Stair (9, 486) · Ashlocks (6, 486).**

Figures are premises of record; years are the year of the last reconciliation. **Hark Holm is not on the eleven** — it was struck in C.Y. 484 — and it is a ferry hamlet eleven miles up, four households, and **one of the four is alive at Wexford Landing, is not one of the four premises there, is the fifth house on that reach, is about seventy-nine, keeps the book at the hall, and asked the warden whether she had remembered the dog.** Kade named that house to Owen on the night of Day 91 and has not said it again.

### The limits of the qualification, which are the engine of the batch

An examiner may challenge **arithmetic, procedure, and the currency of any paper a reconciliation rests upon.** An examiner **may not examine the schedule, may not examine a choice of places, and may not put a word in the reason column.** He may enter a challenge and be heard. **He holds up to three, each a real responsibility toward a named claimant, and a hold is an answerability and preserves nothing.** His review record is one challenge — the Gallants Reach carried sheet — and **it changed no person's water and put a hamlet in front of a form.** The office calls the record thin and he wrote *thin* himself.

**The batch's central procedural engine: a count correction may be overturned on procedure, and this is the first time in this city, and what is overturned is the paperwork, not the people, and one settlement comes back and one person learns what she wanted.**

### Standing prohibitions — absolute, and every one of them is load-bearing

- **The brass tab is inherited shut. The drawer is not opened. No chapter names the man of Alder Row.** The standing extract with the year **461** in it stays unread. **The count schedules are published and that is a different door, and the reason it matters is not a secret.**
- **The six protective returns stay at three of five parts. No custodian is bought or assigned. The word *custodian* is not written beside Ada Rusk, Bram Tillet, Ilse Mern or Cobb Rennie**, and it is not written beside anybody else.
- **Cobb Rennie's pension stays unreachable.** Naming him is worth less than money and more than nothing.
- **Nothing funds the £4,160, restores the pump grant, pays the crew's wage, or opens the crossing to loads.** The repair is £4,160 and unfunded for the whole volume and the notice at the weir path stays up in the weather. **The granary clock must keep moving and must not be rescued.**
- **The two lost names are not touched.** No perception beat, no symptom, no notice by anybody, no third name, and no other character senses anything.
- **Marginalia is not used in this volume.** No panel, no margin notice, no silence, no status reported to Owen by a page. **If a page seems to speak, it is a document and somebody read it.**
- **There is no seventh renewal of District Return 487-LL.** The renewals are six, for review and never binding.
- **Hester Lund's sixth condition stays withheld.** The two tide tables are two and the two women are never merged. The Wexford Landing / Coal Hill quarrel was spoken about once, in Chapter 60, and **is not resolved and is not to be resolved in this batch.**
- **Nell Arbour is not available and does not become available.** No attraction is disclosed, no confession, no hand held. They work a case together and **disagree about it afterwards**, and the disagreement is about the work.
- **Ada Rusk is his sister and not a branch of the return and never was.** The ward's instrument is not reopened. **The scope clause is not restaged or reworded: four words, *Duty, and no status*.**
- **No first name is given to any Docketford or Glass Stair officer**, nor to the examining officer from Concord Hall, the man of sixty, the woman with the slate, the woman who maintains the board, the counting clerk at the second counter, or the hand that wrote *granted* in pencil. **Kade is the first named official in the series since Pike and that remains deliberate.**
- **No new final enemy. Kade is the volume's opponent and is on the ladder at rung two.** She is not softened, not rescued, and **not defeated by a fact about paperwork.** She is right about the danger in front of her in this batch at least twice.
- **The refusal to become a campaign.** If a correction is overturned here, what changes is one settlement and one person, **and the practice is still there**, and somebody has to say the sentence out loud.
