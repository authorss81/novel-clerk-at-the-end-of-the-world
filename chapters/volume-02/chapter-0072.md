# Chapter 72 — Nine Thousand Four Hundred

He was in the room at ten past eight on the Friday morning of the sixteenth day of the second month of the Concord Year 488 because the office shuts at three and the only person who is there in the morning is one man, and Owen Rusk had known that since a woman with a scraper had told him in a field, and had thought about it on and off for a fortnight. The man put the copy on the counter himself, which he had said he would do, and did not go behind the counter to fetch it, and said nothing about having done it.

**RETURN OF AUTHORISED DRAWS FOR THE SEASON OF THE CONCORD YEAR 488. THE WATER OFFICE OF DOCKETFORD DISTRICT. Made the twenty-eighth of October in the Concord Year 487. Sent to the Provincial Counting Office. Four columns: settlement, premises of record, authorised draw, year of the last reconciliation. One hundred and ninety-four lines.**

"It is a copy of the one that went," said the man. "It is not the original, because the original is in Concord Hall and has been since the twenty-eighth of October, and it is a copy of the one that went, which is a different thing, and if anybody ever tells you a copy of a return is the return, they are telling you they have not looked at the head of it."

"I wrote the wrong year on the form on Friday the second."

"You wrote the season of four hundred and eighty-eight as the date of making, which is October in the year before, and I stamped it, and I did not correct you, and I would like you to know that I noticed." He put the counterfoil down beside the copy. "**The copy is the right one. There is only one of them.** The season is named in the first line and the season of four hundred and eighty-eight was made in October of the year before, and if you had put the year after you would have been asking me for a sheet that does not exist yet, and I would have had to tell you so, and you would have gone away with nothing, and in a few years somebody in a building would have said that the district office asked for the wrong document and that is the kind of thing that is remembered."

"Thank you."

"Do not thank me, I have a bad knee and the tray is at the end." But he said it almost kindly, and then he put the cup down and said, "**Read it standing up. It takes a person about an hour and there is a stool behind you and you may have it.**"

---

Owen Rusk read it standing up for fifty minutes and then read it again from the top with the stool. The four columns are the four columns of the stand in the frame by the door, and the second column is the district's own book and not the Water Office's, and the third column is the only column anybody chose anything with.

The first column runs to one hundred and ninety-four lines. It is not the eleven. It is every settlement of record in this district and a great many things that are not settlements, and he could have told you before he began that about half of them would be a road, a standpipe, a trough, a boundary or a group of buildings with one name over them, and that a return of premises does not have a word in it for any of those.

**And the eleven were on it.** Down among the one hundred and ninety-four, in the order the Water Office files them, which is the order of an old book and not an alphabetical one, at lines he wrote on the back of his own hand without meaning to because he has never in his life been able to help it.

He added the eleven. He did it twice, the second time with a pencil on the counter because the man had pushed the pencil across without being asked.

**Nine thousand four hundred.**

And the head of the sheet carries the district's allocation in a box, and he took the district's own total off the hundred and ninety-four lines and took the box off the head, and the difference between the two was the quantity the river is required to hold to this district and not one drop more.

**Nine thousand four hundred.**

"Right," said the man of about fifty-five, from his own side of the counter. "Say that out loud again and I will believe you have had it right twice."

"You have had it right twice as well."

"I have had it right in October for twenty-two years and I have had a card against Docketford on a painted list saying RETURNED and a date on it, and I have never once been asked." He came round the counter, which he had not done before, and stood at the same end of it as Owen Rusk, which is a thing a person does when they have decided to say the rest of it in the open. "**You have the sheet. Say the rest of it.**"

---

The rest of it took about nine minutes and it is the reason the season is the shape it is.

"There is nothing on this sheet about when any of it was measured."

"No."

"It is made by taking last year's and correcting it. You told me that on the second of the month and I wrote it down, because it is the only fact in this room that everybody in this county has never been told. **A return of authorised draws is not a measurement. It is last year's measurement, carried forward, altered where somebody told us it was wrong.**" He put his finger on the fourth column. "And here is what nobody upstairs has ever looked at, because it is a column of years and a column of years is not a thing anybody has ever wanted. **This column is a settlement's last reconciliation. It is the last time a person stood in a place and asked whether the people there could answer for themselves.** So on the same sheet, in the same line, is a quantity and a year, and the year is the year somebody was last in the place, and the quantity is a number that has been carried forward ever since by a man who is allowed to change it when somebody tells him it is wrong."

"And nobody tells you."

"**Nobody tells me. I have twenty-two years of nobody telling me and that is why the numbers are what they are and it is not anybody's villainy, it is a form with four columns and a man who is not asked.**" He straightened a form in a frame. "Now here is the thing you have not seen yet and I have been looking forward to since Friday the second. Take the eleven and look at their fourth column."

Owen Rusk took the eleven and looked at their fourth column.

Three of the eleven had a reconciliation in the Concord Year 479. One had 481. Seven had 486.

"So that is the board."

"That is the board, and here is the other half of it and I would like you to have both halves at once, because I have watched a man come in here with one half before." He tapped the third column. "**These figures are not the figures of the year in that column. These are the figures of October last, corrected.** And I know which of them have moved since the year in the fourth column, because I am the one who moves them, and the answer is four of the eleven, and the other seven have not been touched in nine years and the four that have moved have been moved by a letter."

"So the eleven were chosen on figures that are as much as nine years old."

"**The eleven were chosen on figures that are as much as nine years old, and they were chosen on them deliberately, because a figure that has not moved is a figure that is safe to add up.**" He said it without weight, which was how he said the things he was certain of. "And there is one more thing and I have thought about whether to say it for two weeks and I am going to say it. **This sheet went out on the twenty-eighth of October. Your schedule was printed in the last week of December. That is two months.** For two months this sheet has been sitting in a building in Concord Hall with one hundred and ninety-four lines in it and a third column, and there is nothing on it that says which places are going to be looked at this season, because that column is not my column and it is not on my form."

"Two months," said Owen Rusk.

"**Two months, and a pencil, and a room, and no place to go.** That is where it happens. Not in a field. Not in this room. Two months in a room, and nobody has to travel, and that is the whole of what I have got and I have had it since the Friday before you came in and I have not been able to say it to a person in a room for twenty-two years because there has not been one."

---

Owen Rusk stood at a counter in a room with a window that does not shut and worked out in about ninety seconds that he had exactly three things he could do with a document. He could put it in a challenge, and a challenge is a hearing, and a challenge is heard before an officer of the count having no district in the matter, and a challenge about this document would be a challenge about a return, and a return is not the schedule, and a challenge about a return is a thing his card might actually reach, and if he entered it the document would be a document about the schedule in about four minutes and there is only one thing he is allowed to do with a document about the schedule.

He could carry the sum. And a sum made by a man with a pencil on the back of his own hand is a conversation, and he had torn a sheet in half about a fortnight ago for that reason and put the halves in different pockets. And he could give it to the one person in this county who had wanted to see it for twenty-two years and had never asked.

The man of about fifty-five was watching him do the third of those and had worked out the same thing, and he said so, which took about four seconds off the time Owen Rusk would have spent on it.

"**You are going to give it to me.**"

"I am going to give you something. I do not know yet whether it is a weapon or a letter and I have not had long enough to find out and I am not going to pretend I have."

---

It took him until about half past three to write it and he wrote it in the room because he could not carry a sum anywhere and there was nowhere else to be. It is two sides of a sheet and it is in his own hand and it has no heading, because a heading is a title and a title is a thing you put on a document when you intend to use it.

It says what he found on the stand, and it says the sum twice and says that the two figures are the same figure to the unit, and it says that a shortage is a round thing and that is not round. It says that the return is made by correcting the season before it and that the eleven were chosen on figures that had not been measured since their own fourth column, and that three of them had not been reconciled since the Concord Year 479. It says that the sheet went to Concord Hall on the twenty-eighth of October and the schedule was printed in the last week of December and that the choosing happened in between, in a room, with a pencil, and that no office in this county has ever sent anybody to any of the places on it. And it says that he may not examine the schedule and that he has not tried to and that he will not, and that a document about the schedule is a thing he is permitted to do nothing with.

And at the foot of the second side, four lines, and the four lines are not a request.

*I am not asking you for anything. I am not going to enter a challenge about this return, and I have thought about it for a day and a half and here is the reason: the moment this sheet is a challenge it is a document about the schedule, and there is one thing I am allowed to do with a document about the schedule and it is nothing, and I would rather hand a man a piece of paper in his own hand than put a room in the way of a man who has waited twenty-two years.*

*You asked me to put a question to an officer of the count having no district in the matter, in a room, on the record, with a name on the answer that is not mine. I am going to tell you that I think you are right and that I am not going to do it this season, and if you ask me why, the answer is that the fifth column went into a return on the Monday and there is no survey in this district to fill it with, and a challenge about a paper that asks for a survey would be answered by stopping anybody from doing one, and I would rather find that out in a field than in a room.*

*So here is a letter, and it is not a weapon, and it is worth exactly what a piece of paper in my hand is worth, which is nothing, and you were right to ask for something better and I have not given it to you.*

*And I would like it noted in whatever you keep, that a man of about fifty-five in this room told a stranger on the sixteenth day of this month that nobody has asked him anything in twenty-two years, and that he was not complaining, he was putting it on the record in the only direction that is available to him.*

He read it back to the man of about fifty-five in about six minutes, standing at a counter, and the man of about fifty-five read it over his shoulder with his hand on the counter and did not interrupt, and at the end of the second side he put his hand over his mouth for a while and then took it away.

"**You have given me a letter.**"

"Yes."

"I asked you for a question in a room." The man of about fifty-five straightened up. "That is a correct sentence. I asked you for a question in a room and you have given me a letter in your own hand and you are right and I am right and we cannot both be served, and that is going to be true in about nine years as well, because that is what it is like when a thing is true."

He put the two sheets together and squared them, which is a thing a man does who has spent twenty-two years at a counter.

"I am not going to thank you and I want you to hear the reason and not the politeness of it. **If I thank you then I have been given something, and I have not, and there is a difference and I have been the man who is given something twice in twenty-two years and I did not get anything out of either of them.** Twenty-two years of not asking is not a thing that gets paid back by a fortnight, and I have not got worse at it in a fortnight and I am not going to. And in three weeks one line of my return is going to be a line shorter and nobody will tell me why, and that was true when I told you on the second of the month and it is more true now, because you have been in this room and somebody in a building has seen that somebody has been in this room."

He put the two sheets into the inside pocket of his coat.

"**And there is a thing on the board at the foot of the frame and it is not mine and it is a strip of paper the size of a receipt, and I will read it to you because I have read it and because you have earned it, and because I am not going to be a man who finds things out on his own twice.**"

---

The strip of paper was dated the tenth of the month, which is a Saturday, and it is a request, and it went to every water office in this county, and the Water Office of Docketford district is not exempt from anything because it has one man in it in the mornings.

**YEAR OF LAST SURVEY OF EACH SETTLEMENT OF RECORD IN THIS DISTRICT. To this office within fourteen days. Returned to the office from which it is sent.**

"It was on the counter when I came in on the Saturday morning and I filled it in over the weekend and the Monday and posted it on the Tuesday, and the fourteen days run to the twenty-fourth, and I have got a hundred and ninety-four lines on my stand and about half of them I have never been asked anything about by anybody in my life, and I have written **not known** against somewhere near ninety of them, which is the answer, and I sent the sheet back because it was asked for and because it is in my own hand and I am not a man who holds things."

Owen Rusk read the strip of paper twice and then read the date on it a third time.

"It came down on the tenth," he said. "The form with the fifth column on it came down to this district on the twelfth, and it was made in a building on the ninth, and the two of them are not the same office and I know that."

"The two of them are not the same office and I know that as well, because the strip came out of one building and the form came out of another, and they went on different days, and they are about the same thing." The man of about fifty-five put the strip of paper flat on the counter. "**They have never asked me anything in twenty-two years. They have asked me this, in fourteen days, in a form with a date on it and a line at the foot for the office to sign.** And I want you to be the person who works out what that is, because I have not got the whole of it and I have got the part that matters, and the part that matters is that they are going to get the year of the last survey of every settlement in this district out of a water office, and a water office has never been to any of them."

"A return is not a survey."

"**A return is not a survey, and the year a return was corrected is not a year anybody looked at anything, and a man in a water office is the only person in this county who will be asked for that year, because a water office is an office and offices are easy to write to and nobody goes to them.**" He tapped the strip of paper once with a finger. "I have filled it in honestly. I have written *not known* about ninety times on a form that came from a building which has never once been told the answer to anything, and I did it because it was asked for, and there is nobody to be angry at and nobody to thank, and that is the whole of what I have found out this month and it took me three days and I am not sure it is worth anything."

He put the strip of paper in the frame by the door, on a nail that had been there longer than he had.

Then he went back behind the counter and picked up the cup and said the last thing, standing down, in the voice of a man who is going to shut at three whether anybody has been in his room or not.

"**A letter is not a weapon and a weapon is not a letter and you are the only person in this county who has had to work out which one a sheet of paper is, and I have had twenty-two years of not knowing, and I would like you to understand that you have made my week worse and not better, and that I am glad you came, and that I am not going to say either of those two things again this season.**"

Owen Rusk was back up Fetter Lane at half past three and did the tray and was on it at a quarter past six, and there was nothing on it from across the cut, and the two sheets of a letter were in an inside pocket of a coat a mile away and were going to be looked at by a man of about fifty-five at about six o'clock that evening in a room with a window that does not shut.
