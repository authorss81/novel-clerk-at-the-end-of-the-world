# Open Threads

This file is author-facing planning. It is not a list of facts already known by the opening cast.

## Central Mystery

- Can Form Zero be completed without a single person answering for everyone else?
- What did the Registry conceal about the original distributed rule, the Blanking, and the Assumption Clause?
- How did Owen's eight-person infancy sponsorship become a distributed root claim, and what does it cost him to use it?
- How can a bounded completeness procedure protect a real world while honestly recording the Free Counters and other branches it cannot see?
- What obligations does the new civic order owe people whose claims remain unresolved after activation, and how do they use appeals without letting either Lound's old machinery or Owen's Custodian role reclaim them?

## Immediate Volume 1 Promises

- Lower Lock and the Ninth Weir need a lawful district return. The Day 14 roll has lapsed the crossing's status and frozen the ward, so the return is not a formality after Chapter 10: **entering it on Day 15 is the only cure**, and it cures by restoring the crossing as a duty the ward performs and as nothing else.
- Halden Pike's stability order must be challenged through evidence and appeals, not a hidden power.
- Owen and Nell must work together while retaining their professional disagreement.
- Ada must be allowed to negotiate Lower Lock's future rather than becoming Owen's protected ward.
- Any crossing hold preserves a legal appeal window, not the physical safety or usability of the Ninth Weir.
- The distinction between claiming a crossing's **status** and claiming the ward's **maintenance duty** is the hinge of the volume and has now been taught on the page: the return claims the duty and disclaims safety, condition and use in the same sentence.
- Nell must refuse to be Owen's witness, advocate, or relief, and the refusal must hold for the rest of the volume. She has said the terms out loud in Chapter 10 and offered help with the **appeal route** only.
- Junia Pell refused Owen's short-form attestation in Chapter 9 and remains under no obligation to him. She signed the return's observation and dated his statement, and the statement was about the ward's notice rather than the notice itself — a distinction she insisted on and it is still a live exposure if anyone looks for the hand inside the transfer's foundation.
- Six of the thirteen erased entries are children or people unable to answer. Lower Lock has no custodian, no unrelated second witness, no witnessed care act and no appeal route. Pell has put all four gaps on a slip. **This is not solved by the end of Volume 01.**
- **Cobb Rennie's pension, the four dissenting households' separate branch, Dory Anstruther's injury, and the ward's granary reserve** are open threads inside the volume. Cobb is outside the thirteen households and outside the thirteen erased entries, so nothing in this case reaches him; his thread must not be quietly folded into the ward's return. The four dissenters — Ada Rusk, Bram Tillet, Holm Ferris, Wenna Sarr — are named in the ward's own book as having been wrong, forever, and they are worse off than the nine.
- Pike's justification — the withdrawal that saved people elsewhere and hid its own cost — must surface without excusing it.
- **Stability Order 114 has no issuer until Chapters 17–18.** It is signed on the sixteen failing returns Pike named in Chapter 3, all sixteen of which are now written out by name in Owen's private notebook with Lower Lock among them. It must be a separate instrument from the Chapter 8 load-restriction suspension and from the spring engineering notice, and it is the instrument that reaches public appeal.
- **The Day 14 lapse and the freeze** stand. The pump grant is suspended, not extinguished; every household is frozen; no name is struck; the load restriction is in force on top. The Chapter 9 petition holds none of it and the Glass Stair clerk said so before anyone asked. Entering the return on Day 15 lifts the freeze and nothing else. The written answer on Day 19 restores nothing, and the pump grant is never made whole inside the volume.
- **The return's entry and its first review** are the opening act of Batch 0002. The return is finished, witnessed by Pell and Voss, narrowed, disputed, complete on its face, and sitting unentered at the back of Pell's tray with a covering slip naming Owen as the person who delivered it. It is entered on **Day 15** as Owen's first qualified filing, which starts the return's own seven-day review, **due Day 22** — the same window as Stability Order 114. One of the nine branches will not survive that first review, and the return narrows instead of collapsing.
- **The Council's review quarter closes on the eighteenth.** This is now on the page in the Chapter 10 circular and it is a hard date on the ward: after it, the district office returns community filings unexamined. Batch 0002 runs inside it. It is a Council instrument with a seal and a Chancellor's countersignature and nobody's name on it, and **who at the Council issued the countersignment standard, and why, is reserved to Batch 0003.**
- **The seven-day answer clock** opened in Chapter 9 must land in Chapters 12–13. It runs to the written answer, not to a hearing, and the answer gives a date. Without that beat the volume has a clock and no payoff. Batch 0002 opens on Day 15 with the answer due on Day 19.
- **Owen's destroyed continuity statement** is a live exposure, not a redemption. Two holders, neither safe: **Pell**, who read it and said it was true, and **the ledger**, which has already proved it can leave the building. The earliest beat that may touch it is Batch 0002's attack on Pell's separability; it may also surface in Batch 0003–0004 if anyone but Owen reads the notebook. It must never be paid by Owen confessing it in the first person, and it must never be the thing that earns him forgiveness. Pell is under no obligation to hold it for him.
- **The three requests refused inside the backdating window** are visible in Chapter 1, surrendered with the date in Chapter 5, and unexplained through the end of the batch. The Office register outlives Owen's withdrawal, so someone is still reading it. Batch 0003 is the earliest beat that may begin to pay it, and it must not be paid by Owen alone.
- **The backdated annex schedule** is the one thing Owen was provably right about, and he withdrew his own proof of it in Chapter 5. It is never corrected.
- **Alder Row and the brass tab** need beats at Ch 14–15 (the index request) and Ch 34–36 (the four streets), not just the Chapter 10 discovery and the Chapter 50 image. Owen has written the number, the shape of the tab and the date in his ledger and has not opened the drawer. The drawer is never opened in Volume 01.
- **Lound's second circular** must arrive in Chapters 33–35 and name the review rather than the hearing. Her two circulars are her whole presence in Volume 01, and the first has already named the deadline that closes the local-return route.
- **Pell's independence is contestable** because she is a Registry officer signing a ward's duty instrument, and she has said so against herself in the margin in her own words. The question must stay open through Batch 0002, where the answer is *accepted for review* rather than accepted as binding. It is also where the first pressure lands on the only person besides Owen who knows what he destroyed at the weir.
- **The ward's appeal route** is live now and is the part of the return that works without entry. Nell Arbour has offered to help with it and nothing else. Batch 0002 needs a person at the other end of it who is not Owen, not Nell and not the Office, and Nell has said there are about four people in the delta who could be, and that two of them are women she has had words with. Do not invent a fifth without paying that line.

That thread is open and must not be resolved inside Volume 01.
