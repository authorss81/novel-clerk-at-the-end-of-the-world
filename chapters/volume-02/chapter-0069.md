# Chapter 69 — What She Would Not Sign

The tent came down on the Thursday night and it took two men and a boy from the kiln and four hours, and the stone went into its case at about ten, and the case went into a cart at eleven, and the cart was going up the hill to Concord Hall in the morning with a season's work in it and a figure on the front of it that was not the quota.

Owen Rusk was in the tent at about four in the afternoon of the eighth day of the second month with a request he had been carrying up a hill for three days and had rehearsed on the boat and had not been able to shorten.

**Name a day.** The recitals bar had been lifted on the twenty-fourth at ten past one in the afternoon in a room with high windows, and the words on the face of it were that the settlement of Lower Lock may be reconciled on any day the Warden names, and six days had gone by and no day had come, and there were two weeks left on a printed sheet and neither of them was the ward's, and he had asked the woman who maintains a board and the counting clerk at the second counter and a man of about fifty-five in a room across the cut, and all three of them had said the same thing, which was that the Warden names the day or the Warden does not.

He said it to her with the ledger shut, which was the only piece of ground he had, because on the Monday of the twenty-second she had said *do not read the extract in front of me* and he had not read it in front of her and had not read the third row from the bottom at all.

She let him get all the way to the end of it and then she said no.

"**No,**" said Warden Kade, "and I am going to say why, and then I am going to make you say it back to me, and you are not going to enjoy either."

---

"There are two settlements on that sheet I have not been into," she said, "and there have been since the sixth day of this month and I have known it since the sixth day of this month, and I have not written a day for either of them, and you know that, because you have been up a hill three days asking everybody in this county who is not me."

"I have."

"Then you know what the answer is, and you have come anyway, which is a compliment and I will not return it." She put her hand flat on the case the stone was in. "**A count correction has effect for the season in which it is made.** I have not been into Lower Lock and I have not been into Ossery and I have been into the other nine, and I am not going to pretend that that is a virtue. The officer of the count said those words in a room with high windows on the Thursday before last and she said them about my own instrument and she was right and I have not disagreed with one of them. A reconciliation is a statement about premises. If I name a day for Lower Lock in the last week of a season in which an examiner's fourth limb was sustained in this district, then that reconciliation goes up to the province with a determination on the second page of it saying that a figure of four was carried four seasons in a standing extract in this district, and every figure in it will be checked twice."

"Checked twice how."

"Because it will be checked once by me and once by somebody who has read that determination and now knows what a challenge is." She said it without any weight at all, which was how she said the things she was certain of. "**A settlement that is checked twice is a settlement whose figures move.** And a ward whose figures move is a ward that gets a reason in a box, and a reason in a box is a figure, and I have been the woman who puts those figures in for nineteen years and I am telling you what one of them does to a place, because I put a four in one on Hark Holm in the Concord Year 484 and there was a wall where there was not one eleven days later and eleven households went off that reach, and I would do it again, and I have never once been able to say that sentence in a room without wanting to be somewhere else."

He stood in the tent with his hands at his sides for a moment, because she was right, and because being right was going to cost his sister a season, and because he had spent this whole season trying to get a warden to be reachable and had never once in his life wanted her to be reachable *about this*.

"Say the other one," he said.

"The other one is the quota and you already know it, so I will say it quickly. **The count is certified in the morning. Docketford district goes up short by seven, the count is qualified, and a qualified count goes up to the province, and the district was also reported short in the Concord Year 484 and in 486.** If I name a day for your ward I take one settlement off the shortfall of a day-book, and the day-book is a public sheet, and the man who reads it in Concord Hall is the same man who reads the total. And the day I do a settlement to move a number is the day the fourteen days and the thirty are a letter and not a law. I would rather be short. I have been short before."

"You have never been short on purpose."

"I have not," said Warden Kade. "**I have never been short on purpose in nineteen seasons, and I have never once done it for a person I liked, and I have never once done it for a person I did not like, and I am telling you that so that you can put down your pencil.** Make me say it back."

He said it back. She made him say it twice. The second time was not for the argument.

---

Then she did the thing he had not asked for and would not have dared to ask for, which was that she told him the cost in advance, in words, with a place in it.

"**Your ward's eleven doors are not on a count in the season of the Concord Year 488 and there is no instrument that puts them on one, and there is not going to be one this season, and you are the officer of the record of the district returns out of which Lower Lock is taken, and there is no premises at which you may be asked, and you wrote that sentence yourself on a form in a fortnight and underlined it.** There is a second settlement. Ossery. Twelve households, a lime kiln, forty minutes off the nearest water, and I have not been into it and I did not go into it and I am not going to tell you why I did not go into it, and it is not because of the quota."

"Then why."

"Because I have been into most of that sheet this season and half of them said a version of a thing to me in those words, and one of the others was saved in 484, and I am not able to go into the two at the end of it this week and sign what I sign at the end of it, and that is a thing about me and not about Ossery, and I would like it entered that way and not the other." She shut the ledger, which she had not opened. "**That is what it costs. Two settlements, one of them yours, one of them a place you have never been, and a district short by seven, and a count qualified, and my name at the top of a piece of paper that says I stopped.** Carry it. That is all I am going to give you today and you did not ask for it."

"I know you are right," said Owen Rusk. "I worked that out on the boat on Wednesday and I came up here anyway."

"I know you did," said Warden Kade. "That is why I told you the cost instead of making you guess at it. **A man who comes anyway has a reason I have not been told, and a man who comes because he has been told has a decision, and I have to know which one I am talking to before the hour.**"

---

On the Friday morning of the ninth day of the second month of the Concord Year 488 the count of the Concord Year 488 for Docketford district went up on a board at the west gate of Cutter's Fields, and the woman who maintains it wired it with two loops of tarred cord in about thirty-five minutes, and the man of sixty held the ladder and did not say anything while he did it, and about twenty people stood about and read it as it went up, which is the thing nobody who has not stood at a board in a field can be made to understand, and which is the whole reason the count has ever been published.

**THE COUNT OF DOCKETFORD DISTRICT. Season of the Concord Year 488. Certified the ninth day of the second month. Qualified.**

*Draws, premises of record, and works maintained, as reconciled by the Census Guard: **9 of the 11 settlements named on the schedule of settlements to be reconciled.***

*Not reconciled, and not to be reconciled in this season: **Lower Lock. Ossery.***

*This count is qualified. Docketford district is reported short by seven in its return of premises, and was also reported short in the Concord Year 484 and in the Concord Year 486. A qualified count is reported to the province.*

Owen Rusk read it four times standing in the cold, and the fourth thing on it was the one he would still have in his head at the end of the year, and it was that **there is no row for a settlement that was not reconciled.** For nine weeks there had been a row for everything. There was a row for eleven doors that were a correct figure of nothing else, and a row for four doors produced of nine recorded, and a row for a place that had never been looked at, and a row for a place that was looked at and put out of the count on a figure. There was no row here for the two that were not done. The board was tidier than it had been all season and it had taken the ward and a lime-kiln hamlet off it by doing nothing at all, and nobody had put a figure anywhere, and the tidiness was the whole of the method.

That is the thing he has never been able to explain to anybody in a room, including the woman who maintains the board, who read it over his shoulder twice and said the sentence he was about to say and then did not say it either.

Ada Rusk was there at about eleven. She had not been asked and she came, and she had come on the boat with a girl of seventeen who does the tide tables at a landing and who was asked by nobody and said nothing, and Ada stood in front of the board with her hands behind her back and read the two names and then read the two names again.

"**There is no row for us,**" she said.

"There is no row for Ossery either."

"I had noticed." She put her head on one side. "It is not the same thing and I would like it written down that it is not the same thing, because I have watched people in this ward for eleven weeks now say the two as though it were one, and it is not one, and the day somebody uses the two together is the day this ward is finished. Ossery is a place that was put on a list and never looked at. **We are a place that was on a list and looked at and left off.** Those are two different injuries and one of them is ours and I intend to be exact about which."

"You are."

"I have been exact about it since the twenty-fourth of the month, when a woman lifted a bar in a room and then did not name a day." Ada Rusk read the count once more from the top. "When is she going to say the rest of it."

"She is going to say it at noon and she is going to say it at the gate, and I would rather you were not standing next to me when she does."

"Why not."

"Because she will say it to the field," said Owen Rusk, "and I have been the man in the field all season and today I would like to be a man standing next to his sister in a coat."

Ada Rusk looked at him for a moment longer than she usually did, which was about a second and a half, and then she said, "**All right,**" and went and stood ten feet off on her own, which is the greatest thing she has ever done for him and neither of them will ever mention it.

---

At noon she wired up a second thing.

It is a single sheet and it is the size of a large envelope and it is wired to the frame under the count with one loop of tarred cord, and the hand on it is not a clerk's and is not a printer's and is the hand that has put a figure in nine boxes this season.

**SUSPENSION OF OPERATIONS. DOCKETFORD DISTRICT. Season of the Concord Year 488.**

*I suspend the reconciliation of Docketford district with the hour of the certification of this count. Two settlements named on the schedule of settlements to be reconciled have not been reconciled and will not be. The reason is not a figure and there is nowhere on any form in this county to put one, so I am going to write it at length.*

*Lower Lock and Ossery. I have not been into Ossery this season. I have not been into Lower Lock. I have reconciled nine places out of eleven and I have stood in all nine, and on the two I have not been into I have nothing in my hand that I could attest, and I will not put a number in a box because I have not stood in the place, and I would rather leave two settlements unreconciled and short of a quota than carry a figure I did not make.*

*The count is qualified. Docketford district is reported short by seven. I know what that costs and I am not going to explain it away.*

*This suspension is an act of operations and not a finding. It may be reviewed by the office. It ends when the office ends it and not before, and the season does not stop because I have stopped, and that is the whole difference between me and every office in this city.*

— **W. O. Kade, Warden.** Signed in her own hand at the west gate of Cutter's Fields, on the ninth day of the second month of the Concord Year 488, at noon, and not in a tent, because the tent was struck on the Thursday night and she would not sign it in a building somebody else had put up.

Nobody thanked her, and it is worth saying that plainly, because it is the shape of the whole of this season and because everybody who has stood in front of this woman since the fourth day of the month has been waiting to say something to her. Owen Rusk stood about six feet off with his hands in his coat and worked out in about four seconds why he was not going to say it, and the reason was that a thank you is a receipt, and there is nothing here to receipt. He had asked a woman for something on a Thursday and she had given him a worse thing instead, and the worse thing had cost two settlements and a district a figure, and he was the officer of the record and he was going to be the officer of the record of both of them, and there is no form in this county on which a man can be thanked for the state of a thing he is the record of.

Cobb Rennie said the thing that was actually true, and he said it to the sheet and not to her.

"**That is the first reason anybody has written in this county that is not a number,**" he said. He had walked up from Fetter Lane and he was fifty-eight and he was the only person at the gate who had not come to see anything happen. "I have wanted to read one of those for nineteen years and I have not had one and I am not going to get one, because a warden signs nine of those in a season and one of them is not going to be for me. **But it is on a board.** I want that said and I want it said by somebody who is not me, and I am saying it anyway."

Nobody answered him. He had asked for the same thing at the end of a good morning in June and he had got it, and he said out loud in a hall in June that naming him was worth less than money and more than nothing, and he was not going to be thanked for a sentence he had already given away once.

Elda Varn was at the back. She had come down from Wexford Landing on the Friday morning in a boat with a girl who pilots, and she is seventy-nine and she had asked at a post on the reach whether anybody from the count had been up it, and had been told twice, no.

She read the count, and then she read the second sheet, standing quite still, and she did it twice, and Owen Rusk watched a woman of seventy-nine work out in ninety seconds that there is no column in this county for the fact that two places were not done, and that the reason is on a public board in words, and that neither of those things is the thing she came for.

"**Then it is a column,**" Elda Varn said, to nobody. "Four days old and it is a column. Eleven is not on it and that is all right, I have stopped wanting eleven on things. **What I wanted was a book that said there were eleven households on that reach.** There is not one. There is a piece of paper at a gate with a sentence on it about a ward and a kiln, and the reach is not on the paper at all, and it will not be on the next one either, and I am seventy-nine and I have stopped asking, and I would like it noticed that the stopping is new and that I did not do it on purpose."

That is the true shape of what happened in Docketford district in the Concord Year 488, and it is in a document, and it is on a board, and it cost the ward a season and it cost a hamlet a season and it did not cost a single person anything that anybody can put in a column.

---

Kade waited until the field had gone down to about a dozen people, which took a quarter of an hour and was not an accident, and then she read the sentence out loud. She did not read it from the sheet. She stood with her back to the frame and said it to the field, in the ordinary voice, at the volume of a person who has said things in fields for nineteen seasons.

"**Now I am going to say the part none of you came for, and I am going to say it once, and Owen Rusk is not going to say it because he cannot and I have known that since the seventh day of the month.**

"**The practice is still there.** It is going to be there in June. The reason box is a figure and it is going to be a figure next season and the season after that, and the schedule for the season of 489 is printed in Concord Hall in the last week of a month by people who have not been to any of the places on it, and I am the woman who signs under the figure, and I signed nine of those this season and I did not write one of them. **The fourteen days and the thirty are a law and they are going to keep being a law, and the day they stop being a law is the day a reach goes down, and I have stood on one.**"

Somebody at the gate said, "Then what has any of it done."

"**Nothing,**" said Warden Kade. "That is what has happened, and it is not a defeat and it is not a win, and I would like it entered in whatever the office keeps that it is worth nothing and cost two settlements. I suspended my own operations this morning and I did it in a field and I did not ask anybody's leave and nobody thanked me, and I have got what I wanted, which is that in about nine years there will be a piece of paper in this county with a reason on it that is not a number. **Nine years is a long time and it is the only door I have and I have had it for nineteen seasons and I have never once walked through it before, because a warden who writes reasons in words is a warden who has to be able to say what the words mean, and I have never been able to do that, and this morning I have written one anyway and it is a page long and there is nothing on it I could defend in a room.**"

Owen Rusk said, "It is true."

"I know it is true," she said. "**It is also true that I have put two settlements came off that board this season and I did not go into either of them, and one of them is a village with four doors, and nobody in this county will ever know which, including me, in a year, and that is the job and it will be the job in June, and there is not one single thing on that board which changes any of it.** Visibility is not remedy. Your sister said that to a hall in a different month and she is right and I am not going to take it off her."

And then she picked up her bag and went up the hill, and the man of sixty took the ladder down, and the woman who maintains the board began at the left-hand end with a scraper, because the season's account was up and there was another one to come and the frame took two.
