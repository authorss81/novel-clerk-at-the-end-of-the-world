# Chapter 19 — The Review

It was twenty past ten on the Thursday night, in the back room of the hall with the door shut, and Marguerite Bray was in it because a thing that is written in a delivery book in a room with a door shut is a thing she can vouch for, and Ada Rusk was in it because she had said she would be, and Owen Rusk was in it with a form with a box on it that he had been unable to fill in for three days.

"I have got it," said Ada. "Read me what you have written."

"'Any adult of the district of Lower Lock who is not a party to this return.'"

"No," said Ada.

"That is the class. It is the only class there is. It is in the form and the form is the form and I have been three days looking for a better one and there is not one."

"There is a better one and it is worse, and you are going to hate it, and I am going to say it anyway, and Mrs Bray is going to write it exactly." Ada took the form off him and turned it round so that she was looking down it the way she looked down a drawing. "The class is not *any adult of the district*. The class is *the eight persons whose branches are in this return, and any person the district office names in writing before this review is concluded.* Eight. Not nine, because nine was last week. And a person the office names, which is a door for the Office to open and not for us."

"That excludes the four."

"Yes."

"Ada—"

"Yes," she said. "It excludes the four, and it excludes me, and it is going to be in a document in a drawer for ever, and in five years somebody is going to read the class on that form and count nine and find eight and think they have caught a clerk in an error, and they will not have caught anything, and I will not be able to tell them." She put the form down. "That is what it costs to have said no in a room with witnesses in it, and I said it on the seventh day of the month with my hand up, and I have had three weeks to think about it and I have not found a way round it, and neither will you, and if you put a wider class in that box you are not protecting them, you are insulting them."

Marguerite Bray wrote it in the delivery book first, in pencil, and then Owen wrote it on the form in longhand, and then Ada put one sentence underneath it in her own hand, and Owen read it twice and did not take it out.

*The four adults of this district who did not consent to the transfer of the seventh day are recorded in this return as dissenters, they are not parties to it, and nothing in this return is offered to them for their objection.*

---

The review room in the district office on the Friday morning of the twenty-fifth day of the month is a room with six chairs, a table, a clock, and a reviewing officer, and the door is shut, and everything said in it is written down afterwards by somebody who is not in the room.

There were five people in it, and the arrangements had been made by the ward and not by the Office, which was on the record because Ada Rusk had insisted on it being on the record and had been told, correctly, that there was no form on which to insist. Junia Pell, second desk. Wend Voss, counter clerk, Mill Ward annex, who had walked up the hill and come back down it again in the dark and had not told either of them he was going. Ilse Mern, forty-four, laundress, who was a branch of the return and had been the ward's choice and had asked on the way in whether she was to speak. And Owen Rusk, who was not a party and was not asked and who sat in a chair and read two pieces of paper.

The papers on the table were nine pages of return, of which one was a withdrawal, and one page of amendment, and a challenge in four paragraphs, and a page of a junior clerk's own hand sustaining it, and a margin in a counter clerk's hand saying twice that the interest was not separable, and the delivery-book extract of the twenty-fourth, and the form with the box on it that Ada Rusk had filled in on Thursday night in a room with the door shut.

District Return 487-LL came up on the Friday for a first seven-day review that was three days late on its own face, and the delay was on the record, and the record said in whose it was.

The first review fell due on the twenty-second. On the twenty-second, at nine in the morning, an objection had come down through the countersignature desk against the second observer, and the second observer had sustained it, and a reviewing officer does not conduct a review of an instrument while the standing of its observers is in question, so the review was adjourned to the next day a reviewing officer was in, which was the twenty-fifth, and the three days went on the file as a matter of procedure, and on the file, in the column for the reason of adjournment, there was a space of three quarters of an inch and it was empty.

Owen read the empty space for a while and then had to look away from it, because it was the most honest thing in the file and it had been left empty by a clerk who was not going to put a sentence in it, and he was the reason for all three days, and nobody was going to write that down either.

The reviewing officer was about fifty and read everything twice and wrote nothing until the end. He had the manner of a man who has decided in advance to be fair and is going to be fair, whatever it costs the file, and he did not look at Owen at all for the first twenty minutes, because he was reading the nine pages, and the eighth, and the amendment, and the challenge, and the four-sentence margin.

Then he said: "You are the assisting clerk."

"Yes, sir."

"You entered this return on the fifteenth. You live in the district. You are the co-petitioner in the docket of the petition it answers."

"Yes, sir."

"Then you are not speaking in this review and you are not asked to, and if you have something to say you may say it in the ward meeting and it will be in a different file." He turned a page. "The ward's acts."

He went through them, one at a time, with the ward's own log in front of him, and asked nothing about ability.

That was the thing Owen noticed and wrote down afterwards and has never forgotten. He did not ask whether the ward could afford it. He did not ask whether the ward could do it in the winter. He did not ask whether the crew of nine were paid, or whether the marker would be set in February, or what happened if the river came up on a Thursday and six of the nine were on the mill's stone contract. He had a document in front of him that named a repair at four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and declined the repair, and he did not ask a single question about money, because a review of a return is not an inquiry into solvency, and a man who had spent his whole life on solvency sat in that chair and did not ask.

"Marker set the eighteenth and again this morning. Channel cleared on the night of the sixteenth, with a bar of silt and rubbish across it that was not there on the ninth, which the ward's watchman recorded. Pump primed on the eighth, ninth, tenth, fourteenth, fifteenth, sixteenth, seventeenth, eighteenth and nineteenth days, and again on the twenty-first, and the prime of the twenty-second did not hold and was repeated at four the next morning, and since the twenty-first four households have been carrying water from the standpipe at the top of Fetter Lane." He looked up. "Is that accurate."

"It is in the ward's log and the log has been produced."

"It is. The review does not go into whether the pump will run in the winter. It is not a question the form asks and I am not going to ask it as an officer, because the moment an officer asks it, it is on the file, and the day somebody puts it in a column it will be a reason." He wrote for a while. "The order."

He turned to the amendment and read Ada Rusk's first clause, and the second, and then he stopped in the middle of the third one and read it again, and then he read it a third time, and Owen understood that he was doing exactly what Owen had done on the seventh day of the month, in a hall, with a sheet of paper.

"'This amendment alters no act, no scope, no term, no observer and no duty recorded in this return. It is a new entry from the hour named above and it erases nothing,'" said the reviewing officer. "Who dictated it."

"Ada Rusk. She is the crew leader of the ward. She is not a party to this return. I wrote it out from her mouth and it is her document and I have told her so and she has told me so in front of the ward."

"That is a strange thing for a clerk to do with a document."

"Yes, sir."

"Then it is a strange thing and it is not irregular and I am not going to touch it." He made a note. "Now. Mr Rusk, I am going to put a question to nobody in this room, because nobody in this room may answer it, and I want it on the record, and then I am going to answer it myself, and I want the ward to have the answer in writing whatever I say."

He wrote for about a minute and a half, and then he turned the sheet round so that Owen could read it, which is not done.

"A stability order under my Deputy Registrar's solvency authority closes or suspends the standing of the returns named in it. It does not suspend a duty. It cannot, because a duty is not a status: a status is a thing this office holds on the roll, and the order is about the roll, and a district that is suspended has lost its standing and has not lost its hands. A district may be suspended and may continue to perform, and the performance may be recorded, and the record stands, and the standing is a separate question that this office has already answered in a different instrument and is not answering again in this one." He put the sheet down. "That is the answer. It is the ward's answer as much as mine; the ward asked for it in the third clause of an amendment yesterday, and I am not going to pretend to a reviewing officer that I arrived at it independently, because a reviewing officer who takes a party's submission and returns it as his own finding has turned a submission into a decision, and that is the one thing this review is not."

"Then what is the renewal?" said Owen.

"Renewed **for review only**," said the reviewing officer, "on terms, and I am going to read the terms out because somebody will read them out in a hall in a fortnight and I would rather they were correct the first time."

*That the acts recorded in this return be performed and recorded in the same manner and at the same interval, and that no act be added to it and no act be performed in it that is not recorded, and that the record be the ward's own and not an office's summary of it.*

*That the two observers be separated in interest in writing at each renewal, and that a separation made once does not carry to a second renewal, and that the Office may hold the separability of either observer open at any renewal without that being an objection to the return.*

"Which means me," said Pell's voice from the second chair.

"It means you," said the reviewing officer. "And it means Mr Voss. And I want to say this to both of you plainly, because I have read what you each wrote on the seventeenth and I have read the objection and I have read your answer to it, and the answer is better than the objection and I am not going to pretend otherwise." He looked at her. "You wrote that no statement you can make is capable of separating your interest from the outcome. That is correct, and it is the reason this instrument is renewed **for review** and not as a binding continuation, and it is the reason I can renew it at all, and if you had not written it I would have had to find some worse reason."

Junia Pell said nothing. She had her hands flat on the table in front of her and she was looking at the clock, and Owen Rusk, who had spent a month learning to read people who were being careful with him, understood that she was not being careful with him and that this was the only time he had ever seen her with nothing to say.

"And there is one more," said the reviewing officer, "and it is a note and not a finding, and I have thought about whether to make it."

He made it. He wrote it in the margin of the disposition, in the same hand, in about the same pen, and it said:

*Noted for the record: District Return 487-LL is one of sixteen returns named in Stability Order 114 of the twenty-fourth day of the month. The suspension of a return is not the suspension of a duty performed in it. The two instruments are separate and neither is a finding as to the other.*

Owen read it four times.

The first time he read it as information. The second time he read it as courtesy, because a reviewing officer is permitted to note an instrument that bears on a return and the note is harmless. The third time he read it as a fact, and stopped, and read the words *the two instruments are separate and neither is a finding as to the other* and understood that it was the answer he had wanted given in his case and had been given without anybody knowing he had wanted it, and that the answer had been in it for four days, in Ada's mouth, in a hall.

The fourth time he read it and understood what the note actually was, and it was not a courtesy, and it was not information.

It was a drawer.

The Office had put the ward's return and the order in the same drawer. Deliberately, on purpose, in a reviewing officer's own hand, on the margin of a disposition, where a clerk who opened one of them in three years' time would come across the other. Anybody who read the return would read the order. Anybody who read the order would read the return. And a return with a stability order stapled into its own file was not a return that anybody in this building could quietly treat as a small matter about a bridge on marginal land; it was a return that had a document in it saying that sixteen districts had lost the same thing on the same afternoon for the same reason, and that the reason was a word, and the word was *fund*.

Owen sat in a chair in a review room and worked backwards through every person who could have put that note there, at length, and got nowhere.

It could not be the reviewing officer, who was not writing for a public file and did not know what was in it. It could not be Pell, who had no standing to note anything. It could not be the ward, because the ward had asked for the opposite of what the note did; Ada's third clause had asked for a reviewer's position, and the note recorded a reviewing officer's position, and the ward had been given the thing it asked for and had not been asked for the thing it got.

It could have been a docket clerk, in a room, on a Friday, with two documents that belonged together and one of them about a bridge. It could have been an office that had spent four hundred and eighty-seven years putting things in drawers so that whoever came later would find both.

He could not find the person and he wrote nothing down about it, not in the file and not in the notebook, and that decision took him about two seconds and it is the closest he has ever come in this book to understanding that some of the things in the Registry are not done by anybody.

---

The ward hall at half past seven on the Friday evening had the four new acts read out, because Marguerite Bray read out whatever was put in front of her, and what was put in front of her was a sheet with the Thursday marker on it and a sheet with the channel clearance on it and the pump book.

"Marker set at seven forty this morning," said Ada. "Two of us on the top path and three at the path end. Nothing to report."

"Nothing to report is what you put in the book," said Bray, "and I am going to write it as *nothing to report* and not as anything else, and if that is not good enough for the district office then the district office may come and watch us do nothing to report at seven forty in the morning on a wet Friday."

"Channel's clear. It was the bar again on the Wednesday night, which means it is coming off the mill's outfall twice a week and it did not used to, and I have written to the factor about it and he has written to me about it, and neither of us is going to say what the letter said."

"Pump's going," said Ilse Mern, from the middle of the room. "Nine times since the fifteenth and once it didn't take and we did it again at four in the morning, and there are four houses on the standpipe still, and the Rennies are on the standpipe, and Cobb Rennie is on the standpipe because he is sixty-one and has the cough and there is nowhere else for him to be on a night, and I am not going to be asked about that in a room."

Nobody asked her about it. Owen watched the room not ask her about it, and understood that this was a thing the room had learned to do in a month, and that he had not taught it, and that it had been taught by a hand pump and a roster on a slate.

"Right," said Mern. "And I am off the granary count."

The room went quiet in the way it had gone quiet for Nessa Kray on the Wednesday.

"Not off the return," said Mern. "Off the count. I have stood in that granary three nights a fortnight since the seventh of the month and double-counted the bins against the book and put my hand to it, and I have not been paid for the count or for the pump and I have a roof going soft at the ridge and I am doing it because somebody has to, and I have decided that somebody is going to be Nessa and Kray, who is seventy-one and has just been the first person in this ward to walk off something, and I am not going to be the second thing in a month that everybody has to be told about."

"That is your right," said Marguerite Bray.

"I know it is my right. That is what I want said. I am not doing anything dramatic, I am giving up a job I did not get paid for, and the pump is mine and the marker is Ada's and I am still in this return with my name on it, and I want it read into the book that I said I was staying."

"It is in the book," said Bray, and it was.

At nine o'clock Owen got up to go, and Ada stopped him at the door with two fingers on his sleeve.

"Mr Rusk. Before you go and do whatever it is you are going to do with your evening." She looked past him at the room, which was emptying. "You put a form in front of a reviewing officer this morning with a box on it that I filled in, and in five years a clerk in that building is going to read the class in that box and count and find eight and think somebody has done him. I want you to have a copy of the page, and I do not want a speech."

"I did not think you were going to make a speech."

"No, I am going to make an observation, and then you are going to take a piece of paper and go down the hill." She folded her arms. "On the third of the month I said to my brother in a kitchen that a man who writes the trap and then defends his own accuracy is a man who is going to be the reason somebody dies. You said I was wrong about you and you were wrong about me, and we were both about a notice." She looked at the stove. "This morning I sat in a room in the district office while a man with a clock on the wall read out my third clause and then gave me the answer to it, and did not pretend he had got there by himself, and I have been thinking about that all day and what I have decided is this. That is the first time in twenty years that an officer of that building has handed a ward something it asked for and then told the truth about where he got it. I do not know his name. I am not going to find it out. I am telling you about it because you are the one who will be at that counter every week for the rest of your life and I want you to know that the building is not one kind of thing, and I want you to know it from me and not from a list."

Owen Rusk stood in the doorway of a ward hall with a piece of paper in his inside pocket on the left and a good deal of weather in his coat and no answer at all, and after a moment he said, "That is the second true thing," and Ada said, "It is the second," and let him go.

He walked down the hill in the rain. The return was entered and reviewed and amended and renewed for review and not bound, and its two observers had written the worst facts about themselves into it in their own hands, and one of its nine pages was on Marguerite Bray's shelf in a drawer with nothing else in it, and a woman of forty-four had given up a granary count she was not being paid for and was still in the return, and the crossing was still closed to loads, and the pump grant was still suspended, and the ward's granary was going to be three weeks at the end of the month.

And the thing Owen could not put anywhere, on the walk down, in a notebook or a margin or a form, was that the instrument had worked. It had been narrowed, challenged, sustained against itself, amended, and it had gone on, and it was doing exactly what Ada Rusk had built it to do on the ninth day of the month in about forty minutes of argument, and he had not one feeling about that at all except a kind of tired recognition that the thing he had been carrying was now considerably larger than his opinion of it.

Which was the condition of every other clerk in that building. He had simply never before had a personal example.
