# Volume 02 Batch 0001 Outline — The Season Opens

Batch number: 0001
Chapter range: 51–60
Story time: **The seventieth day of the last month of the Concord Year 487, through the twelfth day of a month in the Concord Year 488.** Thirty-three days. The last month of 487 ran to ninety-one days and the year turned on a Tuesday. **Day 1 of C.Y. 487 was a Tuesday**, so Day 70 is a Monday, Days 71, 78 and 85 are Tuesdays, **Day 91 is a Monday**, Day 73 is a Thursday, Day 77 a Monday, Day 80 a Thursday, Day 84 a Monday, Day 87 a Thursday, Day 90 a Sunday, and C.Y. 488 Days 1 and 8 are Tuesdays, Day 7 a Monday, Day 9 a Wednesday and Day 12 a Saturday.

## Batch Goal

Open Volume 02 on the hour the hold fell, and turn Volume 01's victory into Volume 02's problem. By the end of the batch: the certification has expired and the five-line list is on nobody's instrument; the tray of unfiled correspondence has delivered its first item about a *place*; the ward has been asked by the count for premises of record and cannot answer; the season's schedule of settlements to be reconciled is nailed up in public with **Lower Lock on it**; the Census Guard has come up the river and Warden Oress Kade is on stage; Owen's first written objection to a strike is correct in law and fails on a fact; the year turns; Owen passes a review record and becomes a **Continuance Examiner** with three active holds and three named claimants; and **Nether Sill is struck from the count with the reason published on a public board, and the reason against Lower Lock is a figure nobody can explain.**

## Starting Condition

Owen Rusk is a Sealed Witness with **no hold**: his certification expired at the hour of the seal on Day 70, at about half past three in the afternoon, and the preservation of the Ninth Weir's legal status fell with it. The crossing's status is suspended again and the Day 10 load-restriction appeal window has closed with it. He is posted to the Fourth Desk, the tray of unfiled correspondence, and the record of the district returns. His card forbids a continuation, a certification, a suspension and a hold. He holds one named duty, for the household of Ivy Sarr, which does not close because the end date is the child's.

Nell Arbour is working and is not available to him. Halden Pike is alive, unpromoted, and the first name in a register of contested closures. Junia Pell is fourth on a nineteen-name district-exclusion roll. Ada Rusk leads Lower Lock's own paper, which says four words. Marguerite Bray rules a delivery book that has been in her family for fifty-one years.

### What expired, and what that means on the page

The certification ran seven days from the seal at about half past three on Day 63 and ended **at the hour of the seal on Day 70**. Chapter 51 opens at that hour. Consequence, all of it on the page and none of it a surprise to anybody who was there:

- The **crossing's status is suspended** again, the position it stood in before the Day 63 seal. The load-restriction suspension of Day 10 is unaffected. **The crossing remains closed to loads** and the repair is **four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and unfunded** and the notice at the weir path still invites any district able to perform it.
- The **Day 10 appeal window has closed**, because the hold that kept it open fell with the certification. Nobody is to be told this as a surprise; Chapter 46 said on the page that a hold does not survive the certification which carries it.
- The **list of five lines** is on the instrument and stays on the instrument. What ends is the *duty* to carry it. **No later chapter may have Owen carrying a hold or a list he is not holding.**
- **The Day 14 roll entry stands unamended for the rest of the series.** The Day 15 entry started a new status and did not touch the roll.
- **The receipt**, promised in the answer of Day 19, is still owed and is **still on a spike on the ground floor**. Chapter 52 puts a retention notice on that spike. It is not disposed of in this batch.

### The four instruments of this volume, kept apart from each other and from Volume 01's

1. **The Count** — the annual count of draws, premises of record, and works maintained. Published. Docketford's water, its maintenance fund, and the right to be witnessed on a district act come out of it.
2. **The schedule of settlements to be reconciled** — a public list, nailed up at Cutter's Fields and at the Glass Stair door, naming **eleven settlements** in Docketford district. **It was written in the last week of the Concord Year 487, before anybody surveyed anything.** This is the batch's planted tell and it is not resolved in this batch.
3. **The emergency suspension of fourteen days** — person-level, per person, with a date and a named care act. The law of this world, honestly applied.
4. **The count correction** — the district-level instrument, made by the Provincial Counting Office and attested by the Census Warden, with a **schedule of premises attached**, a **date**, and a **reason**, and **the reason is a figure**. Effect: *struck from the count of this district for this season, the premises in the schedule attached, and the households drawing in respect of them, and the water not drawn is returned to the district's main and is not reserved to the settlement.*

**None of these is a reopening of anything in Volume 01, and none is a Stability Order.** Stability Order 114 is a local instrument on sixteen named returns and it is still in public appeal; the Count is a regional act on draws; the volume's central confusion is that a district officer and a census warden each think their list is the list. Keep them apart in every sentence.

### The five places of this volume

- **Cutter's Fields** — flat ground behind the tanneries where the Census Guard pitches and where the count and the schedule are nailed to a public board.
- **Nether Sill** — eleven households on a silted bend two hours upriver. A sluice, a mill, and a count sheet from an earlier year recording **nine premises** where the place can now produce **four standing structures**, with **eight of the eleven households living in two rooms of the mill**.
- **Hark Holm** — a ferry hamlet struck in the Concord Year 484. Four households, eleven days before the low bank came down. **Named in Chapter 58; paid in Batch 0002.**
- **Pegler's Cut** — a drainage district with a good return and an inconvenient draw. Named only; not on stage in this batch.
- **Slackwater** — twenty-two households, a chapel, and a draw the city depends on. **Not named in this batch.** It is the volume's centre and the volume's climax.

### Numbers this batch fixes

- **Eleven** settlements on the Docketford district schedule. **Eleven** is the quota, set in Concord Hall in the last week of C.Y. 487.
- **Nine** premises on the Nether Sill count sheet. **Four** standing structures. **Eleven** households. **Eight** of them in a mill.
- **Fourteen** days of emergency suspension. **Thirty** days before no supported living restoration is possible. **Seven** days for a review of a reconciliation to be heard.
- **Three** active holds at Stage 2, and **three** named claimants, one of whom is the household of Ivy Sarr.
- **Eleven is not spent on anything else in this batch** — not on minutes, inches, sheets, or repeated numbers. Volume 01's four uses of eleven belong to Volume 01.

### Standing prohibitions for this batch

- **The brass tab is inherited shut.** No chapter opens the drawer. The count schedules are **published**, and that is a different door, and the man of Alder Row must not acquire a name. The year 461 appears on a public board in Chapter 60 and **Owen does not read that column**, and the chapter says so without making a mystery of it.
- **The six protective returns stay at three of five parts.** No custodian is bought. **The word *custodian* is not written beside any of the four people in Lower Lock who could be asked** — Ada Rusk, Bram Tillet, Ilse Mern, Cobb Rennie. Chapter 53 is the chapter where the temptation is strongest and it must not be taken.
- **Cobb Rennie's pension stays unreachable.** Naming him is worth less than money and more than nothing.
- **Ada's wage, the Fetter Lane pump grant, the £4,160 and the granary are all still unfunded and none of them is funded in this batch.**
- **The two lost names are not touched.** No perception beat, no symptom, no noticing by anybody, no third name, and no other character senses anything. **Chapter 59 must not come near it** even though Chapter 59 is about what it costs to hold things.
- **Marginalia is not used.** No panel, no margin notice, no silence, no status reported to Owen by a page. If a page seems to speak in this batch, it is a document and somebody read it.
- **Owen signs nothing he is forbidden to sign** until Chapter 59, where the acquisition happens on the record and the instrument is his own qualification. Nothing in Chapters 51–58 is signed by him on any instrument.
- **Nell Arbour is not available and does not become available.** She works alongside him on a case that is partly hers, they are useful to each other, and neither of them says anything about the other. No attraction is disclosed. No confession. No hand held.
- **No first name is given to any Docketford or Glass Stair officer.** The District Officer, the seal-stair clerk, the counting clerk and the Glass Stair counter clerks stay institutions with hands. **Kade is the first named official in the series since Pike** and that is deliberate.
- **Ada Rusk is not a branch of the return and never was.** The ward's instrument is not reopened by this volume.
- **Ada Rusk is his sister. Nell Arbour is not.** The volume keeps every relationship in its own tense and adds nothing.
- **The scope clause is not restaged, rewording, or explained again as a revelation.** It is four words and it is the ward's own and it is on the file. Owen's answer when asked in Docketford remains *the district has told you*, plus the sheet.
- **No new final enemy.** Kade is Volume 02's opponent. She is not softened before Chapter 90 and she is not beaten with a document.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 51 — The Hour of the Seal

- **POV and location:** Owen Rusk, the ground floor of the River Desk, from about half past two in the afternoon on Day 70 to the ward hall in the evening.
- **Goal:** Work the tray until half past three, and then go up the hill and tell the ward what has happened at the hour the seal runs out.
- **Resistance:** Nobody in the building treats it as an event, because nothing happens. A certification that expires by its own term produces no form, no notice, and no conversation. And he has no instrument to point at, because the instrument is spent and the thing it held is not restored by its stopping.
- **Major turn:** At about half past three the crossing's status is suspended again, and the only person who ever intended to look at that crack has no duty to, and **the notice at the weir path is the only document in this city that still refers to the repair.** Then, on the same walk, on a post at the top of Fetter Lane, a notice that is not from his office: **the counting season opens on the first day of the month in the Concord Year 488**, and the schedule of settlements to be reconciled for Docketford district is posted at Cutter's Fields.
- **Consequence:** Lower Lock is on the schedule. He reads the list on the post and there are **eleven** names on it and one of them is the name of his own ward, and he has spent seven weeks learning what it costs to be on a list.
- **Emotional beat:** Ada Rusk asks him one question — *what did it cost* — and he cannot answer it in a sentence, and she does not help, and it is the first time since Volume 01 that he is in a room with nothing.
- **Ending hook:** He walks down to the counting ground's fence the next morning and there are already eleven sheets of paper on a board that was not there on Friday, and he has a tray of two hundred and forty sheets upstairs, and the count is not going to be carried by him.

### Chapter 52 — The List Comes Off

- **POV and location:** Owen Rusk, the ground floor, Days 71–72.
- **Goal:** Find out what a person does on the Tuesday after a term ends, and put the five lines where they belong.
- **Resistance:** The instrument is on file with the list still written on it, and the list is nobody's business now. He is a stranger in the building and a man who kept a thing for seven days is not a man anybody is going to ask for it back.
- **Major turn:** He takes it up the hill himself and reads it out in the ward hall without a sheet, in order, because that is the only office anybody in that room ever needed him to fill, and **Marguerite Bray rules it into the delivery book with the day and the hour and rules under it that no person in the room is under it.** The five lines are the same five lines. Nothing has been paid. Cobb Rennie's pension is a pension that is not a pension, the six children are six children, the four pages are four pages, four thousand one hundred and sixty is four thousand one hundred and sixty, and the receipt is a receipt that is standing on a spike.
- **Second turn, on the ground floor, on the Wednesday:** The tray does the thing the tray is for. A quarterly notice comes up for **Pegler's Cut**, a drainage district, and cannot be routed because there is no premises of record for it, and the notice goes back, and the second one goes back, and the third one comes back with a hand on it in a different ink. **The tray is not a punishment. It is where the correspondence of places the Office cannot find goes, and it is the first honest description of the count Owen has read in a form.**
- **Ending hook:** He learns one more thing about the spike: there is a **retention notice** on it now, and the notice has a period on it, and the period is not long, and the receipt is the oldest thing on the spike.

### Chapter 53 — Premises of Record

- **POV and location:** Lower Lock, Days 73–76, a Thursday to a Sunday.
- **Goal:** Answer the count. The counting season sends the district a **return of premises** and Lower Lock has thirteen households of record and two hundred and forty people and four maintenance acts and a return with four words of scope.
- **Resistance:** The count does not want duties, or acts, or a schedule of performed acts, or a withdrawal right, or a person who will stand in a room. **The count wants premises of record, at which water is drawn.** The ward has a pitch at the bottom of a slope that has no premises on it and is under a foot of silt, a pump whose grant is suspended, and a standpipe rota with five names on it, one of which is a man of fifty-eight and one of which is a household of two people.
- **Major turn:** The ward's own count does not go through, and the reason is **Nessa Kray's pitch**, and the ward's paper still does not claim it, and Ada will not let it be stretched over it. And the second reason is the child: the count's form has a line for every person at a premise, and **Ivy Sarr is a draw at a standpipe at four in the morning and the line wants a household and the household is two people and one of them cannot be asked**, and the ward does not write what it does not have.
- **Emotional beat:** **Wenna Sarr writes to Owen for the first time in his life**, one paragraph, about the line on the form, and it is not a plea. Owen's named duty and the form are the same problem in two buildings, and he cannot solve either of them, and he says so in a hall and is not consoled.
- **Prohibition observed:** The word *custodian* is not written beside any of the four. The five hours a week is on a page. The six stay at three of five. Ada asks the room whether anybody will take it and nobody answers and she rules that nobody is to be asked.
- **Ending hook:** The ward's return of premises goes up to the district office with a line missing on it, and the district office returns it in nine days, and the return says *incomplete* in a hand nobody has looked at, and it is the first time in this case that a thing has come back marked **incomplete** rather than refused.

### Chapter 54 — The Board at Cutter's Fields

- **POV and location:** Cutter's Fields and Arbour Kitchen, Days 77–79.
- **Goal:** See the schedule. Owen goes because the count is nailed up in public and public things can be read by anybody, which is a sentence he has believed in a hall for seven weeks.
- **Resistance:** The board is not a comparison sheet and there is no queue. It is a **published list of eleven settlements in three columns — name, year of last reconciliation, premises of record** — and the second and third columns are the same class of figure, and **there is no column on that board for a reason at all.** The reason exists in a box the size of the warden's hand on the correction form, in her own hand, and it is not published until Chapter 60, when Owen gets a fourth column onto the board on the word *granted* written in pencil.
- **Major turn:** Eleven names. Eight Owen has never been in. **Nether Sill is first on the list.** And one of them is four streets from where he sleeps, and the figure against it is one he can read. And there is a line at the head of the board giving the **quota for the district**, and the quota is eleven, and the quota was **set in the last week of the Concord Year 487**, and the board went up on the first working day after the new year.
- **Nell Arbour on stage:** she has a claimant household from one of the eight and needs somebody who can read a count sheet quickly, and she asks him, and she is working, and she is not available, and Owen notices that he asked her for nothing and that she noticed too. Neither says anything. She says the sentence that becomes the volume's question, in a kitchen, without noticing that she has said it: *somebody decided, and it was not the field, and I want to know on what.*
- **Ending hook:** Under the third column, at the foot of the board, in a smaller hand, a line about **when the schedule was made**. Owen reads it twice. He does not go and find out.

### Chapter 55 — The Boat and the Tent

- **POV and location:** The counting ground at Cutter's Fields, Days 80–84.
- **Goal:** See the method properly, from the people who run it, in daylight, with the stone out of the case.
- **Resistance:** The Census Guard is not a counter-argument. It is a **service with a season, a quota, a method, and a hundred and forty years of predecessors**, and every part of it is lawful, and the person running it is right about the danger in front of her.
- **Major turn:** **Warden Oress Kade**, thirty-nine, on stage for the first time anywhere in this novel. She states the method without softening it — fourteen days per person, thirty days before restoration is impossible, the correction at the end of fourteen — and she is not defending it, she is describing it, the way a doctor describes a dosage. And Owen asks her the question he has spent a volume learning to ask, which is not *is this right* but **who decided**, and she answers it correctly and it does not help: *the schedule is not mine. I reconcile what I am given. I have never once been asked to make one and I have thought about making one and I could not tell you what I would put in the reason column, and that is not humility, it is that the column wants a number.*
- **Second turn:** She reads his name off the district's list of officers and tells him she has read the Lower Lock file and **did not put it on her list**, and asks him why he thinks that is a favour, and he has no answer that is true.
- **Ending hook:** The quota for Docketford district is **eleven** and the schedule is **eleven** and both were made before anybody surveyed anything, and Kade says the only thing she has ever wanted anybody to ask her: *if you can find out who wrote the list, write to me and not to the Council, because I will do nothing with it and I will not pretend to.*

### Chapter 56 — Nether Sill

- **POV and location:** The river and Nether Sill, Days 85–89, a Tuesday to a Saturday.
- **Goal:** Find out what a settlement on the schedule actually is, on the ground, in the rain, with a woman who keeps the sluice.
- **Resistance:** The place is not a mystery and not a horror. It is **eleven households, four standing structures, a sluice that a woman maintains alone, a mill that is working, and eight families living in two rooms of the mill because the bend flooded in the rain season and their houses are under silt.** They are fed. They are not on the count. Nobody has done anything to them. That is the horror and it is made of silt.
- **Major turn:** Owen gets the count sheet — the last reconciliation, from an earlier year, the only record of what the place was — and it is a **public document** and it records **nine premises**. The place can produce **four**. And the difference is not a lie, and it is not concealment, and it is a **mill room**, and **a room in a mill cannot be made premises of record without an act of the district office, and no act has been made, and the district office has never been told they are in a mill, because the count sheet has never been sent to the district office.**
- **Cost in the chapter:** the water. He stands at the district main and watches a draw refused. Not violently. At a counter, in a form, correctly.
- **Nell:** she does not go up the landing, and the reason is on the page in one line and it is Wexford Landing and Coal Hill and two women who have not spoken in two years, and the two tide tables are two, and **Hester Lund's sixth condition is not asked about and is not asked about again in this batch.**
- **Ending hook:** Owen understands exactly what he has to do on Sunday, and it is not what he wants to do, and it is the argument of a hall in Fetter Lane seven weeks ago.

### Chapter 57 — The Objection

- **POV and location:** Nether Sill, Day 90, a Sunday. **The batch turn.**
- **Goal:** File a written objection in the correct form, before the fourteen days are up, and stop the correction.
- **Resistance:** He writes it well. It is correct, it is in the right form, it is signed by nobody, and **the counting clerk's answer is a form, and the form is right.**
- **Major turn:** He has argued the reasonableness of a demand and has not supplied the alternative. The alternative existed and it was in the room: **premises of record can be made by an act of the district office, and he could have found the sheet that would have started it, and he did not, because he spent the week arguing about a form instead of asking a woman in a mill what her name was.** The objection is answered in a paragraph that grants nothing and is entirely correct. He is not a fool and he is not beaten by a smarter person; he is beaten by the fact that a thing he did not do would have worked.
- **The turn:** he says the sentence out loud in a room and it is Ada Rusk's sentence and he has never once said it in Docketford, and Kade is in the room and **does not stop him and does not help him**, and afterwards, on the bank, she tells him the one thing that makes it worse: *that is the first true thing anybody has said in front of my count and it is not a fact about the count.*
- **Consequence:** the fourteen days run out on the twelfth of the month in C.Y. 488. The correction is prepared. Nothing anybody says on Sunday changes it.
- **Ending hook:** Kade asks him, on the bank, in the rain, whether he is going to read the schedule before he writes to her about who wrote it. He says he will. He does not say when.

### Chapter 58 — What the Warden Knows

- **POV and location:** The counting tent at Cutter's Fields, the night of Day 91, and the first day of the Concord Year 488. **The year turns.**
- **Goal:** Find out whether the warden can be dealt with.
- **Resistance:** She cannot. She is competent, she is candid, she keeps a list of her own, and she has decided which losses are acceptable before she has heard whether the affected people would rather be deleted.
- **Major turn, part one — the method, precisely:** a review of a reconciliation may be requested by a person of the district, must show **a current local act performed by the settlement**, and is heard before **a named officer of the count having no district in the matter**, and there is a **seven-day limit** on the request, and the person requesting it must be named, which is a name on a page, which is what a return is made of.
- **Major turn, part two — Hark Holm, named flatly, not confessed:** in the Concord Year 484 she struck four households off the count eleven days before the low bank came down. With those four off the count and the ferry's work not counted as maintained, there were fewer premises to protect and the slip could be rebuilt by the people who lived on it. She says it as a fact about a season she worked. She does not defend it with the number of lives, because she does not know the number of lives and never counted them and the correction form does not have a place for them.
- **Ending hook:** The year turns. There is no ceremony. The first thing the Concord Year 488 does in Docketford is put another sheet on the board at Cutter's Fields, and the sheet is the **day-book of the count**, and it is the **quota**, and the quota is eleven, and Owen has been in this city his whole life.

### Chapter 59 — Three Holds

- **POV and location:** The River Desk and the Glass Stair, C.Y. 488, Days 2–6.
- **Goal:** Become somebody who can examine a reconciliation, because the alternative is being a man with opinions about forms.
- **Resistance:** The qualification is not a reward. It is a **supervised case and a review record**, and the review record is one challenge, and the challenge is a **procedural error** — a count sheet carried forward from a lapsed year without being reconciled, which is a real error and which changes **no person's water**.
- **Major turn:** Owen is examined by an officer of the count and the examining officer makes him ask the standing question, and Owen asks it *well* this time, and is answered. He is a **Continuance Examiner**. **Up to three active holds, each a real responsibility toward a named claimant**, and the first of the three is **the household of Ivy Sarr**, which means his named duty and his qualification have become the same object without anybody planning it and without it being a cure for anything.
- **The two other claimants** are named, are real, are not wards he is fond of, and each costs him something specific in the four days. One of them is a woman who wants a review of a count and does not want it to succeed.
- **Pell:** offered something — a posting on the count's district correspondence, which is a step up — **and does not take it**, in her own words, in one sentence, and the reason is that she would be the district's face for a count she has not looked at.
- **Ending hook:** the counting clerk sends up the prepared correction for **Nether Sill**, dated the twelfth, and Owen may read the reading out, as the examiner, in public, because that is what an examiner does with a reconciliation he has examined.

### Chapter 60 — The Reason

- **POV and location:** Cutter's Fields and Lower Lock, C.Y. 488, Days 7–12, a Monday to a Saturday. **Batch climax.**
- **Goal:** Read the reading out, and stop it if there is anything on the form that can be stopped.
- **Resistance:** There is not. The correction is correct, dated, witnessed, sealed with a stone that has been used four thousand times, and eleven households are struck, and the water is returned to the district main, and the appeal lies to the Glass Stair and will take a season.
- **Major turn:** Owen does the one thing he knows how to do, and it is a small thing: he asks — **as the examiner, in writing, in the proper form** — that the **schedule of premises and the reason be entered on the public board at Cutter's Fields with the settlement's name against them.** Nothing requires it. The correction's own form does not provide for it. It takes an afternoon, a conversation with a woman who maintains the board, and a signature. **And the reason is a figure, and the figure is the same class of figure as the one against Lower Lock, and the practice is now visible, and visibility is not remedy, and this time the sentence is said out loud by a person who is not Owen.**
- **Ada and the ward:** the reconciliation for Lower Lock is **scheduled** for later in the season, and the figure against Lower Lock on the published board is one he can read and cannot argue with, and the ward's paper says four words and the count does not want four words, it wants premises, and **the ward performs and is not counted, and says so itself, in its own hand, in the delivery book.**
- **Unresolved and carried:** Nether Sill is not restored. The eleven households keep the water they have. The receipt is on a spike with a retention notice on it. Cobb Rennie is a draw at an address with no record and his pension is uncured. The six are at three of five. Ada's wage is not funded. The repair is four thousand one hundred and sixty and the notice at the weir path is up in the weather. The sixteen are sixteen. The drawer is under black cord.
- **Ending hook / forward pull:** the season is ahead with **ten settlements** on it and one of them is **four streets from where he sleeps**, and the volume's question is now in a room and cannot be put back: somebody decided on a figure, the figure is a department's, and the field does not make the list. The last beat is the board at Cutter's Fields at about four in the afternoon, with eleven settlements and eleven reasons, and **the year 461 in a column he has not read.**

## Batch Turn and Batch Payoff

- **Batch turn, Chapter 57:** the first challenge fails, correctly, on a fact, and it fails because Owen supplied an argument where the thing needed was an act. Everything after this is a consequence of learning it twice.
- **Batch payoff, Chapter 60:** the correction stands and cannot be argued off, the reason is on a public board because a clerk asked for it, and **the row against Lower Lock is a figure nobody in the volume can explain.** The practice is visible and is not stopped. The season continues with ten settlements.
- **Acquisition, Chapter 59:** Stage 2, Continuance Examiner, once, on the record, with three holds and three named claimants.

## Character and Power Changes

- **Owen Rusk** — Stage 1 with no hold → Stage 2 Continuance Examiner with three holds and three named claimants. He loses the crossing's hold, loses the five-line list as a duty, fails publicly once, and is given a thing he did not ask for by people who think they are helping.
- **Oress Kade** — arrives with the Docketford ledger and a quota of eleven, names Hark Holm unprompted, and does not stop Owen and does not help him. She is not softened. She is correct and she is not Owen's ally and she does not pretend to be sorry.
- **Nell Arbour** — working, unavailable, useful, and the person who says the volume's question out loud without noticing. She asks Owen for a skill and not for anything else, and she is owed a thread of her own (a claimant household from one of the eight) that the volume must not fold into his case.
- **Ada Rusk** — the ward cannot answer a count sheet and will not stretch its paper over a pitch it does not claim. Her sentence from Volume 01 is said out loud in a different building by a different man and she is not there and does not have to be.
