# Volume 02 Outline — Clean Count

Volume: 2
Chapter range: 51–100
Story year: Concord Year 488 (twelve years before Civic Zero), opening in the last days of the Concord Year 487
Power span: Stage 1 Sealed Witness to Stage 2 Continuance Examiner

## Central Pressure

Every year the **Provincial Count** is taken. The count is not a census of people. It is a count of **draws** — of premises of record that draw water, of works maintained, of households that can be witnessed at an address — and out of the count comes everything this river city has: the water, the maintenance fund, the right to be witnessed on a district act.

The **Census Guard** is the field service that reconciles the count. It comes up the river in the late season with a **quota** for the district, a **sealing stone** in a case, a **counting tent**, and a **schedule of settlements to be reconciled**, which is nailed up in public before anybody has looked at it. The method is not a secret and it is not new. A settlement is visited. The Guard asks whether the people there can answer for themselves. If they cannot, each person is entered under an **emergency suspension of fourteen days**, which is the law and is humane, and the days run. At the end of fourteen days a **count correction** is made by the Provincial Counting Office and attested by the Census Warden: *struck from the count of this district for this season, the premises in the schedule attached, and the households drawing in respect of them, and the water not drawn is returned to the district's main and is not reserved to the settlement.* At thirty days no supported living restoration is possible. The Guard knows the arithmetic exactly. It is written on the form. The form is correct.

And the reason column on the correction is a **figure**.

**Docketford district's schedule for the season names eleven settlements. Lower Lock is one of them.** The ward that spent seven weeks and a flood becoming legally responsible for itself is on the same list, in the same column, in the same season, as eight river settlements nobody in Docketford has ever sent a form to — and the reason against every one of them is a number.

Owen Rusk has a tray of correspondence that cannot be routed, a card with four lines on it he may not sign, a term, and a named duty for a household of two that does not close. He is thirty-two years into a life that has just been told, in a lane, in the rain, that the people who did the work were never the people he was carrying. He is also the only person in this building who can read a count sheet quickly enough to be useful, and this is the first season anybody has needed that.

## Starting State

Owen is a **Sealed Witness** whose certification **expired at the hour of the seal on the seventieth day of C.Y. 487**, leaving a crossing suspended, a repair unfunded, a pump hand-primed, a granary at a fortnight, and a list of five lines with nobody under it. He is posted to the tray of unfiled correspondence and the record of the district returns, which is a lateral move he did not ask for, and he is a stranger in his own building.

**Kade and the Census Guard have not been on stage anywhere in Volume 01.** They arrive in this volume with the Docketford ledger. The question waiting for them is Nell Arbour's, made in a public room at the end of Volume 01: **the order is a rule that picks a place by a column struck at eight in the morning, and the objection is not that it is unkind to Lower Lock, it is that a rule picks a place — and it goes to sixteen places and not one.**

## Starting Relationships

- **Owen and Oress Kade:** strangers who have read each other's files. He has heard of a warden who deletes. She has read a certification signed by a man who lives in the ward he certified. Neither has an opinion yet and both have a file.
- **Owen and Nell Arbour:** the route exists, so the condition that kept her unavailable is discharged, and she is **working again and not available to him.** She has her own claimants, one of them a settlement on the list. Owen goes to a river settlement because she needs somebody who can read a schedule, and that is a real reason and not the whole of it.
- **Owen and Ada Rusk:** sibling equality, hard-won, and the volume's standing contradiction. She told him the people who did the work were never the people he was carrying, and this is the season that sentence gets tested against.
- **Owen and Halden Pike:** a reluctant source. Pike is the first name in a register of contested closures, is alive and unpromoted, and is **not on Owen's side**, and knows something about what a district office does with a settlement it cannot account for.
- **Owen and Junia Pell:** collegial and level. She refused three things in Volume 01 and witnessed one. She is fourth on a nineteen-name district-exclusion roll and cannot get off it. **She is offered something in this volume and does not take it.**
- **Marguerite Bray and Lower Lock:** unchanged, and now on a list. The ward's paper says *Duty, and no status* and the count sheet wants premises.

## Starting Power Level

Stage 1, Sealed Witness, with **no active hold** — the certification ran out at half past three on the seventieth day of the month and the hold fell with it. One named duty that does not close. Marginalia is **not used in this volume**: no perception beat of any kind is staged, and nothing reports a filing's status to him. His advantage is that he is competent at reading a schedule and that people keep asking him to.

## Major Locations

- **Registry River Desk and the Fourth Desk** — the tray, the record of the district returns, the seal stair, the drawer under black cord he has stood in front of eight times.
- **Cutter's Fields** — a flat piece of Docketford ground behind the tanneries where the Census Guard pitches, and where the season's count and the schedule of settlements to be reconciled are **nailed to a public board**. It is the count's public face and the volume's town square.
- **The counting tent** — a canvas room with a table, a lamp, a sealing stone in a wooden case, and a season's work in it. Where Kade is most herself.
- **The Glass Stair** — the appeal hall. Count corrections are appealed here, by people, and the docket is public.
- **Lower Lock** — the ward hall with the delivery book, Fetter Lane, the weir path, the pump house, the granary, and a pitch at the bottom of a slope that has no premises on it.
- **Nether Sill** — eleven households on a silted bend two hours upriver, a sluice, a mill, and a count sheet from an earlier year that describes more premises than the place has.
- **Hark Holm** — a ferry hamlet, struck from the count in the Concord Year 484, four households, eleven days before the low bank came down. **The deletion worked.**
- **Pegler's Cut** — a drainage district with a good return and an inconvenient draw.
- **Slackwater** — twenty-two households, a chapel that closes, a water draw Docketford's ledger depends on. **The volume's centre and the volume's climax.**
- **Ossery** — a lime-kiln hamlet of twelve households where the count schedule is found to have been written before anybody looked.
- **Arbour Kitchen** — Nell's, and the room in which a claimant kitchen is a hearing room because the Registry will not provide one.

## Major Factions

- **The Registry of Continuant Works, Docketford district** — careful, correct, and responsible for the count's local face without having written its schedule. The volume does not make it a villain and does not make it clean.
- **The Provincial Counting Office** — remote, in Concord Hall, and the body that signs the correction. It keeps a **published schedule of settlements to be reconciled** and it keeps a **reason column**, and the reason is a figure.
- **The Census Guard and Warden Oress Kade** — disciplined, dangerous, and frequently the only people preventing a delayed collapse. Kade's belief is that a person cannot consent while their facts are dissolving, and the law she works inside is fourteen days and thirty days and she can recite both.
- **The Water Office** — Docketford's own, and the department the count exists to serve. **Named in this volume as a department and as a set of hands. It is on stage from Batch 0001 and it is not a villain, and the discovery about it is arithmetic.**
- **Arbour Kitchen's claimants** — people who came because the count came.
- **The Provisional Council** — offstage, as in Volume 01, present as a printed schedule and a quota set in the last week of C.Y. 487.
- **Still named and not on stage in this volume:** Verity Lound, Ansel Rive, Iven Sallow, Bettis Marr, Tovan Reed, the Claim Market, the Free Counters, the First Seal Subvault.

## The instruments, kept apart

None of these is a reopening, an exemption or a waiver, and none is any other one:

1. **The Count** — the annual count of draws, premises, and works maintained. Published.
2. **The schedule of settlements to be reconciled** — the public list. Published, nailed up, and **written in the last week of C.Y. 487 before anybody surveyed anything.**
3. **The emergency suspension of fourteen days** — person-level, per person, with a date and a care act named.
4. **The count correction** — the district-level instrument, made by the Provincial Counting Office, attested by the Warden, with a **schedule of premises attached** and a **reason**, and the reason is a figure.
5. **The four instruments of Volume 01**, which continue to exist, none of them merged with any of the above: the spring engineering notice; the C.Y. 486 transfer receipt; the Day 10 load-restriction suspension; **Stability Order 114**; plus **Redaction Batch 471/Q4/19** and **Precedent 37/C**. **Stability Order 114 is a local instrument on sixteen named returns and the Count is a regional act on draws, and the volume's central confusion is that a district officer and a census warden both think their list is the list.**

## Major Turn

A count correction, in the Concord Year 484, on **Hark Holm**, struck four households from the count eleven days before the low bank came down. With those four off the count and the ferry's work not counted as maintained, there were fewer premises to protect and the slip could be rebuilt by the people who lived on it. **The deletion worked and it saved lives, and Kade made it, and it is in her own hand, and one of the four is alive.** Owen cannot hold both that fact and the eleven on the board without becoming a different man, and the volume does not let him.

## Midpoint Reversal

The missing settlements are not all failing.

Some of them were struck from the count **to protect Docketford's water ledger** — that is, struck when their recorded draw was inconvenient and the district's balance was short, and the striking done by a schedule written in the last week of the Concord Year 487 by a department that had not surveyed anything because it did not need to: it already had their numbers. **The Census Guard reconciles what it is given. The schedule is not the Guard's.** Owen's first two challenges both failed against the Guard, correctly, and his third is against the schedule, and the schedule is a document, and a document can be examined.

## Escalation Sequence

1. **Pressure 1 — the term ends and the list comes off (Ch 51–53):** the certification expires at the hour of the seal, the crossing's status is suspended again, the appeal window closes with it, and the five lines go back to the ward's book with nobody under them. The tray delivers its first item that is about a place. **Lower Lock is on the published schedule of settlements to be reconciled**, and the count sheet wants premises of record, and the ward has a pitch under silt that is not premises and a pump whose grant is suspended.
2. **Pressure 2 — the schedule and the boat (Ch 54–56):** the schedule is nailed up in public, eleven settlements, a reason column of figures, and one of the figures is four streets from Owen's own bed. The Census Guard comes up the river with a quota and a stone. **Owen travels to Nether Sill** with Nell and gets the first thing he has wanted since Volume 01 ended: a hearing, in a room, with a form on the table.
3. **Pressure 3 — the first challenge fails (Ch 57–58):** Owen's objection is well made, correct in law, and answered by a fact, because eight of eleven households are living in two rooms of a mill and a room in a mill cannot be made premises of record in fourteen days. He has proved the form unreasonable and has not supplied the alternative, and the alternative existed, and the reason he did not find it is that he was arguing about the form. Ada Rusk's sentence, in a new building, to a different man. Then the year turns, and Kade tells him about Hark Holm in a tent at night.
4. **Pressure 4 — the qualification and the three people (Ch 59–60):** Owen passes a supervised case and a review record and becomes a **Continuance Examiner**, and the qualification brings **three active holds and three named claimants**, one of whom is already a household he holds a duty for. Nether Sill's count correction is signed, the eleven households are struck, the water goes back to the district main, and **the reason is published on a public board because Owen asked for it and nothing required it.** Visibility is not remedy, and this time a person other than Owen says the sentence out loud.
5. **Pressure 5 — the schedule and the ledger (Ch 61–80):** field hearings, river travel, local witness conflicts, **Nell's kitchen used as a hearing room**, the second settlement, the third, and the discovery at the volume midpoint that the schedule was written to protect a number. Owen's first attempt to challenge **the schedule** succeeds and succeeds too well, and the Office gets carefuler and not kinder.
6. **Pressure 6 — the price of being the examiner (Ch 81–90):** three holds, three claimants, and the fourth duty that has nowhere to sit. Ada's Lower Lock is reconciled in a season in which it is performing and cannot be counted, and the count's figure against it is the same class of figure as the one against Nether Sill. Pike becomes a source. Kade and Owen do a thing together neither of them wanted.
7. **Pressure 7 — Slackwater (Ch 91–100):** the climax. Twenty-two households, a chapel, a draw the city depends on, and a count correction with a date on it. **Owen and Kade jointly stabilize the settlement long enough for the households to choose their own return. They choose to remain, and not under the Registry's ownership, and not under the Guard's**, and Kade strikes her own schedule for that district and writes the reason in.

## Volume Climax

In the last weeks of the counting season, with a count correction dated and a sluice failing and the water in Slackwater already committed to Docketford's main in somebody's ledger, Owen Rusk and Warden Oress Kade do the thing neither of them is built for: they hold a settlement together — his with a hold that is a claim and not a duty, hers with the fourteen days and the thirty and a sealing stone — for long enough for twenty-two households to be asked what they want.

They choose to remain. They file a return of their own, narrow, with separately consenting adult branches and a withdrawal right, and it is recognized. And the second half of the choice is the half Kade did not expect: **they choose it without the Guard's schedule and without the Registry's ownership, and they say so in a room, and the room writes it down.** Kade does not argue. She strikes the district schedule for that season in her own hand and records the reason, which is not a figure, and it is the first time in the volume that a reason in a Warden instrument is not a number.

## Concrete Resolution

- **Kade's immediate operation is stopped for Docketford district.** Her schedule for the season is struck in her own hand with a stated reason, and she does not resign and is not disgraced, and the Guard's season ends eleven settlements short of its quota.
- **A local return is recognized at Slackwater**, made by its households, with a custody limit written into it that Owen's own return does not have.
- **The public learns that redaction is routine policy**, not an event, because the count schedule, the reason column, the quota, and the fourteen and thirty are all printed and were all printed before the season, and a public board at Cutter's Fields carries the settlements struck with the reason against each.
- **The count for the season is certified with a schedule of unresolved settlements printed in it.** That is a first in this city's record and it is not a remedy and it is not supposed to be one; it means the season's arithmetic can be audited by anybody who can read a column.
- **Owen becomes a Continuance Examiner** and holds three, and carries three named claimants, and the six protective returns in Lower Lock are **still** at three of five parts, and **no custodian has been bought.**
- **Nether Sill is not restored inside the volume.** The correction stands. The eleven households keep the water they have and there is a chapter where they are the argument of the whole book and they lose it.
- **Pike is a reluctant source and is not forgiven.** **Pell is offered something and does not take it.** **Ada's Lower Lock performs and is not counted, and says so.**
- **The brass tab stays shut.** The drawer is under black cord and is not opened in this volume, and the reason it matters is not a secret: the count schedules for this city are **published**, and there is a year **461** on a public board in a column Owen has deliberately not read, and a ward that was closed in 461 was struck from the count in the same way a river hamlet is, and the man who asked two women at a ward hall door to sign that they had seen him **still has no name.**

## New Question for the Next Volume

If redaction can be necessary, and somebody chose it, and the choosing was done by a form in an office in a city where the arithmetic is correct, then **who decides which communities deserve to survive** — and what is a person to do on a Tuesday morning who has found out that the decision was arithmetic?

Volume 3 inherits that from the Upland Commons, which will not send a return at all until the Registry accepts rotating household proof and internal appeal.

## Final Chapter Image

The counting tent comes down and goes up the river with the stone in its case, and the board stays up at Cutter's Fields with the season's count nailed to it, and beside it a second board, smaller, that was not there before, with the settlements struck from the count for the season and **the reason against every one of them**. Owen Rusk stands in front of the smaller board in the last week of the season and reads the column of reasons, and every reason is a figure, and there are eleven of them, and he counts them twice and gets eleven both times, and that is the entire job he has, and the volume is called after it.

## Continuity Guardrails for This Volume

- **The count is a count of draws, premises and works maintained. It is not a census of people and the volume never lets it become one.** Striking a settlement from the count does not kill anybody. It takes their water and their fund and their ability to be witnessed at an address.
- **The Census Guard is disciplined, dangerous, and often right.** Kade saves real places. The volume does not make her a villain, does not make her Owen's ally before Ch 90, and does not have her defeated by a fact about paperwork.
- **The method is the law of this world, honestly applied**: fourteen days of emergency suspension per person, thirty days before no supported living restoration is possible, and a count correction at the end of fourteen. Nobody in this volume invents a worse rule than the one the bible already contains.
- **The reason column is a figure.** The volume's discovery is *whose* figure and *of what*.
- **A strike is a form with a schedule of premises attached.** It is published, appealable at the Glass Stair, and argued by people.
- **The brass tab is inherited shut.** No chapter opens the drawer. The count schedules are published and that is a different door, and the man of Alder Row must not acquire a name.
- **The six protective returns stay at three of five parts.** No custodian is bought. The word is not written beside any of the four.
- **Cobb Rennie's pension stays unreachable.**
- **Keep Volume 01's six instruments apart from the four of this volume**, and none is a reopening of another.
- **Nothing in this volume funds the £4,160, restores the pump grant, pays the crew's wage, or opens the crossing to loads.**
- **The two lost names are not touched.** No perception beat, no symptom, no notice by anybody, no third name.
- **Marginalia is not used in this volume.** No panel, no notice, no silence, no status reported to Owen by a page.
- **All years are Concord years or a length of service.** The Concord Year 487 had ninety-one days in its last month and the year turned on a Tuesday, the first day of 488, which is the same weekday the Concord year began. Day 1 of C.Y. 487 was a Tuesday, so Days 71, 78, 85 and 91 are Tuesdays and Day 70 is a Monday.
- **No new final enemy.** Lound is offstage. Kade is Volume 02's opponent and is on the ladder at rung two.
