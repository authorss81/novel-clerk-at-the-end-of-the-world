## Volume 01 — The Unfiled Ward

### Owen Rusk, 32 — Copy Clerk, **assisting clerk to the second desk's supervised tray** (a posting, not a qualification), and still posted to the tray of unfiled correspondence

Wants: a person at the far end of the ward's appeal route, and the ability to stop being the only person carrying the case. Holds: an assisting clerk's card that forbids him to sign a continuation, a certification, a suspension or a hold; a private notebook that leaves the building and that he has now named *a register*; a counterfoil proving he is a line in a request register; a cut on his right middle finger that has not healed. Stage 0, and the batch says so by never saying otherwise. **He has had no feeling about his own instrument working, and has understood that the condition he is now in is the ordinary condition of every other clerk in the building.** He has learned, in order: that a filing is a set of checks and not a signature; that the effect field is a menu and a careless clerk is the most powerful person in the room; that a document can be *received* without being *examined* and that the difference is free; that an answer which grants nothing is appealable and an answer which grants a date nobody can compute is not; that a copy of a county intake register in a private book turns the book into a thing a man may be searched for; that his mother's handling of a registry was an error of receiving, not an error of lying; and that careful and right are not the same and never have been. He has begun to do without Nell without noticing he is doing it. He has **not** been paid for the ward's hours. He is a man in the institution, and has stopped pretending that is a temporary condition.

**Batch 0004 cost him something he can put down, which is worse in one way and better in another.** He is now **a standing item** in the Glass Stair waiting hall: people look at the door on the way in, and from Day 47 they do it *expecting* something, and **a room that expects something from you has stopped telling you what it thinks and started telling you what it would like.** He has lost **the ability to be surprised in a room** and he knows the price of it because Pell named it in a wet coat on a Saturday. He has made **an enemy who is not an enemy yet** — a name in a **schedule of duties** on the board by the second counter, against a duty, whom he has deliberately not looked up, and who will write **better** forms because she is frightened. He has also learned, in order: that being right in a room and being useful in a room are **different products** and the second one requires somebody else to be holding the paper; that a day nobody can compute is a wall and a day a clerk chooses can be **wrong**, and the wall was the thing that stopped the date from being anybody's fault; that an office which is watched **performs better**, and better is not the same as fair; and that **visibility is not remedy**. He is a **coverer** of documents, a **witness** to things he did not draft, and a man who has been **entered on the hearing matter as a co-petitioner and an assisting clerk**, in one line, in a form, free, because the clerk told him that describing yourself is free and nearly nobody in the building has ever done it. **He is no longer able to be useful in the way he has been useful**, and on the night the date arrives he finds that having the thing he wanted for a month is **boring**, and the only thing in him that answers it is the old fear, and he would not trade the boring for the old fear.

**Batch 0003 cost him a name, permanently.** The string **VANE, H.**, the receiving officer in the third column of a covering slip, arrived about four hundred times a quarter for nine years. It failed four times in three days in the fourth week of the rain season and it does not come back. It is not a symptom, not a warning, not paid for, and he has told nobody and is not going to. The finding was worse than the loss: the quarterly works notice for 4 Lantern Walk has come back twice, *not known at the address*, and there is **no first name for the man in any register in Docketford**, because the city has written to three letters and a surname for nine years.

**What he has gained instead:** the number of his mother's redaction batch, the officer's initials, the date struck, and three of the other forty entry numbers he handled inside nine years off route lists — two quarterly roll notices and one form 12-B, **which is not one of the forty-one and is not about a residence status**, and which he says so out loud in order not to let the second thing get at the first. Precedent 37/C and the Office's refusal column, one hundred and nine entries with forty-one identical dispositions. The countersignature review standard, its recitals, and the roll it runs on. **And a habit he has now named twice: writing things down so that on a bad day in three months he can point at the entry and say that he did it.** He caught himself doing it in Chapter 29 and left the four lines blank.

**And one name, written once.** *Marta Rusk*, at the bottom of a page of his own register, on the night of Day 39, in his own hand, for the first time in his life. Nothing under it. Nothing restored. It is the only sentence in the volume that is not a fact about the institution. **Batch 0004 did not touch it, and must not until Chapters 46–50.**

**The Alder Row filing.** He has it in his register as **a filing and not a mystery**, underlined once, with a second line under it marked as speculation: *it is not in Docketford and it is not on a table and I have told nobody in that building that I have read a line about it.* He stood in front of the drawer for a minute and a quarter on the Thursday night and went back upstairs. **He has the line, and the drawer is shut, and the two facts are unrelated and he has told himself so in writing.**

### Nell Arbour, 35 — Claimant Advocate, Arbour Kitchen

**Batch 0004: she refused to go and ask anybody, and gave him the reason entire.** Owen asked her to walk up the Coal Hill landing herself and she said no in four seconds, having had it ready since the fourth of the month. The reason is the volume's best piece of relationship writing and it is about the two tide-table women: **the woman at Coal Hill and the woman at Wexford Landing have not spoken in two years, over a tide table, and she has been in the middle of that, and neither of them has ever blamed her out loud** — and if she goes up that landing on a Registry clerk's errand she has made the two of them into a thing that happened between her and them, *in order that a man who lives in Lower Lock can get a Wednesday*, and that is the one thing she has not done in nine years. She also caught him framing the errand as a favour to her, and told him what that is: **a description of her made in order to use her.** She sent him away with the restated question: *go and ask her yourself, and do not tell her I sent you, because I have not sent you, and if you tell her that I have sent you then in about a year it will be true.*

**She stated her condition as a standing and not as a comfort**, and this is the batch's relationship turn. She has said the four sentences three times and always as a comfort: **a comfort is a thing a person does for you, done in a kitchen, between two people, and it can be withdrawn on a Thursday by a woman who is busy. A standing is a thing a person does in a room where you are not the subject; it is done in front of strangers and it cannot be withdrawn, because withdrawing it does not undo it, it only stops the next one, and everybody in the room has already seen it.** She has been that since Stonecross six years ago, and she told him to notice that a woman on a landing has been giving him the second thing to a stranger in a doorway in a yard ever since and that he did not know which one he was asking for. **Her condition is unchanged: the appeal route and nothing else. She is not a witness, not an advocate for the ward, and not his, and the far end is now filled without her.**

**On stage twice in Batch 0003, and she gave nothing.** Owen asked her for one of the four names and she refused and told him what asking cost: *you have come to spend the only thing I have got, which is that when I send somebody, they are mine.* She repeated her condition unchanged — **the route and nothing else** — and told him she had noticed that he did not argue with it, which was new. She introduced nobody, vouched for nobody, and left the room before the answer.

She was also in a Glass Stair corridor in a capacity of her own and said nine words: in Concord Year 481, at Stonecross, she put a form on a counter and a woman wrote a number on it and gave it back, and she has never been told what the number was for. That is her own grievance, it is on the docket, and she is not using it for anybody. She does not know the register, the continuity statement, the intake number, the redaction batch, or Alder Row. The Chapter 4 charge is open and unresampled. **She has still refused to be his witness, his advocate for the ward, or his relief, and those refusals stand.**

### Ada Rusk, 24 — Bridge Mechanic and crew leader, Lower Lock

Wants: her ward to hold an instrument she did not have to be rescued into, and to be asked what she wants rather than what she is owed. She is **one of the four who voted no** and is **not one of the eight branches**; she is inside the return's scope through the crew's acts and has stated out loud, without apology, that she voted against the transfer and is inside a return that performs the duty. In this batch she: told the whole ward about the order at eight in the morning before Owen could, and said in front of thirty people how he came to be telling her; **dictated the four-clause amendment**, the first document in the case she has written entirely herself; **filled in the box Owen could not fill** — the class of persons who may object, narrowed to the eight branches and any person the office names in writing, which excludes her and the other three for ever, at the price of their having said no in a room with witnesses; said nothing at all while Nessa Kray withdrew, which was the hardest thing in the room; and told Owen that a reviewing officer had handed the ward something it asked for and told the truth about where he got it, which she called the second true thing he had said to her. She calls him *copyist*. She has a crew of nine, a repair at four thousand one hundred and sixty that nobody is funding, and a Thursday and a Friday marker at seven forty in the morning.

**Batch 0004 is the batch she is right in four times and he is wrong in three.** She **refused** to let the ward hold a second sheet on a public table in Docketford and gave him the month's real work instead — a paper of the ward's own, in the ward's own words, with her name at the bottom, which he is to carry up the stair and not write a word of; and the reason is not pride: *the day that document is on a table in Docketford it is a ward document in a room where nobody in the room has ever carried water.* She **filed her own objection to her own ward's case**, in her own name, and has made herself unwithdrawable on purpose, because an objection made by a ward is an objection the ward may withdraw and hers cannot be taken off her by anybody in the ward — and she gave the real reason: she is the only person alive who has had both hands on the crack in the abutment at four in the morning, and if she says that in a room then in six weeks the Office has a witness who vouches the condition of a work, and the return becomes a return about a bridge and the roster becomes a piece of paper about hours. She **offered the four dissenters a separate branch** in a hall of twenty-two, in her own words, explained that it is not a pardon, and named the mechanism: **a branch is added to a return by a district office naming its holder in writing** — the one door, and it opens both ways, and the class of persons who may object is **eight plus a written naming, never nine**. She answered *who pays for it* with **nobody** and refused to pretend otherwise, and when Owen called a separate branch a reinstatement she let Wenna Sarr correct him in one sentence and did not defend him. She **wrote four blank pages** with the four names on them and will not chase anybody, and set the deadline at the day of the hearing. And she **stopped the ward putting the crossing into its own papers**, in front of twenty-two people, and gave the reason that is the volume's hinge: *the roster is the only paper any of us has ever produced that nobody can call an error, because every line in it is an hour somebody was there, and a roster is not a claim, a roster is a fact — and the moment we put the crossing in, it becomes a claim, and a claim is what the transfer was.* She signed the ward's request to Coal Hill and put it in the ward's bag from the hall door herself, **because if it goes in a coat it is a request from the Registry whatever the paper says**, and she would not tell Owen whether she had met the woman or written to her, and that was the correct arrangement.

**Batch 0003 is the batch she wins.** She read the precedent before Owen did, worked out in a morning that the ward satisfies three of its four grounds and cannot satisfy the fourth, and said the frightening part out loud in a room: **performance in public is not a duty, it is a shift**, so it is a roster, and a roster is a document, and a document can be read by somebody who was not there, and the hours on it are **everybody's** and not the crew's, and that is the most frightening thing anybody said in the batch and she is right. She negotiated **five terms** in her own hand and had them ruled into the ward's delivery book, and the ward agreed to be checked in public on a calendar by a stranger from another district for as long as the thing runs, on **Marguerite Bray's condition: *and the wage*.** She stopped Owen in front of twenty-two people for trying to slow it down — *a man who keeps a thing alive by holding on to it has no business telling a ward what it may perform* — and he did not defend himself. She sent him away to find a stranger, made him do it badly first, and told him she did not trust it when he had been useful for three days running. She named the day herself, on the ward's own paper, in her own name. **Her terms survive; Owen's do not.**

### Junia Pell, 22 — Junior Copy Clerk, second desk, supervising the assisted tray, and the return's second observer

Wants: to be competent without becoming a custodian of other people's lives, and she has now done six public things that are not that. She sustained a challenge to **her own independence** against herself, in her own hand, on Day 22, before Owen and before Voss, and stayed on the instrument, and asked the reviewing officer to hold her separability open — converting the ward's one weakness into the thing that makes it renewable for review rather than binding. Her separation of interest is on the file twice, in two hands: four sentences in a margin on Day 7, and a page against herself on Day 22 that is longer than the first. She wrote down, at Owen's request, that a stability order can name a district that entered a return later in the quarter, and told him plainly that writing it made her a participant, which is what he had done to her on Day 7. She read the district works inspection roll in public at Owen's request and gave him four lines and no commentary. She refused to name a person for the far end of the appeal route. She told him to his face that she knows what is in his notebook, that she has never told anybody, and that there is no reason in the world for her to be standing near him except that she knows. **She is the only living witness to the destruction of the continuity statement and has not called it a debt and has not used it.** She owes him nothing and has said so kindly.

**Batch 0004: she stopped him twice, in four minutes each, using his own best reasoning.** She killed the second point of law before he could file it — *you have one good answer in a file and you were about to make it look like a campaign, and the difference between an office that is asked a hard question once and an office that is asked the same question twice by the same man is about you and not about them* — and she told him to go and be in the room instead. She named the person the comparison will cost: **a schedule of duties, on the board by the second counter, a name against a duty, and a name in a schedule of duties is not a person in a case.** It is a person who will come in on Monday at the same hour she always does, has never had anybody ask her a question, and **will be frightened, in about the way a good craftsman is frightened of a machine that does the same work better, and frightened people in this building write better forms.** Owen has not looked up the name and must not be written as having done so.

She also identified the answer as **a form**: the same five clauses in the same order as the one that came down on the counter on Day 40, with a sixth clause added by hand and a different signature — and *the reason it is going to be the answer is not that it is good, it is **sufficient**, and sufficient is why it will never be improved and never be withdrawn.* She told him he had made himself a standing item worth a great deal and that he would not be able to put it down, and that **he has lost the ability to be surprised in a room**, and that she was warning him about it in a wet coat on a Saturday and was not going to say she was sorry, because she told him on the sixth day of the last month what he was doing and he said the two sentences out loud. **She also observed that the office posted its answer in a public hall with a queue in it, which it had not been warned about, and that this was because the circular of the Wednesday named a review in this district, and that an office that has been named in a document improves its manners — and that this is why the answer is a form and not a man, and the form will get shorter and cheaper.**

**The office asked her for her separation of interest in writing, with a day on it, in advance of the Day 46 renewal** — the first time anything in that building has done it to her in three years and four months. She gave it. **Nobody may connect that to the continuity statement and nobody may thank her for anything.**

### Halden Pike, 47 — Deputy Registrar, Docketford

Wants: Docketford solvent and a decision actionable by Friday. In this batch he built **Stability Order 114** in a corridor, in about a minute, without concealment: a stability order closes returns on *lateness* rather than *failure*, and the argument that carries it is that the district cannot fund sixteen of them. He told Owen the schedule was struck on the morning of the twelfth and that no document will move a line on it, and he meant it. In the corridor on the twenty-third he would say only that he would sign the order **that week**; the Thursday-at-ten-past-four rule and the Friday Council counter are Owen's own discovery the next morning, and **Pike must not be given the day or the hour in advance again.** He signed at ten past four on the twenty-fourth, and the instrument is filed for the Council's counter and is **public and appealable to the Glass Stair**. He told Owen in advance that the ward must be visible when it gets signed. He has not been written as a liar and must not be. He surfaced the real reason for the first Lower Lock withdrawal on Day 42, in a runner's recorded answer, and **Batch 0004's centre is that it is true.**

**Batch 0003 put his initials in a document.** **H.L.P.**, the officer who struck **Redaction Batch 471/Q4/19** — forty-one residence statuses, one sitting, the fourth quarter of Concord Year 471, on the ground *flood quarter arrears*. Owen has not gone to tell him and has not asked him and has not written his name next to the number in the register. **The batch's own respect for the Office is intact: it is a backlog cleared in an afternoon by a careful man, not a policy, and the same man signed Stability Order 114 in a corridor in a minute and refused to sign anything he meant to bury.** He is a Chapters 31–40 debt, and now there is a second document with his hand on it.

**Batch 0004 gave him the batch's real justification and did not make him a liar.** He saw Owen's manoeuvre coming and said so before a word of it: *a man who can be put in a hall and made to answer has not been caught, he has been recorded, and recording is worse.* He gave the reason for answering anyway: a memorandum is a thing he writes alone, in his own words, with no question on it and no date on it, and in five years somebody will read it as the Deputy Registrar's account of a conversation nobody saw. He said *do not be early* — if asked at a quarter to, the runner has not got his book open and he will stand there and say it was taken in a corridor.

He then put the whole of the flood year on the sheet, in a hall of forty people, at ordinary volume. A district engineer wrote a finding on the **fourth** of the second month of the Concord Year 474 that four hundred yards of the bank at the bottom of Fetter Lane would go that year; **Pike signed the withdrawal of this district's return on the ninth**; **one hundred and eighty-one persons were moved off it**, the water was in it on the **thirteenth**, and the ground was clear. **Twenty-two people were drowned on the seventh**, in Mill Ward and on the Havelock bank, on ground that was not cleared, in districts that had come into the office with nothing for two quarters and that could not compel a man to move a chair, *because a district office cannot compel anybody of anything, that is the entire difference between a district office and a river.*

Why it is not in the instrument: **because the instrument is about a place.** A duty is somebody's own act; a withdrawal of a place is a statement about where a thing is and who is standing on it. The twenty-two are a fact about a river and *this Office does not keep a column of facts about rivers*, and he has read that sentence back to himself many times and never made it sound like what it is. **He instructed the runner to write both halves — that he was asked and answered, that he did not lie, that he would sign it again, and the hundred and eighty-one and the twenty-two not in one place and not the other, *because a sheet with the hundred and eighty-one on one line and the twenty-two on another is how a document becomes two documents*.** He lost the corridor and knows exactly what it was worth. **He is alive, is not a liar, and must survive the volume: he loses control of the file, must answer on the record, and comes out unpromoted.**

### Marguerite Bray, 61 — Ward Steward of Lower Lock

Keeps the ward's roll and, in its back half, a **delivery book with a second column for paper that comes back**, which she has **ruled by her own hand for thirty-six years, since she was twenty-five**; the book itself has been in the Bray family for fifty-one years, and she is sixty-one. That book is the only reason Chapter 20 could happen. She read the answer, came to the Glass Stair in the rain without asking, asked for the receipt clause to be read into plain words, and refused to thank anybody. She told the ward about the transfer's real origin out of her own book and her own pencil, and her reading is the one that matters: *the office did not answer him and did not refuse him, it received a document.* She asked Owen what the refusals were worth and wrote nothing down when he answered. She is not his ally, has never thanked him, and now holds the answer to a case he had to give away. She wants the ward to exist without the Office and does not believe he is the person who will get it there.

**Batch 0004 made her the petitioner.** She **filed the application for a hearing herself**, in her own name and in her own words, having gone to the Mill Ward annex and got a form nobody had told her she was permitted to have. She wrote it standing up, read it into a hall of twenty-six people, and let anybody stop it going; **nobody stood up and Ada said nothing at all**, which was the most eloquent thing in the room. She asked for **a week and not a period**, and the Glass Stair clerk stopped writing because nobody in that building had ever written a week in that box. She carried it to the counter in the rain with no umbrella, and Owen stood at the far end of the hall and made himself stay there, which he understood afterwards was the hardest thing he did all week. **The hardest sentence she has ever said to him is *do not come on the day*** — not at the back, not in the corridor, not in the queue at nine so she can see he did it — *because a room with you in it is a room that is looking at a Registry clerk instead of a steward, and if they look at you they will hear an office.* He understood in four seconds that it was **not an insult, and that that was why she had said it.**

She **asked the first of her seven** in the hall on the Tuesday of Day 50, of a woman of about fifty-two who asked whether a person could be put back on a roll if a whole street asked them, was told *no district office competent*, did not go again, and had never told anybody for nineteen years — and **recorded the number and not the answer, because an answer is not a fact about a ward and a number is.** Six remain.

And she **found the Alder Row line herself**, in her own inward column, matching household references out of the Fetter Bend register against a hand roll she has kept for thirty-six years, and found that the seventh is **a place and not a house** — the only time that column has said so in nine years. She turned the book round in a hall of thirty and let the room read it, including the hand that is not hers, and then ruled the whole of it into the book in her own hand with the day and the hour: *the ward was correct and the return was received for intake and has not been examined.* She added, and said out loud as she wrote it, that **she did not write his name and did not ask him his name and he did not offer it, and if he came to that door tomorrow she would not know him from the man who brings her coal.** She also noted on Day 39 that *nobody has asked me this and I am writing it down because if nobody asks me I will forget it in a month*, and wrote *and then it will be a fortnight* under the granary figure on Day 45.

**Batch 0003 gave her the volume's thesis.** She attached **the condition** to Ada's five terms without being asked and read it into the book in front of the room that was agreeing to be read: *we will be checked in public, on a calendar, by a person from another district, for as long as the thing runs, **we will do it if the wage comes out of it*** — and then, on the same page, in a different pencil, *Condition of M. Bray. Wage not funded.* She refused to argue with Ada about it. **That minute, in that book, with her name at the foot of it, is the ward's written request to Ivet Sterne** — the outsider's third condition is that a ward may ask her and the Registry may not, and this is the ward asking, on the ward's paper, a day before anyone had named a day. On the night of Day 30 she read the Coldwater schedule aloud in the granary at the double count because a man from the mill asked what the paper was. And on Day 37 she took a sheet of nine sub-table refusals that Owen had no standing to hold, put a name to household references he could not, and **ruled in the delivery book that she will ask the seven that are hers, one at a time, in the hall, to anybody who chooses to come.** **She has refused to ask about the house at 4 Lantern Walk and said so, and it is not Owen's business either.** She also wrote the granary figure into a pump book for nobody, on Day 28, with *and then it will be two weeks* under it — and on Day 38 it was two weeks and a bit.

### Cobb Rennie, 58 — Lower Lock

Nineteen years at the same two rooms and off the Registry roll for two of them. Outside the thirteen households and outside the thirteen erased entries; nothing in this case reaches him and nothing is going to. Still cannot draw a pension. He walks a twelve-year-old home every night in the dark. Ivy Sarr has independently concluded that he is the most independent person available because nobody can say he wants anything. **That is not a cure for his pension and must never be written as one.**

**Batch 0004 did not move his pension and must not be written as if it had.** He is on the standpipe rota; the rota is on the roster; the roster is now four weeks long and is attached to a petition on a public file where **any person at any counter may read it**, and in Chapter 40 he is named among the people who will be in the room on the fifty-ninth: *a man of fifty-eight who is not on any roll in this city.* **That is the whole of what the volume has done for him and it is not a pension.** Naming him again as an independent possible witness for Ivy Sarr is **not a cure** and must never be written as one. **Batch 0003 put his hours on a public document and it changed nothing about his pension.** He is on the standpipe rota, the rota is on the roster, the roster may be read by any person from outside the ward without notice, and an outside observer read his name out of it on Day 38 and wrote down who was *not* there. **A roster records hours and does not record service, and no hour that Cobb Rennie stands on a standpipe in the dark goes anywhere near his pension, and this must never be written the other way round.** There is also a quarterly works notice addressed to three letters and a surname at a house in Fetter Lane that has come back twice, and **Bray says it is not hers and Owen says it is not his, and it is on the top of the stack.**

### Ivy Sarr, 12 — Lower Lock, lives with her aunt Wenna Sarr

First line of the no-local-record schedule, one of the six who cannot answer, and now the only person in the volume whose face Owen knows. She cannot read the notices and has been carrying her aunt's to the mill's factor on Tuesdays for a fortnight. She counts seed trays, keeps seven against an official nine, and is right. She named five care acts performed this week in her own words and concluded, unprompted, that the man no record places anywhere is the best one to ask. Owen met her once, on a step in a lane, and got four minutes of the best kind he has had in a month. She needs a protective return; the ward has three of five parts and two are nobody's to pay for. Unresolved at the end of the batch and unresolved at the end of the volume.

**Batch 0004: her name is now on a document going to a Council in Concord Hall.** She is on the standpipe rota, the rota is on the roster, the roster is in the ward's schedule of performed acts, and the schedule was filed on Day 50 and is readable at any counter. ## The two lost names — UNCHANGED AND ABSOLUTE

**Neither name occurs anywhere in Chapters 51–60, and nothing in this batch is near them.** No perception beat, no symptom, no silence, no notice, no third name, and no other character senses anything. **Chapter 59 is about what it costs to hold things and it must not come near it, and it did not.** The prohibition stands: no later chapter may restore, resolve, repeat, confess, price, write down or notice either, and no character may witness, remark on, or recover either.
