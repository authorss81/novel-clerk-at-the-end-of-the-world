# Volume 01 Outline — The Unfiled Ward

Volume: 1
Chapter range: 1–50
Story year: Concord Year 487 (thirteen years before Civic Zero)
Power span: Stage 0 Copy Clerk to Stage 1 Sealed Witness

## Central Pressure

Docketford's weekly continuance roll omits Lower Lock while the Ninth Weir crossing remains structurally unsafe. The ward is not late and it is not simply forgotten: a *transfer* entry moves the ward's maintenance duties — the crossing, the Fetter Lane pump, the granary — into a Mill Ward district return that has no crew, no funds, and no way to reach the weir. The entry carries Registry witnesses and no Lower Lock witness. If Lower Lock files nothing, its crossing status lapses with its return and the ward begins to lose its own name, its pump grant, and its households' standing. If the ward files for itself, it becomes legally responsible for a structure nobody will fund.

Owen Rusk has two working weeks before the transfer's review and no authority to stop any of it. The review itself is not a scene in Batch 0001: it happens off-page inside that fortnight, it confirms the transfer, and Owen is not on the notice for it.

## Starting State

Owen is a senior copy clerk at the Registry's River Desk, thirty-two, eleven weeks into a clean quarterly run. He transcribes, compares, notices discrepancies, and performs ordinary witness acts. He cannot change a status. His name has never appeared on a filing that mattered. He lives in the ward he is about to fight for, which he has never once considered a professional liability.

## Starting Relationships

- **Owen and Halden Pike:** supervisor and deputy registrar. Mutual professional respect, no intimacy. Pike is not a liar; he is a man who believes a decision must be actionable by Friday.
- **Owen and Ada Rusk:** protective inequality. He has protected her since their mother died; she has been a bridge crew leader since she was nineteen and has told him so.
- **Owen and Nell Arbour:** former Registry colleagues who have not spoken in two years and last met badly. She is a claimant advocate now, running Arbour Kitchen in Mill Ward, which is the district that received the transfer.
- **Owen and Junia Pell:** new junior clerk, twenty-two, competent, quick, and openly unimpressed by seniority. She is the first person inside the Office who asks who consented.
- **Owen and Lower Lock:** residents first, colleagues never. He does not yet know enough of their claims to represent them, and does not yet know that he must not.

## Starting Power Level

Stage 0, Copy Clerk. Any correction requires a qualified signer and a local act. Owen's marginalia perception is limited to thin or contradictory files he is already handling; he cannot read a held or closed record at all. He has no holds, no duties, and no case of his own.

## Major Locations

- **Registry River Desk** — the long copying hall, numbered desks, the seal stair, the drawer of unfiled correspondence.
- **Glass Stair** — the Registry's public appeal hall, the only place a ward can be answered in public.
- **Lower Lock** — flood-prone ward of roughly 240 residents: repair sheds, a granary, a seed stall, a hall with a table, and homes built on marginal land with weak historical rolls.
- **The Ninth Weir** — flood-damaged public crossing, load-restricted, with a cracked abutment and no funded repair.
- **Fetter Lane pump** — the ward's water, and the reason a majority of its households want the consolidation grant.
- **Arbour Kitchen, Mill Ward** — Nell's claimant kitchen, and the district office that received the transfer.
- **Mill Ward annex** — the receiving district return, administered from a room with a counter and a queue.
- **Concord Hall** — offstage, present only as a name on a circular.

## Major Factions

- **The Registry of Continuant Works** — mixed. Careful clerks, exhausted clerks, and a review standard that rewards decisions that are defensible in writing.
- **Docketford's Deputy Registrar's office** — Pike's. Solvency-driven, procedurally immaculate, and responsible for a ward the moment it stops being affordable.
- **Lower Lock's ward hall** — Marguerite Bray's paper roll, Ada's bridge crew, a granary committee, a pump committee, and four households who voted against everything the ward did this season.
- **Arbour Kitchen's claimants** — people who came to Nell because the Office did not come to them.
- **The Provisional Council** — offstage pressure, arriving as a countersignature standard and a review deadline.
- **Named but not on stage in this volume:** the Census Guard and Warden Oress Kade (distant provincial operations only), the claim brokers of the Market, Ansel Rive (one countersignature), Verity Lound (two circulars, Ch 10 and Ch 33–35, never in person).

## Midpoint Reversal

The omission is not an error. Eleven months ago a Lower Lock resident named **Tal Sarr** signed and had witnessed a receipt moving the ward's crossing maintenance into the Mill Ward return. His authority covered his own household and nothing else. He drowned in the spring flood. The entry is a supported fact: real signature, real witnesses, real seals, validly made, and then used far beyond what the signer was entitled to give. The only correctable thing in eleven months of paperwork is the annex schedule's two-day backdating — and Owen surrenders that, because the moment the Office can correct a date, it closes the file as a clerical matter and keeps the scope.

So Owen's entire approach — prove the record is wrong — dies on the record itself, and dies by his own hand. The case reclassifies from **error** to **scope**, which the Office has no incentive to fix and cannot fix cheaply. And the only route that remains is the one the Registry has spent ninety years treating as nonbinding: **a district return performed by Lower Lock itself**, where the ward claims a *duty* it can actually perform, witnessed by people it does not control, reviewed every seven days, appealable, and narrow enough that a single household can withdraw from it without collapsing the rest.

The ward can only be saved by becoming legally responsible for itself.

## Escalation Sequence

1. **Pressure 1 — procedure (Ch 1–5):** Owen discovers a valid transfer receipt whose annex schedule is dated two days before it could lawfully take effect, walks his own ward, learns what "no local record" does to a living man, files a continuance inquiry in his own name, is corrected by Pell and by Nell, and finds the dead signer. The receipt itself is sound, so the case changes category from error to scope — and Owen deliberately gives up the one defect he can prove, because a correctable error is the Office's preferred way to close a file. Tool acquired: the district return's terms.
2. **Pressure 2 — political and material (Ch 6–10):** Owen drafts the ward's own ratification notice, and it is accurate, and it destroys the ward. The transfer is ratified nine to four. The crossing's status begins to lapse. The Council's circular closes the local-return route. Owen is suspended from the desk and his name enters a public file against the Office's interest and his own.
3. **Pressure 3 — training and bodies (Ch 11–20):** the rain season peaks. The seven-day answer clock opened in Chapter 9 lands in Ch 12–13 and gives a date instead of a hearing, which is the first thing the clock buys and the first thing it costs. Pump-priming by hand, granary floors, a night inspection at the weir, a young apprentice in the spillway, a narrowing return that survives its first withdrawal, and Owen's first qualified filing as an assisting clerk. In Ch 17–18 Pike signs **Stability Order 114** on the eleven failing returns he named in Chapter 3 — a separate instrument from the Chapter 8 load-restriction suspension. Cost: physical injury, unpaid hours, and the discovery that a *closed* file answers him with silence, which is worse than a thin one.
4. **Pressure 4 — the past and the dead (Ch 21–30):** the 481 withdrawal. Owen traces his mother's residence status into the same redacted batch, finds the same delay that cost her priority medicine was repeated for dozens of other people, and discovers that Lower Lock filed a valid return once and was paid for it. **Volume midpoint (≈Ch 25):** the Registry's own precedent requires it to honor a renewed district return, if the ward can show current performance. The ward is therefore required to perform in public what it has just been told it cannot afford. Owen also finds a thread running above Pike — the countersignature standard of Chapter 3 turns out to have been *issued*, not requested, and the Council's own reasons surface.
5. **Pressure 5 — public argument (Ch 31–40):** the Glass Stair hearing. A second circular from Concord Hall arrives in Ch 33–35 and names the *review* rather than the hearing, which is how the Office learns the Council is watching the appeal. Pike's real justification surfaces: the withdrawal was signed to move people off marginal land before a flood that killed elsewhere, and the cost was hidden in a favourable reading. Ada negotiates her ward's own terms instead of accepting Owen's plan. Nell refuses to witness, and the four dissenters are offered a separate branch instead of a pardon. The hearing is set at the end of the rain season, and the ward must show performance, not intent.
6. **Pressure 6 — the price of being needed (Ch 41–45):** flood night. The return is prepared, narrowed, and reviewed in public. Owen is asked to accept a named duty for a single household and discovers the cost of a hold: unresolved cases enter as exact sensory detail and forgotten names, and one of the forgotten names is a man he has copied for six years.
7. **Pressure 7 — climax and resolution (Ch 46–50):** the certification, the crossing hold, the forced public appeal, the amended record, and the volume's closing image.

## Volume Climax

In the last week of the rain season, with the crossing under load restriction and the ward's return on the docket, Owen certifies **a narrow Lower Lock district return** and, inside it, **a temporary preservation hold on the crossing's last supported legal status**. The hold does one thing: it keeps the crossing's legal status and its appeal window alive while repairs proceed. It does not make the Ninth Weir safe, funded, repaired, or usable; the crossing stays closed to loads, and Ada's crew — unpaid, exposed, and committed — becomes the crew that must make the claim true. The Office is forced to answer on the record at the Glass Stair with the ward's own witnesses standing behind the ward's own acts, and **Pike's Stability Order 114** — a separate instrument from the Chapter 8 load-restriction suspension, and the one Docketford can no longer file quietly — is carried into public appeal.

Owen wins the argument and loses the fantasy that winning the argument is the work.

## Concrete Resolution

- **Lower Lock remains inhabited.** The ward holds a lawful, narrow, seven-day-review district return with separately consenting adult branches, a named appeal route, and an explicit withdrawal right. It is not protected, funded, or safe. Its people did the work.
- **Ada Rusk leads her own ward's filing.** She negotiated the terms, kept the dissenters on the record inside the return, and refused Owen's plan to have the Registry absorb the maintenance debt. She is a bridge crew leader who also holds a ward's paper, not a rescued ward.
- **Pike loses control of the file.** The order is on public appeal, he must answer on the record, and he survives — alive, unpromoted, and required to sign the contested closures he once approved for the rest of his career.
- **Owen obtains an amended record for his mother.** Marta Rusk's residence status is corrected, with the delay recorded as a delay, eight months of disputed burial included as fact, and no claim that the correction returns the medicine, the time, or the life. The amendment names the Office that delayed and the batch it was in.
- **Owen becomes a Sealed Witness** by completing a qualified filing, accepting a named duty, and learning on the page what the entry cannot support. One active hold. He begins to lose names.
- **Nell Arbour reenters his life as an adversary who recognizes his work.** She is not his witness, not his advocate, and not available. She helps because the case is right, and she says so to his face.

## New Question for the Next Volume

The brass tab: why does the Registry's sealed archive drawer carry a return from **Alder Row**, a Docketford ward officially closed in Concord Year 461, four streets from Owen's own bed, and why does its file number belong to a series that the Registry's own index says was never opened?

Volume 2 inherits a harder question from the same door: if redaction can be necessary, and someone chose it, who decides which communities deserve to survive?

## Final Chapter Image

Owen at the River Desk, restored to a desk he no longer wants, reading a district return filed under a number that is not his name: **District Return 487-LL**, one page, four words of scope, twenty-one living witnesses, and not one of them him. The crossing is still closed. The pump still runs by hand on the worst nights. In the drawer beside his, under black cord he is not permitted to touch, a brass tab reads **ALDER ROW — 461**, and the index card behind it says *never opened*.

## Power and Cost Ledger

- **Opening:** Stage 0. No authority, no holds, no cases of his own.
- **Batch 0001:** no stage change. Owen gains a private ledger outside the Office book, a continuance inquiry in his own name, and the terms of a district return. He gains a signed statement against his own drafting. He does **not** complete a qualified filing; that belongs to Batch 0002. He loses his posting and his standing in his own ward. A name will not come in Chapter 10 and comes back — a near miss, not a cost, because he holds no duty that could take it.
- **Batch 0002:** Owen completes his first qualified filing as an assisting clerk and accepts his first named duty. Marginalia becomes reliable on thin files and legibly absent on held ones. Cost: an injury, unpaid hours, a first case that narrows instead of strengthening him.
- **Batch 0003:** no stage change, but the cost of a named duty becomes visible — the same name that would not come in Chapter 10 and came back will not come at all.
- **Batch 0004:** no stage change. Owen's reputation inside the Office is destroyed and rebuilt as something else, which is less useful and more honest.
- **Batch 0005 (Ch 46–50):** **Stage 1, Sealed Witness.** Acquisition: a completed qualified filing, a named duty accepted, and a demonstrated knowledge of what the entry cannot support. Cost: **one active hold.** The unresolved Lower Lock cases — Cobb Rennie's pension, the six children and non-responders with no protective return, the four dissenters' branch, the crossing's repair — enter his working life as exact sensory detail and as names he cannot always summon. He is now a person whose accuracy has a price and a term.

## Continuity Guardrails for This Volume

- The Ninth Weir is unsafe, load-restricted, and closed to loads for the entire volume. The preservation hold keeps a **legal status and an appeal window** alive. It never makes the structure safe, funded, repaired, or usable.
- **Stability Order 114** is issued by Halden Pike under the Docketford Deputy Registrar's solvency authority in Ch 17–18, on the eleven failing returns he named in Ch 3. It is never confused with the Chapter 8 load-restriction suspension. It is the instrument that goes to public appeal.
- **The transfer's receipt is validly made and is never correctable.** The one provable defect in the file is the annex schedule's two-day backdating, which Owen proves in Ch 1 and then withdraws from his own inquiry in Ch 5, because a correctable error is the way the Office closes a file. It is never corrected. The Chapter 5 reversal depends on this: the error route is not merely closed, it is surrendered on purpose.
- Lower Lock's instrument is a **district return** throughout this volume, matching its final file number 487-LL. "Local return" is the general category for any community-filed return and is never a second instrument.
- Owen does not become a Sealed Witness before Chapter 46, and never signs a continuation, certification, or hold before that chapter. Before then he is a copy clerk, a petitioner, a co-petitioner, and an assisting clerk. His first qualified filing is in Batch 0002.
- A household return is a set of separately consenting adult branches. No adult signs for another, and a withdrawal narrows the return rather than destroying it.
- A protective return requires two unrelated living witnesses, a current care act, and a named appeal route. Six of the ward's thirteen erased entries are children or people unable to answer, and the ward has no protective-return structure in place at the end of the volume. **This remains unsolved.**
- Nothing restores the dead. Marta Rusk stays dead; Tal Sarr stays dead; the correct record says so.
- Redaction may turn out to have prevented deaths elsewhere. It is still not consent, and the volume does not let the good news launder the harm.
- Ansel Rive appears only as a countersignature and a file name. Verity Lound appears only through circulars. Warden Kade, Iven Sallow, Bettis Marr, Tovan Reed, and the First Seal Subvault do not appear on stage in this volume.
- The eight-person infancy sponsorship is not referenced, hinted at, or foreshadowed anywhere in Volume 01.
