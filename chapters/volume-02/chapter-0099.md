# Chapter 99 — A Column of Reasons

**Two documents that anybody in this county is talking about exist at the west gate of Cutter's Fields, and they are not the same document, and the only person who has ever been asked to keep them apart is a woman who has asked for no name for nine years and has not acquired one.**

The first is the certification of the count of the season of 489 for Docketford district, dated the twenty-eighth day of the third month of the Concord Year 488, qualified, with **no figure of premises of record upon it at all** — none, for the first time in the record of this county — and the schedule attached, being five columns, ten rows, the ten settlements named in the schedule printed on the twenty-eighth of the second month, no settlement added and none removed, the fourth column *no* ten times and the fifth *yes* ten times, the tenth row a building, and not one word about why written anywhere on it.

The second is a board, and it is smaller, and it went up beside the frame at about eleven on the Friday morning of the twenty-sixth day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488, and it carries the settlements struck from the count for the season and the reason against every one of them.

**The two are not a pair. The certification says a district counted nothing in a season and names ten places that were not looked at. The board says what a reason is and it is a figure every time. Neither of them is about the other, and the only thing they have in common is a county and a field, and the connection between them is that a column of reasons has arrived, and the column is made of the same numbers that are already on the other sheet, and that is not a remedy and it is not a step.**

There are eleven rows.

---

**The eleven are the settlements struck from the count for the season of the Concord Year 489, being the settlements in the eleven districts the Census Guard worked this season, and the eleven is not the ten on the certification's schedule, and it is not the fourth kind on a sheet dated the second month of the Concord Year 474 in a room in Docketford, which is nine, and it is not the eleven households who went off a reach in the Concord Year 484, and it is not a figure of barrels, and it is not the nine premises of record now standing against a place nobody had looked at, and about four people in this county are going to be asked about the word eleven in the next year and the answer is a sheet.**

Four of the eleven are in Docketford district. Six are in districts Owen Rusk has not been to and are not his business, and the seventh is in the district he was standing in a gate in on Wednesday, and he did not examine it and did not try to, and the board is public and anybody may walk to the west gate of Cutter's Fields and read it, which is the whole point of it and the whole of what it does.

**SLATE OF SETTLEMENTS STRUCK FROM THE COUNT FOR THE SEASON OF THE CONCORD YEAR 489, and the reason against each. One, settlement. Two, premises of record, being the figure of the premises of record at the date of the strike. Three, reason.**

*Marden Fell, Ravensmoor. 4. 4.*
*Slackwater, Docketford. 9. 9.*
*Nether Sill, Docketford. 7. 4.*
*Ossery, Docketford. 11. 11.*
*Quill Bank, Docketford. 9. 7.*

*Alder Gap, Colne. 6. 6.*
*Two Sisters, Ilm. 2. 2.*
*Rushford, Kelm. 8. 3.*
*Netherwey, Lath. 11. 11.*
*Bight, Merrow. 7. 5.*
*Coalyard, Sten. 5. 7.*

**And that is the eleven, and Owen Rusk read the whole column twice on the Friday morning from about eight feet off and there is nobody in this county who can say what any of the eleven reasons means except the one about Slackwater, and he is the man who made that one mean something by standing in a lane with a woman of sixty.**

**And it is not the eleven settlements in Ravensmoor district that were not reconciled on the twenty-fourth of this month, and it is not the ten on the certification's schedule, and in about four years nobody in this county is going to be able to keep any two of the sets apart, and neither can anybody else, and the difference between them is that one of them is a list of places a warden struck in her own hand and the other four are lists of places a building struck with a figure.**

**Six of the eleven have the same figure in the second column and the third, and that is the whole of what a column of reasons is, and the other five do not, and the eleventh has a reason larger than its premises, and Quill Bank has a seven against nine premises and there is nobody in this county who can say what the seven is about, and the man of about fifty-five in the Water Office at Docketford put a figure in a cell in the Concord Year 486 and it has been carried forward three seasons and it is on a public board behind a tannery, and nobody is going to tell him it is there, because a kindness and a conversation are the same act and the only difference is whether anybody writes it down afterwards.**

**And the reason against Slackwater is nine, and the second column is nine, and they are the same figure, and it is the same figure in five rows out of eleven, and a person can check the whole of it in about four seconds, and it is the first document in this county's record in which anybody could have checked the reasons against anything at all.**

**And Nether Sill is four, and four is not the figure of its premises, and nobody in this county has ever been able to find out what four is, and it has been in a reason box since the twelfth day of the second month and eleven households have kept the water it was about.**

And Ossery is the eleventh row and its reason is eleven, and the eleven is the figure of its premises written a second time, and it says not one word about the fact that the ground Ossery sits on is under a tank carried up four miles of unmade track twice a week by a man the office has never asked anything, and there is no figure in this county that is that, and there never has been, and the office did not print a dash because a dash is not a figure and a column of reasons is a column of figures.

"You said every reason is a number," said Cobb Rennie, who had come up the hill on his own two feet. "**And you said it before that board was made and you were right, and I would like it written down that you were right, and I have been saying it in fields since the seventh of the third month and I have given it away twice and this is the third time and I am not going to say it a fourth.**"

"Six of them are the doors written twice and five of them are not," said Owen Rusk. "**And the five that are not are the interesting ones, because nobody in this county can say what five of the eleven reasons mean, and a man with a bad knee in a room across the cut wrote one of them out of a book in the Concord Year 486 and has never been asked and is not going to be.**"

---

**The woman who maintains the board had the ladder out at about ten and she was about thirty minutes on it and she came down and stood back twice and she has done this in nine years and the frame at the west gate is hers in the sense that nobody has ever told her what goes on it and nobody ever has.**

"I want the field to hear three things about that board and I am going to say all three and I have not been asked for any of them and there is no form in this county on which I could have been asked and none on which I could have been told no."

"Go on."

"**One. That column was not there when they printed the schedule in the second month, and there was no column. There was a schedule and a reason box on a form in a building two hundred miles up a river and a warden wrote a figure into a box, and I have put up papers with figures on them since 476 and I never once had a column to hang.**" She put her hand flat on the board. "**Two. I have put the board up beside the frame and not on it, and it is smaller, and it is that size because there are eleven of them and the frame is for a district and that is eleven counties, and if somebody in four years says a county has put up a second board then I would like it in a file somewhere that a woman with a ladder put it up because a column had arrived, and that is what a county does when a column arrives. It is not a first. There was a first on the twenty-eighth of the third month and it was a schedule of ten places nobody went to, and everybody read it, and I put that at the bottom of the frame under a strip of paper the size of a receipt because I will not put a sheet over a sheet on the last morning of a season, and that was my doing and not the count's, and this is my doing and not the count's, and I have not been thanked for either and I am not going to be thanked for this one either.**"

"And the third thing."

"**The third thing is that it does not stop anything,**" she said. "**It is a column of reasons. It is eleven numbers and it is the same eleven the forms already said. It is a thing a person can read and check and check it against what the form already said, and the form already said it, and so the column is the form again, and the reason anybody in this county is going to be interested is not that the reasons are new. It is that the reasons are old and were not printed, and that is all it is, and I have nine years of putting up what I am given and I would like about thirty people in this field to take that away and not the other thing.**"

---

**And the practice did not stop, and it is now legible, and on the Friday morning of the twenty-sixth day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488 about thirty-four people were in nine flat acres behind the tanneries reading two documents that are not the same document, and the whole of the season of 489 was still standing exactly as it had been on the twenty-eighth of the third month.**

The survey of settlements of record in Docketford district is suspended. Nine persons are affected. The reason is not given and no form in this county provides for one, and the survey round is at its foot as it stood on the twenty-third, and under the round there is a printed form headed SUSPENSION BY A WARDEN OF THE COUNT, four ruled lines and a date, the twenty-sixth, not signed, **and the four lines are empty, in the fourth month of the Concord Year 488, and no person in this county filled them in, and nobody has asked anybody to.**

Warden Kade is suspended in her own hand in a field on the ninth of the second month with a reason that is a page long and is not a figure. **Nobody has reviewed it, it is not hers to end, and she is going to be standing in exactly that state in June. On the Wednesday of the twenty-fourth day of this month she struck a whole district schedule in another district in her own hand with a reason that is not a figure, and the two instruments are not the same act, and neither has been reviewed, and the office has ended neither.**

The fifth column of the district return for the season of 490 will be completed in that season from the returns of district offices and of water offices, and a return for the season of 490 is to be made in the district office at Fetter Bend, and it is not written yet, and its officer of record has spent nine days of the fourth month in a yard four miles up a track and has done nothing at all about it. **And the figures for the season of 489 for the ten settlements on the printed schedule are still the figures of 486, carried forward, and the count is certified as the return was received, and the district is reported short, and the report of the shortage is the ten.**

The crossing at Fetter Lane is shut to loads. The crew is nine and their wage is four months and a bit in arrears. The granary has been open and empty since the seventh day of the second month and the figure is nothing for the eleventh week running. The Fetter Lane pump grant is suspended and not extinguished and the pump is primed by hand at four in the morning on the Thursday. The six persons in a ward of eleven doors whom it cannot ask are six persons and are at three of five parts and the word is not written beside anybody's name. Cobb Rennie's pension is two years and one month. Stability Order 114 is on a docket in public appeal and has not been withdrawn and the sixteen are sixteen and Lower Lock is sixth. The repair is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and is unfunded and the notice at the weir path is in the weather and is going to be there in June.

**Marl Dykes is not on the certification's schedule and is not on the new board and is not restored, and there is no form on which anybody may ask why it went, and it is a ward with two doors and nine households and it is not on either sheet, and Ada Rusk has said that the reason a list of places that were not reconciled is a list of the places somebody else chose, and neither is she nor the Warden nor the man with his name on the preparation line permitted to choose places, and that is the reason and there is no form for it either.**

**Nether Sill is not restored, and the reason in its box is four, and eleven households kept the water it was about and are not on any of these three documents. Hark Holm is not restored. The eleven households off the reach in the Concord Year 484 are not restored and are not affected by any of them.**

**And nobody was thanked. Ada Rusk was not thanked for anything on the Friday morning and was not asked for anything and is not on either board and is not on the schedule and her ward is the eleventh door on a sheet in a field and is not on this one, and she has not asked to be, and she has said in a hall that she is not going to say the sentence about visibility a third time because a third time is the way a thing stops being true, and she stood at the back of the frame with her hands behind her back and read the eleven reasons out loud in a field, one at a time, to about thirty-four people, and did not say anything else about it.**

---

**Pell did not come down the hill on the Friday morning and had sent nothing, and about four people noticed, and one of them said the sentence she has said in this county four times this year, which is that a Registry clerk who has gone down a hill has not done anything, and it was not said in order to be said, and it is not in a file anywhere.**

**And the woman of about thirty with a slate stood at the new board for about nine minutes and then went and stood at the old one, at the bottom of the frame, under a certification with nothing on it and a schedule of ten places with *no* ten times, and she read both boards in about four minutes and said one thing to the counting clerk at the second counter on the Saturday morning and it is the only observation she has made in this county this year that anybody has written down.**

"**The big board says a district went nowhere. The small board says a county went to eleven places. Both of those are true and about thirty people have read both of them in one morning, and the two of them are not the same size and they are not the same kind of thing, and it is going to be a very common thing in about four years for a person to point at the small board and say the county has admitted what the big board says, and the small board does not say that, and the small board says a number, and the big board is the only thing anybody has ever printed in this county that admits anything at all.**"

"Which of them would you rather was not up," said the counting clerk.

"**The small one,**" she said, "**and I would like it noticed that I said that and that I work in the building that printed it.**"

---

**And on the Friday afternoon of the twenty-sixth day of the fourth month the fourth week of the season was over, and the season of 489 for Docketford district had been over since the twenty-eighth of the third month, and the last morning of the season of 489 as the Guard kept it was not going to be for another two days, and the counting tent at Cutter's Fields was to come down on the Sunday morning of the twenty-eighth and go up the river with the stone in its case, and about twenty people were in that field on the Friday afternoon reading a board about a season that was over.**

Ada Rusk read the eleven out loud, one at a time, in a field, with about thirty-four people in it, and she did not say anything else about it, and nobody asked her to.

Then she said one sentence to her brother, at the frame, at about ten past two in the afternoon, with her hands behind her back and about nine people within earshot, and it is the last thing she says to him this year.

"**The eleventh door on that other board is ours and it is not on this one and I did not ask to be, and the reason we are not on this one is that a column of reasons is made out of figures and there is no figure about this ward in this county, and that has happened thirteen times and it has happened again this morning, and I have said the sentence about the second of four in a hall thirteen times and once in a room and once in a yard and I am not going to say it a fourteenth time anywhere, so I am going to say nothing, and I am going to stand here, and you may write down that I said nothing.**"

"You said nothing," said Owen Rusk.

"**I said nothing,**" said Ada Rusk, "**and it is in a book, and you will put a day and an hour on it, and in about four years somebody will read that book and find a line in it that says Ada Rusk said nothing, and that is the only line in it that is worth a thing.**"
