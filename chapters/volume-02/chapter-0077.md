# Chapter 77 — The Season Is Done

Warden Oress Kade came down the hill on the Thursday morning of the twenty-ninth day of the second month of the Concord Year 488 at about a quarter to nine, alone, without a case and without a slate, and stood at the west gate of Cutter's Fields in front of three sheets of paper for about ten minutes before she said anything to anybody. The count of the ninth was above. Her own sheet was in the middle, one loop of tarred cord, a page long, the reason that is not a figure. The notice of the twenty-sixth was at the bottom, and it is the same instrument as the middle one in every particular that matters, and she looked at it for a long time, and Owen Rusk stood about eight feet off with his hands behind his back and did not go closer, and Ada Rusk was at the frame reading the bottom one the way she reads everything, from the top, twice, and Cobb Rennie was at the rail with a woman of about thirty with a slate and about four other people who had come to look at a piece of paper.

"**Nobody signed it,**" said Warden Kade.

"No," said the woman with the slate. "It is not signed and it does not need to be. It is a notice and a notice is a thing a building says about itself."

"Then a building in Concord Hall has taken the form off me." She said it flatly and she said it to the notice and not to anybody. "**I wrote that at noon on the ninth in a field and I have had four letters this season and three of them were about it and not one of them was from a person who had read it.** I did not expect it to be read. I want that understood. I expected it to be wrong about the season and to be corrected and to come back with a figure on it. I did not expect a building to take it and print it and hang it up."

---

"You have a letter," she said, at about a quarter past nine, without turning round.

"I have a letter."

"You have had one since about the middle of this month and you have carried it up a hill four times and you have not sent it, and the season was over at noon on the ninth and you have known that since the ninth, and you are standing in a field with about nine people in it handing it to me in front of all of them." She turned round. "**Do you know why you are doing it that way.**"

"Because I did not want to write it in a room."

"That is half of it. The other half is that in a few years somebody is going to look for a date on this, and a date in a field in front of nine people is a date that cannot be argued with, and you have been careful about dates all season in a way that I have watched and have not said anything about." She held out her hand. "Give it to me."

Owen Rusk gave it to her. It was two sides of a sheet and it had been in his coat for eleven days and it was not folded in half and it was not folded at all. She read it standing up, in the cold, in about four minutes, in front of about nine people, because she is a person who reads in fields and has never once in nineteen seasons asked anybody to wait for her. Ada Rusk read it over her shoulder at some point and did not say anything about that either. Cobb Rennie read the bottom of the second side over Ada Rusk's other shoulder and said nothing at all, which for Cobb Rennie is a considerable achievement.

When she had finished she folded it once, along a line that was not there before, and put it inside her coat, and did not thank him for it, and did not say she was sorry, and said:

"**You have written to me four times this season and this is the fifth, and it is the first one that does not ask me for anything, and I have been waiting nineteen seasons for one of those, and I am not going to tell you what it is worth, and you should not have written it in a field.**"

"I know."

"You do not know, and I am going to tell you the reason and then I am going to tell you the other thing and then this conversation is over, because there are about nine people standing here and one of them is a man of fifty-eight who has learned to read columns and I would rather he did not learn this one from me."

---

"**The first thing is that you are right, again, and it is going to cost somebody who is not you.** You have won two things in six weeks. The first one put a figure back where a season belonged and restored nobody. This one has stopped every survey in this district for a season, and it is a correct thing, and the woman who determined it wrote down in her own determination that it is the correct answer to a challenge about a paper and the wrong answer for a county, and she was right, and she said so in a room, and she will not be thanked for saying it because she is an officer of the count and that is not what officers are for.

"And here is what I am going to say and I have said it to nobody, and I am saying it to you because you have written me a letter and because a letter is the only kind of paper in this county that a person is allowed to put a sentence like this in.

"**In about four years every officer of the count in this county will know that currency is the third limb. And the answer to currency is a suspension.** Not a survey. A suspension. If a person in a village brings a challenge in the season of 490 that a paper about their premises is out of date, then the correct response — the response the rules require, the response I would have to give, the response that would be printed — is that the reconciliation of that settlement is suspended until somebody looks at it. And the fourteen days will run. **The fourteen days will run on a place because a man in a building noticed a date.** And a person who cannot answer for themselves cannot say no to that, and cannot say yes either, and the correction at the end of the fourteen is made by an office and attested by me, and the reason will be a figure, and I will have to put it in the box."

"You are right about it."

"I am right about it and I have been right about it since Sunday morning, and a warden who is right about a thing a fortnight before it happens has not been given a gift, she has been given a job, and I have had nineteen seasons of them and I would take it again." She looked at the frame. "**I am not going to be thanked by you and I would like us both to be clear about why. A warden who has been thanked is a warden somebody has paid. I have been suspended in my own hand in a field with a reason a page long and nobody has thanked me and I did not want it, and you worked that out in about four seconds standing six feet off me on the ninth and you have not mentioned it since, and that is the correct amount of times.**"

---

"**The second thing is what you will not get out of me, and I am going to be exact about it so that you do not go away with a wrong idea about how this went.**

"There is a fifth letter. I wrote it on the sixteenth day of this month, in the morning, before I knew what you were going to do with the copy, and it went to the Council and not to the office, and you did not know about it, and I did not tell you, and I am not going to tell you now what is in it. You will get the fact that there was one and you will get nothing else, and the nothing else is deliberate, and I want you to have been told that it is deliberate, because a man who thinks he was kept out of something is a man who goes and looks for it, and a man who has been told he was kept out of a thing is a man who decides for himself that he was right not to be told."

"I will not look for it."

"I know you will not, and that is not why I am doing it." She put her hands behind her back. "**Do not write to me for a season.** I am suspended. A suspended officer who is in correspondence is an officer with a file, and I have a file, and in about four years there is going to be a question asked in a room in Concord Hall about what the Warden of Docketford district was doing in the season of 489, and the answer is going to be in a file, and a file is the only place in this county where a letter lives. In four years I want there to be four letters in it and no fifth one from a Continuance Examiner of the Count of Docketford district."

"That is fair."

"It is not fair. It is the correct answer and I have given it to you in a field in front of nine people and I would like somebody to write down that I did that on purpose and that it is worse than the other way round, because the other way round is a refusal and this is a door I have shut between two people who have been writing letters at each other for six weeks."

---

At about twenty past ten she turned round and asked him a question, and it was not a soft one, and she asked it in a field in front of about nine people because she does not ask a person anything in a corridor.

"**What is on the tenth row.**"

Owen Rusk did not answer for about two seconds, and about two seconds is a long time in a field.

"I read the sheet on Wednesday morning at about ten past three," he said. "I read the heading and the first two rows and then I stood and looked at my own notice, and I have not read it properly since and I am going to on Friday."

"That is not the question I asked and you know it is not, because I have been in nineteen districts and I have never once in my life asked a question twice." She had her hands behind her back. "**You have just worked something out. You worked it out on Wednesday and you have had it for two days and you have not given it to me, and I have been standing at this frame for ten minutes in front of a document that is not mine waiting for you to say it, and that is the first thing you have done this season that I would have said was you.**"

"Yes."

"Then say the reason and not the thing, because the thing is yours and I have not got a claim on it and there is no form in this county that would give me one."

"**The reason is that I may not examine a schedule, and a choice of places, and the tenth row is a choice of places, and the moment I say it out loud in a field to a Warden it becomes a document about the schedule, and the only thing I am permitted to do with a document about the schedule is nothing, and you would have it in a way I could not get it out of.**"

"That is a correct reason," said Warden Kade, "and it is the whole of the difficulty, and I have watched you find it and I have watched you find it about four other things this season and every single one of them turned out to be the same one. **You have discovered that the only safe act available to you in this county is not to say a thing, and you have got very good at it, and in about four years you are going to be the best-informed man in this county and the least able to use any of it, and nobody is going to be able to prove that you could have used it, and that is the worst condition a person can be in and it is the only condition this county rewards.**"

"That is the third time somebody has said a version of that to me in a season."

"Then it is true three times and I am not going to soften it to be kind." She turned back to the frame. "Read it on Friday. Do not tell me what is on it. And when you next write to me, and you will not write to me for a season, write down the things you did not tell me, in order, with the days on them, because a man who writes down what he has not said is worth something in this county and there are not many of them and I have never once been one of them and I have wanted to be for nineteen seasons."

Cobb Rennie asked her one question, at about half past ten, standing at the rail with his hands in his pockets, and he asked it the way he asks everything, which is as though he is asking about the price of something.

"Ma'am. Has anybody reviewed it."

"**No,**" said Warden Kade. "Not that I have been told, and nobody is required to tell me. It is an act of operations and not a finding, and an act of operations may be reviewed by the office, and it ends when the office ends it and not before, and the office has not ended it, and I am going to be standing in exactly this state in June."

"You could end it yourself."

"I could end it in about four seconds and I have thought about it on a boat twice this month." She did not soften at all and she was not going to. "If I end it, it is a decision. A decision has a ground and a ground has to be said out loud, and the ground is *a warden got frightened in a field and put it back the way it was*, and that is a true sentence and it is in a file, and then the thing I did on the ninth is a thing a frightened person did, and in about nine years somebody in Concord Hall is going to read that and understand it, and then nobody will ever put a reason that is not a figure on anything again. **I would rather be an act of operations with no finding behind it for as long as it lasts. I have been one of those twice and I know exactly how long it goes on for and I know exactly what it costs, and the cost is that nobody can thank me, and I have decided that is the cheapest thing available.**"

Ada Rusk said one thing to Owen Rusk at about eleven, at the frame, with her hands behind her back, in a voice that nine people in a field could not hear.

"Your second term cannot be performed and I have ruled that and I have not withdrawn it, and I am not going to say anything to you about the rest of it, and the roster is read on a Thursday and it is performed whatever the return does and whatever the count does and whatever you do."

---

He went up the hill at about eleven and did the tray and was on it at a quarter past six, and the tray had nothing on it that he had not put there himself, and the letter was in a coat that was going down the river.

He worked out at the end of the tray that he had got one thing out of that field and it was a fact with nothing in it, and that he had handed over two sides of a sheet and got a date and a shut door, and that the shut door was the correct answer and was given in front of nine people on purpose, and that he had got exactly what a letter is for.

A letter is a thing you write when you want a person to know something and you cannot put it in a room, and a letter is not a filing and it is not an instrument and it is not evidence and it is not a hold and it is not a challenge and it is not one of the three things on a card, and **a letter is the only document in this county that cannot be produced against anybody, and that is the whole of its value and the whole of its uselessness, and he had known both of those things on the sixteenth day of the month and had handed it over anyway, and the woman he had handed it to had read it in a field in four minutes and had given him back a season in which he may not write to her.**

The season was over. The count was qualified. The district was reported short by seven and had been reported short in two years out of the last four. The day-book had closed inside the sixth week with nine lines against a quota of eleven and there was no row on any board in this county for the two that were not done, and a survey round was suspended with its fifth row empty, and a return had been accepted in the state in which it was made, and eleven premises at Nether Sill were not going to be on anything, and the schedule for the season of 489 had gone up on a frame behind the tanneries the day before with ten names in five columns and a column that nobody in this county could fill.

And the schedule had been printed, and it would not be amended after printing, and a Warden might return it and might not amend it, and he had asked her for a day in the first week of the season before last and she had refused him and been right twice, and he had gone on coming up the hill afterwards anyway, twice, and had been right about a document once, and the right thing had cost a man with a chain and a rule whom he had never met and would not name, and a woman with a slate and no coat had written both halves of it into a determination and read them out in front of nine people, and it was in a file in Concord Hall with two names on it, and **Owen Rusk had asked for a day and had been refused, and he had not been given a day, and there is no form in this county for a thing that was wanted and was not got, and the season was over.**
