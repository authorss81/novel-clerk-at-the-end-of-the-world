# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline — The Omission

Batch number: 0001
Chapter range: 1–10
Story time: C.Y. 487, the second and third weeks of the rain season, fourteen days (Days 1–14; see Verified Canon Rulings). The transfer review sits off-page inside that span, is confirmed rather than contested, and Owen is not on the notice for it. The ward's return is finished and witnessed by the end of Day 14 and is **never entered in this batch**.

## Batch Goal

Take an apparent clerical omission and turn it into a public, docketed question: whether a neighborhood can file its own return. By the end of the batch, Lower Lock is on the docket, Owen's name is on the docket beside it, the crossing's status has lapsed on the roll and the ward's standing is frozen until it can file, and the Council has closed the local-return route.

## Starting Condition

Owen Rusk is a senior copy clerk at the Registry's River Desk with ten weeks of clean quarterly run behind him and no authority to change a status. Lower Lock is his home ward, which he has never treated as a professional fact. The Ninth Weir is load-restricted. Civic Zero is a calendar item thirteen years out. Owen's private fear is that the Office is careless; his private competence is that it is not.

## Starting Relationships

Owen trusts Ada's judgment about bridges and not her judgment about herself. Owen respects Pike and has never had to. Owen and Nell have not spoken in two years, and the last thing they said to each other was in a hearing room. Pell is new enough that Owen still believes seniority is a kind of argument.

### Eleven days and eleven months

**Eleven days** is the consolidation letter and the families already sitting at Arbour Kitchen when Owen arrives. **Eleven months** is the Sarr receipt, created in C.Y. 486. Never blend them; the near-identical numbers are the point, and the ward lived inside the gap between them.

**Tal Sarr signed while he was alive.** The receipt was signed, witnessed, sealed, and received in C.Y. 486, and it was validly made then. He drowned in the spring flood of C.Y. 487, months afterward. His death did not disturb the entry, and no version of the prose may make it a posthumous filing, a forgery, or a receipt taken from a corpse. The file was correct when it was written and stayed correct after it stopped being true.

### Four instruments, kept apart

1. **The spring engineering notice** (pre-existing) — the load restriction on the Ninth Weir that came with the spring inspection. A technical notice. No appeal route.
2. **The transfer receipt** (C.Y. 486) — moves the crossing's *maintenance duty* to the Mill Ward return. Validly made, uncorrectable, challengeable only on scope.
3. **The load-restriction suspension** (Ch 8) — Pike's formal instrument, on the Registry engineer's finding that the abutment crack has moved five inches. This is the first instrument in the case that carries a real appeal window, and it is the ancestor of the crossing hold at the climax. It is **not** Stability Order 114.
4. **Stability Order 114** (Ch 17–18) — signed on the sixteen failing returns named in Chapter 3. Separate again. The instrument that reaches public appeal.

### Three separate seven-day clocks

- The **return's own review interval**: seven days, running from the date the ward's return is **entered as a filing**. Nothing in Batch 0001 enters it. It is entered in **Chapter 11, batch Day 15**, as Owen's first qualified filing as an assisting clerk, so its first review falls due on **Day 22** — inside the same window as Stability Order 114. The ward's return is drafted, witnessed, and finished by the end of Chapter 10; it is not filed, because in Batch 0001 no qualified signer exists for it.
- The **answer clock on the petition**: seven days from the Chapter 9 docket entry, running to a written answer, landing in Chapters 12–13. Due **Day 19**.
- The **closure interval**: the seven days a failing return can be repaired before unfiling begins.

They coincide in length and mean different things. Do not let one satisfy another. Chapter 10 must not claim that the return's review is coming due: the review interval does not exist yet at the end of Batch 0001.

### The lapse on the Day 14 roll

The crossing's status lapses inside this batch, on the **Day 14 weekly continuance roll**, in Chapter 10. It is not averted, deferred, or argued out of existence; the petition entered on Day 12 does not hold it, and the chapter says so in those terms.

What the lapse is, precisely: with no supported district performing the crossing's maintenance, the Ninth Weir's status is entered as **lapsed — no supported district**. Consequences, all of them real and all of them still true at Chapter 50:

- The Fetter Lane pump grant is **suspended**, not extinguished.
- Lower Lock's households cannot be continued, licensed, or witnessed on a district act until a supported district return naming the crossing is entered on the roll. Nobody's name is struck. Everybody in the ward is frozen.
- The Chapter 8 load-restriction suspension is untouched and still in force. The crossing remains closed to loads and unsupported, which is worse than either alone.

The only cure inside Volume 01 is the act Batch 0002 opens on: **entering** the ward's district return on Day 15 lifts the suspension on the ward's standing and restores the crossing's status **as a claim the ward now performs**. It does not make the crossing safe, funded, or open, and it does not decide what the return is worth — the Office's answer and its acceptance in Batch 0002 do that.

### Two leaves of one annex

The transfer travels with an annex of **two leaves**, separate documents with separate scopes. They are never both called "the schedule," and nothing in the batch may treat them as one instrument.

1. **The annex schedule** — dated. Carries the effective dates of the three maintenance acts the transfer moves: the Ninth Weir crossing, the Fetter Lane pump, and the granary. This is the document whose two-day backdating Owen proves in Chapter 1 and withdraws in Chapter 5.
2. **The no-local-record schedule** — carries the thirteen names and asserts "no local record" against a **single named place: the crossing**. It is a claim about a place, not about a ward.

Lower Lock maintains four things. The transfer moved three, and the seed stall was never in either leaf.

### Lower Lock's arithmetic

- Thirteen households sit on the Office's last accepted roll. Nine ratified the transfer in Chapter 6; four voted no and those four sign no branch anywhere in this batch. Their separate branch is a Chapters 31–40 beat.
- The draft return therefore carries **nine separately consenting adult branches** by the end of Chapter 7. One of them withdraws in Batch 0002, and the return narrows instead of collapsing.
- The three households that refuse to share a page with another adult's name in Chapter 7 are **not** the four dissenters. They are inside the nine; their refusal is a formatting demand, and it is granted.
- The ward holds about 240 residents. **Cobb Rennie is not one of the thirteen households** and **not one of the thirteen erased entries**. He was dropped two years before the batch, in an earlier review quarter, which is why his pension cannot be cured by anything filed in this case. It stays uncured at Chapter 50.
- The thirteen "no local record" entries on the **no-local-record schedule** — the second leaf of the annex — split **six children or people unable to answer** and **seven adults who can answer**. Ivy Sarr, 12, is in the six. Dory Anstruther, 19, is in the seven. The remaining names are in the file and need not all be read aloud.

### What marginalia can and cannot do here

Owen perceives marginalia only when he is personally comparing a genuine record with the physical act in front of him. The batch stages **exactly two** of these beats and no more:

1. **Chapter 1, one plain notice.** At his own desk, comparing the counter's receipt stamp and register entry — the act as performed — against the annex schedule's stated effective date, he gets a single brief margin notice in ordinary language. It tells him the date is early. It tells him nothing else, and it does not solve anything. This is the baseline the silence is silent against.
2. **Chapter 7, one silence.** The ward's copy of the transfer was **closed** at the Day 5 review, and a closed file returns nothing at all. A thin file answers him; a closed file does not, and the silence is information he cannot yet read.

The Chapter 10 drawer is closed in the ordinary physical sense and yields nothing, and is not a second staged beat. Never give him a margin notice on a held, closed, or sealed record in this batch. Keep notices rare, brief, and in plain language: no numbers, no bars, no skill lists, no solution buttons, no software jargon.

## Batch Shape

- **Beginning (Ch 1–3):** ordinary work, the ward, the supervisor. The genre promise arrives in the second chapter: a living man who is officially a person who was never there.
- **Escalation (Ch 4):** the tool is offered by the person Owen is most wrong about, and the offer changes what he is trying to do.
- **Midpoint reversal (Ch 5):** the record is not false. The case reclassifies from error to scope, and the only remaining lever is a district return the Registry has spent ninety years calling nonbinding.
- **Escalation and cost (Ch 6–8):** Owen authors the trap, a vote ratifies it, and the crossing is closed to loads by a finding that is probably correct.
- **Batch climax (Ch 9):** Owen's best idea is refused by the junior clerk who is right, and the ward's steward saves the case with the cheapest lawful act in the volume — a petition.
- **Aftermath (Ch 10):** the Day 14 roll enters the crossing's status as lapsed, the ward's standing freezes, and every other consequence lands after that — a circular closes the route, a name will not come and then comes back anyway, and a brass tab in a drawer he is now trusted with names a ward that officially ceased to exist.

## Batch Midpoint (Chapter 5)

The thirteen names are not denied by omission. The transfer was *accepted* eleven months ago on a signed, witnessed, sealed **receipt** attributed to Tal Sarr, a Lower Lock resident who drowned in the spring flood and whose authority covered his own household only. The receipt is validly made; there is no version of this file in which the entry itself is wrong. Owen's error-correction path is dead, and he closes it himself: the one defect he can actually prove — the annex schedule's two-day backdating — he withdraws from his own filing, because the moment the Office can correct a date it closes the file as a clerical matter and keeps the scope. He then files the charge of scope in his own name and proposes the one thing that can work: Lower Lock performs a duty and claims it as its own.

## Batch Climax (Chapter 9)

Owen's short-form attestation — accurate, fast, and unwitnessed — is refused by Junia Pell in a corridor, and the window is lost. In the same session, Marguerite Bray files her own petition for a hearing in her own words, and the clerk on duty cannot refuse a request for a hearing without writing a reason. It is entered, not granted. The Office must answer the petition in writing within seven days — a clock that runs to the answer, not to any hearing, and lands in Batch 0002.

## Required Character Changes

- **Owen:** learns that his precision can author a trap, and that being right was the thing that made him dangerous. He ends the batch less competent-looking and more useful, and he does not yet have a method for the difference.
- **Owen and Nell:** reestablish contact as adversaries who recognize each other's work. She refuses to be his witness, his advocate, or his relief. He accepts the terms and keeps them.
- **Owen and Ada:** she refuses to be the subject of his rescue and refuses his money. He is present as a copyist who stayed, not as a protector.
- **Owen and Pell:** she corrects him in public and in private and will not become his instrument. He resents her and begins, unwillingly, to think with her question.
- **Owen and Pike:** Pike stops managing him and starts using him, which is worse.

## Required Power Changes

No stage change. Owen remains a **Copy Clerk (Stage 0)** and must not be written as signing anything, holding anything, or perceiving a closed record. He gains: a private ledger kept outside the Office book, one continuance inquiry filed in his own name, the working terms of a district return, one signed statement of his own part entered in the docket against his own drafting, and a finished return he is not allowed to enter. He pays: his posting, his standing in his own ward, the fast route to a fix, the belief that accuracy is a defence, and the wording of a forgery he copied into his own notebook. His **first qualified filing as an assisting clerk belongs to Batch 0002 (Ch 11–20)** and must not be credited here — and it is that filing, not anything in this batch, which lifts the Day 14 freeze.

## Required World Reveals

- Unfiling is real, local, and already happening to named people.
- "No local record" is a positive status, not a gap, and it can be entered without a single resident's knowledge. It is asserted against a **place** — the crossing — not against a ward, and a ward cannot answer a claim about a place by saying it exists.
- The transfer travels with two leaves and moves three of the ward's four maintenance acts. The seed stall is in neither leaf and nobody explains it.
- A lapse is a real act with real consequences: on the Day 14 roll the crossing's status goes, the pump grant is suspended, and every adult in the ward is frozen. Nobody's name is struck and it does not matter.
- A duty can be validly received *for* a community without that community ever being asked.
- A district return is a legal object with a named scope, current acts, independent witnesses, a seven-day review, an appeal route, and separately consenting adult branches. The Registry dislikes it, not because it is invalid but because it is slow. Lower Lock's instrument is a **district return** throughout, matching its final file number 487-LL; "local return" is the general category word for any community-filed return and is never a second instrument in this volume.
- The claim a ward can make about a structure is not "the structure is safe." It is "we perform this duty, here is the act, here is the review date."
- Form Zero, the Common Return, is the only instrument that could supersede a stability order. It has no valid signature path. It is due in thirteen years.
- An unattributed countersignature review standard is already in force at Docketford by Chapter 3 and cannot be traced to any Docketford office. The Provisional Council's circular in Chapter 10 names itself as the issuer, states the countersignature requirement, and sets the deadline that will close the route. **Who issued the Chapter 3 standard, and why, is reserved to Batch 0003.**

## Open Questions After the Batch

- Why was a dead man's household receipt accepted as authority over a whole ward, and who checked?
- Eleven months is a long time for a transfer entry to sit. What happened in those eleven months, and who was waiting?
- Why does a countersignature standard exist at all, who issued the Docketford copy in Chapter 3, and who is it protecting?
- Six of the thirteen erased entries are children or people unable to answer. Lower Lock has no protective-return structure and cannot build one alone.
- Four households voted against the transfer and still live inside it. What is owed to people who are right and outvoted?
- The Day 14 roll froze the whole ward in one line. Who reads a frozen roll, and what happens to a ward whose people cannot be licensed or witnessed while the answer clock runs?
- What is the brass tab in the drawer Owen is now trusted with, and why does the index say *never opened*?

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0001 — The Fourth River Desk

POV: Owen Rusk, third person limited.

Location/time: The Registry's River Desk, Docketford; C.Y. 487, second week of the rain season; before the lamps are lit through the last of the afternoon light.

Immediate goal: Get the Fourth Desk's weekly continuance batch copied and countersigned with not one correction, the way it has run clean for ten weeks.

Resistance: The batch arrives short, and not by accident of staffing. Lower Lock's return is not missing and not late. It has been replaced by a *transfer* entry that assigns three of the ward's four maintenance acts — the crossing, the Fetter Lane pump, the granary — to a district return administered from a Mill Ward annex. The transfer carries two Registry witnesses. It carries none from Lower Lock. The receipt itself is sound: signed, witnessed, sealed, and received on the date it was received. The transfer travels with an **annex of two leaves**, and they are not the same document: a dated **annex schedule**, carrying the effective dates of the three acts moved, and a second leaf he has no reason to open yet, because his batch turns on dates and a second leaf is somebody else's problem. It is the first leaf that carries a defect. The annex schedule states an effective date **two days earlier** than the earliest date any part of it could lawfully take effect.

Information revealed: The five conditions of a supported fact enter through work, not exposition — an apprenticeship filing, a widow's name change, a ferry-inspection copy. Owen handles all three competently and without thinking, which is the point. A roll is evidence, not truth. A roll can also be an instrument, and this one has been used as one.

Action or decision: Owen refuses the routine countersignature on the Fourth Desk batch and marks the entry "referred for local confirmation," which is a real procedural act with a real cost. Then the one perception this batch stages, and it is small: comparing the receiving counter's stamp and register entry — the act as somebody actually performed it — against the annex schedule's stated effective date, he gets a single line of plain marginalia in the margin. It says the date is early. It says nothing about who is responsible, nothing about the second leaf he has not read, and nothing he can act on. He reads it twice, decides it is correct, and cannot make it do more work. Then he starts his own ledger in a pocket notebook — his own book, not the Office's — and puts the transfer's number in it with the date it was received and the date it takes effect. Proving the backdating means pulling the request register for the two days in question, and the register is not empty: three requests were refused inside that window, initialled by an office whose clerk he cannot name. He copies all three refusals into his own ledger and cannot learn what they were about. Nobody in the hall will read them with him, and it does not occur to him to ask who still is.

Emotional change: The private, orderly pleasure of a clean desk curdles into the specific dread he has felt exactly twice before, both times in flood years: something is being moved before anyone is asked to look at it. He makes a list. The sharpened pencil stays in the case; he uses a dull one.

Power or resource change: No magic, no authority, no hold. He acquires one thing that is not power and is the only thing he builds in this chapter: a private record that the Office does not control.

Continuity fact to preserve: Lower Lock is a real, inhabited ward of roughly two hundred and forty residents made up of thirteen households on the Office's last accepted roll. The ward maintains four things — the crossing, the Fetter Lane pump, the granary, the seed stall — and the transfer moved three of them; the seed stall was never in either leaf and nobody explains it. The Ninth Weir is load-restricted under a spring engineering notice. The transfer names a Mill Ward return. Pike is Owen's direct supervisor. The Fourth River Desk produces the weekly continuance. The transfer's **receipt is validly made and cannot be corrected**; the only defect anywhere in it is the annex schedule's two-day backdating, which Owen proves here — with one brief margin notice, the batch's only perception — and deliberately withdraws from his own inquiry in Chapter 5. The second leaf, the no-local-record schedule, is never charged with the backdating, and Owen does not read its names until the end of Chapter 2. The request register for that two-day window carries three refused requests, and nobody in this batch learns what they concerned.

Ending type: Discovery.

Next-chapter pull: Something in this building needed the ward to look already moved two days before anyone was told. Owen goes home to Lower Lock that evening to find out how much the ward already knows.

### Chapter 0002 — What the Ward Knows

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: Lower Lock; dusk into the small hours; the rain begins during the chapter.

Immediate goal: Learn whether Lower Lock has been told about the transfer, get his sister to stay out of the ward meeting, and take the problem back to the Office alone.

Resistance: The ward has been told, softly. A letter asks residents to "consolidate claims at the Mill Ward annex for better protection," and it does not mention a crossing. Some families are relieved, because consolidation carries a slate roof and a pump grant. Ada is not relieved, and Ada is also not the person who gets to decide: the meeting is her crew's wage meeting and she is going. Owen's attempt to run the room fails in the first five minutes. He is told, in front of his own neighbors, to sit down.

Information revealed: The Ninth Weir's real condition — a cracked abutment, load restriction, a repair estimate nobody will fund. The ward's actual economy: the crossing markers, the granary, the pump, one seed stall. And unfiling as a lived condition: **Cobb Rennie**, fifty-eight, has been off the Registry roll for two years while living at the same address, known by everyone, unable to draw a pension because no record places him there. He is not one of the thirteen erased entries the no-local-record schedule carries in Chapter 3. His absence is two years old and belongs to an earlier review quarter, which is why nothing filed in this case will ever reach him.

Action or decision: Owen does the one thing he is genuinely good at. He sits in the ward hall with **Marguerite Bray's** paper roll — sixty-one, keeping Lower Lock's roll by hand because the Office's is useless to her — and copies every name, act, and date the ward can prove, with two witnesses per entry where the ward can supply them. It takes the whole night. It is the first time his skill is aimed at a case he cannot win by being right.

Emotional change: Ada tells him, flatly, that he does not get to be the reason she is safe, and Owen understands it as a fact about filing rather than about courage. He understands it. He does not change what he does.

Power or resource change: He acquires the ward's own record, hand-kept and unanswerable by any Registry page, and his first real grasp of the difference between what a ward can *prove in the Office's format* and what it has actually *performed*.

Continuity fact to preserve: Marguerite Bray keeps Lower Lock's paper roll. Cobb Rennie has been off the Registry roll for two years, is not one of the thirteen erased entries, and is not one of the thirteen households on the last accepted roll. The ward's duties are the crossing, the Fetter Lane pump, the granary, and the seed stall. Ada's crew is paid from crossing and granary work. Ada refused Owen's money and his plan to handle it. The consolidation letter is about eleven days old here and is not the transfer, which is eleven months old.

Ending type: Discovery, with cost.

Next-chapter pull: At dawn Owen finds thirteen names on the second leaf of the transfer's annex, the no-local-record schedule, marked "no local record" — not "missing." A positive statement is a statement someone vouched for, and this one is made against a single named place rather than against a ward. Owen takes the leaf to Pike and asks who stood behind it.

### Chapter 0003 — Two Witnesses

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: The copying hall, the Registry's interview alcove, and the stair; the following day.

Immediate goal: Identify the two Registry witnesses on the no-local-record schedule by ordinary request, and keep the ward's hand-copied roll out of Pike's hands.

Resistance: Pike is calm, correct, and gives Owen exactly what the rules require. The witnesses are genuine Registry officers acting under a current review standard. The two Registry witnesses on the no-local-record schedule are the same two officers who witnessed the transfer, and neither of them has any particular memory of Lower Lock. Pike's real resistance is not denial; it is arithmetic and a conscience he is using as a tool: sixteen wards have failing returns this quarter, and a stability order has to be actionable by Friday. He says the sentence that carries the rest of the volume — "Every district I stabilize is a district that does not drown somewhere else" — and Owen files it away as an insult. Then Pike offers Owen a lateral transfer at better pay, on the grounds that he is wasted on a routine desk. Owen refuses it, which is the first time he chooses to be poorer.

Information revealed: Office witnesses can be Registry officers, and their separability of interest is thin when one review standard produced all of them. There is a countersignature review standard already in force at Docketford, and the copy in front of Owen carries no Docketford number and answers to no office any clerk in the hall can name. A person can be "no local record" and still be owed.

Action or decision: Owen files a **continuance inquiry** in his own name. It is not authority; it is a record, and it is public, and it says out loud that a Registry clerk doubts the Office. He does not file it against Pike. He files it against the no-local-record schedule, and as filed it is an error case: he is alleging that thirteen people were struck from the record without cause. He also learns, from Pell, that the inspection log he spent a day mourning was always available to any clerk who asked for it by its proper name. Before he leaves the hall he copies the **sixteen failing returns** of this quarter by name into his own ledger — the list Pike recites aloud and declines to put in writing — and one of them is Lower Lock. He has no reason yet to fear a list of sixteen. It is only the sixteen places Docketford cannot pay for this quarter, and he is a man who writes things down.

Emotional change: Owen's pride curdles. He has spent fifteen years believing his precision was a form of honesty, and Pell's question shows him that his precision had exactly one reader in mind, and the ward was not in the room.

Power or resource change: A continuance inquiry starts a visible docket and names its filer. Owen now holds two things he chose: a private ledger and a public doubt. He still cannot sign.

Continuity fact to preserve: A continuance inquiry is filed in the filer's name, is public, and suspends nothing. A countersignature review standard is in force at Docketford from this chapter; it is **unattributed here** and is named to the Provisional Council only by the Chapter 10 circular. The two Registry witnesses on the no-local-record schedule are the same two officers who witnessed the transfer, and both remain unnamed institutional roles in this batch. Pike has said that a stability order has to be actionable by Friday; no such order has been signed yet. "No local record" is a positive status, and this chapter is the one that establishes that it was asserted against a place rather than a ward. Pell is twenty-two, junior, and is the first person in the Office to ask who consented. ### Chapter 0005 — Nobody Else Will Perform It — Batch Midpoint

Action or decision: Owen accepts the reassignment without appealing it, because appealing it would put Marguerite's petition back in front of the Deputy Registrar. Then, because the roll has taken the ward's standing and he is the one who put it there, he does the only thing left that costs him nothing to refuse: he copies the Day 14 roll entry into his own ledger in full — the lapse line, the suspension of the pump grant, the freeze — and then, against his own instinct, he copies the ward's finished return out of Marguerite's hands and carries it to Pell's tray, unsigned by anyone qualified to enter it, and leaves it where it can be entered by the next clerk who is allowed. He is not entering it. He is making sure that the delay is a fact on paper and not an accident that looks like one. He writes down what he can carry: the ward's acts, the four dissents, the six names needing protective returns, and — as an experiment rather than a plan — one household he is willing to be responsible for. He tells Nell one true thing about his part in the notice and nothing else, and she gives him the sentence that becomes the volume's engine: you are going to have to ask people to be responsible in public, and you are not going to be able to do it for them. Then, in the drawer he is now trusted with, he finds a brass tab that is not Docketford's: **ALDER ROW — 461**, a ward officially closed four streets from where he sleeps. He is not permitted to open the drawer. He does not open it. He writes the number in his own ledger and puts the pencil down.

Emotional change: Owen is a little less alone and much less in control. The relief is real and so is the shame and he lets both stand without arranging them. The chapter's hardest turn is smaller than the others: he reads the freeze on the roll and understands, in front of witnesses, that the ward's standing is now a thing he has to enter rather than a thing he has to defend. He makes no resolution.

Power or resource change: Owen is a reassigned copy clerk with a private ledger, a public statement against himself, and no authority at all. The ward is worse off by one line on a roll and cannot fix it itself: its pump grant is suspended, its households are frozen, its crossing is unsupported, and its return still cannot be entered. He has formally begun to learn the thing the volume must teach: an unresolved case intrudes. At the unfiled tray a name will not come — a man he has copied for six years — and he finds it again in the ledger a minute later with his own hand beside it. He notices himself noticing, writes the number down, and cannot tell whether writing it down helped. **This is a near miss, not a cost.** Nothing is permanently lost in this batch, because he holds no duty that could take a name; the permanent loss is the price of the Stage 1 named duty and arrives in Batch 0003 and again after Chapter 46. He has not become a Sealed Witness and must not be written as one.

Continuity fact to preserve: Owen is reassigned to the unfiled correspondence tray and has not appealed. The reassignment is an **administrative posting, not a legal suspension**: it takes him off the copying floors and does not touch his standing as co-petitioner, his ability to file in his own name, or his signed statement in the docket. **The Day 14 roll enters the crossing's status as lapsed — no supported district.** The ward's own witnessed ratification is in the file and does not stop the roll: consent to a transfer is not a district performing a duty. The pump grant is suspended, not extinguished. Lower Lock's households cannot be continued, licensed, or witnessed on a district act until a supported district return naming the crossing is entered; nobody's name is struck. The Chapter 8 load-restriction suspension is untouched and still in force, so the crossing is closed to loads and unsupported at once. The petition does not hold any of this. **The cure is the act Batch 0002 opens on:** entering Lower Lock's return on Day 15 lifts the freeze on the ward's standing and restores the crossing's status as a duty the ward performs — and does nothing else, making the crossing neither safe nor open. Lower Lock's return is finished, witnessed, sitting on Pell's tray, and unentered at the end of this chapter; **it is entered in Chapter 11, and its first seven-day review falls due on Day 22.** The Provisional Council's circular names itself as the issuer of the countersignature standard and states the "no community return after the present review" language; both take effect from this chapter. The unattributed Docketford standard of Chapter 3 is now attributed. It arrived sealed at the end of Chapter 9 and is read here. It carries the Council's seal and the Chancellor's countersignature, so the reader learns that a Chancellor exists and issues instruments; the reader learns nothing else about her, and she is never on stage in Volume 01. Who at the Council issued the standard, and why, is not established until Batch 0003. The brass tab reading **ALDER ROW — 461** sits in the drawer Owen now works; he has recorded its number and has not opened the drawer, and the drawer is closed in the ordinary physical sense, so it gives him nothing to perceive. Six of the thirteen entries are children or people unable to answer, and Lower Lock has no protective-return structure. Owen remains a Stage 0 Copy Clerk and signs no continuation, certification, or hold. Pell is under no obligation to him. The four dissenting households are worse off than the nine and are not rescued in this chapter.

Ending type: Earned hook.

Next-chapter pull: Seven days from the docket date, the Office must file its written answer to Marguerite's petition, and the answer it gives is a date rather than a hearing. The freeze does not wait for that answer: the first thing the next batch can do is enter the ward's return, which nobody was qualified to enter in this one, and one of the nine branches will not survive its first review. Pell's tray is holding a supervised case that needs an assisting clerk, and Owen is the only clerk in the hall who has not yet been taken off the copying floors. On the upper floor, a man who has stopped saying the word *maybe* is drafting an instrument on sixteen named returns, and he will not need Owen to be in the room when he signs it.
