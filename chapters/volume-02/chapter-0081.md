# Chapter 81 — Four Papers and No Instrument

The first working day of the season of the Concord Year 489 was the Sunday the second day of the third month, and the schedule had gone up on the Wednesday three days before it with a column in it that nobody in this county could fill, and on the Monday morning of the tenth day Owen Rusk carried four pieces of paper down the hill in a cardboard folder that had been flattened at some point in the last nine years and had to be carried with both hands, and he had chosen the four himself.

He had the choice because the other twenty or so sheets in the district tray on the Friday evening had not been about the season. The tray had a standing at that office and everybody in the building knew what the tray was for. He did not take the tray down to a ward hall. He took four papers, and he told Ada Rusk on the Sunday evening at the hall that he was coming down on the Monday morning with four papers and about half an hour, and she said, "Then it is not a roster night," and he said it was not, and she said, "I will tell them it is not a roster night and they will come anyway," which is what happened, and there were about twenty-two people in the ward hall at about half past seven on the Monday evening with the stove on and the long table cleared and Marguerite Bray at the end of it with the delivery book shut.

Nobody in that hall was asked to sign anything. That was the first thing he said and he said it in the ordinary voice.

"**Nothing on the table is going to be signed by anybody in this room tonight, including me, and I am going to say four things and then I am going to sit down, and it is not a fifth thing, and I know that it is going to sound like a fifth thing.**"

Ada Rusk was at the wall with her arms folded and said nothing, which is what the wall is for.

---

The first paper was the notice of the twenty-sixth day of the second month. He had it on a board because a sheet of foolscap does not stay open.

**SURVEY OF SETTLEMENTS OF RECORD. DOCKETFORD DISTRICT. Season of the Concord Year 489.**

*Field verification of premises of record in this district is suspended… Nine persons are affected in this district. The reason is not given and no form in this county provides for one.*

"Nine persons in four hundred," said Owen Rusk. "**And the four lines under the survey round on that sheet are still empty, and there is still no name on it, and the date on it is still the twenty-sixth, and the Office has not taken it down and has not filled it in, and that is not going to change in June.**"

He put the return down and put his finger on the first of the two notes, which is the one that came back on the Tuesday the thirteenth and is four lines long and is the ordinaryest thing an office has ever written to a district.

*Fifth column not completed. The Office does not accept a sentence in a column reserved for a year. Send again when the column is completed.*

"That is the first one and it is dated the thirteenth," he said. "**I wrote a sentence across ten cells because there was no year in any of them and no survey in this district in this season to put one in, and it came back in four lines, and every word of the four lines was correct, and the third word of the second line is the whole of this season: *a year*.**"

The second was the determination of the twenty-fifth, made on a Sunday morning in a room with high windows by an officer of the count having no district in the matter, and he read the operative words and nothing else, because a hall does not need the rest of it and a hall would get the rest of it wrong.

"No reconciliation shall be conducted in Docketford district in the season of the Concord Year 489 in reliance upon the district return for that season, unless and until that return is completed and current." — and the words *in the matter of an officer of the count having no district in the matter* are on the top of it, and they are the reason a determination of that kind is possible at all, and nobody in Docketford district has ever been inside a room in which that is done.

"That is yours," said Cobb Rennie from the back.

"That is mine," said Owen Rusk. "**I put it there. I want that said in this hall before anything else is said in this hall, and I have said it at the west gate in a field to about thirty people and I am saying it in here because about thirty is a different number from twenty-two and Ada told me in a field on the seventh of the month that everybody in this county has now said the thing about visibility twice and that saying it a third time is the way a thing stops being true.**"

Nobody said anything. The stove ticked.

Then he put the third paper down, and it was the district return for the season of 489, folded once, with two notes on it in the same hand at the same counter, and the second of the notes was the one that mattered and he turned the sheet round so that the hall could see it upside down and read it out.

*Received in the state in which it was made. Figures carried forward. No verification of premises in this district in the season of 489.*

"**That is a return made procedural.** The ten rows on it are the figures of the season before last, carried forward by a machine, for a further year, with no person in any of the ten places, and the fifth column is empty on all ten and is going to be empty in June."

And the fourth paper was the schedule for the season of 489, the printed one, that had been on a frame behind the tanneries for ten days and was on a post at the head of Fetter Lane and had ten settlements on it in five columns and the fourth and the fifth empty on all ten and the tenth row a building, and he did not read it out. He put it on the table face up and left it there, and about nine people in that hall could read it and the rest of them knew it by the shape of it, and the hall did what a hall does with a printed sheet that has been on a post for eight days, which is nothing at all, which is the entire point of a public document.

"**I have brought four papers and I have read them out and I have not been asked a question about any of them, and that is because there is nothing on any of the four that a person can do anything with.**"

---

Then he set out the ways of looking at them, and it took him about eleven minutes, and he had it in his own hand on the back of a sheet from a tide table and had not shown it to anybody, and Ada Rusk had not asked to see it and found out about it along with everybody else, which was the correct way for a sister to be treated by a brother who was not the person who spoke for the ward.

"**One. If nothing is done, the season closes the way the season of 488 closed and the way 484 and 486 closed, and this district is reported short, and the report is in a drawer in Concord Hall, and there is no row for a settlement that was not reconciled, and two places came off the board last season by doing nothing at all, and that is the ordinaryest outcome there is and it is the one that happens.**"

"**Two. If the office ends the survey suspension, then anybody anywhere may bring a challenge that a paper is not current, and stop it, and the fourteen days will run. Warden Kade said that out loud in a field in front of about nine people on the twenty-ninth of the month before last and she was right and she gave it to me in advance and it has cost her nothing to be right and it is going to cost a settlement nobody has been to.**"

"**Three. And this is the one nobody has put on a table yet, including me, until about four days ago. A district may survey its own book at any time it likes. There is nothing in any instrument in force in this county that stops it. The Office has never had any view on the matter and I have been told so in writing, in a form, at a counter, and the form is correct.** It is a desk act. It costs nothing. It takes an afternoon. **And it puts a year in ten cells that means nothing, and the year it puts there is a year a person corrected a piece of paper in a room two miles up this hill, dated and signed.**"

Cobb Rennie said, from the back, "And in about a year somebody will have a year in a column that says somebody looked at something."

"Yes," said Owen Rusk. "**And it will be a better-protected piece of paper than the truth, because it will be dated and signed, and the truth is not either of those things.**"

A man at the back, who is about sixty and has been coming to that hall since the flood, said, "**So who is it we write to.**"

"You write to this office," said Owen Rusk.

"That is what you are," said the man. "**That is not an answer to the question. We have been trying to write to this office since the seventh of the month of rain. We have had a form back with four words on it and a number on it.**"

"Then that is the answer to the question, and it is the correct one, and I have read you the four words and the five clauses under them in this hall on the sixth of this month and about thirty of you heard them, and one of the five clauses is that a disposition is a decision and a decision is entered with the initials of an officer and the day of its making, and the initials were not mine and the day was the sixth and it is in a drawer in this building and it is in a frame behind a tannery with a number and a month on it, and if anybody in this hall wants the number it is on the frame and I will not say it here."

"**I have read it,**" said the man. "**I want somebody to put their name on something.**"

Nobody answered him, and Owen Rusk understood, standing at the front of a ward hall with a flattened folder in his hands, that there was a sixth way of looking at four pieces of paper and he had not said it out loud, and that the sixth way was not a way of looking at the papers at all.

**The sixth way is that a ward of eleven doors has been trying to write to an office for two months and has been answered, four times out of eleven, in four words, and the person who can change that is at the front of the room holding a folder, and he has three active holds and three named claimants and not one of the three is a household in Fetter Lane, and a hold is an answerability and preserves nothing.**

He did not say the sixth way out loud, and that was the correct thing to do, and he worked out about four seconds later that it had been the correct thing to do for the wrong reason, and that the reason was that if he said it out loud in a ward hall then the ward would ask him for it, and if the ward asked him for it he would do it, and there are three holds on a card and the card is the answer to a question he asked himself in a hall two months ago.

"**Four. If the office does nothing and the district does nothing, then on the twenty-eighth of this month this district certifies a count of a season in which it went nowhere and looked at nothing, out of a book of figures that belong to a season before last, and it signs it, and it puts its name to it.**"

He put his hands flat on the table for a second and then took them off it again.

"**Those are four ways of looking at four pieces of paper and none of them is a way out, and I have not pretended otherwise, and I have not got a fifth, and if I had a fifth I would not have come down here tonight.**"

---

Ada Rusk spoke for the first time since about half past seven and it was about four sentences long and she said them in the voice she uses for a roster.

"**I am going to ask for something to be entered in a record and I am going to say the request out loud first, and I want it heard that I am asking and not Owen.**"

Marguerite Bray had her pen out. She had had it out since about half past six, which she did not mention and would not have mentioned.

"**This district asks to be reconciled in the season of the Concord Year 489.** That is the request. It is four months since the season of 488 was certified and it went qualified with two places off it, and one of them is this district, and there has been no day named and none will be. I want it written down that we asked."

Owen Rusk said, "**Ada—**"

"You said the first thing," said Ada Rusk. "Say the rest of it and say all of it, and I have thought about it for a day and a half."

He said the rest of it. It took him about forty seconds and it was the correct answer and it was the answer of an office and not of a man.

"**There is no instrument.** A filing may be made by a person. A challenge may be brought by a person. A review may be asked for by a person, and a request may be made by a person, and every act in this county that gets anything done is an act somebody does as a person at an address where they may be asked. **This district is a ward. It is not a person. It is not in a settlement, because Docketford district is a district and not a settlement, and I have written that sentence on an application in the first month of this year and underlined it. There is no form on which a ward may ask to be reconciled, there is no form on which a settlement may ask to be reconciled, and there is no form on which a place may ask for a day.**"

He stopped. He had about four seconds and he used them, and what he said in them was not to anybody.

"**And the bar is up anyway. The Warden may reconcile a settlement on a day she names and she has named none, and I asked her for a day in the first month of this year and she refused me and she was right twice.**"

"So that is the answer," said Ada Rusk.

"That is the answer."

"Then say the next part, because you have not said it, and Marguerite is holding the pen."

Owen Rusk said the next part.

"**I am the officer of the record of the district returns. A filing made by me is a filing of this office and not of any person, and I may not file a thing on behalf of a place, and if I did it would be the district office asking for something and the district office is the office the count is administered out of and the office that made the ten rows.**"

Cobb Rennie said, quietly, to nobody, "**Third building.**"

"I heard it," said Owen Rusk.

"**I said it to nobody. I have been saying it to nobody all season and I have got very good at it and it is the only thing I have got.**"

---

Marguerite Bray read what Owen Rusk had said back twice before she wrote it, because that is the third clause of the rule they work by, and then she wrote it in the delivery book in her own hand with the day and the hour on it, and read the ruling out, because she reads a ruling out.

**Ruled the tenth day of the third month, in the hall, with about twenty-two persons present. That the ward of Lower Lock has asked to be reconciled in the season of the Concord Year 489, and that there is no instrument in this county on which a ward may ask for anything, and that the officer of the record of the district returns has said so in this hall in the correct words, and that this is the first request of its kind made by this ward in nine years and it was made in the ward's own name and not by any person in this room on its behalf.**

Then she stopped, and she waited, because there was a second line and she had a rule about that too and nobody had asked her for it.

Then Ada Rusk said, "Write the other one."

Marguerite Bray wrote it and read that out as well.

**And it is ruled that a steward's book is not a summary. That this ward's book is fifty-one years in this family and thirty-six in this hand, and that it has outgrown the Office's copy of this ward's case, and that it is the only place in Lower Lock where a fact about this ward is not a summary. That a request which no instrument in this county will carry has been carried here, with the day and the hour, and that it is the first time any person has written down that this ward asked for anything from the count.**

Nobody thanked anybody, and nobody has thanked anybody in that hall in nine years, and the reason is in the book and the reason is that a thank you is a receipt.

Then Ada Rusk said the thing that was in the ward's book from the first month of the year, in about four seconds, and it was not about the record.

"**And none of those four is any use to us, and I want that said while the paper is still warm, because in about four years somebody is going to read that this ward asked to be reconciled and is going to assume that asking was the hard part.**" She unfolded her arms. "**We did not ask to be counted. We asked because there is a column in this season with nothing in it and a schedule with our name on the third line and nobody has been to us since the ninth of the month before last, and in about four years the reason for that will be a piece of paper and I would like the piece of paper to have one line in it that says we asked and were told there was nothing to ask with.**"

She looked round the hall.

"**And you are all waiting for me to tell you what the fifth one is, and the fifth one is that we are going to be on a printed list at the end of this month with nothing in the fourth column and nothing in the fifth, and there is not one person in Concord Hall who is going to be able to do a single thing about it, and I have known that since the Wednesday.**"

Somebody said, "**Then what is it for.**"

"**It is for the marking on it,**" said Ada Rusk. "**The marker goes up on the Thursday and on the Friday at seven forty and there is a figure on it and a person wrote that figure because they had been at the flats and looked at the water, and anybody who walks down this lane can go and look at the flats and see whether the figure is right. That is the only figure anybody in this ward produces that anybody can check. And I have understood something about that this season and I am not going to say it out loud at half past eight on a Monday evening, and Cobb can have it when he has earned it, and Marguerite can rule it when it comes.**"

"**And the crossing is shut to loads on Thursday at seven forty and it has been shut since the month of rain and the repair is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and it is unfunded, and the pump goes at four in the morning on Thursday by Bram and by a household with a girl of twelve in it, and the marker goes up on Thursday and on Friday, and the channel is done on Wednesday night because the channel is a Wednesday, and none of the four of those things is a reconciliation and all four of them are going to happen.**"

Cobb Rennie said, "**And the granary's been empty five weeks and a bit.**"

"It has been empty since the seventh day of the second month," said Marguerite Bray, "and I ruled the figure on the Sunday the second and it was zero, and the fourth week running of zero is on the Thursday afternoon of the sixth in this book in my hand, and I am not going to shorten it, and on Wednesday night I am going to count the bins again because the Wednesday is when the channel is done and I do it with a lamp because I am sixty-one and everybody in this hall knows I do it with a lamp."

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"**Thursday, then,**" said Ada Rusk, "at seven forty, at the marker," and that was the end of it, and about nine people in the hall stood up at about a quarter to nine, and Ivy Sarr was at the end of the long table with a tray of seed trays in front of her and did not get up, and Owen Rusk carried the four papers back up the hill in the flattened folder with both hands and did the tray, and the tray was on at a quarter past nine and had been on since about nine with nothing on it from anywhere, and it is the same tray it has been on every evening since the first of the month.

**The count of the season of 489 for Docketford district is certified on the twenty-eighth day of this month.** He had the date off the printed notice on the post at the head of the lane and he had known it since the twenty-eighth of the month before last, and it is the eighteenth day away, and he wrote it on the back of a sheet from a tide table in the standing order and then read the sheet back and did not send it, because there is nobody to send it to and because a date in a drawer is a date in a drawer.

**Three holds and three named claimants and no fourth.** Ivy Sarr's household, of two rooms in Fetter Lane, a person who cannot be asked. Mavis Rudge, of Witherow. Ned Callow, of Quill Bank, who asked for a person in a room and will not ask again. A hold is an answerability and preserves nothing and not one of the three is a household in Fetter Lane.

**And a ward of about eleven doors at the bottom of a hill that has asked this office for the first time in nine years to do a single thing for it, and has been told, in the correct words, in front of twenty-two people, that there is no instrument in this county on which it may ask.**

He had asked the hall a question and the hall had answered it and the answer had been four ways of looking at four pieces of paper, and then the ward had produced the fifth, which was a Tuesday, and then a Wednesday night, and then a Thursday at seven forty, and the fifth was the only one of the five that anybody in that hall could act on, and it is not a reconciliation, and it is not on a printed list, and no office in Concord Hall is going to hear about it in any season at all.
