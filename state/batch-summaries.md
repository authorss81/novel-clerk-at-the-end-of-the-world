# Batch Summaries

One compact section per completed batch, newest last. This file is the rolling batch memory. Keep each section short enough to read in under a minute; the detail belongs in `state/chapter-summaries.md` and `state/continuity.md`.

## Volume 01 — The Unfiled Ward

### Batch 0001 — Chapters 1–10, *The Omission* (written)

Fourteen days of story time, Days 1–14 of the second and third weeks of the rain season, C.Y. 487. Day 1 is a Tuesday, so the ward ratification (Day 7) and the continuation roll (Day 14) both fall on Mondays. Reversal at Chapter 5, batch climax at Chapter 9, aftermath at Chapter 10, where the Day 14 roll enters the crossing's status as lapsed and freezes the ward.

**Goal.** Take an apparent clerical omission and turn it into a public, docketed question: whether a neighbourhood can file its own return.

**What happened.** Owen refuses the Fourth Desk countersignature on the day he finds a *transfer* entry where Lower Lock's return should be, and proves one real defect in it — the annex schedule is dated two days before the transfer was received — using the batch's single margin notice. At Arbour Kitchen Nell Arbour shows him the second leaf of the annex and destroys his frame: the Office's claim is about a *place*, and a ward cannot answer a claim about a place by saying it exists. In the ward hall Marguerite Bray's delivery book shows the transfer was never a proposal; it was a receipt of a claim signed by Tal Sarr for his own household while alive, eleven months ago, and the Office received it then. The receipt is sound, so Owen withdraws his own provable error in writing and reclassifies the case from **error** to **scope**. He then drafts a ward notice whose every word is true and omits the crossing, and the ward ratifies the transfer nine to four. The ward builds a real district return with nine separately consenting adult branches, two outside witnesses, and a scope claiming a *duty* and expressly nothing about safety, condition or use. Pike closes the crossing to loads on a correct engineering finding, a night inspection puts Dory Anstruther in the spillway, and Owen writes a real forgery, copies it into his own ledger, and tears it up in front of Pell. Pell refuses his short-form attestation in a corridor; Marguerite files her own petition; the Office must answer in writing by Day 19. On Day 14 the roll lapses the crossing's status, suspends the pump grant, and freezes every household in the ward.

**What changed.** Lower Lock is on the Glass Stair docket, in its own name. Owen is a co-petitioner, a public signatory against himself, and posted to the tray of unfiled correspondence. The ward's return is finished, witnessed, and unentered on Pell's tray with a covering slip and a date. The Council has closed the local-return route and a seven-day answer clock is running.

**What it cost.** The run (ten weeks) is broken. Owen's standing in his own ward is at zero. The four dissenters are named in the ward's own book forever. Dory is in hospital. The granary is four weeks from empty. The crossing is closed *and* unsupported, which is worse than either.

**What is now owed.** Enter the return on Day 15. Answer the petition by Day 19. Six protective returns nobody can build. Cobb Rennie's pension, untouched by any of this. Owen's next name, and the one after that.

### Batch 0002 — Chapters 11–20, *The Ward's Own Return* (written)

Thirteen days of story time, Days 15–27 of the third and fourth weeks of the rain season, C.Y. 487. Day 15 is a Tuesday, so the answer lands on a **Saturday** (Day 19), the chapter about the index runs on a **Sunday** (Day 20), the review falls on a **Friday** (Day 25), and the batch ends on a **Sunday** (Day 27). Reversal at Chapter 16, batch climax in the political register at Chapter 18, reckoning at Chapter 19, closing discovery at Chapter 20.

**Goal.** Enter a return nobody can enter, find out what the Office will do with it, and keep nine people in a document together for one week.

**What happened.** The entry is a set of checks in an order, and the third one is that a clerk named in the case or living in the district may not enter it. Owen is appointed an **assisting clerk** to the second desk's supervised tray by the seal stair, in a card that is a posting and not a qualification and that forbids him to sign a continuation, a certification, a suspension or a hold. He narrows the return twice in a day — once in its composition, once in its **effect**, reading all five printed clauses aloud and entering two. District Return **487-LL** is entered on the fifteenth; the freeze on the households lifts; the crossing's status returns as a duty and as nothing else; the pump grant stays suspended; the load restriction is untouched; the Day 14 roll entry stands unamended. Pell has read the effect field and warns that he has made the instrument unimpeachable rather than strong.

Then the machinery shows its face. A **district return is reviewed by the district office**, which is the Office. The Office's written answer, due on the nineteenth, gives a **date in the last week of a rain season nobody has fixed the end of** — a date that cannot be missed and therefore cannot be wrong and therefore cannot be appealed — and records the return as **accepted for review, not binding**, with the observers' separability expressly not determined. Owen files the one objection that is free, the answer improves, and it gets worse. On a Sunday at the Front Desk index he traces the transfer's route to a **county intake number** at the Fetter Bend sub-table, finds **ALDER ROW — 461** in the same intake series with an index card reading *never opened*, is written into a request register himself, learns that a copy of an intake volume in a private notebook is a thing a man may be searched for, and does not take it. Wenna Sarr asks him one question about her niece, and the answer is that a protective return needs five parts, that the ward has three, and that two of the five are Registry functions nobody is paid to do.

A challenge is raised against Pell's independence. It is correct. She sustains it against herself, in her own hand, on the day, and does it first, and stays on the instrument; Owen refuses to guess at the filer and goes to Voss instead, who puts a third reservation about himself in his own ink. Pike builds Stability Order 114 in a corridor — *lateness* rather than *failure*, and the word *fund* — and signs it on the twenty-fourth at ten past four, suspending sixteen named returns together, Lower Lock sixth. Ada Rusk dictates a four-clause amendment and the return survives it. **Nessa Kray withdraws her branch** in a hall on the twenty-third, in her own words, for a reason she can state, and the return narrows by one page and stands. The first seven-day review, adjourned by the challenge, is completed on the twenty-fifth: a duty is not a status, a suspended district can still perform one, the return is **renewed for review only** on terms the ward can meet, and the two observers are re-separated in writing, in two documents now, in two hands. In a delivery book Marguerite Bray has ruled for thirty-six years, three returned refusal notices turn out to carry, in her own pencil, the words *ward public works — schedule wanted*; the ninth is the ninth because the ninth is the day he asked — and Owen has to admit he read that day-book at the gap on the Sunday before and walked away from it.

**What changed.** The ward has an instrument. Eight branches, four recorded dissents, a withdrawal on a shelf, an amendment, a review cycle, a stability order on a public docket, and a date that cannot be computed. Owen has an assisting clerk's card, a named duty, and the knowledge that the instrument is now larger than his opinion of it.

**What it cost.** A cut hand. Two trays' worth of unpaid work. The ward's granary heading for three weeks. A stability order signed on its ward. Ada's class of persons who may object, which excludes her and three others for ever. Kray's name, forever, as the first person in the ward to walk off something. And Owen's own book, which now knows more about him than the Office does.

**What is now owed.** The three refused requests are explained at last, and the withdrawn charge is not reinstated and cannot be. The appeal route's far end is still empty. Six protective returns have three parts and not five. Cobb Rennie is still on no roll. Alder Row is still shut. The Registry's precedent is still on the shelf somewhere.

### Batch 0003 — Chapters 21–30, *The Amended Name* (written)

Twelve days of story time, Days 28–39 of the fourth week of the rain season, C.Y. 487. Day 1 was a Tuesday, so the batch opens on a **Monday (28)**, the precedent is read on the **Wednesday (30)** afternoon, the ward agrees its terms on the **Thursday (31)** and the renewal falls on a **Friday (32)**, the comparison is printed on a **Saturday (33)**, the standard is exposed on a **Monday (35)**, the name is found on a **Wednesday (37)**, and the ward performs on a **Thursday (38)** and renews on a **Friday (39)**.

**Goal.** Get the ward's next seven days into a shape that will survive a review, and find out what a redaction batch is when you are not the person in it.

**What happened.** Marta Rusk's residence status sits in **Redaction Batch 471/Q4/19** — struck on the twelfth of the fourth quarter of C.Y. 471, forty-one entries, one sitting, one officer, ground *revision of residence status, flood quarter arrears*, and the initials on the line are **H.L.P.**. A residence status places a person at an address for priority medicine, priority ration and burial; hers was **pending revision for eleven days**, and the eleven days is not the fever, and the eight months of disputed burial is a different and longer figure. Owen takes away a batch number, an initial and a date, copies nothing, and tells nobody. The same interval is on all forty-one rows, and three of the other forty numbers are numbers he handled inside nine years off route lists, two of them quarterly roll notices and one a **form 12-B** carried against the same entry number — which is not one of the forty-one and is not about a residence status. Pell gives him the procedure and the whole of it is forty separate people each having to ask.

On Day 30 the Registry's own **precedent 37/C** is found, two hundred and nine words, recording the Office recognising a community return and entering a **maintenance grant** of a hundred and forty pounds a year against a named public work, on four grounds: a roll, two witnesses, performance in public, and **a published schedule of the office's refusals**. The batch's midpoint lands inside it: the precedent does not compel the Office to honour a return. It compels the Office, before it refuses one, to have published a schedule of what it has refused. The fight is not to be forgiven. It is to be **listed**.

Ada Rusk reads it before Owen does, works out that the ward satisfies three of the four grounds and cannot satisfy the fourth, and negotiates **five terms** for public performance in front of twenty-two people — a printed **roster**, two witnesses, any outsider may read it on any day without notice, any person may complain, and the roster is performed whatever the return does. Marguerite Bray attaches **the condition**: *and the wage* — *wage not funded*. Owen is stopped in front of the room and does not defend himself. Ada then sends him to find a stranger, and he is told **no kindly twice**, and gets a conditional yes for one day from **Ivet Sterne**, who is not an inspector and has six written conditions better than anything he could have written — the third of which he nearly breaks by claiming a request the ward had not yet made, and only does not because he tells her the truth about the minute in the delivery book instead.

On the Saturday Owen inspects the documents produced in his own petition and finds the district office's **refusal column** in extract: **one hundred and nine entries** in the quarter and **forty-one dispositions that are the same four words**. He does not file. He prints a comparison on a public table with a queue in front of it and no conclusion, and makes enemies who are not enemies yet. On the Monday he finds the countersignature review standard on the district office's Schedule of Instruments in Force — **issued by the Provisional Council in the Concord Year 44, amended never** — with recitals that are sound, and the **roll of countersignature**: nineteen names, district-exclusion, and Pell fourth on it, appointed at nineteen because she was the only person free on a Tuesday, and unable to get off it without leaving the next instrument with one observer.

On Day 35 the string **VANE, H.** — the receiving officer in the third column of a covering slip, four hundred times a quarter for nine years — stops arriving. It comes back twice, fails twice, and on the fourth occasion he finds it on a returned quarterly works notice in the unrouteable tray, twice returned, *not known at the address*, and the finding is worse than losing it, because there is no first name for the man anywhere in Docketford and the city has been writing to three letters and a surname for nine years.

On Day 37 the sub-table's count comes out at **nine refusals** against the named place, the ninth being the one Owen surrendered, and Pell tells him he is not the injured party, and he puts the sheet on the ward's table and gives it to nobody, and **Marguerite Bray** rules in the delivery book that she will ask the seven that are hers, one at a time, in the hall.

On Day 38 the ward names the day itself, on its own paper, in Ada's name, and **performs in public** before a stranger: marker, channel, pump, standpipe rota, the granary double count written up in full, and the roster read aloud. Ada names the fright before they begin — a roster is a document about every hour of everybody in it and it is never about the bridge. Owen's one job is to read back what the checker wrote; he summarises, Ada stops him in front of twenty-four people, and the second reading is right. The act is entered on the Friday, the return is renewed for review for the third time in a fortnight, and nothing is won and everything has been done.

**What changed.** The ward has performed in public, on a calendar, with a roster, before a person from another district, and the act is in the return. It is the ward's first **repeatable** act rather than its first claim, and it is the ground the Registry's own precedent requires. Owen has a schedule of forty-one identical dispositions on a public table, a precedent number, an issued Council instrument with recitals, a redacted batch, and a name-shaped hole in his working week.

**What it cost.** A permanent, unpayable loss of a name that used to arrive four hundred times a quarter. A roster that makes eight people's hours reviewable by any stranger who turns up. A wage of four months that is not funded and is not funded anywhere in the volume. A granary that has come down from **three weeks and a bit to two weeks and a bit** in ten days, which is the first time the figure has gone down inside a month and completes a sentence Marguerite Bray wrote in a pump book on Day 28. An Office that now has to answer about forty-one dispositions. And Owen's own relationship with his instrument: he finds that the ward did not need him and the document needed him, and that those are different things.

**What is now owed.** A hearing that does not exist. Pike's real justification. A separate branch for the four dissenters. Ada's wage. Lound's second circular. The brass tab, shut. Six protective returns with three parts of five. Cobb Rennie's pension, still unreachable. The far end of the appeal route, still empty. And Marta Rusk's record, which is not amended and is owed to Chapters 46–50.

### Batch 0004 — Chapters 31–40, *Public Appeal* (written)

Fourteen days of story time, Days 40–53 of the sixth and seventh weeks of the rain season, C.Y. 487. Day 1 was a Tuesday, so the batch opens on a **Saturday (40)**, the runner's question is put on a **Monday (42)**, the application is entered on a **Tuesday (43)**, the Council's circular is broken on the **seal stair on a Wednesday (44)**, the Alder Row line is read out on a **Thursday (45)**, the fourth renewal and the branch are on a **Friday (46)**, the office answers in public on a **Saturday (47)**, the far end of the route is asked for on a **Sunday (48)**, the ward files on a **Tuesday (50)**, and the hearing date is fixed on a **Wednesday (51)** and the fifth renewal falls on a **Friday (53)**.

**Goal.** Get an answer out of the Office about the forty-one dispositions before anybody can shape it, and find out whether a man who has been right in public all month is also useful in public.

**What happened.** The Office answers the comparison in **five clauses and a stamp with no name on it**, and every clause is true: a disposition is a decision, a decision is entered with initials and a date, a disposition is not a reason, and the office keeps no column of reasons and is not required to. It restores nothing, explains nothing, and it is the answer to this question in every quarter from now on. Owen reads it out in the ordinary way to nine people and **prints nothing further** — a second sheet is drafted in his coat and left there, because a room that has seen a man print twice has learned to expect it. Pell identifies the person who will be asked about the forty-one: a **schedule of duties** on the board by the second counter, a name against a duty, not a villain and not yet an enemy, and a woman who will write better forms because she is frightened. Ada refuses to let the ward hold a second sheet in Docketford and gives him the month's real work instead — a paper of the ward's own, in the ward's own words, which he is to carry and not to write.

Pike's justification is put in front of a runner's pencil on the Monday, and it is **real**: in the second month of the Concord Year 474 the district engineer found four hundred yards of Fetter Lane bank would go in that year's flood, the withdrawal was signed on the ninth, **one hundred and eighty-one people** were moved off it, and the water came on the thirteenth and the ground was clear. **Twenty-two people were drowned on the seventh** on ground that was not cleared, in districts whose offices could not compel a man to move a chair. It is not in the instrument because *the instrument is about a place*, and a withdrawal of a place records what was done to the place and not what a river did. Pike instructs the runner to write both halves himself, including *I did not lie* and *I would sign it again*. Owen does not spend the sentence he came to say. He records it once in the ward's hearing statement and **Ada strikes it out** and writes four lines with no numbers in them about the standpipe at four in the morning; Bray rules both into the book in one page. And the discovery at the bottom of the hill: the precedent, the schedule and the maintenance grant were produced by an office ashamed of what it had done in a flood year, which does not make them wrong and makes them the work of frightened people.

**Marguerite Bray files the application**, in her own name and in her own words, after standing at a Glass Stair counter four times in her life, having gone and got a form from the Mill Ward annex herself. She asks for a **week and not a period** and the clerk stops, because nobody in that building has ever written a week in that box. It is **entered, not granted**; the district office objects to nothing at first, which the counter clerk identifies as an office declining to spend a day. The hardest sentence she says to Owen is **do not come on the day** — a room with him in it is a room looking at a Registry clerk instead of a steward. Ada then files **her own objection, in her own name**, asking that the hearing be confined to the return and the duty claimed in it, on the ground that the one thing she is competent to speak to is the abutment, and a district that vouches the condition of a work becomes a return about a bridge. She has made herself unwithdrawable on purpose. Owen has nine ways of making it the ward's and every one of them overrules her, and he uses none of them.

The Council's **second circular** is broken on the seal stair on the Wednesday. It names the **review** and not the hearing, says the Council has no jurisdiction over the interval and does not propose to acquire one, and was **issued on the day the application was entered**. It asks for three lists — the schedule of performed acts with names and hours, the ground of the second examination, and the disposition forms used in the quarter with their counts — and its second sheet says the district's public performance on a roster before a person of another district **requires watching**. That is how the Office learns the Council is watching the appeal, and what it does about it is **get better**: a separation of interest requested in writing before it was asked for, the first request sent to Lower Lock in six weeks, the Friday review moved to a long room where papers may be produced and read, and papers produced early by name. Owen reads the whole circular out in a hall of thirty and Ada says the true thing: the Council has asked for the ward's own paper, and the ward has been making three lists for a month without being asked, and both being pleased and being frightened are correct.

On the Thursday, putting names to the seven household references, **Marguerite Bray finds that the seventh is not a house.** The column says *place* and the place is **Alder Row**, and her inward column for the seventeenth day of a month in the intake year records one folded sheet brought to the ward door in the wet, a return made out for Alder Row with two leaves and no witness, asked this ward to witness it, **not witnessed, returned the same night** — and under it, in a hand that is not Bray's, Ada's or Owen's, ten minutes of a bad slow hand: *I am a man of Alder Row. My name is my own. I am asking you to sign that you have seen me.* He wrote that because Bray wrote a rule for it, twenty-two years ago, for a man who could not read what the office sent him, and it has been used about nine times since. The return was then **received for intake as a number in a column, in the same unbroken column as the transfer, four weeks later, and never examined.** The beat is the filing, not the geography: a ward refused it correctly, a receiving table did the only thing it is built to do, and the not examining was the **default and not a decision**, which is what *never opened* on the index card has always meant. The drawer is not opened and the cord is not touched. Owen admits the frightening half out loud — that he felt it in his hand, because a line can be picked up and a bridge cannot.

At the **fourth** renewal the reviewing officer says the sentence that governs the batch: *a route with nobody standing at the far end of it is a piece of paper with an address on it*, and asks the district, once, in writing, to name a person. In the hall the four dissenters — **Ada Rusk, Bram Tillet, Holm Ferris, Wenna Sarr** — have read everything and worked out what Owen has not: the ward's new paper is the first document about the ward in four years that none of them is in. Wenna's line is the chapter's: *you have all become legible and I have not.* Ada offers **a separate branch** in a hall, in her words, and explains the mechanism: a branch is added to a return by a **district office naming its holder in writing**, which is the one door, and it opens both ways, because the class of persons who may object is **the eight branches plus any person the office names in writing — eight, never nine.** It is not a pardon, and Owen is wrong about that in front of twenty-two people and is corrected in one sentence. **Bram Tillet refuses it on the evening it is offered**, because being read by somebody who can stop is not the same as being read by somebody who can stay. Four **blank** pages go into the ward's book with the four names at the top, to be answered by the day of the hearing, and Ada will not chase anybody.

The Office answers the comparison **in public** on the Saturday, with a queue in it, and adds the clause that it will not answer a comparison as a filing in any other quarter. Owen puts the argument in about ninety words without a sheet — a district performing in public can show the face of a duty, an office refusing without a reason cannot show the face of a refusal, and the only witnesses to what an office decides are offices and districts, and the districts are not doing it because **being legible is not the same as being paid**. Pell tells him the answer is a **form**, and that a form is kept because it is *sufficient*, and that he has made himself a standing item and lost the ability to be surprised in a room. Standing in a lane he works out the truest sentence of the batch: the forty-one are not stopped and not even slowed, and **visibility is not remedy**.

Nell refuses to go and ask anybody, and tells him why: the women at Coal Hill and Wexford Landing have not spoken in two years and she will not make the two of them into a thing that happened between her and them, on a Registry clerk's errand, in order to get a man in Lower Lock a Wednesday. She restates her four sentences and adds the distinction the batch needed: she has said them three times as a **comfort**, and a comfort is done in a kitchen and can be withdrawn on a Thursday; a **standing** is done in a room where you are not the subject and cannot be withdrawn. Owen walks a mile in the rain to Coal Hill and asks badly, and **Hester Lund**, nineteen years on that tide table, says no, and tells him the question was wrong in the exact words a Registry uses: *nobody stands at the far end of a route; what is at the end of a route is a name on a page.* What the name does is stand in a room and say *this is what the ward did, on this day, here is the document, and I have read it* — and its only power is that it is the ward's own, and its only cost is that a name goes on a page, **which is what a district return is made of.** She gives **seven conditions**, keeps the sixth to herself (*if I tell you how it ended then you will be careful with me, and a careful person is a person who has been got at*), and sends him away with the real question in **fifteen words: a standing in the room with a document on the table in front of it.** Bray rules the name into the delivery book with the day and the hour. The condition is a **standing**, not a hearing and not an appearance, and the ward's written request must reach the woman from the hall door and not out of a coat.

The ward files **its own case** on the Tuesday afternoon in eleven minutes with no Registry in it: a schedule of performed acts with a covering page, two observers' names, a withdrawal right, four weeks of hours, and four **blank** pages sent blank on purpose. Ada states the volume's hinge first — *the moment we put the crossing in, the roster stops being a fact and becomes a claim, and a claim is what the transfer was* — and the honest answer to the Council's second question is hers: **the district cannot be examined by anybody who lives in it**, and glad is not the same as enough. Bray asks the **first** of her seven in the hall and records the number and not the answer. Owen is the covering, reads the bundle back with his hand on the page, and is stopped twice.

The **date is fixed on Day 51 by the clerk of the docket**, in her own hand, with her initials in the box: **the fifty-ninth of the month at ten in the morning, in the long room, at the Glass Stair**, open to objection for four days — and Owen discovers that **a day can be wrong**, which is the one thing a category could not be, and that the wall he spent six weeks building was the thing that stopped a date from being anybody's fault. **Stability Order 114 goes before it on the same docket, to be heard the same day, and is not merged with it.** The two documents the ward has been given remain apart: the Day 19 answer gave a **receipt**, the docket has given a **hearing**, a hearing is not a receipt, and a clerk is not permitted to join them. Nobody in Lower Lock has thought that **the fifty-ninth is a Thursday** — the marker day, the pump day, the only day of the week on which anybody in this ward can be shown to have done anything at all — and it happened by accident, and Wenna Sarr will not be glad about it yet and asks for that to be ruled. The return is renewed for review for the fifth time, next due Day 60, and nobody in the room mentions the hearing.

**What changed.** The hearing is **set and dated**, with the order before it and the ward's own schedule on the file, and every part of it belongs to somebody other than the Registry: the application is Bray's, the objection is Ada's, the day is a clerk's, the evidence is the ward's, and the room will be argued by people rather than by a clerk. The four dissenters hold something of their own — a branch offered in a hall, one refusal on the record with its reason, and four blank pages travelling to Concord Hall on purpose. The far end of the route is **conditionally filled** by a woman with a name on a page and seven conditions, and the filling was done after Owen was told no kindly three times by people who owed him nothing. The Office's answer on the forty-one is on a public table and in a form, and it is correct and grants nothing, and the practice is now **visible and not stopped**. And the ward's roster has become the first document in this case that the Office asked for and the ward can give, in the ward's own hand.

**What it cost.** Owen is now **a standing item** in a public hall and can no longer be surprised in a room, which is a real loss nobody warns a man about. He has made **an enemy who is not an enemy yet** — a name on a schedule of duties, frightened, good at her work. He has a reputation in a building where the corridor used to be worth more than the hall. He is a **coverer** of documents and a **witness** to things and the **far end of nothing**, and on the night the date arrives he finds that having the thing he wanted for a month is **boring**, and that the only thing in him that answers it is the old fear, and that he would not trade the boring for the old fear. And the granary has fallen from **two weeks and a bit to just over two weeks** in nine days, on a bin that went in a corner, and Bray has written *and then it will be a fortnight* under it.

**What is now owed.** The **hearing itself**, on the fifty-ninth, and it is argued by people. The **temporary preservation hold** and the **narrow return**, and **Stage 1, Sealed Witness**, which is acquired at the Chapter 46 certification and not before. The **crossing hold doing one thing and one thing only**. The **forced public appeal** of Stability Order 114. **Marta Rusk's record**, to be amended with the delay recorded as a delay and nothing restored. **Pell's and Owen's continuances separated again in writing.** The four dissenters' pages, two of which are still blank and one of which has a no on it. Hester Lund's letter, in the ward's bag, and her answer to it. The far end of the route being a real name on a page rather than a piece of paper with an address on it. The six protective returns with three parts of five. Cobb Rennie's pension, still unreachable. Ada's wage, unfunded. The sixteen, still sixteen. The **brass tab, shut**, with *never opened* on the card behind it. The **nine dispatches** sitting unread in the drawer, on a floor he is not cleared for. The **Alder Row filing**, which is a filing and not a mystery, and which he has told nobody in Docketford he has read. The **precedent's four hundred and forty-three years** of unamended recitals, which remain right. And a name that does not come, which is not coming back, and which nobody is ever to be told about.

### Batch 0005 — Chapters 41–50, *The Unfiled Ward* (written) — **VOLUME 01 CLOSES HERE**

Seventeen days of story time, **Days 54–70**, the seventh week of the rain season and the first week after it, C.Y. 487. Day 54 is a Saturday and the flood comes in the small hours; Day 59 is the Thursday of the hearing; Day 60 is the Friday of the sixth renewal and the narrowing; Days 61–63 are Saturday to Monday and carry the certification; Days 66–67 are the public appeal of the order; Days 68–69 are the amendment and the four pages; Day 70 is a Monday and the last chapter of the volume. Turn at Chapter 43, cost at Chapter 45, climax at Chapter 46, cost arriving at Chapter 47, resolution at Chapters 49–50.

**Goal.** Get through the flood, get the return narrow enough to be true, certify it, and find out what winning the argument is actually for.

**What happened.** The last water of the season comes up on the night of Day 54 and the ward performs in it anyway: the pump is primed by hand on a Saturday and takes on the seventh stroke at twenty to six, a girl of twelve carries a pail on a stick up Fetter Lane twice before six in the morning, a man of fifty-eight is on the bucket chain, and the marker goes in at eight in the morning in the rain with three people on a path above the second bend. **The seed stall at the bottom of the pitch is gone**, and it is gone because it is in neither leaf of the annex and never was, and a woman of seventy-one who has held that pitch for forty-one years is owed nothing and says so in a hall. Owen's only office on the night is to read back what a checker wrote, and **Nessa Kray stops him** when he softens *gone* into *damaged*. Two days later the granary write-up comes out at **a fortnight** and Bray rules the six words she wrote on Day 45 and then writes the next line. ### Batch 0005 — review fix pass, first round

**Ten findings, ten fixes, nothing restarted.** One reviewer pass over the finished batch; no chapter rewritten, no plot moved, no arc touched, and the register and paragraph shape left as they were. Two findings were errors in the prose that had already hardened into the state files and into the close prompt, and in both the prose was made to agree with the canon.

- **The seal day was impossible and the state files were right.** Chapter 46 had the certification on the Saturday of Day 61 with a term ending on Day 70. **The chapter now spans two days, as its own batch card said it should: the question is asked on the Saturday of Day 61, and the long room, the boxes, the examinations, the hold, the list, the signature and the hill are the Monday afternoon of Day 63, sealed at about half past three.** Chapter 47's eight *Saturday* references became *Monday*; the one hall *on Monday* became the Friday hall the chapter itself describes; Owen's third box now owns the fact that the flood of Day 54 sits outside the seven days the sheet asks for; and Chapter 50 ends with **about forty minutes** on a certification, where it had about an hour at ten minutes to three. **At ten past two it is still about an hour and that line is untouched.**
- **Chapter 43 denied the fifth part** and said *three of the four*. **The five are enumerated — witnesses, care act, appeal route, a named place, a named person** — the review and the reservation of consent are conditions and not parts, and **the two missing parts are the place and the person**, which is exactly what Sarr names in the Chapter 48 hearing. - **Pike was answering a verb Owen had not used.** The eight words are now said in the room: *and did you hide them inside a favourable reading, and sign it?*
- **The one meta reference in the batch is gone.** The two forty-ones are not a thing said in a hall; they are Owen's own arithmetic at half past six on the evening of Day 39, in his own book, in no file.

- **Hester Lund's standing is fifteen words** in both places. **The certification's permission is no longer proved by re-defining the card's nouns** — the card says *a certification*, he signs one, and the answer is the box that says *this one has no office in it*. **A typo** (*fortysixth*) and **a crowd count** (about thirty in the hall, not twenty-six) are corrected.

## Volume 01 — *The Unfiled Ward* (Chapters 1–50, C.Y. 487) — closed

**The sentence the volume is about.** A ward can be saved only by becoming legally responsible for itself, and the people who did the work were never the people he was carrying, and a man who keeps a thing alive by holding on to it has no business telling a ward what it may perform. Ada Rusk said the last of those in the fourth week of the month in a hall in front of twenty-two people, and Owen Rusk has been living inside it ever since.

**The arc in four moves.** A valid transfer receipt is used far beyond what one dead man's signature could give, and the case reclassifies from **error** to **scope**; Owen proves the one defect he can and surrenders it on purpose, because a correctable error is how the office closes a file. The ward then discovers it can only be saved by filing a return for itself, and that a return is a set of **separately consenting adult branches** that narrows and does not collapse. The Registry's own **Precedent 37/C** compels publication rather than honour, so the fight is not to be forgiven but to be **listed** — and listing works: a hundred and nine entries with forty-one identical dispositions are now on a public table with a queue in front of them, answered by a form that is correct and sufficient and will therefore never be improved. And finally the ward gives things up. It is the giving up, not the winning, that makes the instrument certifiable.

**The two fixed numbers.**
- **The scope clause is four words and it is the ward's own: *Duty, and no status.*** Fixed in Chapter 46 in Ada Rusk's capitals, carried in Chapters 47–50, and it must never be reworded.
- **The final return carries twenty-one living witnesses and not one of them is Owen Rusk.** The arithmetic, shown once in Chapter 50: **eight** adult branches, **two** observers (Pell and Voss), **four** named objectors holding their own papers (A. Rusk, B. Tillet, H. Ferris, W. Sarr), **six** persons whose acts are recorded and who are not branches (Bess Crale, Tam Ockey, Enid Salter, Ruth Mapp, Sully Dace, Wil Pomfret), and **one** steward (Marguerite Bray). Eight + two + four + six + one = twenty-one.

**Power position.** Stage 0 to **Stage 1, Sealed Witness**, acquired at the Chapter 46 certification and only there: one completed qualified filing, one named duty accepted, and the demonstration of what the entry cannot support. **The certification was sealed on Day 63, a Monday, at about half past three in the afternoon, and runs seven days; about forty minutes of it were left at ten minutes to three on Day 70, where the volume stops.** **One active hold.** Cost: unresolved Lower Lock cases entering his working life as exact sensory detail and as names he cannot always summon — two now, and neither was a price anybody charged him. **Each of the two names is spoken once in the volume, both in Chapter 47, and neither is spoken again.**

**What the volume paid.** The name **VANE, H.**, gone permanently, never restored, never spoken of. A pitch of forty-one years. The hardest hours of a ward, off the instrument and onto a wall. A mother amended and nothing returned. A man of fifty-eight who is owed a pension and is owed five hours a week.

**The two questions it hands Volume 02.** The brass tab: a district return for **Alder Row**, a ward officially closed in the Concord Year 461, four streets from Owen's bed, in an intake series the Registry's own index says was never opened. And the harder one from the same door: if redaction can be necessary, and somebody chose it, who decides which communities deserve to survive?

### Batch 0001 — Chapters 51–60, *The Season Opens* (written)
