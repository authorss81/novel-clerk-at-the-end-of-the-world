# Chapter 45 — The Narrowing

The return came up at ten past two on the Friday afternoon of the sixtieth day of the month in the long room at the end of the second floor with the high windows, for the sixth time in seven weeks, and Owen Rusk had it on a tray in front of him and could not enter it, because the box on the form that said *entry of a filing on this tray* was the only box on the card he had that was his, and there was nothing on the instrument to enter.

There is no act yet. That was the whole of it, and it had taken the ward two days to arrive at the sentence and it had arrived at the sentence by itself, without anybody in Docketford doing anything, which is the first time in seven weeks that has happened.

Marguerite Bray and Ada Rusk came up on the Friday morning boat with about four other people and the delivery book in a bag. The long room had forty-odd people at the back of it again, because a reviewing officer books a room with high windows and the room fills itself.

The reviewing officer had the small print ready, because the small print is now the practice of this room, and he read it out without being asked.

"No grant is restored. The Fetter Lane pump grant is suspended and its suspension stands to its own review. The load-restriction suspension of the tenth is not affected and is not superseded. The roll entry of the fourteenth stands and has not been amended. The order of the twenty-fourth stands. A claim to a status is not a claim to a duty. And the return comes up at the interval and is a review, and a review is not a hearing, and a review is not binding."

Then he turned a sheet round.

"Mr Rusk. I am going to say one thing this afternoon that I have not said in this room in six weeks, and I am going to say it before anybody else does, and I am going to say it in the form this office uses, and I would like the room to notice that the form is correct and that what it says is true and that neither of those two things helps anybody.

"A preservation of a status — a hold — is a temporary instrument. It has a subject, a term, an effect and an end. **It cannot be carried by an instrument that is renewed every seven days and is not binding**, because a hold that expires with a review has not got a term, it has got a habit, and there is a difference and I am not going to pretend to the ward that there is not.

"That is the mechanics, and the mechanics are the easy half. The other half is worse and I am the officer of this district and it is my duty to say it and I am going to say it once. **A duty the district cannot afford to keep is not a duty.** It is a thing the district has said it will do. The evidence that this district cannot afford to keep it is this district's own schedule, which is on the file, which anybody may read, and which this morning came to me from a clerk who told me the bin in the granary is a sixth gone and the pitch is under a foot of water and the crew's wage is four months in arrears and one of the four on the standpipe rota is a household of two people.

"I am not going to tell the ward what to take off. I have no function here and it would be an insult to a room. I am going to tell you what is in front of me and then I am going to let you get on with it."

He put the sheet down.

---

The ward hall at half past six on the Friday evening had the stove going and about twenty-six people in it, and four of them were not there to be helpful, and one of the four was about to say no in writing for the first time in his life.

Ada Rusk had the slate and she did not put it down once.

"Right," she said. "You have all been in this hall waiting for me to be clever and I am going to be the opposite, and I am going to do it now, and Mrs Bray is going to rule it, and then somebody is going to read it back and Mr Rusk is going to be stopped twice, and I am not going to help him."

"Nobody stopped you last Thursday," said somebody.

"Mr Rusk was not in the room on Thursday morning," said Ada Rusk, "and I want that noted, and I am going to say why in a moment and it is not modesty."

She turned round to the room and stood with her back to the fire, which she does not do, because the fire is the only thing in that hall that anybody trusts.

"Here is what I am giving up and it is mine to give because it is in my crew and I lead the crew and every hour on that sheet on Saturday night was somebody in my crew's or somebody in this ward's, and I am the person who can take it off and nobody else in this room can.

"**I am taking the night off the return.**"

Nobody said anything.

"The pump prime at four in the morning. The standpipe rota. All of it. Off. It does not stop — Mrs Bray, write that down in capitals, write *the work does not stop* — the work does not stop tonight or any night, and it is on the roster, and the roster is at the weir path and in this hall, and any person in this county may read it on any day without notice and take a copy away, and Cobb Rennie's name is on it and the Sarr household's name is on it and they will be on it next Thursday in the dark at four in the morning.

"And it comes off the **return**. And that means that in about four weeks' time, if a person in Concord Hall reads this instrument and asks what this district has undertaken, the answer is that it has undertaken four things, and none of the four is at four in the morning, and the four in the morning will be nowhere on the paper that a body with a seal can hold.

"That is the loss. I am not going to dress it. In seven weeks the hardest hours this ward has ever done have been the hours we are best at being seen doing, and I am taking them off the one piece of paper that makes them count, and I am doing it because a district that claims a duty it cannot keep is a district whose duties a careful man in a room can take away in about a minute, and we have a careful man in a room on Friday afternoons, and he has just told us he cannot do anything else."

"Then we lose the argument," said Dory Anstruther from the door.

"No," said Ada Rusk. "We lose the **claim**. The argument is not the claim. That is the entire difference and it is the difference between my sister being right in this hall for six weeks and this instrument existing, and I am not going to spend the next hour explaining it to a man of nineteen with a coil of line in his hand, and I will if you ask me after."

Nobody asked her after.

---

"And in exchange," she said, "I am not signing the return as a crew. I am signing it as six people."

She read the names off the slate and she did not read them fast, and she gave the act with each one, because an act with nobody's name on it is the thing she has spent seven weeks arguing against.

"**Bess Crale — the marker.** Set at seven forty on the Thursday and the Friday at the top of the path above the second bend, and taken out and looked at. One person. Any one of us could do it. I have done it alone in the wet twice this month and it is not a crew act, it is a walk.

"**Enid Salter — the channel report.** Walk the top path and the weir path after any water and write down what is there, in a hand anybody can read, and rule the day on it. One person. It is a walk and a pencil and it is the only honest thing anybody in this ward has ever produced about that river, and we have never written it down.

"**Tam Ockey — the count.** The granary, Wednesday night, bins against the book, and the write-up Thursday afternoon in full. One person does the count and one person is not the same person, and the second person is not a branch and is not on the return and is named on the roster.

"**Ruth Mapp — the reading.** The week read out loud in this hall at ten past six on the evening the count is written up, from the roster, with the book open on the table. One person, out loud, in public. It is the hardest one to find somebody for and it is the one I would keep last.

"**Sully Dace — the bins.** What goes, what is spoiled, how much, written in the pump book with the day. One person, at the back of a granary, in the dark, alone, and nobody has ever once thought that was worth a name.

"**Wil Pomfret — the copy.** The ward's copy of the roster carried up to the weir path and hung where anybody coming down the path can read it, and brought in again in the wet and hung again. One person. Mrs Bray's second term."

She put the slate down.

"That is six people and six acts and not one of them needs nine people and not one of them needs a wage we have not got and not one of them is a bridge. And any one of them can be done by one person on a Tuesday in February, which is the sentence Mr Ferris said to me in this hall two weeks ago and which I did not have an answer to at the time and which I have had since Sunday night."

Holm Ferris, who is fifty-eight and keeps the night watch and is one of the four who voted no on the seventh day of the month and had said nothing in this hall for a fortnight, said:

"That is the duty, then. That is what the four of us would have to say."

"That is a duty one person can do alone," said Ada Rusk. "Yes. And that is what I am writing on the instrument, and it has taken me two days and it has cost me the hardest hours this ward does, and I would like nobody in this hall to congratulate me, because I did not do it for the instrument."

---

"Mr Rusk," said Ada Rusk. "The scope clause. Read the last sheet."

Owen Rusk had the copy in his hand and he had read it in the lane on the way up and he had been stopped twice in his own head and he read it anyway.

"*The district claims a duty, and no status.*"

"Stop," said Ada Rusk.

He stopped.

"You read it in your own words. It is in my capitals and it is six words on a sheet and you read five of them." She turned round. "**Duty, and no status.** Four words. That is the whole of the scope and it is in my hand and Mrs Bray is going to rule it and it goes on the instrument in my hand and not in yours, and you are not going to write it, and if anybody in that building asks you what the scope of this return is you are going to say *the district has told you* and hand them the sheet."

"Four words," said Owen Rusk.

"It claims a duty and it refuses a status in the same sentence, and a duty is somebody's own act and a status is a statement about where a thing is, and this case has been about that difference since the first week of the month and it will be about it after all of us are dead." She put her hand flat on the table. "And it is four words, and I have thought about it for two days, and I want to say why it is four, because somebody will ask me and I would rather say it in this hall than in a room in Docketford. Because if I put a *safety* in it, it is a claim. If I put a *crossing* in it, it is a claim. If I put *funded* in it, it is a lie. Four words is what is left when a ward has been honest for seven weeks, and it is going to be the sentence that is read out in a room in a month by somebody I have never met, and I want it to be mine."

Nobody said anything, and Marguerite Bray wrote it in the delivery book in her own ruled hand with the day and the hour, and read it back, and Ada Rusk said *that is the whole of it* and nothing else.

---

Bram Tillet wrote on his page at about a quarter to nine.

He wrote it in his own hand, which is a cook's hand and not a clerk's, and he wrote it on a page with his own name at the top of it in Marguerite Bray's writing, and he did it standing up at the long table, and about twenty people watched, and he did not ask anybody to help him and nobody helped him.

It said:

*I am offered a separate branch of District Return 487-LL and I refuse it. My reason is the one I gave in this hall on the fortysixth day of the month and it is the same reason today. Being read by somebody who can stop is not the same as being read by somebody who can stay, and a branch is for ever and a Thursday is one day, and I will not be on a page in a document that has nobody at the end of it who can stay. This is a refusal and it is not a request to be put back into anything. It is dated today. It is in my own hand. Do not ask me again.*

Marguerite Bray read it back to the room in her own voice and ruled it with the day and the hour, and then she ruled the ward's answer to it, which was one line, in her own hand, and which read: *The offer stands and the refusal is recorded and neither of them is withdrawn by the other, and the ward has not asked the district office to name anybody.*

"Rule that nobody is to be asked," said Ada Rusk, "and rule it in capitals, because in about three weeks somebody in a good mood is going to try."

"It is ruled," said Marguerite Bray.

And Wenna Sarr, from the wall by the door, with her basket at her feet, said why hers was blank.

"Because the page is for a thing I would do," she said, "and I have not found the thing yet, and I would rather hand you a page with my name on it and nothing on it than a page with my name on it saying nothing."

Nobody chased her. Nobody has chased her since the seventh day of the month and nobody chased her on Friday, and the page went into the bundle on Saturday morning with the other three — one refusal in a cook's hand, one blank on purpose with its owner standing in a room and saying why, and two with nothing on them at all — and the bundle went to Concord Hall on the same post it had gone on the previous week, which is the fourth time, and a blank page that is sent on purpose is not a hole, it is a page.

---

The return came up on the Friday at ten past two and it was renewed for the **sixth** time in seven weeks, and it was renewed **for review**, which is what it has been every single time and what it will be next week, and it came up on narrower terms.

The two observers were separated in writing in their own hands, once for this renewal, as they have been five times before, and a separation made once does not carry forward.

Pell's was in her own hand and it is short, and it says the same four sentences as the margin of the seventh day of the month, re-made for this renewal, because a separation made once does not carry forward and never has. Voss's came up from the annex on the Thursday afternoon in an envelope with the mill's postmark on it and a line on the outside in his own hand that said: *this is the fifth and not a copy of the fourth.* He has now five written reservations about his own interest on the file, one of them longer than the others, and nobody has ever asked him for any of them.

The withdrawal right is intact and it is in Ada Rusk's capitals and it says a person may take their own branch out and the rest does not fall over. The class of persons who may object is the eight persons whose branches are in the return and any person the district office names in writing. It is eight. It has been eight since the twenty-fourth of the month and no instrument in this city has ever named anybody in writing, and the ward has not asked.

And the schedule of performed acts went up the stair again with a covering page and a disclaimer printed three times, and behind it the four weeks of hours, and behind those four pages with four names at the top of them.

And at the top of the return, where the scope is, in a bridge mechanic's capitals, in chalk on a slate and then in ink on the sheet and then in a steward's ruled hand in a book and then in ink again by nobody, because the ward does not have it drafted by a clerk:

**Duty, and no status.**

---

They came off the hill at about six and the water had gone down another foot and the weir path was out and the bar was not in it, and the notice nailed to the post at the top of the path was still there, saying that any district able to perform the repair is invited to perform it, and four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds is the number on nobody's paper in this city.

Owen Rusk walked down Fetter Lane at about seven and worked out what he was carrying, standing up, in the lane, in the wet, because it had become the only way he could think and he had stopped apologising for it.

The return was narrower by about a third than it had been on Wednesday. A hundred and forty hours had become a hundred and something, he did not know the new figure and it was not his figure. The hardest hours this ward does were off the instrument and on the wall. A man of fifty-eight and a girl of twelve were off the return and on the roster. Six people who had never been named in an instrument in their lives were on it, by name, with one thing each that they could do alone.

And it was now, in his honest estimate, small enough to be true, and a true thing was the only thing in this building that could carry a hold.

Which meant that the only person in this city who could put a hold on the crossing's legal status and keep its appeal window alive while four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds went unfunded was a man with a posting to a supervised tray and a card in a drawer with four lines on it he was forbidden to sign, and who lived in the ward, and who had been in a hall two hours ago and heard a woman of twenty-four say that she wanted what she was about to do to be hers and not his.

He got to the bottom of the lane and turned round and looked back up it at the wet roofs and the hall with the lamp on in the room at the back, and then he went to find out whether a copy clerk of the River Desk may certify a return for a ward he lives in, which was a question with a form attached to it somewhere in this city, and which nobody in Lower Lock could answer, and which he would have to ask in a building where he is a stranger.
