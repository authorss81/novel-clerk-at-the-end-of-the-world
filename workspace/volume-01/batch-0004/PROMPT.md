## Where the Batch Starts

### Chapter 0031 — The Office Answers the Column

POV: Owen Rusk, third person limited.

Location/time: the Glass Stair waiting hall and the copying floor; Day 40, a Saturday, into the week. Political pressure.

Immediate goal: get an answer to the comparison sheet before the answer can be shaped by whoever is drafting it, and find out who is going to be asked about the forty-one.

Resistance: a comparison on a public table is not a filing, and the Office can answer it the way it answers everything — in a standard form, in a paragraph, about the form and not about the forty-one. Owen's three moves: a request for a hearing on the comparison (wrong instrument, and it would give the Office a date to control); a point of law (already used, and the office is on its feet about it); and the oldest thing in the building, which is a **public comparison in a room with a queue in it**, which is Ada's move and not his, and which only works if somebody else holds the second sheet.

Information revealed, in the room: the answer is a paragraph and it is correct and it says that *No district office competent* is a statement about the office's own reach and not about any person, and that a disposition records a decision and not a reason, and that the office does not maintain reasons in a column. Owen learns, and Pell tells him plainly, that the person who will be asked about the forty-one is **not a villain and not a Deputy Registrar**: it is whoever maintains the standard forms, and there is a schedule of duties in the building with a name on it, and the name is a person, and the person is a clerk.

Action or decision: he does not print anything else. He takes the answer down off the queue's conversation and **does the thing nobody in this building does**: he reads the answer aloud in the waiting hall to whoever wants to hear it, in the ordinary way, without a speech, and then he goes and asks the ward's question, which is a different one.

Emotional change: Owen discovers that being right in public and being useful in public are different products, and that the second one requires somebody else to be holding the paper.

Power or resource change: no instrument and no authority. **A correct paragraph on a docket that says the office keeps no reasons**, and the identification of a person, in a schedule of duties, who maintains the standard forms.

Continuity fact to preserve: the Office's answer on the forty-one is correct, is not an admission, and **restores nothing and explains nothing**. The Office does not keep reasons in a column, only dispositions, and that is now a stated position on a public file. The forms are maintained by a clerk on a schedule of duties, unnamed on stage, and **Owen has just made that person an enemy who is not an enemy yet.**

Ending type: Discovery, and a door opened in the wrong direction.

Next-chapter pull: Pike's real justification surfaces in the corridor outside the Deputy Registrar's office, and the man who will have to say it out loud is the man who has not been able to say anything since Chapter 24.
