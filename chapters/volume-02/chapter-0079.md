# Chapter 79 — The One Thing He Is Asked For

The granary at the bottom of Fetter Lane had been open and empty for three weeks and four days, and the way the ward found out that it was still empty was that nobody was hungry, and the way anybody in the district office found out about it at all was that Ada Rusk had said so in a hall on the first day of the second month in front of about twenty people and had written **nobody** in the delivery book in her own capitals with the word underlined, and nobody in that hall had gone and bought a quarter of anything, and there has been no meal into that building since the seventh day of the second month and there is not going to be one. Marguerite Bray ruled the figure on the Sunday morning of the second day of the third month of the Concord Year 488, which is the first working day of the month, and the figure is zero, and she ruled it in her own hand with the day and the hour and then she ruled a second line under it, which is the sixth time in fourteen weeks she has ruled a sentence nobody asked her to rule.

**Nothing. The last of it went into a barrow on the seventh day of the second month. It is the first week of the third month and this is the first working day and there is nothing in the bins and there has been nothing in the bins for twenty-five days. I am writing the word nothing in a steward's book because a zero in a steward's book reads like an omission and it is not an omission, it is a decision, and the decision was made by Ada Rusk on the first of the second month and was correct, and I would like it in this book in my hand so that in about four years nobody has to guess about it.**

Nobody thanked her for that either. There is a great deal in this county that nobody is thanked for and it is not ingratitude, it is that a thank you is a receipt and there is nothing here to receipt.

---

Owen Rusk was in the hall on the Sunday because he is in the hall on Sundays now, and there were about twenty people, and he was not asked to say anything and did not, and Ada Rusk noticed that he had not been asked and did not point at it, which is the third time in a season she has let a thing stand that she could have used. The ward's second season started with nothing. That is the shape of it and it is worth saying in the order it happened rather than in the order it is dramatic.

The crossing is closed to loads and to vehicles above four tons and the repair is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and is unfunded, and the notice at the weir path is in the weather and invites any district able to perform the repair to perform it, and it has been in the weather since the flood and it has brought no district, and it is the only document on that post that is in the condition it was put up in. The Fetter Lane pump grant is suspended and not extinguished and the pump is primed by hand at four in the morning on the Thursday by two people, one of whom is a household with a girl of twelve in it.

The crew is nine and the wage is four months in arrears and is a written condition of a roster that nobody funds. The granary is empty and there is a girl of twelve who does the granary weights on the Sundays in the shed, and the weights are the same weights, and the bins are the same bins, and there is nothing in them, and the shed is where she is on the Sunday because the shed is warm and the hall is full.

Stability Order 114 is on a docket at the Glass Stair in public appeal and has not been withdrawn and the sixteen are sixteen and Lower Lock is sixth, and **none of that was touched by the Count and none of it moved in a season in which a determination was sustained in a room with high windows about a column that cannot be filled.**

---

She asked him on the Monday.

The hall on the Monday evening of the third day of the third month had about twenty-two people in it and the stove on and the delivery book on the long table, and Ada Rusk had four lines written out before anybody came down, on the back of the back page of a count sheet, and she put them face down and did not turn them over, which is the third time this year.

"Turn it over and read it and sit down," she said. "It is four lines and I have read it to two people and one of them was Cobb Rennie and one of them was Marguerite."

He turned it over and read it and sat down.

"**You are going to enter this into the district's record,**" said Ada Rusk. "In the district's own hand, dated, with your name on it as the officer of the record of the district returns. It is not a return. It is not a claim. It is not an instrument and it is not a hold and it is not one of your three. It is a statement by this district office of what this district is, made by the person who keeps the district's returns, and it goes in the record the same day."

"Ada—"

"**Say the objection first and say all of it, because I have had it for nine days and I have thought about it and I am asking you anyway.**"

"The objection is the sixth of the second month," said Owen Rusk. "I wrote NONE in capitals on a quarterly works return for a mill two hours up a river, in the district's hand, in about four seconds, out of your eighth line, and I had not thought about that line as a form for nine weeks, and in about four years somebody in a building is going to ask the district office for the reasoning behind a form and the district is going to produce a line in the district's own hand that says there is no person. It is the district's document now and not the ward's, and I did it in four seconds because I already knew the answer, and it will bind the district, and that is a thing I cannot undo and have not been able to undo since the sixth."

"Yes," said Ada Rusk. "**That is the objection and it is correct and it is the reason I have had this for nine days, and I have had it for nine days because I knew you would say it, and I am asking you anyway, and I would like the hall to notice that I have said the word anyway out loud.**"

"Then why."

"Because in four years the district is going to have one document in its own hand that says what this ward is." She was not using the wall voice. She was standing still in the middle of the floor with a count sheet in her hand. "Right now the district's record of Lower Lock is a count sheet and a return of premises that came back **incomplete** in the first month and a quarterly works return with **none** in capitals on it in the second month, and a line in my hand on a piece of paper that the district office has had for nine weeks and has not once thought of as a form. **There is one true sentence about this district that is in the district's own hand and it is that there is no person in it who keeps anything for anybody, and that is the answer to a column, and it is not an answer to anything else, and in four years the district is going to be the office that says it, and there is going to be nothing else in the drawer.**"

Owen Rusk did not answer for a while, and the thing he was doing in the four or five seconds was the thing Ada Rusk told a man in a shed in the first month of the year he does, and he caught it, which he has caught twice in a season, and he caught it by saying it out loud.

"Stop me," he said. "I have been about to answer a question I have not been asked. I have been about to say *you should not ask me for this*, and that is not a reason, it is the shape my hands make, and you have not asked me a question yet. **What question am I being asked.**"

Ada Rusk stood in the middle of a ward hall with about twenty-two people in it and said it straight at him.

"You are being asked whether the district office will put one true thing about this ward into the district's own record, in the district's own hand, dated, with a name on it, before anybody from the count comes with a form. That is the question. It is the only question and I have not dressed it up and I am not going to."

"Yes," said Owen Rusk. "**I will do it.**"

---

He wrote it on the Tuesday at the end of the second desk and it took about forty minutes, and he wrote it as a person and without a seal, and he wrote it that way because a form in this county that has a person's hand on it and no office in it is the only kind of paper that is allowed to be a statement, and he has written exactly one in his working life and it was a certification and it expired. Ada Rusk made him change the line at the foot twice.

"You are signing it as the officer of the record of the district returns."

"I am the officer of the record of the district returns. It is true and it is the reason the thing is worth anything and I am not going to leave it off."

"Then sign it as **Owen Rusk, of Fetter Lane**," said Ada Rusk, "and let the sentence at the top of it say whose office it is. The day this document gets read in about four years by somebody who wants a district to be on one side of a thing, I want there to be a man on it and not a post."

He signed it as Owen Rusk, of Fetter Lane.

---

**DISTRICT OFFICE, DOCKETFORD. Statement of the district office concerning the district of Lower Lock, made by the officer of the record of the district returns. Not a return. Not a claim. Not an instrument. A hold and a challenge and an appeal are not made out of it and it is none of them. The third day of the third month of the Concord Year 488.**

**One. This district performs and is not counted and does not intend to stop. The paper of this district says four words and the four words are *Duty, and no status*, and the four words have not been reworded by anybody.**

**Two. This district has six persons whom it cannot ask. It performs a care act for all six. There is no person in this district who keeps anything for anybody. The word for that is not written beside any name and is not to be. The cost of the gap is five hours a week, and that is a figure of hours and not a person, and the hours of the person who is there do not stop.**

**Three. The crew of this district is nine. Their wage is four months in arrears and is a written condition of a roster that nobody funds, and it is their wage and not the district's. The crossing is closed to loads. The repair is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and is unfunded. The notice inviting any district able to perform the repair has been in the weather since the flood and has brought no district. The granary has been open and empty since the seventh day of the second month and nothing has been bought for it and the decision not to buy was correct.**

**Four. This district is named in the schedule of settlements to be reconciled for the season of the Concord Year 489. No day has been named and none will be. No reconciliation will be conducted in reliance upon a district return that asks this district for a year it has never had, and the four words in the first of these paragraphs are what this district has instead of a year.**

Owen Rusk read all four paragraphs out loud in the ward hall on the Tuesday evening at about seven, to about thirty people, and Ada Rusk made him read the second one again, and he read it again, and nobody in the hall said anything about it, and one of the people in the hall is one of the six.

Ivy Sarr was at the end of the long table and she is twelve and she read it twice off the sheet in front of her, which she can do, and then she said:

"Does it open the crossing."

"No," said Owen Rusk.

"Right," said Ivy Sarr, and went back to the tray of seed trays at the end of the table, which is what she is at a ward hall on a Tuesday evening, and nobody thought it was remarkable, and it was not, and it is the only sentence in that hall that is not about a figure.

Marguerite Bray read it back twice, as is the third clause of the rule they work by, and then ruled it into the delivery book on the fourth day of the third month, in her own hand, with the day and the hour, and under it she wrote, in the inward column, the thing she had done five times in a season and not once before it:

**Entered in the record of the district office at the officer's own hand, and not entered by the ward, and the ward did not ask for it to be entered and did not stop it, and I record that both of those are true and that nobody in this hall is to be thanked, because a thank you is a receipt and there is nothing to receipt.**

Cobb Rennie read it four times that week, because it was on the table in the hall and he is the sort of man who reads what is on the table, and on the Thursday evening at ten past six he stood at the end of the table after the roster had been read and said one thing, and the one thing was this.

"**I have been owed a pension for two years and one month and it is not a pension and there is no column for it, and you have put a paper in a drawer in a building and it says the right thing and it does not pay me and I am not going to pretend it does, and I want it noticed that I read it four times and I am going to read it on Friday as well, and that is the whole of what I have got and it is more than I had on Friday.**"

He was owed a pension on the Sunday after and he is owed it now, and a document in a drawer is not money and naming him in a document is worth less than money and more than nothing, and neither of those has changed in a season in which a column that can be printed and cannot be filled was the reason that a district will not be surveyed.

---

On the Wednesday night the granary was counted again, because that is what the ward does on a Wednesday night, and on the Thursday afternoon the count was written up bin by bin, and the figure is nothing for the fourth week running, and the writing up took Marguerite Bray about two hours and ten minutes and she did not shorten it. She read the six names out of the register that afternoon in the hall and she read them slowly, and they are the six persons in this ward who cannot be asked, and the care act is performed for all six of them at four in the morning by people who are on a roster nobody funds, and the entry in the district's own record says that there is no person in this district who keeps anything for anybody.

**The two missing parts of a protective return are a named place at which the thing is kept and a named person who keeps it, and both of them are Registry functions, and the cost of filling them in has been a figure in a ward hall since the month of rain: five hours a week, for as long as the care situation lasts, and the office may put somebody else in the place without asking the ward, and the hours of the person who was there do not stop. No custodian has been bought. The word is not written beside Ada Rusk, or Bram Tillet, or Ilse Mern, or Cobb Rennie, and it is not written beside anybody else, and it was not written beside anybody on the Thursday afternoon, and there were about thirty people in the hall and one of the six of them was in it, and she was not asked anything, and she did not have to be, and that is the whole of what this district has and the whole of what it does not have.**

Owen Rusk stood at the back for the writing up and read the four paragraphs of his own statement off the table twice while it was going, because Ruth Mapp was reading the roster at ten past six and any person not of the district and not of the office may read the ward's paper on any day without notice, and nobody has ever once asked anybody why they read it, and the ward knows exactly who reads it and has never said so out loud. He went up the hill at about eight and did the tray and was on it at a quarter past nine, and there had been no post all evening, and the second season of the ward of Lower Lock had begun with a crew of nine, a crossing shut to loads, a pump primed by hand, an empty granary, a wage four months in arrears, a repair at four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds, a notice in the weather that had brought nobody, six persons who could not be asked, a girl of twelve doing the weights in a shed, a man of fifty-eight who read everything on the table four times, and a sheet of paper in a drawer in a building two miles up the hill with a man's name at the bottom of it and his sister's sentence in the second paragraph.

**Nobody thanked him. He worked out at the end of the tray, at about ten past ten, that he had got two documents in a district file in one season with his name at the foot of them, that the second one was better than the first one by a long way, and that in about four years somebody in a building was going to ask the district office of Docketford for the reasoning behind both of them, and that the district was going to produce them, and that he would not be in the building when it did, and that this was the correct arrangement and not a comfort, and that there is no form in this county on which a man may put a thing back that he has found out was the true thing.**
