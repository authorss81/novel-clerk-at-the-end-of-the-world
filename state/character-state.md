# Character State

Where each active character stands, what they want, and what they are carrying. Updated after every batch. This is state, not biography; fixed background lives in `bible/characters.md`.

Positions below are as of the end of **Chapter 30, batch Day 39** — the Friday after the ward's public performance.

## Volume 01 — The Unfiled Ward

### Owen Rusk, 32 — Copy Clerk, **assisting clerk to the second desk's supervised tray** (a posting, not a qualification), and still posted to the tray of unfiled correspondence

Wants: a person at the far end of the ward's appeal route, and the ability to stop being the only person carrying the case. Holds: an assisting clerk's card that forbids him to sign a continuation, a certification, a suspension or a hold; a private notebook that leaves the building and that he has now named *a register*; a counterfoil proving he is a line in a request register; a cut on his right middle finger that has not healed. Stage 0, and the batch says so by never saying otherwise. **He has had no feeling about his own instrument working, and has understood that the condition he is now in is the ordinary condition of every other clerk in the building.** He has learned, in order: that a filing is a set of checks and not a signature; that the effect field is a menu and a careless clerk is the most powerful person in the room; that a document can be *received* without being *examined* and that the difference is free; that an answer which grants nothing is appealable and an answer which grants a date nobody can compute is not; that a copy of a county intake register in a private book turns the book into a thing a man may be searched for; that his mother's handling of a registry was an error of receiving, not an error of lying; and that careful and right are not the same and never have been. He has begun to do without Nell without noticing he is doing it. He has **not** been paid for the ward's hours. He is a man in the institution, and has stopped pretending that is a temporary condition.

**Batch 0003 cost him a name, permanently.** The string **VANE, H.**, the receiving officer in the third column of a covering slip, arrived about four hundred times a quarter for nine years. It failed four times in three days in the fourth week of the rain season and it does not come back. It is not a symptom, not a warning, not paid for, and he has told nobody and is not going to. The finding was worse than the loss: the quarterly works notice for 4 Lantern Walk has come back twice, *not known at the address*, and there is **no first name for the man in any register in Docketford**, because the city has written to three letters and a surname for nine years.

**What he has gained instead:** the number of his mother's redaction batch, the officer's initials, the date struck, and three of the other forty entry numbers in his own handwriting from a route list. Precedent 37/C and the Office's refusal column, one hundred and nine entries with forty-one identical dispositions. The countersignature review standard, its recitals, and the roll it runs on. **And a habit he has now named twice: writing things down so that on a bad day in three months he can point at the entry and say that he did it.** He caught himself doing it in Chapter 29 and left the four lines blank.

**And one name, written once.** *Marta Rusk*, at the bottom of a page of his own register, on the night of Day 39, in his own hand, for the first time in his life. Nothing under it. Nothing restored. It is the only sentence in the volume that is not a fact about the institution.

### Nell Arbour, 35 — Claimant Advocate, Arbour Kitchen

**On stage twice in Batch 0003, and she gave nothing.** Owen asked her for one of the four names and she refused and told him what asking cost: *you have come to spend the only thing I have got, which is that when I send somebody, they are mine.* She repeated her condition unchanged — **the route and nothing else** — and told him she had noticed that he did not argue with it, which was new. She introduced nobody, vouched for nobody, and left the room before the answer.

She was also in a Glass Stair corridor in a capacity of her own and said nine words: in Concord Year 481, at Stonecross, she put a form on a counter and a woman wrote a number on it and gave it back, and she has never been told what the number was for. That is her own grievance, it is on the docket, and she is not using it for anybody. She does not know the register, the continuity statement, the intake number, the redaction batch, or Alder Row. The Chapter 4 charge is open and unresampled. **She has still refused to be his witness, his advocate for the ward, or his relief, and those refusals stand.**

### Ada Rusk, 24 — Bridge Mechanic and crew leader, Lower Lock

Wants: her ward to hold an instrument she did not have to be rescued into, and to be asked what she wants rather than what she is owed. She is **one of the four who voted no** and is **not one of the eight branches**; she is inside the return's scope through the crew's acts and has stated out loud, without apology, that she voted against the transfer and is inside a return that performs the duty. In this batch she: told the whole ward about the order at eight in the morning before Owen could, and said in front of thirty people how he came to be telling her; **dictated the four-clause amendment**, the first document in the case she has written entirely herself; **filled in the box Owen could not fill** — the class of persons who may object, narrowed to the eight branches and any person the office names in writing, which excludes her and the other three for ever, at the price of their having said no in a room with witnesses; said nothing at all while Nessa Kray withdrew, which was the hardest thing in the room; and told Owen that a reviewing officer had handed the ward something it asked for and told the truth about where he got it, which she called the second true thing he had said to her. She calls him *copyist*. She has a crew of nine, a repair at four thousand one hundred and sixty that nobody is funding, and a Thursday and a Friday marker at seven forty in the morning.

**Batch 0003 is the batch she wins.** She read the precedent before Owen did, worked out in a morning that the ward satisfies three of its four grounds and cannot satisfy the fourth, and said the frightening part out loud in a room: **performance in public is not a duty, it is a shift**, so it is a roster, and a roster is a document, and a document can be read by somebody who was not there, and the hours on it are **everybody's** and not the crew's, and that is the most frightening thing anybody said in the batch and she is right. She negotiated **five terms** in her own hand and had them ruled into the ward's delivery book, and the ward agreed to be checked in public on a calendar by a stranger from another district for as long as the thing runs, on **Marguerite Bray's condition: *and the wage*.** She stopped Owen in front of twenty-two people for trying to slow it down — *a man who keeps a thing alive by holding on to it has no business telling a ward what it may perform* — and he did not defend himself. She sent him away to find a stranger, made him do it badly first, and told him she did not trust it when he had been useful for three days running. She named the day herself, on the ward's own paper, in her own name. **Her terms survive; Owen's do not.**

### Junia Pell, 22 — Junior Copy Clerk, second desk, supervising the assisted tray, and the return's second observer

Wants: to be competent without becoming a custodian of other people's lives, and she has now done six public things that are not that. She sustained a challenge to **her own independence** against herself, in her own hand, on Day 22, before Owen and before Voss, and stayed on the instrument, and asked the reviewing officer to hold her separability open — converting the ward's one weakness into the thing that makes it renewable for review rather than binding. Her separation of interest is on the file twice, in two hands: four sentences in a margin on Day 7, and a page against herself on Day 22 that is longer than the first. She wrote down, at Owen's request, that a stability order can name a district that entered a return later in the quarter, and told him plainly that writing it made her a participant, which is what he had done to her on Day 7. She read the district works inspection roll in public at Owen's request and gave him four lines and no commentary. She refused to name a person for the far end of the appeal route. She told him to his face that she knows what is in his notebook, that she has never told anybody, and that there is no reason in the world for her to be standing near him except that she knows. **She is the only living witness to the destruction of the continuity statement and has not called it a debt and has not used it.** She owes him nothing and has said so kindly.

### Halden Pike, 47 — Deputy Registrar, Docketford

Wants: Docketford solvent and a decision actionable by Friday. In this batch he built **Stability Order 114** in a corridor, in about a minute, without concealment: a stability order closes returns on *lateness* rather than *failure*, and the argument that carries it is that the district cannot fund sixteen of them. He told Owen the schedule was struck on the morning of the twelfth and that no document will move a line on it, and he meant it. In the corridor on the twenty-third he would say only that he would sign the order **that week**; the Thursday-at-ten-past-four rule and the Friday Council counter are Owen's own discovery the next morning, and **Pike must not be given the day or the hour in advance again.** He signed at ten past four on the twenty-fourth, and the instrument is filed for the Council's counter and is **public and appealable to the Glass Stair**. He told Owen in advance that the ward must be visible when it gets signed. He has not been written as a liar and must not be. He has not yet surfaced the real reason for the first Lower Lock withdrawal, which is a Chapters 31–40 debt.

**Batch 0003 put his initials in a document.** **H.L.P.**, the officer who struck **Redaction Batch 471/Q4/19** — forty-one residence statuses, one sitting, the fourth quarter of Concord Year 471, on the ground *flood quarter arrears*. Owen has not gone to tell him and has not asked him and has not written his name next to the number in the register. **The batch's own respect for the Office is intact: it is a backlog cleared in an afternoon by a careful man, not a policy, and the same man signed Stability Order 114 in a corridor in a minute and refused to sign anything he meant to bury.** He is a Chapters 31–40 debt, and now there is a second document with his hand on it.

### Marguerite Bray, 61 — Ward Steward of Lower Lock

Keeps the ward's roll and, in its back half, a **delivery book with a second column for paper that comes back**, which she has **ruled by her own hand for thirty-six years, since she was twenty-five**; the book itself has been in the Bray family for fifty-one years, and she is sixty-one. That book is the only reason Chapter 20 could happen. She read the answer, came to the Glass Stair in the rain without asking, asked for the receipt clause to be read into plain words, and refused to thank anybody. She told the ward about the transfer's real origin out of her own book and her own pencil, and her reading is the one that matters: *the office did not answer him and did not refuse him, it received a document.* She asked Owen what the refusals were worth and wrote nothing down when he answered. She is not his ally, has never thanked him, and now holds the answer to a case he had to give away. She wants the ward to exist without the Office and does not believe he is the person who will get it there.

**Batch 0003 gave her the volume's thesis.** She attached **the condition** to Ada's five terms without being asked and read it into the book in front of the room that was agreeing to be read: *we will be checked in public, on a calendar, by a person from another district, for as long as the thing runs, **we will do it if the wage comes out of it*** — and then, on the same page, in a different pencil, *Condition of M. Bray. Wage not funded.* She refused to argue with Ada about it. And on Day 37 she took a sheet of nine sub-table refusals that Owen had no standing to hold, put a name to household references he could not, and **ruled in the delivery book that she will ask the seven that are hers, one at a time, in the hall, to anybody who chooses to come.** **She has refused to ask about the house at 4 Lantern Walk and said so, and it is not Owen's business either.**

### Wend Voss, 51 — Counter clerk, Mill Ward annex

The man whose counter received the transfer. Second observer, and now the holder of **three** written reservations about his own interest, the last of which he wrote on Day 22 as the price of standing on a document a Registry officer had just been challenged on. He walked up the hill twice in this batch on his own time, came to the review in the dark, and did not announce any of it. He said: *The Office's interest is not separable. I have never pretended it was. The only reason any of it is tolerable is that it is written down in my own hand where a reviewer can find it.* He is not a convert and not a coward. He wants a district that performs what his district cannot afford to keep, and he wants it named.

### Cobb Rennie, 58 — Lower Lock

Nineteen years at the same two rooms and off the Registry roll for two of them. Outside the thirteen households and outside the thirteen erased entries; nothing in this case reaches him and nothing is going to. Still cannot draw a pension. He walks a twelve-year-old home every night in the dark. Ivy Sarr has independently concluded that he is the most independent person available because nobody can say he wants anything. **That is not a cure for his pension and must never be written as one.**

**Batch 0003 put his hours on a public document and it changed nothing about his pension.** He is on the standpipe rota, the rota is on the roster, the roster may be read by any person from outside the ward without notice, and an outside observer read his name out of it on Day 38 and wrote down who was *not* there. **A roster records hours and does not record service, and no hour that Cobb Rennie stands on a standpipe in the dark goes anywhere near his pension, and this must never be written the other way round.** There is also a quarterly works notice addressed to three letters and a surname at a house in Fetter Lane that has come back twice, and **Bray says it is not hers and Owen says it is not his, and it is on the top of the stack.**

### Dory Anstruther, 19 — Bridge-crew apprentice, Lower Lock

First of the seven adults on the no-local-record schedule, and now a man with a broken collarbone and a chest that has gone the colour of candle wax, holding a coil on the top path and not going in the water, and being extremely good at it. He is a branch of the return. Two descriptions of him stand side by side in the file and the chapter is the point. He has not been asked to sign anything and is not going to be asked by Owen.

### Ivy Sarr, 12 — Lower Lock, lives with her aunt Wenna Sarr

First line of the no-local-record schedule, one of the six who cannot answer, and now the only person in the volume whose face Owen knows. She cannot read the notices and has been carrying her aunt's to the mill's factor on Tuesdays for a fortnight. She counts seed trays, keeps seven against an official nine, and is right. She named five care acts performed this week in her own words and concluded, unprompted, that the man no record places anywhere is the best one to ask. Owen met her once, on a step in a lane, and got four minutes of the best kind he has had in a month. She needs a protective return; the ward has three of five parts and two are nobody's to pay for. Unresolved at the end of the batch and unresolved at the end of the volume.

**Batch 0003: she is on the standpipe rota.** She is a girl of twelve and she carried two pails on a stick up Fetter Lane twice before eleven o'clock on the Thursday, and an outside observer asked her two questions about it and wrote her name down. Owen stood on a step of the pump house, looked at five names on a sheet of paper, thought about six protective returns and two Registry functions nobody is paid to provide, **could not think of a single sentence to put on a form, and did not try.** She is the most legible person in this ward and the least protected, and that is the volume's standing contradiction.

### Wenna Sarr — Lower Lock, Ivy's aunt, one of the four dissenters

Does the actual arithmetic of her niece's protection herself and gets it exactly right, including the custodian the office may replace without asking the ward. Cannot read the notices the Office hands her and has been sending the child to a counter instead. Said *I am not a witness. I am the only grown person that child has got* in Chapter 7 and has now spent a fortnight being that and doing it well. She asked Owen one question and made him answer it with the first thing in the case that was not a complaint.

### Ivet Sterne — Wexford Landing, ferry tally and tide table

Not an officer of anything, holds no office, cannot be cited, has no seal and no mark, and says all of that in the first nine minutes she gives Owen. One of Nell's four candidates, and one of the two women Nell has had words with. Her whole power is that she was there, and the second half of her power is that she can stop: if she does not come again the thing is not being checked, it is being witnessed, and no office in Docketford can take that off her. **She gave six written conditions** — not a witness to the return and no signature on anything the ward performs; comes when she says and not otherwise and nobody may ask her on the day why she has not; **a person of the ward may ask her and the Registry may not, and if the request comes from that building she will not come at all and will not say that at the time**; paid nothing and takes nothing; reads the roster and not the ward's book; do not ask her who sent her. She gave a **no** with a list of conditions better than anything Owen had written in a month, and then a **conditional yes for one day**, on a day the ward names itself, on the back of a ferry ticket. She came on Day 38 from first light to dark and wrote the wage in her own line, unsoftened. **She is not the far end of the appeal route and the volume says so.**

### Elias Trant — millwright, above Wexford Landing

One of the four. Said no kindly, in a yard, in about four minutes, and gave the reason entire: a man with nothing to lose is not a check, he is a tourist, and you want somebody who could lose something and does it anyway. Looked at a mill return once, sixteen years ago, gave a district office a second name because a name seemed a small thing, and was a millwright for nine years afterwards. He told Owen to write it down so that he would not go and tell a hall that a man at a mill refused a ward. **He is not a fifth candidate and he is not a fifth anything.**

### Nessa Kray, 71 — Lower Lock, seed stall

Forty-one years on the same pitch, in neither leaf of the annex, and the second person this month to tell Owen what a paper was actually about. She went to the Mill Ward annex and was told there was no provision for a person to take herself out of a return; **she withdrew her branch on Day 23, standing up, unasked, in her own words, and the return narrowed by one page and stood.** She is not leaving the ward and the ward knows it. Two people have already told Owen she walked. She is in Marguerite Bray's drawer with nothing else in it and that is where she stays.

### Ilse Mern, 44 — Lower Lock, laundress, a branch

Wants the roof she voted for. Demanded her own page in Chapter 7 and got it. Watched the nine adults carry the ward's burden out of the hall on the fourth morning and did not go with them, and said the reason out loud, and was not thanked, and did not ask to be. Gave up the granary double count on Day 25, stayed in the return, and said in the hall that she was not going to be the second thing in a month that everybody had to be told about. Holds the second lamp at a night channel clearance. **Her Thursday four-in-the-morning pump prime is now a line in a public document, and Ada Rusk named her by name in a hall when she said that if the ward is going to be read then the roster is everybody's and not the crew's.** Four households on her standpipe rota and Cobb Rennie on it, and Ivy Sarr's name on it too, and Mern said the sentence that identified her to a stranger: *that is Ivy Sarr and she is on the rota and I am on the rota and that is the rota.*

### Marta Rusk — deceased

Fifteen years dead of fever after a medicine shortage in a flood-year closure. The Registry's delay in revising her residence status did not cause the fever; it delayed her priority medicine and left her **burial disputed for eight months**.

**Batch 0003 traced it.** Her residence status at Fetter Lane sat **pending revision for eleven days** in the fourth quarter of Concord Year 471, inside **Redaction Batch 471/Q4/19** — forty-one entries, struck in one sitting by one officer, initials **H.L.P.**, ground *revision of residence status, flood quarter arrears*, schedule lodged. The presenter column says *presented by O. Rusk, of Fetter Lane; relationship: son; claimant informed* — he was sixteen, he was present when the form was presented, and that is what *informed* means in this Office. **The same interval is on all forty-one rows, so the eleven days is the sitting and not her row**, which is the discovery: this is a practice, not an accident, and forty people are in it and thirty-seven of them will never know. **Her record is not amended and is not amended until Chapters 46–50.** The eleven days and the eight months are two different figures and are never swapped. On the last night of the batch Owen wrote **Marta Rusk** in his own hand at the bottom of a page of his register, for the first time in his life, and wrote nothing under it.

### Marguerite Bray's delivery book, as an object

Not a person, and it has no voice of its own, and it is the only thing in Docketford that can prove what a ward was asked for. It has an inward column and a returns column, it has been in Marguerite Bray's family for **fifty-one years**, and **she has ruled and kept it for thirty-six of them**, since she was twenty-five. It recorded the day the ward's copy of the transfer came back, the day the ward ratified nine to four, the day of Kray's withdrawal, the date of the amendment, and — behind a flyleaf, on the backs of three grey notices — the words *ward public works — schedule wanted*. It is the ward's own book, and the Office has never explained those three refusals and never will.

**Batch 0003 put three more things in it in three different hands:** Ada Rusk's **five terms** in her own capitals and a ruled heading; Marguerite Bray's **condition** in a different pencil on the same page, *and the wage — wage not funded*; and, on Day 37, a ruled line saying that **she** will ask the seven sub-table references that are hers, one at a time, in the hall, to anybody who chooses to come. **The book has outgrown the Office's copy of the ward's case and it is the only place in Lower Lock where a fact is not a summary.**

### Verity Lound — offstage

Her first circular is on the page (Chapter 10) and closed the local-return route. Her second is owed in **Chapters 33–35**, which is inside Batch 0004. She is not named in person anywhere in Batch 0003 and must not be. **Batch 0003 named the Provisional Council only as the issuer of item 14 of the Schedule of Instruments in Force, in the Concord Year 44, and did not attach a person to it — and that is correct, because a Council instrument is issued by a body and a body has no author.**
