## Where the Batch Starts

It is **batch Day 15, a Tuesday**, the third week of the rain season, C.Y. 487. Day 1 was a Tuesday, so Day 7 and Day 14 were Mondays and Days 12 and 19 are Saturdays. Three things are already running when Chapter 11 opens, and none of them waits:

- **The Day 14 freeze.** The Ninth Weir's status is entered on the continuation roll as *lapsed — no supported district*. The Fetter Lane pump grant is suspended. No household in Lower Lock can be continued, licensed, or witnessed on a district act. Nobody's name is struck. The Chapter 8 load-restriction suspension is untouched and still in force, so the crossing is closed *and* unsupported.
- **The Council's review quarter closes on the eighteenth.** After that, the district office returns community filings unexamined. That is a hard date, it is on the page in the Chapter 10 circular, and it is three days into this batch.
- **The answer clock.** The petition was entered in the Glass Stair docket on Day 12. The Office's written answer is due **Day 19** and lands in Chapters 12–13.

## The Act This Batch Opens On, and the One It Cannot Do

**Chapter 11 is the entry of Lower Lock's district return as a filing.** That is the act Batch 0001 could not perform: in that batch there was no qualified signer, and the return sat unentered at the back of Pell's tray with a covering slip naming Owen as the person who delivered it. On Day 15 the supervised tray has an assisting clerk slot, Owen is the only clerk in the hall not yet taken off the copying floors, and Pell is supervising. He enters it. It is **his first qualified filing**. That single act:

- lifts the freeze on the ward's standing;
- restores the crossing's status **as a duty the ward performs** and as nothing else — not safe, not open, not funded, not repaired;
- starts the return's own seven-day review, first due **Day 22**;
- puts Owen's name on a qualified instrument for the first time in his working life, and puts Pell's name on it as supervisor.

He is **Stage 1, Sealed Witness** in the sense the bible defines — a completed qualified filing, a named duty accepted, and a demonstrated knowledge of what the entry cannot support. He does **not** hold a preservation hold, does not have three or four cases, and does not lose a name. Stage 1's real costs arrive at Chapter 46 and in Batch 0003. Do not spend them here.

## Rules That Govern This Batch

These are not suggestions. Violating one breaks the volume.

- **Owen is an assisting clerk now, not a Sealed Witness in full.** He may sign a filing he is assisting on. He may not sign a continuation, a certification, a suspension, or a hold. His first signing authority beyond the assisted filing belongs to the Chapter 46 certification. If a card below seems to need more authority than he has, the card is wrong; take the authority off the instrument and put it on a person, a ward act, or a witness.
- **The freeze lifts; nothing else does.** Entering the return does not restore the pump grant, does not make the crossing usable, does not fund the abutment, and does not undo the Chapter 8 load restriction. The pump grant stays suspended for the rest of Volume 01.
- **The return claims a duty, not a status.** Its scope, as already written, is the ward performing the maintenance of the Ninth Weir crossing, claiming nothing as to safety, condition or use, declining the abutment repair, renewing the marker, channel clearance and pump prime weekly, and inviting any district able to perform the repair to perform it. Do not widen it silently and do not let anyone widen it by paraphrase.
- **Nine branches in, and one of them leaves.** One of the nine adult branches withdraws during this batch (Chapters 15–17), and the correct outcome is that **the return narrows by one page and stands**. It does not collapse, it is not refused, and nobody is asked to leave the ward. The withdrawal must be for a reason the person can state, and it must cost them something socially, which is the whole point of the instrument.
- **A withdrawal is a right, and using it is not betrayal.** The four dissenters (Ada Rusk, Bram Tillet, Holm Ferris, Wenna Sarr) are *not* among the nine and sign no branch in this batch either. Ada's own branch is in the nine and is in scope; the fact that she voted no on the transfer and is now in a return that performs the duty is a deliberate position she states out loud and does not apologise for.
- **Two witnesses, and the question of their independence is the batch.** Junia Pell and Wend Voss are on the instrument. Pell's independence is contestable **because she is a Registry officer whose office has an interest in the filing succeeding** — a sentence she wrote against herself in Chapter 7. Voss's interest is separable only because his district inherited the crossing's cost. The Office's answer accepts the return **for review, not as binding**, and does so largely because it can hold both witnesses' separability open. That is the batch's payoff and it is a smaller thing than a victory.
- **Four instruments stay apart:** the spring engineering notice on the Ninth Weir; the C.Y. 486 transfer receipt; Pike's Chapter 8 load-restriction suspension; and **Stability Order 114**, signed by Pike in Chapters 17–18 on the sixteen failing returns named in Chapter 3. Stability Order 114 is a **stability order under the Deputy Registrar's solvency authority** closing or suspending named failing district returns together. It is not a load restriction, not a closure interval, and not a suspension of an individual status. It is the instrument that reaches public appeal.
- **Three seven-day clocks stay apart.** The return's review interval, first due **Day 22**. The answer clock, due **Day 19**. The closure interval. None satisfies another. The written answer on Day 19 restores nothing and holds nothing.
- **The Day 14 lapse is never walked back.** It stands in the roll for the rest of the volume. Entering the return on Day 15 does not amend the Day 14 entry; it starts a new status from the day of entry.
- **The two annex leaves stay apart**, and neither carries the backdating in this batch because the backdating was surrendered in Chapter 5 and never corrected. The three requests refused inside that two-day window stay logged and unexplained. Batch 0003 is the earliest beat that may pay them.
- **Marginalia is thin and contradictory files only.** The batch may stage one or two more perception beats and no more, both on genuinely thin or contradictory records. A thin file answers him. A held, closed or sealed file is silent, and Owen is now learning what silence is worth — that is the Chapter 14–15 material. Never a notice on a sealed record, never numbers, bars, skill lists, solution buttons, or software jargon. No third "system."
- **The instrument is a district return**, file number **487-LL**. "Local return" is the category word. The Council's own language may use it.
- **Nothing restores the dead.** Marta Rusk and Tal Sarr stay dead, and the correct record says so. Dory Anstruther's injury and treatment record stand beside the Office's schedule as two descriptions of the same living man.
- **A claim to a status is not a claim to a duty.** Owen knows this now and must hold it under pressure, including in Chapters 17–18 when Pike's order treats Lower Lock as a failing return again. Do not let him stumble into the confusion.
- **Nell Arbour is not a witness, not an advocate for the ward, and not Owen's.** She offered to help with the **appeal route** and nothing else, and she has said there are about four people in the delta who could hold the far end of that route and that two of them are women she has had words with. ## The Ten Commitments for This Batch

1. The return is entered, the freeze lifts, and the entry changes what the ward *is* — a district that performs a duty — without changing what anyone can cross.
2. Owen's first qualified filing works, and it works partly because the return was built by other people and signed by people who were not him.
3. The Office's written answer gives a **date instead of a hearing**, and the date is worse than a refusal in one specific way: it moves the volume's real hearing downstream without resolving anything.
4. The first withdrawal tests the instrument. The return narrows and stands, and the person who withdraws is still in the ward and still spoken to.
5. Owen perceives that a **closed file is silent**, and cannot yet read the silence. He is frightened by it in a way he cannot put in a notebook usefully.
6. The first pressure lands on Pell's separability, and it is the first time anyone has ever had a reason to look at what she signed.
7. **Stability Order 114 is signed by Pike in Chapters 17–18**, on the sixteen named returns, in the same window as the Day 22 review, and it treats Lower Lock as a failing return again.
8. Material work: hand-priming the pump, granary floors, a night inspection, the repair nobody is funding, unpaid hours and an injury.
9. The batch pays the *accepted for review* payoff bought with the narrowing and with Pell's office, and it is explicitly not a victory.
10. The batch ends with the Day 22 review falling due and the order on the docket, and with the ward materially worse off and one thing more capable than it was.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0011 — Entry

POV: Owen Rusk, third person limited.

Location/time: the River Desk supervised tray, the seal stair, and Lower Lock; batch Day 15, a Tuesday; steady rain.

Immediate goal: get Lower Lock's return into a docket as a filing, today, before anything else changes.

Resistance: the mechanics, not a villain. An entry is not a signature, it is a set of checks in an order, and the first one is that a filing must be complete on its face; the second is that the supervising officer's countersignature is required; the third, and the one that costs the day, is that a return may not be entered by a clerk named in the case or living in the district it concerns, and Owen is both. Pell reads that rule to him herself, having found it, and the two of them have to solve it honestly: Owen is appointed an assisting clerk on the supervised tray by the seal stair, in the ordinary form, on a case that is not his to control, and Pell countersigns because she is the supervisor and has said all along that she is not a party to the drafting. It takes most of a day. The three requests refused inside the backdating window are visible in the register the entry clerk must initial past, and nobody explains them, and Owen does not stop to ask, because he gave them up on purpose in Chapter 5 and the price was that he would not look.

Information revealed, in work and not in lecture: what a filing actually is. A subject, a term with a date, a jurisdiction, an effect, an appeal route, and two observers. The entry does not decide anything. It records that nine named adults have claimed a duty and that two people with separable interests have seen it performed, and from that moment the claim is a thing the Office can be asked about. Owen learns that the effect field has a menu and that a return can be entered with a narrower effect than the one asked for, and that the menu is where a person is quietly swallowed if the clerk who fills it in is careless.

Action or decision: he enters District Return 487-LL. He reads the effect aloud before he fills it, the way a man reads a thing he has just learned not to assume. The effect is the ward performs the maintenance of a named public crossing, at a named place, on a named interval, and nothing else. Pell countersigns. The date is the fifteenth. The return's first review falls due on the twenty-second, and the return says so on its face, in its own words, and Owen reads that too.

Emotional change: nothing happens to him, which is the point, and it is worse than something happening. A man who has spent a fortnight being frightened of his own precision gets a clean result and finds that the only feeling available is that the file is now bigger than he is.

Power or resource change: **his first qualified filing**. The freeze on the ward's standing lifts. The crossing's status returns as a duty the ward performs and as nothing else. He holds a named duty, and for the first time in a month there is a document in Docketford that exists because a set of people did things and a clerk wrote it down at their request. He still has no hold and no authority over anything.

Continuity fact to preserve: the return is entered on **Day 15** and its first review is due **Day 22**. Entering it lifts the freeze on the ward's standing and restores the crossing's status as a claimed duty. It does not make the crossing safe, funded, repaired or open; the pump grant stays suspended; the Chapter 8 load restriction stays in force; the Day 14 roll entry stands unamended. Pell's countersignature is a supervisory countersignature, and Pell has said in writing that she is not a party to the drafting and that her office has an interest in the filing succeeding. Owen's appointment as assisting clerk is a posting, not a qualification, and it is on a supervised tray.

Ending type: Earned relief, with a date.

Next-chapter pull: the return exists, and existing is not the same as being heard, and the Office has four days in which to decide what to do with it before the Council's quarter shuts, and three days after that in which to answer a petition it has not yet answered. A runner tells Owen the district office has requested the return for the Deputy Registrar's review, and the review is not a hearing.

### Chapter 0012 — The Answer Is a Date

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: the copying floor, the Front Desk, and the ward; Day 16 into Day 17. Administrative pressure and character work, deliberately placed immediately after the entry so the reader feels the difference.

Immediate goal: get the ward's return in front of somebody who can do something about it before the quarter closes on the eighteenth.

Resistance: nothing he does works, and the reason is structural. A district return does not go to the Council. It goes to the district office. The district office is the Office. The return has been asked for by the very office that received the transfer, and the Deputy Registrar's office is the one body in Docketford that can answer it, and it is also the one body that will be asked the question in public. Owen understands the trap a day too late, and Pell is the one who explains it, flatly, at the second desk, and is right: a return filed *at* a district office is a return the district office may review, and a review is not a hearing and a hearing is not a grace. Every route he tries is a route into the same room. He also learns, from the same clerk who explained it, that the Office's written answer to the petition is being drafted by a man who has been given a template, and that a template can be refused as inadequate by the docket clerk — a fact that matters in Chapter 13 and that Owen does not yet have a use for.

Information revealed: what the Office can do with a document without deciding anything about it. It can hold it. It can ask for a further observation. It can return it with a request for the naming of a person who is not named, which is a request that can be complied with by naming nobody and would be read as an admission. Owen watches a return get held.

Action or decision: Owen does the thing that is actually available and is not a trick: he asks the **district works inspection roll** to be read against the return's four recorded acts, in public, at the Front Desk, by any clerk who cares to attend. He does not attend. He cannot attend, because the moment he stands in that room the return is a thing he wanted. Pell goes, and reads, and says nothing afterwards except that the roll is a public document and always was.

Emotional change: Owen's sense of what he is for shifts by one degree. He has spent a fortnight as the man who finds the detail, and the detail that matters this week is a document's proper name, given to him by a twenty-two-year-old who will not do his work for him.

Power or resource change: no change in authority and one in capability. Owen can now name things correctly in a way he could not before, which is worth more in this building than a posting. The return is held, not refused, and holding is a state that expires.

Continuity fact to preserve: a **district return is reviewed by the district office**, not by the Council, and a review is not a hearing. The Office's answer to the petition is being drafted from a template and is due **Day 19**. A return can be held, returned, or asked to be further particularised, and none of those is a refusal and all of them cost time. The district works inspection roll is a public document at the Front Desk and has been all along.

Ending type: Cost, and a small discovery.

Next-chapter pull: the answer is due on the nineteenth, which is a Saturday, and the man who has to sign it will have to be told what a date is allowed to be. And at the ward, one of the nine has been to a clerk's counter in Mill Ward and has been told that a return is a thing a district office reviews and not a thing a person may withdraw from.

### Chapter 0013 — Saturday

POV: Owen Rusk, third person limited, with the Glass Stair docket clerk as a working presence rather than a named character.

Location/time: the copying floor, the ward hall, and the Glass Stair; Days 18 into 19. The answer lands, and the batch's political turn sits here.

Immediate goal: make the Office's answer say something that is not a date, before it is signed.

Resistance: he cannot do this and he has to watch it happen. The answer is short, correct, and worse than a refusal in exactly one way: it grants a **date**. It says the Deputy Registrar will receive the ward and the ward's witnesses on a named day in the last week of the rain season, and it says the receipt is a receipt and not a hearing, and it says the return is accepted for review. The docket clerk reads the refusal-of-a-receipt clause to Marguerite Bray in plain words and is helpful, and the helpfulness is the cruelty. Owen's own instinct is to attack the answer, and Pell stops him with one sentence: an answer that gave nothing would have been appealable, and this one cannot be appealed, because nobody can say it is wrong. She is right. He is sickened and it is the correct instinct being correctly defeated.

Information revealed, in the room and not in a lecture: **accepted for review is not binding**, and the Office has said so in writing, and a document that says it is only being reviewed is a document that can be put down at any time without anybody having lied. Also, the answer does not mention the load-restriction suspension at all, and its silence on the crossing's safety is itself a statement, and Owen understands that the Office has declined to say the crossing is unsafe while declining to say it is safe, because the first is a finding and the second is not.

Action or decision: Owen files nothing. He copies the answer into his notebook, in full, with the date, and he does not write anything next to it. What he does instead is walk to Lower Lock and read it out in the ward hall, in his own voice, including the sentence that is a date and not a hearing, and let twenty-eight people be angry at a piece of paper rather than at him. Ada asks the only question that matters, which is who wrote it, and Owen does not know and cannot find out, and says so.

Emotional change: Owen's first experience of losing an argument by being right, and of finding that the loss is survivable, and of noticing that it is survivable because a clerk on a stair and a ward steward he does not own are both better at this than he is. He is not humble. He is outmaneuvered and he knows it and it does not break him, which is new and slightly frightening.

Power or resource change: none in authority. The answer is on the docket, the return is accepted for review, the crossing's load restriction is untouched, the pump grant is still suspended, and the ward's standing is no longer frozen but is nowhere near safe. Owen has a date in the volume's future and nothing to do with it.

Continuity fact to preserve: the Office's answer is due **Day 19** and gives a **date in the last week of the rain season**, not a hearing. It records the return as **accepted for review**, which is not binding and not appealable, and the docket clerk says so in terms. The answer does not mention the load-restriction suspension. The real Glass Stair hearing remains a **Chapters 31–40** event and must not be pulled forward or treated as scheduled by this answer.

Ending type: Reversal, with a foothold nobody asked for.

Next-chapter pull: the answer is public, the answer can be read by anyone, and a reading of a public answer in a county record office is an act that takes a queue, a day, and a clerk who is bored. Two people in the ward who have not spoken to Owen want to read it, and one of them wants to read the sixteen names.

### Chapter 0014 — The Index

Location/time: the unfiled correspondence tray, the Front Desk index room, and Lower Lock; Day 20. Investigation and the batch's first perception-on-a-thin-file beat.

Emotional change: the private pleasure of a man with a shelf in front of him, curdling into the specific fear of Chapter 1, because this time the thing he has found is his own name in a register that is not his.

Ending type: Discovery, with a cost.

Next-chapter pull: the ward's copy of the transfer is a record he cannot read, and the Office's own index says its originating entry has a review interval that lapsed once, which means somebody renewed it twice and then did not renew it a third time, and that is a decision somebody made on a day. And in Lower Lock, Wenna Sarr has read the answer and has asked Owen, in front of her aunt's stall, a question about Ivy that he has been avoiding for two weeks.

### Chapter 0015 — What Ivy Needs

Location/time: the ward hall, Wenna Sarr's stall, and the copying floor; Day 21. Emotional consequence, and the six names given a human face.

Immediate goal: answer Wenna Sarr honestly about her niece, and find out what a protective return would require from a ward that has none of the parts.

### Chapter 0016 — Separability

POV: Owen Rusk, with Junia Pell as a co-presence who is not a POV carrier.

Location/time: the copying floor, the seal stair, and the ward; Day 22. The batch's hardest pressure, and the first beat that may touch the continuity statement.

Immediate goal: get the return through its first review without losing a witness, and find out who is attacking Pell.

Resistance: the attack is procedural, sourced and correct. A district office may challenge the independence of an observer on the ground that the observer's office has an interest in the outcome. Someone has raised it — in writing, through the countersignature desk, in the correct form, on the correct day. The challenge does not say Pell lied. It says that a Registry officer of the Registry of Continuant Works signed a community-filed instrument about a public work that the same Office transferred eleven months ago, and that the Office's interest in the filing succeeding is not a separable interest, and that the return should stand or fall on Voss alone. Voss is one witness. The return needs two. Owen has a morning.

Emotional pressure, and it is the batch's real content: Owen's first instinct is to find out who filed the challenge, and Pell tells him not to, and gives him a reason that is not moral. A challenge that names a filer can be answered; a challenge that is answered by guessing at a filer becomes a fight about character. But the second thing she says is worse, and it is said at her desk, not in a corridor. She tells him that the challenge is correct, that she has read it twice, and that if she were a reviewer she would sustain it, and that she is going to sustain it herself unless he can produce something better in a day.

Information revealed: what it is to be the only person in a document who can be attacked. Pell separates her interest in the margin, and the challenge is not about whether she separated it. It is that separation is a statement and a statement by an officer of the Office is a statement by the Office. Owen understands, and understands it because of Chapter 4, that this is the exact structure of the transfer: a smaller thing validly received and then used past the edge of what it could carry. The ward's own instrument has just been handed the same trick, in reverse, and he is the one who handed it over.

Action or decision: Owen does the thing that costs him. He does not defend Pell's independence and he does not attack the challenge. He goes to **Wend Voss** and asks him, as a favour and not as an instruction, whether he will stand on a document that a Registry officer has just been challenged on, and he tells him exactly what he is asking and exactly what it costs, including the possibility that a second challenge would fall on Voss next. Voss says yes for the reason he gave in Chapter 7, which is that a cost he has been carrying for eleven months with no district behind it is a cost he would rather see named, and he says one more thing: that the Office's interest is not separable, and that he has never pretended it was, and that the only reason it is tolerable is that he has written it down in his own hand where a reviewer can find it. Pell sustains her own challenge in writing, on the day, against herself, and does it first.

Emotional change: Owen's first experience of being told, by the person he wronged, that she is right about herself. It is the most uncomfortable thing that has happened to him in the volume and he handles it badly and survives it. Nothing is confessed. The continuity statement is **not** discussed. What happens is that Owen understands he has been keeping a notebook in the same way the Office keeps registers, and the realisation is a relief, because a register is at least honest about what it is.

Power or resource change: Pell's independence is now officially contestable on the record, in her own hand, and that contestability is what buys the return its **accepted for review** status rather than binding force. Voss's reservation stands. The return survives the challenge by one page of margin. Nothing is won.

Continuity fact to preserve: a challenge to an observer's independence is made in writing through the countersignature desk, is correct, and is **sustained by the observer herself**. Pell is a Registry officer and her office has an interest in the filing succeeding; her separation of interest is a statement by the Office and cannot cure that. The return therefore has **two living witnesses whose separability the Office can hold open**, which is precisely why the Office accepts it **for review** rather than binding it. The continuity statement is not raised, not confessed, and not treated as a debt by anybody.

Ending type: Defeat with a structural, unsatisfying foothold.

Next-chapter pull: the return survives a challenge and nobody is satisfied, and on the morning of the twenty-third a clerk in the Deputy Registrar's office asks a question at a meeting about the quarter that Owen is not supposed to be at, and the question is about sixteen returns and whether they are failing or merely late, and the answer changes what the Deputy Registrar does next.

### Chapter 0017 — Sixteen

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: the Deputy Registrar's office corridor, the copying floor, and Lower Lock; Day 23. Political pressure and the pivot into the order.

Immediate goal: work out, from a corridor conversation he was not invited to, what Pike is about to do with the sixteen.

Resistance: Owen is in the corridor because he is carrying a bundle, and Pike says the sentence that builds the instrument, and Pike is not hiding. The distinction that closes a stability order is not failure but **lateness**: a return that is late, short, or unsupported can be closed together with the others on the argument that the district cannot fund them all, and the word *fund* does the work that *failure* could not. Pike says the number again and it is not a number, it is a column, and Owen has the column in a notebook and cannot use it, because using it means putting his hand on it, and a clerk who puts his hand on a schedule becomes part of the schedule. He says so, out loud, to a man who is looking at the floor. He also learns that the instrument is drafted and waiting, and that it needs one signature, and that Pike has been waiting for a Friday for a quarter.

Information revealed: what a **stability order** is and is not. It is signed under the Deputy Registrar's solvency authority; it closes or suspends named failing district returns together; it is the instrument most likely to reach public appeal because it is the one that cannot be filed quietly; and it is not a load restriction, not a closure interval, and not a suspension of an individual status. And the list it works from is the sixteen, and Lower Lock is on it, and Lower Lock has been on it since before Owen filed anything, and being on it is not a consequence of anything he did.

Action or decision: Owen does not go to the ward with this. He goes to Pell, and he asks her the question that is actually available — whether a stability order can name a district that has entered a return in the current quarter, and whether being on the sixteen before a return was entered changes the schedule. She finds the answer, and the answer is yes, and the reason is that the schedule was made in the morning of the twelfth and a schedule is a schedule. He asks her to write it down. She writes it down and then tells him that writing it down makes her a participant, and he understands that he has done to her what she did to him, and that neither of them is going to be thanked for it.

Emotional change: Owen's first encounter with a decision he can see coming and cannot stop, and the discovery that the ward he has spent a month defending is already written into a column in a room he has never been in, under a word — *solvency* — that is not about the ward at all.

Power or resource change: no authority and no instrument. But Owen now knows the order's shape, its list, and its date, and the only thing he can do with that knowledge is make sure the order is not filed quietly, and the only way to do that is for the ward to be visible on the day it is signed. Which means Ada, and which means a conversation Owen is not looking forward to.

Continuity fact to preserve: **Stability Order 114** is drafted and waiting at the end of this chapter and is signed by Halden Pike in Chapter 18, on Day 24, under the Docketford Deputy Registrar's solvency authority. It works from the sixteen named failing returns of the current quarter. Lower Lock was sixth on that list. Being named is a function of the quarter's arrears, not of anything filed after it. The order closes or suspends named failing returns together and is **not** the Chapter 8 load-restriction suspension and **not** a closure interval. The schedule was made on the morning of Day 12.

Ending type: Political pressure, closing on a decision.

Next-chapter pull: the order needs a signature and a Friday, and the ward needs to be visible when it gets one, and Ada Rusk is not going to want Owen in the room. And in the ward, the person who withdrew from the return is still living four doors away and is still being spoken to by people who are being told she left.

### Chapter 0018 — The Signature

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: the seal stair, the Glass Stair antechamber, and the ward hall; Day 24. The batch's climax in the political register: the order is signed and it is public.

Immediate goal: be in the ward when the order is signed, and be visible doing nothing.

Resistance: Owen cannot be in the ward and cannot be at the seal stair, and he chooses the ward, and the choice costs him the sight of the signature, which he gets second-hand and inaccurately, and the inaccuracy is the point. What reaches Lower Lock is a rumour before it is an instrument. Ada has got to the ward hall early and has told the hall what is coming and has told them what it is, and she has done it better than Owen could, and when he arrives she tells him so. The order itself is worse than a closure: it does not close Lower Lock, it **suspends the district's standing to take the ward's return alongside fifteen others**, and the difference between being closed and being suspended with fifteen others is that the first is a decision about you and the second is a queue you are in.

Information revealed, in the document rather than in a lecture: what a stability order does to a person who is not named in it. Fifteen other districts lose the same thing Lower Lock loses on the same afternoon, and for about four days nobody notices, and then somebody does, and the public-appeal route opens because an instrument that cannot be filed quietly has been filed loudly. Owen is not proud of the order and is not sorry for it either, and that is a new and unpleasant position to be in.

Action or decision: the return is **amended** on the day, by a witnessed amendment reaching the district office before the stroke, in two respects only: it records the suspension as a fact the ward did not cause, and it names the order. An amendment is a new entry from a stated time forward and it never erases the earlier entry. This is the amendment machinery in use for the first time in the volume, and Ada dictates it, and it is the first document in the case she has written entirely herself. Owen's name is on it as the assisting clerk who entered the return. He signs the entry and nothing else.

Emotional change: Owen is relieved that the order will be public and ashamed of the relief, because the ward is losing the same thing fifteen other wards are losing and the only reason the loss matters is that it is visible. He says this out loud to Ada, once, and she tells him it is the first true thing he has said to her in a month, and that she already knew it.

Power or resource change: **Stability Order 114 is signed and names Lower Lock.** The return survives the order as a document and its seven-day review is now due into a review of the order. The batch's real cost lands: the ward's grant machinery is frozen again, the pump stays suspended, the crossing's status is contested, and the thing that has changed is that the loss is now on a public docket. Nothing has been made safe. One instrument is now public, and public is not the same as won.

Continuity fact to preserve: **Stability Order 114 is signed on Day 24 by Halden Pike** under the solvency authority, on the sixteen named failing returns, one of which is Lower Lock. It is distinct from the spring engineering notice and from the Chapter 8 load-restriction suspension, and it is the instrument that reaches public appeal. Ending type: Cost, made public.

Next-chapter pull: the order is on a docket and the ward's return comes up for its own review in the same week, and the review now has to decide whether a return that is suspended with fifteen others is a return that can still be renewed, and the person who reviews it is a clerk who is going to be careful.

### Chapter 0019 — The Review

Location/time: the district office review room and the ward hall; Day 25. Recovery and reckoning. **The return's first seven-day review fell due on Day 22 and was adjourned by the challenge to Pell's independence in Chapter 16. Immediate goal: get the return through a first seven-day review that happens while a stability order is closing over it.

Resistance: the review is three days late on its own face and the delay began as his fault, and the review is careful, procedural and correct, and it turns on a question Owen cannot answer with a document. ### Chapter 0020 — Who Sent It

POV: Owen Rusk, with Marguerite Bray carrying the scene's weight at the end.

Location/time: the ward hall and the copying floor; Day 26 into Day 27. The batch's closing discovery and its forward pull.

Immediate goal: find out who put the transfer into the world, now that defending the ward no longer requires him to know.

Resistance: the discovery is small and it is worse than a conspiracy. The delivery book has a second entry eleven months back, in Bray's own hand, four days after the receipt arrived: a request, made by a person in the ward, for a consolidation schedule to be sent out. The request was refused twice and granted on the third, and the refusals are in the Office's register and are the same three refusals Owen surrendered in Chapter 5. So the two-day backdating was not somebody concealing a date. It was somebody who had already asked three times and been told no, and who then dated the schedule to the day before the answer, and whose answer came on the day it was stamped. The person who made the request is not named in the book, because the person who made it asked not to be named, and the person who asked not to be named is the person whose household the receipt covered. Tal Sarr asked three times and was refused and went back a fourth time with a sheet, and the fourth time it was taken as an acceptance.

Information revealed: the office is not a machine that fails. It is a machine that answers, and the answer it gave a man three times was no, and the fourth thing it received was a signed sheet, and it took a signed sheet as a settled matter because a settled matter is what it is built to receive. Owen's surrendered proof turns out to have been the receipt of a refusal being reused as a consent, which is the same error the Office made with the transfer's scope, one level down, in the same direction. He understands that he gave away the only thing he had because he thought it was clerical, and it was not clerical at all.

Action or decision: Owen does not reinstate the withdrawn error. That is the whole of his growth in one line: he has the proof back, and he does not put it back, because a withdrawn charge cannot be re-entered by the man who withdrew it, and because the charge of **scope** is the one that matters and it is unaffected. He tells the ward, out loud, in the hall, including the part about the three refusals and the part about his having given them away. Marguerite asks him what the refusals were worth. He answers out loud, as he answered Ada in Chapter 5: *Three refusals, and a man who asked three times, and a sheet that came back signed instead of refused, and eleven months.* She writes nothing down this time.

Emotional change: Owen has spent a month proving that the Office is not careless, and arrives at the thing he should have suspected in Chapter 1, which is that the Office is careful and that carelessness is not the problem. He is not in love with the institution and he is not its enemy. He is a man in it. The batch ends with him knowing that the receipt is a dead man's fourth attempt at being heard, and that the ward's return is the only version of that attempt that will still be standing in a year.

Power or resource change: no new authority and no new instrument. He has the origin of the transfer and no legal use for it. The return is entered, reviewed, amended, and under a stability order. The pump is still suspended, the crossing is still closed to loads, the six protective returns still cannot be filed, Cobb Rennie still cannot be found, and the brass tab is still shut. Continuity fact to preserve: the transfer originated in **three refused requests for a consolidation schedule**, refused by the district office, followed by a fourth approach with a signed sheet that the Office took as an acceptance. The refusals are the three requests inside the backdating window, which Owen surrendered in Chapter 5 and which are still unexplained **as requests** — this chapter explains their content without the Office ever having explained it, and it does not reinstate his withdrawn charge. **The withdrawn error is not reinstated.** The inquiry continues to allege **scope**, not error.
