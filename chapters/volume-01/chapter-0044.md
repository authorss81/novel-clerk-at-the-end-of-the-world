# Chapter 44 — Ten of the Clock in the Long Room

The marker went in at seven forty on the fifty-ninth day of the month in the rain, with three people on the top path above the second bend and two at the path end, and it was the same as it had been on the thirty-eighth and the forty-fifth and the fifty-second, and it was the first time in seven weeks that the ward had done it in daylight and gone to a hearing afterwards.

Ada Rusk made the list up at about half past seven in the morning in the shed doorway with a slate and a stub of chalk, and she made it the way she makes everything, which is in a column and without stopping to ask anybody's opinion, and the column had three headings and one of them was a number.

*Who goes.* The column came to nine names by the time she had got to the bottom of it, and nobody argued about the ones that were not on it, because everybody in the shed already knew which ones, and they were a woman of seventy-one with a pitch to be back on and a man with a chest that has gone the colour of candle wax who keeps the night watch on the top of Fetter Lane and was not going to be told by a hearing that he could not see.

"Right," said Ada Rusk, when the list was nine. "Now the hard part. The rest of you are going to walk to the top of the lane and take the path down to the water and the boat, and the first boat goes at six and the second at ten, and the water has not gone down since Tuesday and it is not going to go down today. It will take us four hours to get there and we will be in the room at ten because we will have started at six, and the chair begins at ten and does not wait."

"It is two miles," said Dory Anstruther.

"It is two miles on a Tuesday," said Ada Rusk, "and it is four today, and anybody in this list who cannot do it is not in this list, and I have not got anybody else to send."

Owen Rusk read the list. It was in her chalk, in her capitals, and there were nine names on it, and the ninth was *M. Bray, steward*, and the fourth was *J. Pell, observer, arriving on her own account*, and none of the other eight was a name he would have put on a list.

"Where is Mrs Sarr," he said.

"Wenna Sarr is not going," said Ada Rusk. "She has a stall in forty minutes and Ivy Sarr is in the ward and Ivy Sarr is twelve and somebody has to be in this house."

"And Mr Tillet."

"Mr Tillet is going because Mr Tillet is a man who cooks on a middle crossing and who has said four sentences in a hall in seven weeks and who has a page of his own going to a Council in Concord Hall with his name on the top of it and nothing else on it, and I am not having that man discover what a room is by reading about it." She put the chalk down. "And Owen — you are going in as a co-petitioner and an assisting clerk and you are going to say so out loud when they ask you, in those words and no others, and if anybody offers you a seal of any kind today you get up and leave and I will rule it in the book."

---

They were in the long room at about four minutes to ten and there were about forty people in it, and the room was the one with the high windows, and the clock in it was a different clock from the review room, and the doors were shut and the doors stayed shut.

The chair was the clerk of petition 487-GS-19, a woman of about forty, and she did not open the sitting, and the reason she did not open it was that she had said on the Wednesday she would stop anybody who confused the two things in front of her, and she did it in the first thirty seconds, before any paper was touched, in a room of forty people, in a voice that carried.

"This is a sitting on two matters. They are on one docket and they are to be heard on one day in one room, and they are not one matter, and nothing said in the first half of this morning is evidence in the second half of it and nothing said in the second half is evidence in the first. The first is the petition of Marguerite Bray, steward, in which the district asks for a hearing upon the return entered by it. The second is Stability Order 114 of the twenty-fourth day of this month, on an appeal. **The order was not made by this office and it is not withdrawn by anything said about the return, and a return that is certified this morning is not a finding that the order is wrong.**" She looked down at the paper. "Both are on the file. Both are on the table. Proceed."

---

Marguerite Bray read the ward's own file.

She had it in a leather book with a ruled second column and she had had it for thirty-six years and she read it standing at the table with her hand on the page, and she did not read it as a clerk reads a document, and she did not read it as an advocate reads a brief, and she read it at the pace she reads everything, which is slowly, and once more than was needed.

She gave the date of the transfer and the two leaves of its annex and the fact that one of them is a claim about a place. She gave the ninth day, which she did not get from any Registry volume, and the four returns to a house in Fetter Lane on the sixteenth, seventeenth and eighteenth of the month, and the sentence on the back of the third one, underlined twice, in her own pencil: *can one house consent for a ward*. She gave the roll entry of the fourteenth. She gave the entry of the fifteenth and what the Registry's own small print said it did and did not do. She gave the answer of the nineteenth in the four paragraphs it was written in, and said the number of the words, which nobody had asked her for and which she had counted, and which was two hundred and twenty-four. She gave the six refusals of the sub-table in the intake year, which she had asked about one at a time in her own hall on six occasions to six different people and had not been answered once, and she gave the number and not the answer, because an answer is not a fact about a ward and a number is.

And then she read out the schedule.

That is the part the room had not seen. It is a covering page and four weeks of hours, and the covering page carries the name of the work, the interval, the two observers' names in their own hands, and a withdrawal right, and the reading clause, and the disclaimer printed three times.

She read the disclaimer three times, because Ada Rusk had told her to, and the third time she read it in exactly the same voice as the first two, and about four people in the room looked at each other.

And then she read the standpipe rota, because it is in the schedule, and anybody may read the schedule, and she is the steward of this ward and she read it in a room in Docketford with forty people in it.

Four names and a man of fifty-eight. And one of those four households is the Sarr household, and it is on there, and the household is a woman of about sixty and a girl of twelve.

"Those are the hours of this week," said Marguerite Bray, "and the week before, and the two before that, and the one before that, and I have read them because I keep them, and the four names on the standpipe rota are the four names on the rota and one of them is a household of two people and one of the two of them is twelve years old, and she is not in this room and she is at home, and I am going to say that in a room in Docketford because anybody may read the schedule on any day at the weir path and I would rather say it here first."

Nobody in the room said anything.

---

Ada Rusk was not a branch of the return. She had voted against the transfer on the seventh day of the month with her hand up, and the class of persons who may object to the return is the eight persons whose branches are in it and any person the district office names in writing, and it has been eight since the twenty-fourth of the month and no instrument has ever named anybody in.

She was in the room inside the return's scope through the acts of a crew she leads, and she had said so out loud in a hall on the seventh day without apology, and she said it again from the table, in eight words, before she said anything else.

"I am not a branch of this. I am in it through a crew."

And then she was careful for about six minutes, and the care was the most impressive thing Owen Rusk has ever watched a witness do in a room, because he is the only person in the room who knows how competent she is about the thing she refused to talk about.

She argued the return. She argued that the claim in it is a duty and not a status, and she said the words *the safety, the condition and the use of the Ninth Weir is not a subject of this hearing except by way of the instruments already in this file* in her own hand's exact words, because she had written them on the Wednesday of the previous week and had made a Registry clerk read them back in a hall and had stopped him twice for the differences.

She argued the schedule. She argued the withdrawal right, which is in her own capitals, which says that a person may take their own branch out of it and the rest of it does not fall over.

She argued the four blank pages at the back, and the fact that one of the four has said no with his reason in his own hand, and that three have not answered, and that nobody has been chased, and that the day the ward named as the limit was the day of this hearing, and that the ward has kept to it.

And at no point in six minutes did she say one word about the abutment.

She is the only person alive who has had both hands on the crack at four in the morning. There are four officers in this city who could put a number on it and a Registry engineer behind all of them. She said nothing about any of it, and a district that is silent about a work it maintains is a district that is not claiming a status, and she knew that at the time and had known it since the fourth week of the month, and Owen Rusk sat at the table and watched the single most valuable piece of self-restraint of his career performed by a person who was not his sister in a room he had asked to be in.

---

The second person at the far end of the ward's route came in at about a quarter past ten and was not announced, because nobody announces a person at the far end of a route, and she was carrying a folded sheet under her arm and she gave it to Marguerite Bray and not to the clerk, and she stood at the side of the table.

Hester Lund has kept the tide table at Coal Hill for nineteen years and is not an officer of anything and holds no office and has no seal. She was on the public roll of a district that is one of the sixteen named in Stability Order 114, and she was going to be asked about that in the afternoon.

"A standing in the room with a document on the table in front of it," she said, when Marguerite Bray asked her, in four words, in front of a clerk, which was the whole of it.

"That is a person who will say what the district did," said the chair, "and is not a witness to the return and signs nothing the district performs."

"Correct," said Hester Lund.

"And if the district and you differ, in this room, the district is right and you are wrong, and you will say so out loud in front of these people."

"Yes," said Hester Lund, "and I am going to do it once, now, so that nobody in this room has to wait for it and wonder."

She turned round to the forty people.

"I have read the schedule. I read the sheet and not their book, as I said I would, and I have not seen their delivery book and I am not going to. The ward performed on a roster in public on a day of the ward's own choosing and I have read four weeks of it, and I was not there for the four weeks and I am not going to pretend to have been. That is all I have. I am going to say what the ward did on the day I was asked about, and I have read the document, and that is the whole of what I am here for.

"And there is one thing in it I am not going to let pass without saying, and I am going to say it now because it is the only disagreement I am going to have with this ward in a public room and I would rather have it out of my mouth than have it in somebody else's.

"**The schedule of this ward does not claim a seed stall.** There is a woman of seventy-one in Lower Lock who has had the same pitch for forty-one years and it went into a foot and a half of water on Saturday night, and it is in neither leaf of the annex that took their crossing and their pump and their granary, and there is no instrument in this city that has ever named it, and there is not going to be one. She is owed nothing. She told me so herself on Monday and she told a hall of thirty people, and she is right.

"I am not a witness to that and I cannot speak for her and I have not been asked to. The ward did not claim it. The ward could not have claimed it. I am standing at the end of a route for a district that has been honest about the size of itself for seven weeks, and this is me saying in a room, out loud, that the honest size does not include a thing that ought to be in it, and the district is right and I am wrong, and I would be grateful if the clerk would come and find out who has to be told, because I am not telling her."

Owen Rusk looked at the floor of the long room for about a second and a half.

---

The district office answered at about eleven, and it answered well, and Owen Rusk had known for six weeks that it would.

It answered that the return is accepted for review and is not a binding continuation, and that it confers no right as to the safety, condition or use of the work. It answered that the roll entry of the fourteenth stands and has not been amended. It answered that the load-restriction suspension of the tenth of the month is not affected and is not superseded. It answered that the order of the twenty-fourth is a separate instrument and is not determined by anything said about the return, and that the two will be dealt with separately this afternoon.

And it answered the ward's point of law.

The ward had put one point of law in, and it was Precedent 37/C, and the point was this: the Registry has recognised a district return and entered a sum against a named work on four grounds, one of which is a published schedule of the office's refusals for the quarter, and therefore before this office refuses a return it must have published a schedule of what it has refused, and it has not refused this one, and the schedule is in the room.

And the district office said: the precedent compels publication and compels nothing else, and the schedule is produced, and it is read.

The officer who read it was about thirty and had read the ward's comparison aloud in a waiting hall nine days before, and he had the same face he had had on the Saturday, and he read it in the same voice.

One hundred and nine entries in the quarter.

Forty-one of the dispositions were the same four words.

*No district office competent.*

And he read out that the office maintains dispositions and not reasons, and that a disposition is not a record of a reason, and that the office is not required by any instrument in force in this district to maintain a column of reasons, and that the words are a statement of the reach of this office at the date of the making and are not a finding upon any person, place or work.

And then the chair asked him the question, in front of forty people, and it was the clerk of the docket and not the ward who asked it, and she said: *Is that the office's answer for the quarter, or for this morning?*

And the officer said it was the office's answer in this quarter and would be its answer in the next one, and that a comparison posted at a public counter is not a filing, and the room laughed, and he did not look up.

---

Owen Rusk had one sentence and he had carried it up a hill on the Monday of the previous week and had not spent it in a hall, and it was eight words long and it was the only thing in six weeks he had wanted to say to anybody in his own person.

He stood up as a co-petitioner and said that first, because the clerk of the docket had told him on the Friday that describing yourself is free and that nearly nobody in the building had ever done it, and he had entered himself on the matter in a form in one line.

"I am a co-petitioner and an assisting clerk to a supervised tray," he said. "I am not an officer of this office and I carry no seal of any kind."

"Sit down, Mr Rusk," said the chair, "and ask your question."

"In the second month of the Concord Year 474," said Owen Rusk, "this district withdrew the return of Lower Lock. There is a runner's sheet in this file with the day and the hour on it, and both halves of the answer on it, and the Deputy Registrar's own instruction to the runner to write both. One hundred and eighty-one persons of this ward were moved off four hundred yards at the bottom of Fetter Lane on a district engineer's finding, and the water was in it four days later, and the ground was clear. That is what the instrument was for and it was the right instrument and the man who signed it has said in a hall that he would sign it again.

"The file does not record the twenty-two. Twenty-two people were drowned in the same flood on ground that was not cleared. The Deputy Registrar has told this room why, in a hall, in front of forty people, and the reason is that the instrument is about a place and there is no column in it for them.

"My question is this. **Did you read your own file the way this office reads every file, in the way that was favourable to you, and sign it?**"

There was no noise at all in the long room.

Halden Pike was on his feet at the table with his hat under his arm and he had been on his feet before Owen got to the second word of the question, and Owen noticed that and understood it and thought about it for the rest of his life.

"No," said Halden Pike. "I did not read it favourably. I read it the way the office reads every file, which is by what is in it, and what was in it was a wire, a finding, a clearance and a number, and the reading was the office's and not mine, and I have said on a sheet in this file that I did not lie and that I would sign it again, and I say it now so that the sheet and the room agree.

"And the word you have used is *hid*, and I am not going to argue with it, and I want to be exact about why, because I could win the argument and it would be the worst thing that has happened to me this year. If I argue about whether I hid them then this becomes a fight about a verb, and in about four minutes nobody in this room will remember that there were twenty-two people, and that is how a number gets lost. The twenty-two are in the room. Put them in the record. I am not going to spend the afternoon on whether I hid them. I did what I did and I have never once been able to make that sentence sound like what it is, and the reason I cannot make it sound like what it is is that it is true, and there is no column for it, and this Office does not keep a column of facts about rivers."

---

Nobody objected to the hearing date within the four days, which is on the file, and the chair said so in one line, and then the chair read the order, and the order went into public appeal with a date on it in front of forty people, in a room, by people, and it is exactly the thing that could not be filed quietly and it is the thing the twenty-four of the month made possible by being signed under a solvency authority and being public and being appealable on application, and the application was made on the fifty-ninth day of the month at about half past twelve in the afternoon by a woman of sixty-one who was the petitioner in the other matter and had to be told twice that she was allowed to do it in both.

"**Stability Order 114 is carried into public appeal on petition 487-GS-19, to be listed, and is not withdrawn, and the sixteen returns named in it are sixteen.**"

Sixteen. Lower Lock sixth. A woman keeping a tide table on a stone stair at Coal Hill is the fourth of them, and Owen Rusk did not say that in the room and Hester Lund did not say it in the room and Ada Rusk read all sixteen out in a hall in the third week of the month and every person in Lower Lock has known since.

---

The ward was at the bottom of Fetter Lane at about nine that night, having left Docketford at four, and the hall was open because the hall is open when there is something, and there were fourteen people in it.

Ada Rusk read the week out at ten past six, in public, in the ordinary voice, and she read all of it, and the standpipe rota, and the four names on it, and the man of fifty-eight, and at the end she said:

"That is the week, and it is the same as last week, and there will be fewer of us here next week than there were last week, and Mrs Bray is going to rule the number, and the number is the number and not the weather."

Marguerite Bray wrote it in the delivery book in her own hand with the day and the hour, and read it back.

And Owen Rusk stood at the back of a ward hall in Lower Lock at ten past six in the evening on the fifty-ninth day of the month and found that he had won an argument, in a room with forty people in it, against a careful man, with eight words, and that the thing he felt about it was a kind of flatness, like the flatness of a bell after it has been struck.

Because it was in the record. Eleven days in the fourth quarter of the Concord Year 471, a batch of forty-one entries, one sitting, one officer, and at about a quarter past eleven this morning a district officer of the same office had stood at a table and read out that there were one hundred and nine entries in a quarter and that forty-one of the dispositions in the column were the same four words.

The same number. In the same office. In the same kind of sitting.

And the deputy registrar of this district had signed the first of them, in the fourth quarter of the Concord Year 471, on a ground written as *revision of residence status, flood quarter arrears*, and he was alive, and he was in the building, and he had answered a question about twenty-two people this morning with a sentence about a column that does not exist, and he had never once been told that the initials on his own batch are the initials his office used that quarter, because Owen Rusk has never told him, has never asked him, and has never written the two together in any book.

So there were two people in that office this morning who had each stood at a table and said a number, and neither of them knew that the other's number was the same, and the only person in the room who knew both was a copy clerk who was not a witness to anything.

He stood at the back of a hall and worked out what it was, in about four seconds, standing up, in the way he had learned to do it in the seventh week, and it was nothing. It was a coincidence of a number. Forty-one residence statuses struck in one sitting in a flood quarter and forty-one dispositions in a refusal column in a quarter nine years later are not one practice, they are not connected, they were done by two different people for two different reasons, and Chapter thirty of the first month of this case said so on the page in a hall in front of thirty people, and it is the truest thing in this file and it is the only thing in this file that is neither a document nor a person.

And he wanted it to be something. That was the part he would not have admitted to anybody in a hall. He wanted two numbers to be one practice so badly that for about four seconds in a long room with high windows he was not thinking about the twenty-two at all.

The connection was the only private thing he had left. His mother was a woman who could not get upriver in the wet and was not at an address for eleven days and died of a fever that the delay did not cause, and there is one living person who knows what that is and he is the one who cannot put it in front of anybody, and it is not admissible, and it is a number that also happens to be a number in a column.

Ada Rusk said at the door, with the slate under her arm, in the voice she uses for things she means:

"Mr Rusk. The order is in appeal and it has a date on it and the office has answered in public and it answered well and it gave us nothing, and the twenty-two are in the record, and the sixteen are sixteen, and the crossing is shut, and the wage is not funded, and the repair is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds, and the bin in the granary is a sixth gone and the other one will be next week. And tomorrow the return comes up for the sixth time and I have not decided what comes off it, and I would like you to be in this hall at half past eight in the morning and I would like you to bring nothing."
