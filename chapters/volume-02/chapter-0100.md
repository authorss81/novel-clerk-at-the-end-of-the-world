# Chapter 100 — Clean Count

The counting tent came down on the Sunday morning of the twenty-eighth day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488 at about seven in the morning, in about an hour and a half, by about nineteen people, and the stone went into its wooden case at about half past seven and the case went onto a handcart and the cart went up to the landing at eight, and up the river it went with the season behind it and the season was eleven settlements short of its quota, and nobody put that on the cart and nobody wrote it down and it is in a field behind a tannery and in a gate on the Fetter Bend road in words in a Warden's hand.

**The board stayed up.** It has stayed up since the twenty-eighth day of the second month in the west gate of Cutter's Fields, ten settlements, five columns, the fourth and fifth empty on all ten, the tenth row a building, and above the count of the ninth day of the second month qualified nine of eleven not reconciled Lower Lock and Ossery, and above that the suspension of the Warden, one loop of tarred cord, a page long, the reason not a figure, and under that the notice of the twenty-sixth with the survey round at its foot and under the round four ruled lines and a date, the twenty-sixth, not signed, **and the four lines are empty, in the fourth month of the Concord Year 488, and nobody in this county filled them in and nobody was asked to, and the Warden has not ended her own suspension and it is not hers to end.**

And under all of it, because the woman who maintains the board will not put a sheet over a sheet on the last morning of a season, is the certification of the count of the season of 489 for Docketford district, qualified, with no figure of premises of record upon it at all, and the schedule attached, five columns, ten rows, and not one word about why on it anywhere.

**And beside the frame, on two stakes, at about four feet off the ground, there is a second board and it is smaller than the frame, and it was not there before the twenty-sixth day of this month, and it carries eleven rows, and it is the reason a man came down a hill at half past six on a Sunday morning.**

---

Owen Rusk was in the field from about half past six in the morning and the woman who maintains the board was already there with a lamp, because she had come to take the cords off the frame that nobody was going to need in the winter, which is a thing she does every year, and which is not in a form.

He did not stand in front of the small board for about an hour. He went and read the bottom of the frame first, and then the small board, and then he did the thing he has done since the eighth day of the third month, which is that the county runs short of things to count.

**The small board is the settlements struck from the count for the season, being the eleven corrections of the season of 489, and it is not the ten settlements named on a schedule that were not looked at, and it is not the eleven settlements in another district that a warden struck in her own hand on the twenty-fourth of this month, and none of the three is any of the other two, and about four people in this county have already asked.**

**The count of the season of 489 is certified and short. Docketford district's figure of premises of record for the season is none, and none is a figure, and it is the first time in the record of this county that a count has been certified with no figure of premises of record upon it. The ten settlements named on the printed schedule are unresolved. Marl Dykes is not on the schedule and is not on the schedule of unresolved settlements and is not on the small board, and is not restored, and there is no form on which anybody may ask why it went. Nether Sill is not restored and its reason is four and eleven households kept the water it was about and are not on any document. Hark Holm is not restored. The eleven households off a reach in the Concord Year 484 are not restored, are not on any document, and are not affected by anything that has happened since.**

**And the small board has eleven rows on it, and every one of the eleven reasons is a figure, and six of the eleven carry the same figure in the second column and the third, and the figure for Slackwater is nine, and the figure of premises of record for Slackwater is nine, and that is the whole of what a column of reasons is, and a person can check it in about four seconds.**

He read the column aloud. There was nobody in the field. The lamp was on the woman's side of the board and there was about two feet of light on the first two rows and his own breath on the rest of it.

*Four. Nine. Four. Eleven. Seven. Six. Two. Three. Eleven. Five. Seven.*

He counted them. He did it the way he does it, from the top, and he has counted about eleven hundred columns in nine years in this county and he has never once counted one twice in a field.

Then he counted them again.

*Four. Nine. Four. Eleven. Seven. Six. Two. Three. Eleven. Five. Seven.*

Eleven both times.

**That is the entire job he has.** Not a ward that cannot be counted, not a settlement that is not on a list, not a sluice wheel held together with oak wedges by nine people for fourteen days, not a settlement four miles up a track with nine doors of record out of twenty-two households and a tank and a book in five hands and a custody limit written into a return by the people who live in the yard: **eleven figures, eleven reasons, read down, and eleven.**

He is a Stage 2 Continuance Examiner of the Count, Docketford district, entered on the fourth day of the first month of the Concord Year 488, and he holds three active holds and three named claimants — Ivy Sarr's household of Fetter Lane, a person who cannot be asked; Mavis Rudge of Witherow, whose file says she asked and the answer was no; Ned Callow of Quill Bank, who asked for a person in a room and did not get one and will not ask again. **A hold is an answerability and preserves nothing. There is no fourth hold and there is not going to be, and the thing he claimed in a yard at Slackwater on the tenth day of this month is a claim and not a duty and there is no instrument in this county for it, and it has no term and no interval and no panel, and it is in a longhand book on a shelf in a room he has the key to, and the room has no window and a bulb on a flex taped twice, and the book may not leave the building because there is not one instrument in this county for a man who wants to give something away.**

---

**At about half past eight on the Sunday morning the twenty-eighth day of the fourth month the people of Lower Lock came up the hill, in about four groups, and the ward's week does not stop for a season and never has and there is no form in this county that says a season may interrupt a Thursday.**

The marker had gone up on the flats at seven forty on the Friday and had not been taken down, which is what a marker does, and the number on it in chalk was the number for the Friday, and the channel had been done on the Wednesday night and the silt had been carted back onto the flats, which is the single most expensive thing anybody in that ward has ever done and which nobody has ever costed, and the pump had been primed at four in the morning by Bram Tillet and the household in the two rooms in Fetter Lane, eleven minutes, and the first stroke had brought up the water that was standing in the pipe and it was brown.

Ruth Mapp read the roster out at ten past six in the evening, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and there is no form in this county on which a ward may read anything to anybody.

**The granary is open and empty and has been since the seventh day of the second month of this year, and the figure is nothing for the eleventh week running, and Marguerite Bray has written the day and the hour against it in her own hand every week, and nobody is going to buy anything, and the decision not to buy was made on the first day of the second month by a woman in that hall and it was correct and it is still correct and it will be correct in June.**

The crossing at the bottom of Fetter Lane is shut to loads and to vehicles above four tons. The repair is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and is unfunded. The notice at the weir path says that any district able to perform the repair is invited to perform it, and it has been in the weather since the flood, and it has brought no district, and it is going to be there in June, and the reason it is going to be there in June is that the day the notice comes down is the day that ward has stopped asking.

**Ada Rusk said it at the west gate at about nine in the morning, in front of about twenty people, and she said it as a fact and not as a sentence, and she is the eleventh door in a ward that is on a schedule of ten places nobody went to look at, and the small board has eleven settlements on it and this ward is not one of them, and there is no column anywhere in this county that says she was looked at.**

"There is no row for us on the second board," she said. "**There is a row for us on the other one, at the bottom of that frame, and it says no. The small board is eleven settlements and eleven reasons, and every reason in it is a number, and there is no number about this ward in this county to put on it, and that has happened thirteen times this year and it has happened again this morning, and I am not going to say a fourteenth time what I have said thirteen times, so I am going to say this instead and then I am going to stop.**

"**This ward performed on the Thursday and the Friday and the Wednesday night and at four in the morning. The season's count was certified on the twenty-eighth of the third month with no figure of premises of record on it and a list of ten places on it, and one of the ten is us, and the reason we are on it is that we were named for reconciliation in the last week of the second month by a department that had not surveyed anything, and we asked to be reconciled in our own hall on the tenth day of the third month and were told in front of about twenty-two people that there is no form in this county on which a ward may ask for anything. And none of the six boards on that frame says that, and there is no column for it, and there is not going to be one, and I have stopped asking for it.**"

Somebody at the back said, "**Are you all right, Ada.**"

"**No,**" said Ada Rusk, "**and I have been since the seventh day of the second month, and I would rather it was in a field at the west gate in front of twenty people than in a book, and I have not written it in the book.**"

---

**The people of Lower Lock went down the hill at about half past ten in about four groups and one of the groups had a girl of twelve in it who had a box of seed trays under her arm, because the trays are not stopped and there is nothing in the granary and the trays come anyway, and she has carried them up that hill on about four Saturdays in nine years and nobody has ever thought about why.**

She came back across the field to the small board on her own at about eleven, with the box against her hip, and read the third column, which is the reasons, and then read the second column, which is the premises, and then went and found Owen Rusk standing about eight feet off on the side of the board the lamp was not on.

"Is that ours," she said.

"No."

"**Why not.**"

"Because a column of reasons is made out of figures, and there is no figure about this ward in this county, and I have told you that twice in the last two months and you have written it in a book twice and both times you asked me whether the figure could be a number of doors instead of a number of people and both times I said no."

"**So the column is for places with figures in them,**" said Ivy Sarr, "**and we have not got one, and the bins are empty again and there is not going to be anything in them on Thursday either.**"

"That is correct."

"**Then the board is not about us,**" she said, "**and I have got the trays down by the time you have got your coat.**"

And she went down the hill, and there is no form in this county on which a person may be thanked for that, and Owen Rusk did not thank her and has not written it down, and it is the only exchange between the two of them this year that was not about a figure, and it is not in the book.

---

**The field was empty by about eleven and the count of the season of 489 was over, and a second board was standing in it with eleven rows on it, and the frame had six documents on it and a man of about fifty-five in a room across the cut did not know that one of the numbers in the third column was one he had typed out of a book in the Concord Year 486, and he was not going to be told, and he is not a villain and he is not redeemed and he is not grateful and he is not going to become anybody's instrument, and in October, on the twenty-eighth, a form is going to come back with a year in it that he put there himself and he is going to find out what his honesty became.**

The board stays up. It will be there in June and it will be there in the fourth month of next year, and the fourth column of the strip of paper the size of a receipt will still say the fifth column will be completed in the season of 490 from the returns of district offices and of water offices, and there is no post anywhere in this county with a figure on it for ten surveys of ten settlements of record in a district of four hundred people, and there is one nailed to a post in the weather at the head of Fetter Lane with a figure on it that is four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds and belongs to that crossing alone.

**A ward performed, and is not counted, and says so.**

---

**And the question that the next volume inherits is not answered here and is not on the board, and it is the one that has been underneath this entire volume since a lane in the rain in the Concord Year 487 said it, and it is not Owen Rusk's question and it is not a question a person can put in a reason box.**

It is this. **If redaction can be necessary, and somebody chose it, and the choosing was done by a form in an office in a city where the arithmetic is correct, then who decides which communities deserve to survive — and what is a person to do on a Tuesday morning who has found out that the decision was arithmetic.**

Up the reach, a district that will not send a return at all until the Registry accepts rotating household proof and internal appeal. A ledger with a hundred and ninety-four lines and a door nobody has ever chosen anything by. Three hundred and seventy lines in a clerk's hand from the Concord Year 474, of which three hundred cost this county nothing whatever, and sixty-one were a stroke and no reason, and nine are in a settlement the office has already lost, and the sheet is in a book on a shelf in a room with no window and it may not leave the building.

A drawer under black cord with an index card behind a brass tab that says *never opened*, and a year on a public board in a column somebody else read, and a man of Alder Row who still has no name and must not acquire one.

Six persons in a ward of eleven doors at three of five parts, and the word is not written beside anybody's name and is not going to be, and the cost of the gap is five hours a week, which is a figure of hours and is not a person, and the hours of the person who is there do not stop.

**None of that is on the small board. The small board has eleven rows and eleven figures. Owen Rusk read the column on the Sunday morning of the twenty-eighth day of the fourth month of the Concord Year 488 and counted it twice and got eleven both times, and that is the entire job he has, and the count is clean, and a clean count is a count with nothing wrong in it, and the season of 489 is the cleanest count this county has ever certified, and it is clean because it is empty, and it is not a lie, and it is not a remedy, and legibility is not remedy, and the practice did not stop.**

It was the second board that he read twice and not the frame, and the woman who maintains the board stood at the far end of it with the cord round her arm, and she had not asked him what goes on that field in nine years and she asked him twice on that morning, and she said so before he could put either of them in a book.

"What did you count," she said, eventually.

"Eleven."

"**Once.**"

"Twice. I counted it twice, which I do not do in a field, and I got eleven both times, and the reason I counted it twice is that in about four years there is going to be a version of that sheet in a file in a building in another city with a heading on it and one row missing, and I will not be the man who has only counted it once."

"That is the reason a person gives," she said, "**and the reason a person gives is not the reason. The reason is that it is yours and you wanted to be sure. I have maintained that frame for nine years and I put a certification at the bottom of it under a strip of paper the size of a receipt because I will not put a sheet over a sheet, and nobody asked me and nobody could have told me no, and the reason I did it is not the reason I have given four times. It is that I had been on that ladder since half past six and my hands were cold and it was quicker.**" She put the cord in her apron. "**Both of those are true and the second one is the one that is always true and I have never seen anybody print the second one.**"

Then she went, and she asked him to put the lamp out, because she has done it every year for nine years and it is hers and not the county's, and he said that he would, and about half past eleven he read the third column once more on his own and it was the third time and there was nobody there to see it.

---

He put the lamp out himself, because the woman who maintains the board asked him to and because there is no form on which a person may take a lamp out of a field.
