# Volume 01 Index and Closure Report — *The Unfiled Ward*

Chapters 1–50, C.Y. 487. Written by the volume-close pass. This is an **audit document**. It creates no prose, opens no chapter, and introduces nothing that belongs to a later volume.

**How to check anything in this file.** Every claim carries a chapter number. Every claim that is a number or a phrase carries enough of the wording to be found in under a minute. Where this report says *the volume said X*, X is on the page. Where it says *the volume decided Y*, Y is the volume's act and is not always stated on the page, and the report says so.

**Authority.** This file is the operative index for Volume 01. Where it disagrees with any other state file, this file is right and the other file is stale, and §7 below names the places where that is currently true.

**The difference this report keeps making.** Volume 01 was built on the gap between *what the volume said* and *what the volume decided*. Almost everything expensive in it is a decision that was taken and then written into a document, a book, a hall or a hearing, and almost nothing expensive in it is a promise. A report that blurs those two things has described a different book.

## 2. The hook ledger

Every chapter, the hook it opened, and where that hook landed. Three states are used and the report does not blur them:

- **PAID** — the hook is closed on the page, and the report names the chapter that closes it.
- **OPEN BY DECISION** — the hook is deliberately not closed, and the report names **whose decision** that was. Never "for now."
- **RAISED, NOT RETURNED TO** — the hook is neither closed nor deliberately left open: the volume raises it and never mentions it again. This category is small and §2.3 says what is to be done about each item.

### 4.2 The renewals, the certification and the hold

- **The renewals: six, and there is no seventh.** Days **25, 32, 39, 46, 53, 60**, interval seven days, **every one for review and never binding** (Ch 19, 26, 30, 36, 40, 45). The observers were re-separated in writing, in their own hands, once at each renewal, and **a separation made once does not carry forward and a separation made once has never yet carried forward in this district** (Ch 50, in a reviewing officer's margin). **The Day 70 event is the expiry of a certification, not a renewal.**
- **The certification.** The standing question was asked at the bottom of the seal stair on the **Saturday of Day 61** (Ch 46, opening). The long room with the high windows is the **Monday afternoon of Day 63**; the seal is at **about half past three** (Ch 46). It runs **seven days** and expires **at the hour of the seal on Day 70**. The volume ends on Day 70, a Monday, **at ten minutes to three**, with **about forty minutes** of it left; at **ten past two** there was about an hour (Ch 50). The shape is load-bearing: 61 + 7 ≠ 70, and the two-day span is why the question and the signature are on different days.
- **One active hold, one thing only.** Subject: the last supported legal status of the Ninth Weir public crossing as it stood immediately before the roll entry of Day 14, and the appeal window of the Day 10 load-restriction suspension. Effect: *that status is preserved, and that window is kept open, and nothing else is altered.* **It does not make the work safe, funded, repaired or usable; the crossing remains closed to loads; it is not a grant, not an opening, and not a promise of either.** A hold does not survive the certification which carries it; where the certification is not renewed the hold falls with it, and the status is then the status it stood in before the seal, which is suspended. A hold **cannot be carried by an instrument renewed every seven days and not binding**, because a hold that expires with a review has not got a term, it has got a habit (Ch 45, Ch 46).
- **The list of five lines**, read out in Ch 46, carried in Ch 47, itemised in Ch 50, and **not discharged by sealing and not discharged by any renewal**. (1) Cobb Rennie's pension. (2) Six persons of this district named on a schedule held in the intake year 486, of whom three are children, with no protective return entered, three of the five required parts existing and two being Registry functions nobody in this city is paid to provide. (3) Four pages in a bundle, of which one carries a refusal, one is blank on purpose and two are unanswered. (4) The repair of the abutment, four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds, unfunded. (5) **The receipt** of the district and its witnesses, promised in the answer of Day 19, on a day to be named and an hour to be named, and not given.
- **The five required parts of a protective return, enumerated once, in Chapter 43, and that is the only enumeration in the volume:** two unrelated living witnesses; a current care act; a named local appeal route; **a named place at which the thing is kept**; **a named person who keeps it**, with a power in the office to put somebody else in that place without asking the ward. The seven-day review and the reservation of consent are printed on the same sheet and are **conditions of the instrument, not parts of it**. The custodian is the last two lines. **The two parts Lower Lock does not have are the place and the person.** (Chapter 15's four blanks are Owen's own count and Chapter 43 says so out loud; a four-part sheet may not be restored.)

### 5.3 The two names that went

**`VANE, H.`** — the receiving officer's string in the third column of a covering slip, a Ropewalk district counter, about **four hundred times a quarter for nine years**, about once every hour and a quarter of a working week. It is not restored, not paid for, not a symptom of anything, and nobody is ever told. **The worse finding is underneath it:** the name belongs to a man at 4 Lantern Walk whom a district office has failed to find twice in a quarter, and there is no first name for him in any register in Docketford, because for nine years the city has written to a surname and an initial.

**Hiram Cray** — fifty-one, of the water gate at the upriver end, **thirty years in the post and ten of them on that gate**, who carries a bundle up a stair, puts it on a ledge and says nothing, and whose whole function in this city is to be the last pair of hands between a piece of paper and a shelf. Owen copied the name about four times a week for six years out of the third column of a weekly post sheet — about a hundred and forty weeks, about two hundred and eighty lines in the big book, the same name in the third column of each. It went on **Day 65, at ten past three in the afternoon, in a ground-floor corridor with a bundle on his arm.** It did not come back. He got it out of the big book, read it twice, read the sentence that describes the work because the work is a fact and the name is a person, and then copied the space.

**Neither was a price anybody charged.** *There was no bill. There was no form with his name on it and no entry in a column and no seal and no instrument at all.* What it is, in his own ninety seconds in a lane: *the ordinary Tuesday texture of a thing that cannot be put down.*

**The naming rule, exactly as the prose keeps it.** The standing rule is that **each name is spoken once in the chapter in which its loss is registered.** The string `VANE, H.` therefore occurs in exactly **two** places in the whole of Volume 01: **Chapter 28**, where the loss is registered (written *Vane, H.*, with the comma and the full stop, as the Office had written a man for nine years), and **Chapter 47**, once, in the passage that counts the second loss. The name **Hiram Cray** occurs in exactly **two** places, **both in Chapter 47**: once as the name, and once in full and in capitals with his line of work — **HIRAM CRAY. Fifty-one. Water gate, upriver end. Thirty years in the post, ten of them on that gate. Carries bundles up a stair, puts them on a ledge, says nothing.** **Chapter 50 carries the man of fifty-one by his work and never by his name** — *a man of fifty-one who carries bundles up a stair and puts them on a ledge and says nothing, who is not anybody, and is not written in his register, and is not on this file, and is not to be told* — **and that is the form later prose must keep.**

**The standing prohibition, and it is absolute:** no later chapter may **restore, resolve, repeat, confess, price, write down or notice** either name, and no character may witness, remark on, or recover either. The **first** loss is additionally carried, in Volume 01, as a working condition in Chapters 28, 30 and 40 — *a man with a name-shaped hole in him and a tray of unrouteable correspondence in front of him is, above all things, an ordinary man on an ordinary day* — and that is registration, not repetition, and Volume 02 may not extend the technique to a third name, may not re-describe the condition, and may not have any other character perceive it.

## 8. A plain assessment

**What the volume actually did.** It took a man who was right about a document and made him a man who was right about a person, and it did it by removing the document he could hide behind. In seventy days of story time a ward of two hundred and forty people lost its crossing's standing because a tired man signed a receipt for his own house, and the ward got it back by becoming legally responsible for a duty it could not fund, and then narrowed the claim to what it could actually perform, and then had a clerk in Docketford certify the result with his own name on it and a five-line list attached. Along the way the Registry turned out to be careful rather than careless, its rules turned out to be four hundred and forty-three years old and right, its refusals turned out to be a form, and its one procedural victory for the protagonist turned out to be worth exactly nothing but a sentence.

**Its central sentence, which it does not say until Chapter 50.** *A man who keeps a thing alive by holding on to it has no business telling a ward what it may perform.* Ada Rusk says it in a hall in the fourth week of the month in front of twenty-two people, and Owen does not defend himself, and every single thing that happens afterwards is that sentence working. The second half, which is Ada's half and which the volume gives to a woman of twenty-four: the people who did the work were never the people he had been carrying.

**What is genuinely better about the world at Chapter 50 than at Chapter 1.** Lower Lock is not frozen. It has a lawful, narrow, seven-day-review district return with separately consenting adult branches, a withdrawal right in the ward's own capitals, a named appeal route with a person at the far end of it, and a person who has stood in a room and said out loud where she disagrees with the ward and told the room the ward is right. The ward's own schedule of performed acts is on a public file and readable at any counter, in the ward's own form, with the disclaimer printed three times and four blank pages sent blank on purpose. An office that has been named in a document has become carefuler, not kinder. A Deputy Registrar has put his own name at the head of a register of contested closures. A dead woman's residence status has been corrected by name, by batch, by office, with the delay recorded as a delay. There is a route, a hearing date that a clerk chose out of a list of rooms, and a transcript in which a man said *I did not lie, and I would sign it again*. And Owen Rusk can now stand in a hall and read out five unresolved things in order, without a sheet, and come back and do it again.

**What is precisely worse.** A crossing is closed to loads and will be closed to loads in the first week of the coming month, and the crack in the abutment has moved, and nobody in this city has a duty to look at it. Four thousand one hundred and sixty pounds is unfunded and the notice inviting a district to do the repair is legible to about four people a day. Nine people are four months unpaid. The pump is hand-primed at four in the morning on the worst nights. A seed stall with forty-one years on it is under a foot and a half of river and no instrument in Docketford has ever named it. Six children and non-responders have three of five parts of a thing that would protect them, and the two missing parts are a Registry function nobody has been paid for in nine hundred years, and a custodian's cost is now a number on a page and not a person. A man of fifty-eight who walks a child of twelve home every night is owed nineteen years and there is no column for it. Thirty-seven other people in a batch struck in one afternoon in the Concord Year 471 do not know they are in it. Sixteen districts are suspended on a column struck at eight in the morning and the fifteen who are not in the room are owed by nobody. A receipt promised on the nineteenth of the month has not been given in fifty-one days. A form will be printed forty-one times a quarter next quarter and the quarter after. And a man of fifty-one who carries bundles up a stair has stopped existing for one clerk in a building where nobody can notice.

**What a reviewer of the next volume should look for hardest.** Not for praise and not for criticism — for the five places where Volume 02 could quietly undo Volume 01 without meaning to.

1. **The unsealing of the brass tab.** Volume 01 ends with a hand flat on black cord and a choice made seven times. If Volume 02 opens the drawer for plot reasons, the volume's last act is retroactively an omission. If Volume 02 opens it at all, it must be for a reason that is about a filing and not about a secret, and the man of Alder Row must still have no name.
2. **The six protective returns.** The temptation is to let somebody buy or be assigned a custodian in Volume 02 because the story needs a child protected. That would be the volume's argument winning a point and losing the volume. The cost is five hours a week, the office may replace the person without asking, and the word is not written beside any of the four names.
3. **Cobb Rennie.** He is the most legible man in the case and the volume spent seventy days refusing to convert him into a device. A Volume 02 that lets him be rescued because he was brave on a bucket chain has undone the first line of the list by making it cheap.
4. **The four hundred and forty-three-year-old standard and the Office's good reasons.** Volume 01 refuses to make a villain out of careful people and ends with the same rule both ruining a ward and putting sixteen names in a room. A Volume 02 that produces a sympathetic administrator who explains the rule away, or a faceless one who enforces it silently, has picked a side Volume 01 refused to pick.
5. **The refusal to become a campaign.** The Office's answer is a form because a form is sufficient. The sixteen are still sixteen. If Volume 02 lets a single sheet, a single register or a single clerk's decision end the refusal-column practice, then the practice was visible in Volume 01 for nothing, and *visibility is not remedy* becomes a thing the first volume said and the second volume unsaid.

And one more, for the reviewers rather than the plot: **the two names.** They are the cheapest thing in the manuscript to undo by accident. A line of dialogue in which Owen pauses, or a scene in which Pell looks at him, or a sentence that says *he had begun to forget* — any of these converts a permanent, untellable cost into a symptom, and the volume's whole account of what a duty costs collapses.
