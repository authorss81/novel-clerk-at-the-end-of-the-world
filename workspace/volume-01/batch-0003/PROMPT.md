## Where the Batch Starts

It is **batch Day 28, a Monday**, the fourth week of the rain season, C.Y. 487. Day 1 was a Tuesday, so Day 7, 14, 21 and 28 are Mondays. Four things are already running when Chapter 21 opens, and none of them waits:

- **The renewal cycle.** District Return 487-LL was renewed **for review** on Day 25, not as a binding continuation, and the next renewal falls due **Day 32**, the first Wednesday of this batch. Every act performed between the twenty-eighth and the thirty-second is evidence for a review nobody can be certain of, and the ward knows that.
- **Stability Order 114 is signed and public.** Sixteen returns suspended together, Lower Lock sixth, appeal lies to the Glass Stair **on application**, and **no hearing date has been set**. The order is on a docket that anybody may read.
- **The answer's date is a date nobody can compute** — a day in the last week of a rain season whose end no instrument fixes — and the Glass Stair docket still carries **no hearing date**. These are two different documents and no clerk in Docketford may join them.
- **The material position has got worse, not better.** The granary is at three weeks and falling. The Fetter Lane pump grant is still suspended. The crossing is still closed to loads. The abutment repair is still four thousand one hundred and sixty and unfunded. The crew is four months in arrears. Four households and Cobb Rennie are on the standpipe rota.

## The Batch's Centre and Its Ending

This batch owns the **volume midpoint**. Its shape is:

1. Owen traces his mother's residence-status delay into the same redacted batch as the ward's, and finds that the delay was **not a single delay**. It is a **pattern**, and the pattern has dozens of other people in it, and every one of them waited.
2. He finds that **Lower Lock filed a valid return once, and was paid for it** — and the Registry's own precedent requires it to honour a renewed district return **if the ward can show current performance**.
3. The Registry's precedent therefore compels the ward to **perform in public what it has just been told it cannot afford**, on a calendar, in front of people who have the standing to check.
4. The **countersignature review standard** is exposed as an *issued* Council instrument rather than a local choice, and the Council's reasons surface.
5. The first beat that may touch the **three refused requests** and the **sixteen named returns** lands, and it may be paid only by more than one person and never by Owen alone.

**The volume's real hearing remains a Chapters 31–40 event and must not be pulled forward.** Stability Order 114's appeal route opens; it does not produce a hearing in this batch. Lound's second circular arrives in **Chapters 33–35**, not here.

## Rules That Govern This Batch

These are not suggestions. Violating one breaks the volume.

- **Owen is an assisting clerk on the second desk's supervised tray. He may sign the entry of a filing and the covering of papers, and nothing else.** His card forbids a continuation, a certification, a suspension and a hold, in those words. **He acquires no signing authority in this batch.** The ward's acts are performed by the ward; the renewals are entered by him; the findings about his mother belong to other people on other instruments. If a card below seems to need him to hold something, the card is wrong: take the authority off the instrument and put it on a person, a ward act, or a witness.
- **Stage 1, Sealed Witness, is acquired at the Chapter 46 certification, and the costs of it arrive there.** Do not spend them here and do not name the stage in the prose. **This is the batch where a name does not come.** Owen now holds a named duty and a renewal cycle that asks him things every week. The permanent loss belongs here; the near miss of Chapter 10 does not repeat, and the fact that it did not repeat in Batch 0002 is not a licence to forget it.
- **The return's instrument may not be quietly widened.** It claims a **duty** and disclaims safety, condition and use. Every new thing in this batch that touches Lower Lock is either an act the ward performs, a person who performs it, an observer who sees it, or a document about the *Office's* conduct. It is not about the bridge.

## The Ten Commitments for This Batch

1. Owen finds his mother's delay in the same redacted batch as the ward's, and finds that it is one of **dozens**, and that everyone who waited waited the same length of time.
2. The delay is shown as **a delay and nothing else** — no one is indicted, no one is punished, and the batch's own respect for the Office is intact.
3. Lower Lock is found to have **filed a valid return once and been paid for it**, and the Registry's own precedent is found on the shelf where precedents are kept.
4. The precedent compels performance in public, and the ward must now do what it has been told it cannot afford, **on a calendar, in front of checkers**.
5. Ada negotiates the terms of that performance **herself**, and Owen discovers he cannot protect her from it without overruling her.
6. The **countersignature review standard** is exposed as an issued Council instrument, with reasons, in a document, and not by a villain in a room.
7. **A name does not come.** Owen's named duty and his weekly cycle take something out of his working life that he cannot get back, and the chapter is about the fact that the cost is a name.
8. Material work: the granary at three weeks, the standpipe rota, unpaid hours, the repair nobody is funding, and a night on the weir path in rising water.
9. The **three refused requests** and the **sixteen named returns** are taken further, and **not by Owen alone**.
10. The batch ends with the ward's performance visible, the order on the docket, and Owen's own mother's name on a page in his own hand for the first time in his life.

### Chapter 0021 — The Same Batch

POV: Owen Rusk, third person limited.

Location/time: the unfiled correspondence tray, the Front Desk index room, and Lower Lock; Day 28, a Monday. The renewal is due on Wednesday the thirty-second and it is on the tray in front of him.

Immediate goal: get the ward's next seven days of acts into a shape that will survive a review on Wednesday, and find out what a *redacted batch* is called when you are not the person in it.

Resistance: the mechanics and the calendar. He is an assisting clerk on a tray, he is not the ward, and the acts are performed by the ward at four in the morning; his job is to be a place where the record waits. He learns that a redaction in Docketford is a **batch** with a number, that a batch is a set of entries struck in one sitting by one officer, and that the batch his mother's delay sits in is a batch of **forty-one entries**. He also learns that a redacted batch may be produced for inspection by a person named in it, and that he is the only person named in Marta Rusk's entry, and that naming himself is an act he cannot un-name.

Information revealed, in the room and not in a lecture: a **residence status** is the entry that places a person at an address for the purposes of priority medicine, priority ration, and burial. Marta Rusk's was *pending revision* for eleven days in a flood-year quarter, and the eleven days are a fact with a number in it, and the number is not the fever's cause. Owen reads his mother's entry in a room with a window and does not cry and does not write anything next to it, and the not-writing is now a habit he recognises.

Action or decision: he asks for the **batch**, lawfully, in a request register that will keep a copy, and does not copy the batch. He takes away three things: the batch number, the officer's initials, and the date the batch was struck. He tells nobody in the Office. He does not tell the ward.

Emotional change: the case stops being about his ward for about four hours, and it is worse and better than he expected, because the eleven days were a bureaucratic interval and his mother was a person and both of those are true and the Office did one of them on purpose, in a sitting, with a number.

Power or resource change: no instrument and no authority. A batch number, an initial, a date. The first time in a month his own family has been in the file at all.

Continuity fact to preserve: Marta Rusk's residence status was **pending revision for eleven days** in a flood-year quarter, and the delay is recorded in a redacted batch of **forty-one entries**. Her burial was disputed for **eight months** after her death and that is a separate, longer figure. Nothing is restored by finding the batch. The inquiry is not reopened and Owen files nothing in Docketford about his mother in this batch.

Ending type: Discovery, with a private cost.

Next-chapter pull: forty-one entries in one batch, one officer, one sitting, and the only way to see the other forty is to be a person who is named in one of them or to be a person the district office chooses to show them to. And the ward's Tuesday marker is at seven forty, and Wednesday is the thirty-second.

### Chapter 0022 — Forty-One

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: the Front Desk index room and the copying floor; Day 29. Investigation and character work.

Immediate goal: find out how many other entries are in the batch, and whether the eleven days are his mother's or the batch's.

Resistance: the batch is a redacted batch, and a redacted batch is not a document a clerk may read; it is a document a clerk may *be told the size of*. The Office will produce the **schedule** of a redacted batch, because a schedule is a list of numbers and a list of numbers is not a record of a person, and producing it costs nobody anything. Owen is given the schedule, which is a list of forty-one entry numbers, and he learns that **the eleven days are the batch's, not his mother's**: forty-one entries sat pending revision in one sitting in one quarter, and every one of them sat for the same eleven days, and the eleven days were a quarter's arrears worked off in one afternoon by a man who did not open any of them.

Information revealed, in the room: what a batch is, and what a schedule of one is, and that the Office's disclosure habits are consistent to the point of being a character trait. Owen discovers that three of the forty-one numbers are numbers he has **copied** — they are on the Fourth River Desk's route lists from previous quarters — and that he has been a *mechanism* in this without knowing it, and that this is a thing he can neither un-know nor put on a ward's return.

Action or decision: he does not make a list of forty-one names. He has no right to forty names he has never spoken to, and he says so out loud to Pell, and she tells him the correct procedure, which is that a person who may be affected by an entry may ask for it, and that forty people must each ask, and that the Office knows this and does not make it easy. Owen asks her one question: has anyone ever asked forty people at once. She says no, and then says that nobody has ever needed to until now, which is not a compliment and is not a criticism.

Emotional change: Owen's private, furious, personal grievance is shown to be a **class**, and the discovery makes it smaller and much heavier at the same time. He is not special. Forty-one people waited eleven days, and nobody knows their names, and he has three of them in his own hand from a route list.

Power or resource change: no authority. A schedule of forty-one numbers, and a procedure that requires forty separate people to do forty separate things, which is the whole of what the Office has built.

Continuity fact to preserve: the redacted batch contains **forty-one entries**, struck in one sitting by one officer, all of them pending revision for the same **eleven days**. Marta Rusk is **not the first or the worst**; she is one of forty-one, and Owen has three of the other forty numbers in his own hand as a copyist. **Nobody but Owen may ask for an entry he is named in, and the forty other people have not been asked.**

Ending type: Cost, and a structural discovery.

Next-chapter pull: the only instrument in Docketford that would let one person see the other forty is a **district return's schedule** or a **ward roll produced in a review**, and there is a review on Wednesday, and the ward's return has a list of eight branches that a granary committee has been keeping by hand. And at Mill Ward, Voss's counter has a rack in it with eleven bundles in it that Owen counted in Chapter 12 and has not thought about since.

### Chapter 0023 — What the Registry Did Once

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: the district office record room, the copying floor, and the ward hall; Day 30. Discovery and the volume's first precedent.

Immediate goal: find out whether the Registry has ever, in living memory, honoured a community return of its own — and if it has, what it paid for it.

Resistance: precedents are kept in a **precedent volume**, and a precedent volume is the one set of books in Docketford that cannot be read by a person who is not arguing a case. Owen has a case: the ward's return, and a claim in it that a duty performed is a duty. He discovers that precedents are cited **by number and not by name**, that a precedent can be produced on a point of law, and that the number he wants cannot be guessed at — which means he has to go and look at an index of numbers, which is the Chapter 14 exercise again, and the Office keeps a copy of the request.

Information revealed, in the room: the Registry of Continuant Works has recognised a community return **before**. It did so in a flood year, in a district with no money, on a public work, and it did so **in writing and for money**: it recognised the return, and it entered an annual sum against the named work, and it called the sum a **maintenance grant**, and it did it *because the district had performed the duty in public and had produced a roll and two witnesses, and the Office had a precedent for refusing and did not want one.* That is the precedent. It is a real instrument, it is in the volume, it has a number, and it is **four hundred and eleven words long** for reasons nobody now remembers.

Action or decision: Owen does not go and tell the ward he has won. He goes and reads the precedent four times, and then he does the thing the batch's shape requires, which is to find out what the precedent **compels** — and the answer, which he extracts himself from the form of it over an afternoon, is the batch's hinge: the precedent does not compel the Office to honour a return. **It compels the Office not to refuse one without a schedule of its own.** And a schedule is a list of every district the Office has refused, with the reason, in one sitting, in a numbered column, published.

Emotional change: Owen wins an argument and it is not the argument he wanted. The precedent does not say what a ward is owed; it says what an office must publish about itself. He understands, for the first time, that the Registry's own record of its conduct is a **public list**, and that the fight he is in is not to be forgiven but to be **listed**.

Power or resource change: no instrument and no authority. A precedent number, the fact that the Registry has paid a maintenance grant on a community return before, and the discovery that a **refusal schedule** is the thing he actually needs and does not have.

Continuity fact to preserve: the Registry of Continuant Works has **precedent** for recognising a community return and entering a **maintenance grant** against a named public work, on the ground that the district performed the duty in public with a roll and two witnesses. The precedent compels the Office **not to refuse a return without publishing a schedule of its refusals**. It does not compel the Office to honour a return, and it does not create an entitlement to money. **The grant for Lower Lock's crossing is still not restored and is not made whole inside Volume 01.**

Ending type: Discovery, with a reversal inside it.

Next-chapter pull: what the ward has to do to be the kind of district the precedent talks about is now written down somewhere, and it is a **roll** and **witnesses** and **performance**, and the ward has a roll and two witnesses and a marker, and it has a renewal on Wednesday. And Ada is going to read about it before Owen does, because the granary committee reads everything.

### Chapter 0024 — What Ada Does With It

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: the ward hall, the granary, and the copying floor; Day 31. Character conflict and the batch's turn toward the protagonist's flaw.

Immediate goal: keep the precedent out of the ward's hands until he has worked out what it would do to them.

Resistance: he fails, and he fails in the most ordinary way possible. **Ada has read it.** Not the precedent volume — Ada cannot read a precedent volume — but Marguerite Bray, who reads everything, has been lent a copy of the schedule of the precedent case by a man at the mill, and Bray has read it aloud in the granary on Saturday, and by Monday the ward knows that the Registry once paid a ward for a bridge and that the reason it paid was that the ward *kept doing the thing where people could watch*. He gets to the hall to find a granary committee meeting that is not a granary meeting.

What Ada has done with it is the batch's real work. She has read the precedent's **conditions** — a roll, two witnesses, performance, and a **published schedule** — and she has worked out, faster than Owen, that the ward already satisfies the first three and cannot satisfy the fourth, and that the fourth is the Office's, not the ward's. And she has then done the thing Owen was afraid of: she has asked the granary committee, in a room, whether they are prepared to be **checked in public, on a calendar, by a person from another district**, for as long as the thing runs. And they have said, in a way that costs them something, that they will do it if and if only the wage comes out of it.

Information revealed: what performance-in-public actually means to a woman who has to hand-prime a pump at four in the morning — that it is not a duty, it is a **shift**, and that a shift has to be rostered, and that a roster is a document, and that a document can be checked by a stranger who was not there. Ada's terms are the batch's real negotiation and Owen is not a party to it.

Action or decision: Owen tries to slow it down, because his instinct is to slow everything down, and Ada tells him — in front of the committee, not in private, deliberately — that he is doing the thing she has been telling him he does since the seventh day of the month, and that a man who keeps a thing alive by holding on to it has no business telling a ward what it may perform. He is humiliated in a room of twenty people and does not defend himself, and the humiliation is correct.

Emotional change: Owen's first clear, public, unambiguous loss to his sister, in front of people, and the first time he understands that being right and being *able to be overruled by the people he is helping* are the same skill he does not have.

Power or resource change: no instrument. **Ada has negotiated a set of terms for public performance and the granary committee has a condition attached: the wage.** Which is money, which nobody has, and which is now on the record in a ward book in Marguerite Bray's hand.

Continuity fact to preserve: Ada's terms for the ward's public performance are **hers**, written in the ward's book, and they require a **roster**, which makes performance a shift and a shift a checkable document. The granary committee's condition is **the wage**, and the wage is not funded and is not funded anywhere in this volume. **Owen's terms of engagement with the ward do not survive the chapter.**

Ending type: Character conflict, lost.

Next-chapter pull: the renewal is due on Wednesday and the ward has just accepted terms it cannot meet, and a person from another district has to be found who is willing to come and check a roster, and there are about four people in the delta who could hold the far end of an appeal route and two of them are women Nell Arbour has had words with. And Owen is going to have to go and be told no kindly.

### Chapter 0025 — Be Told No Kindly

### Chapter 0026 — The Schedule of Refusals

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: the district office record room and the Glass Stair; Day 33, a Wednesday, after the morning's renewal. Investigation, political pressure.

Immediate goal: get the Office to produce a **schedule of refusals** for the current quarter, on the precedent, in public, before Friday.

Resistance: the precedent compels publication only on a *point of law* argued by a party. Owen is not a party. He has three moves and two of them are bad. The move that works is the oldest one in the building and it is Ada's: **a district return's schedule is a public document at a review**, and a review was held on Friday, and anything produced at a review is a public document, and the Office has therefore already published a schedule — the one the ward's own review made, which lists one return and one order. The rest of the column is in the Deputy Registrar's office, in a book, on a shelf, and the argument that gets it onto a public surface is the argument that the **sixteen** and the **one** are the same schedule and cannot be published separately without making one of them a lie.

Information revealed, in the room: what a refusal schedule actually is, which is duller and worse than anything in this case so far. It is a numbered column, a quarter, an officer's initials, and a reason in a standard form, and there are **one hundred and nine** entries in the Docketford quarter, and **forty-one of the dispositions in the current quarter are the same four words**, and the reason is not a finding about any of them. Owen is not proud of what he has and is not sorry for it either, and this time he says so out loud to somebody.

Action or decision: he does not enter an inquiry. He **publishes a comparison**, in a room with a queue outside it, of the four words against the ward's return and against the grant file, and lets the record do the work. It is the first thing in the volume that Owen has done by *not* filing, and Pell tells him it is also the first thing he has done that the Office cannot quietly reverse, and that it will make him enemies who are not enemies yet.

Emotional change: Owen discovers that a fact, once printed in the right room, is the only kind of thing in this city that cannot be quietly corrected, and that he has spent a month learning to file and has not once learned to print.

Power or resource change: no instrument and no authority. **A public comparison, a queue, and a document that cannot be unsaid.** And the first evidence that the batch of forty-one is not an aberration but a quarter's practice.

Continuity fact to preserve: the Docketford refusal schedule for the quarter is **one hundred and nine entries**, and **forty-one dispositions are the same four words in the same form**, and the batch Owen traced his mother into is one instance of a practice, not an accident. Owen publishes a **comparison**, not an inquiry. Nobody in the Office is a villain and the refusal column is correct on its face.

Ending type: Political pressure, and a door opened loudly.

Next-chapter pull: the comparison is public, and the Office has to answer a question in public, and the question it will be asked is not about Lower Lock at all but about the forty-one, and Owen has discovered that he has just made himself the person who counts things for a living. And the ward's renewal is done, and the order is still on the docket, and the sixteen are still sixteen.

### Chapter 0027 — The Standard Was Issued

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: the seal stair, the Glass Stair, and the copying floor; Days 34–35. Discovery of the batch's fourth debt.

Immediate goal: find out who issued the countersignature review standard and on what grounds, and get the answer in a document rather than a corridor.

Resistance: the standard has no Docketford number and answers to no local office, and in nine years not one clerk in the building could name its author. It cannot be named. What *can* be done is the thing that has been available since Chapter 10 and has never been done: **a standard is issued by somebody and recorded somewhere**, and where it is recorded is the Council's own issue list, and the issue list is a public document of the Provisional Council because a Council instrument must be discoverable by anyone it binds, and Pike said in Chapter 3 that he had never needed to know and that the reason is that it had never once been the reason a thing he wanted did not happen.

Information revealed, in the document: the standard was **issued**, in a year in the first half of the century, on the stated ground that community returns in flood quarters produced instruments that local offices could not examine in time and that had to be examined twice by somebody with no district. The Council's own reasons are printed on the issue list, and the reasons are **good**, and the effect has been that no community return in Docketford has been examinable without a second officer, and the second officer is appointed from a roll of officers who do not live in the district — and that roll is small, and it is made up of officers like Pell. Owen's professional life is explained to him by a piece of paper and it is not flattering.

Action or decision: Owen does not attack the standard and does not call it a plot. He does the thing the volume has been building: he reads it **as issued**, which means he applies it to himself, and he asks Pell, in front of the hall, the one question nobody has asked in nine years — whether she is on the review roll, and if so, whether she was put on it, and by whom. She answers, and the answer is short, and it is worse for her than anything Owen could have said.

Emotional change: Owen finds that the rule which has been ruining his month was written by careful people for a good reason and has been doing exactly what it was written to do, and that his own discomfort is not evidence of anything. He does not enjoy this. He has been waiting for a villain for a month and the villain is a policy with a rationale and a date.

Power or resource change: no instrument. The standard's issuer, its stated ground, and the composition of the review roll — and the fact that Pell's position on it is the reason she can be attacked and the reason she was useful. **Ansel Rive is not named in person and does not appear.**

Continuity fact to preserve: the countersignature review standard is a **Council instrument with a recorded issue and recorded reasons**, and the reasons are sound. The review roll is made of officers appointed from outside the districts they examine. **No officer in Docketford is a villain.** Lound's second circular is **not** in this batch.

Ending type: Discovery that refuses to be a villain.

Next-chapter pull: Owen has now found the rule, the precedent, the schedule and the batch, and the ward still has no wage and a crossing nobody will fund, and the order is still on a docket, and Ada's roster needs a wage to be real. And a name that has come to him four hundred times a quarter for nine years has stopped coming, and this time it is not a near miss.

### Chapter 0028 — The Name

POV: Owen Rusk.

Location/time: the copying floor, the tray, and Lower Lock; Day 35 or 36. Emotional consequence. **This is the batch's cost chapter and it is the permanent one.**

Immediate goal: get through a week's copying and a week's tray work and a week's renewal cycle without losing anything, and he cannot.

Resistance: he holds a **named duty** now — a return, entered on a supervised tray, renewed every seven days, on which eight people's acts are recorded and which asks him things every week for the rest of his life. And a name that has come to him four hundred times a quarter for nine years comes, and does not come back. Not a near miss. **It does not come back in the notebook either**, because the notebook does not have it either, because the notebook never had it: he copied the wording of a forgery into a notebook for exactly this reason and not for a name.

Information revealed, in the work and not in a speech: a hold costs a name, and the name does not announce itself, and the only symptom is that a person who has been in your hands four hundred times a quarter is gone from your hands, and nobody in the building can tell you, because the absence looks exactly like a clerk who did not read that line today. Owen does not get a dramatic horror. He gets a **gap**, and a bad afternoon, and a sentence at the end of it that is a number.

Action or decision: he does not tell the ward and he does not tell Pell and he does not write it in the ledger, because the ledger is the second copy of a forgery and he has decided in Chapter 14 what a private book is for. He finds the name in the end, on the fourth day, **at the tray**, from a letter in the unrouteable post that has the name on the outside of it, and the finding it is worse than losing it. He does not tell anybody. Ada, who has been watching him for a month, tells him in the doorway of the shed that he has stopped writing things down, and Owen says that he has, and Ada says *no you have not, you have written less, and that is worse*, which is correct and is the only conversation any of them have about it.

Emotional change: Owen meets the specific fear of his life — becoming an official whose accuracy has a term and a price — and it is not frightening, it is **boring**, and that is the real horror of it, and it does not go away and he does not tell anyone, and the chapter does not resolve.

Power or resource change: **the permanent cost lands and stays.** He holds a named duty and the duty has taken a thing out of his working life. He is still Stage 0 in every way the book can measure, and the cost of Stage 0 has arrived early and unasked for, and nothing in the volume is ever said to pay for it.

Continuity fact to preserve: a named duty and a weekly review cycle take **names** out of Owen's working life, as exact sensory detail and as names that do not come. **One name is gone and does not come back.** It is not a near miss, it is not restored, it is not a symptom of anything else, and it is not paid for by anybody's forgiveness. He has not yet acquired a preservation hold and must not be described as having one; what he has is a duty, and the duty is taking the name.

Ending type: Cost, permanent, and quiet.

Next-chapter pull: Owen has a name-shaped hole and a duty and nobody he can tell, and the ward has a roster and no wage, and the schedule of refusals is public, and the Office has to answer about forty-one dispositions in a room with a queue outside it. And the ward has to perform in public on a day that has not been fixed, because the answer's date is a day in a season nobody can compute.

### Chapter 0029 — What Was Refused, and to Whom

POV: Owen Rusk, with **Nell Arbour** in the room in the specific capacity she has agreed to and no other.

Location/time: the Front Desk, the copying floor, and Lower Lock; Day 36. Investigation, and the batch's second refusal beat.

Immediate goal: get the three refused requests of the backdating window out of a register and into a public record, without Owen being the one who asks, and without the Office ever having to explain them.

Resistance: the refusals are the **Fetter Bend sub-table's**, and a sub-table is not a district office, and a sub-table's refusal register is not a district return, not a petition, and not a public document. Nobody in Docketford can compel its production. The route that works is the one Chapter 20 already set up and it is a **precedent** argument: the precedent case shows the Office publishing a schedule of refusals, and the argument is that a sub-table's refusals are refusals, and the Office's own precedent does not distinguish. It is a thin argument. It works because the man at the Glass Stair counter is bored and because the precedent is on the page and because nobody wants to be the office that says a sub-table's refusals are a different kind of thing.

Information revealed: what a sub-table's refusal register is, which is a **much shorter book than the district's** and a worse one, and the number in it, and that the three refusals Owen surrendered in Chapter 5 are a **small** number in it and not a large one. Owen learns that the sub-table refused **nine** things for that named place in that year and that the ninth was the one he surrendered, and that eight of the nine are not his to ask about. He wants to. Pell stops him with the sentence that this batch's whole argument turns on: **he is not the injured party.**

Action or decision: Owen does what Ada did in Chapter 24 and what the precedent demands: he does not ask. He **gives the eight to the ward**, with the register number and the dates and nothing else, and the ward's own steward asks them, one at a time, in her own hand, in a book she has kept for fifty-one years. **The three refused requests are taken further, and it is not by Owen alone.** Nothing is restored. His withdrawn charge stays withdrawn. The Office still has not explained anything.

Emotional change: Owen gives up a thing he has wanted for a month, in the open, on purpose, and finds that the ward's asking is better than his asking, and that he has been protecting a grievance rather than a person. He is not humble about it. He notices, and writes it down, and finds that writing it down is the habit and the habit is the problem.

Power or resource change: no instrument. **A register number, a count of nine, and a ward's hand-kept book with eight new lines in it** — which is more than Owen has ever had and less than he wanted, and it is the first time in the volume that a person other than Owen has moved a document.

Continuity fact to preserve: the **Fetter Bend sub-table refused nine things** for that named place in that year, and the **ninth** is the one Owen surrendered. Owen is not the injured party and cannot ask. The **eight remaining refusals are asked about by Marguerite Bray, in her own book, at the ward's own instance.** The Office has still never explained the three, nothing is restored, and the charge remains **scope**.

Ending type: Discovery with a surrender in it.

Next-chapter pull: the ward has nine questions and a book, and a public schedule of one hundred and nine refusals, and a wage it has not got, and a performance it has agreed to in public. And Owen's mother is a line in a batch of forty-one and the batch is a practice and not an accident, and the volume's hearing is still four batches away.

### Chapter 0030 — Performance

POV: Owen Rusk, third person limited, with Ada Rusk carrying the close.

Location/time: the ward hall, the granary, the weir path, and the copying floor; Days 37–38, the last days of the batch. The batch's climax in the material register.

Immediate goal: get the ward to perform in public, on a day, in front of a person from another district, with a roster and no wage — and to write the first day of it into the return as an act.

Resistance: the material conditions are against it and there is no villain to fight. The granary is at three weeks and the committee has voted the wage as a condition, so the crew is not paid and the roster has unpaid names on it. The pump is at the standpipe rota with four households and Cobb Rennie on it. The weather is the fourth week of the rain season and the river is up. And the one thing nobody in the ward has said out loud is that a roster checked in public by a stranger is not a document about a bridge, it is a document about **every hour of eight people's working lives**, and publishing it makes those hours reviewable by anybody who turns up. Ada says this in the hall and it is the most frightening thing anyone says in the batch, and she is right, and they do it anyway.

Information revealed, in the work: what a **roster** is when it belongs to a ward rather than to a mill — a thing with names and hours and two witnesses and a place to complain, which is the ward's own return's grammar applied to a task rather than to a crossing, and which is therefore the ward's first *repeatable* act rather than its first *claim*. And what it costs to be checked: a person is answerable for an hour they were not there.

Action or decision: they perform. All of them: the marker, the channel clearance, the pump prime, the granary double count, the standpipe rota, and the roster itself being read aloud and checked. Owen is present and is **not allowed to hold anything**, which is the chapter's discipline: he is an assisting clerk, he is not the ward, and his entire function is to write down the act and to sign the entry. He is given one job, which is to read back what the outside checker has said, and he does it badly the first time and Ada makes him do it again, and the second time is right.

Emotional change: Owen watches a thing he had nothing to do with, and finds that the only feeling available is the recognition that **the ward did not need him and the document needed him and those are different things.** He does not get pride. He gets a Tuesday.

Power or resource change: **the ward has performed in public and the performance is recorded**, which is what the precedent asks for, and **it has cost it a roster, a wage it was not paid, four days of the pump at the standpipe, and eight people's hours made reviewable.** The grant machinery is not restored. The pump grant is not restored. The crossing is not safe, not open, not funded, not repaired. Stability Order 114 stands and is public. The return continues. **Nothing is won and everything has been done.**

Continuity fact to preserve: Lower Lock **performs in public** before an outside checker with a **roster**, and the act is recorded in the return. This is the first instance in the volume of the ward keeping a **repeatable** act rather than making a claim, and it is the ground the Registry's own precedent requires. **It restores no money and no status.** The renewals continue, the next due **Day 39** and then every seven days, and the volume's hearing remains a Chapters 31–40 event. The six protective returns, Cobb Rennie's pension, the brass tab and Lound's second circular are all still open.

Ending type: Earned hook, and the volume turns.

Next-chapter pull: Batch 0004 is **Public Appeal**, and the things it needs are all now in the world: a ward that has performed, a schedule of one hundred and nine refusals with forty-one identical dispositions, a redacted batch of forty-one entries and a practice that is not an accident, a Council standard with reasons, a stability order on a public docket with no hearing date, an answer's date in a season nobody can compute, a name that does not come back, and a mother whose eleven days were one of forty-one. The four dissenters' branch is owed and has not yet been offered, and Ada's wage has not been funded, and on the ward's own book, in a hand Owen did not write, there is a line about Alder Row dated after that ward was officially closed, which is Chapters 34–36's discovery and not this one's.
